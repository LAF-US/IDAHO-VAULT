If someone opened a box 250 years from now, what would tell the story of Idaho?

State leaders hope it’s a patriotic mascot, a cutting-edge computer chip, tribal artifacts, and a symbol of Idaho’s role in nuclear energy.

![Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/6f251bf1-00e6-48d7-8365-570820fe299b.gif)

Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow

As part of America’s 250th anniversary celebration, a time capsule has now been sealed and buried at Independence National Historical Park in Philadelphia. It won’t be opened until 2276.

Every state, U.S. territory, and all three branches of the federal government contributed items. Idaho’s congressional delegation selected pieces highlighting the state’s tribal heritage, agricultural roots, and growing role in advanced manufacturing and energy innovation.

The collection for Idaho includes:

![Highlander - 2:1 - June 2026](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f8979e92-c78b-4b98-a8b1-22a788385c9b.gif)

Highlander - 2:1 - June 2026

- **Tribal items** from the Nez Perce, Shoshone-Bannock, and Coeur d’Alene tribes, including a copy of the 1855 Nez Perce Treaty, beadwork, tribal publications, and other cultural items that reflect Idaho’s Indigenous history and traditions.
- A **Spuddy Buddy plush**, representing Idaho’s potato industry and the generations of farmers who have made the state the nation’s leading potato producer.
- An **Idaho National Laboratory challenge coin**, recognizing the lab’s decades of nuclear research, from producing the world’s first usable electricity from nuclear power in 1951 to today’s work on next-generation reactors, including the Antares microreactor.
- A **Micron HBM4 memory chip**, which the delegation said was the world’s most advanced memory chip in 2026. The chip represents Boise’s growing role in semiconductor manufacturing and artificial intelligence as Micron has invested $50 billion to build two leading-edge memory fabs at its Boise campus, [as BoiseDev reported.](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/06/12/micron-boise-second/)

You can read more [about the collection here.](https://www.crapo.senate.gov/media/newsreleases/idaho-delegation-submits-time-capsule-items-for-americas-250th-anniversary)

In a letter accompanying the submission, Idaho’s delegation said the collection reflects “the past, present, and future of Idaho” through tribal culture, agriculture, scientific research, energy innovation, and manufacturing. [According to a press release from America250](https://america250.org/time-capsule/contents/#ID), the Gem State’s submissions also feature a letter from current Idaho Governor Brad Little and an Idaho Star Garnet.

The time capsule was officially sealed on July 4 and includes contributions from all 50 states, Washington D.C., five U.S. territories, and national organizations.
