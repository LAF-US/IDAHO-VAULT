Those of us who’ve been around Idaho politics for a minute understand an important truth:

This is Mike Moyle’s world. We’re just living in it.

Moyle is a brilliant politician who plays the game masterfully. In my 16-year career, I’ve never seen anyone who can get what he wants, every single session, with such consistency.

What Moyle wants, Moyle gets. It really is that simple.

If you need proof, look no further than the Idaho Transportation Department’s State Street campus debacle.

In 2023, state officials sold the sprawling campus to a developer for $51 million. The very next session, the state’s budget committee moved to block the sale. Some lawmakers, bizarrely, argued that keeping and renovating the flood-damaged main building was the more taxpayer-friendly option. It wasn’t.

Moyle led the charge to keep the campus, pushing through a budget bill that forced the state to retain and upgrade the property.

On the other side stood then–Senate Pro Tem Chuck Winder, who correctly believed that offloading the property would generate an immediate cash infusion and future property tax revenue.

For days, the two chambers were locked in a standoff. The Senate voted down Moyle’s ITD budget bill, throwing the end of the session into chaos.

The budget committee met again and, once more, sided with Moyle — advancing a nearly identical bill to block the sale.

Winder objected on parliamentary grounds. Lt. Gov. Scott Bedke, presiding over the Senate, upheld the objection. The Senate backed Bedke’s ruling.

So what happened next?

The budget committee sent over a third version of the ITD budget — this time changing spending by a mere $200 to sidestep the procedural objection. The House passed it and adjourned for the year. The Senate eventually took it up and approved it on an 18–17 vote.

Moyle declared victory.

That’s who Mike Moyle is. He gets what he wants.

Now we’re watching Sen. Jim Guthrie block two immigration bills that conservatives believe would help curb illegal immigration.

And here’s the question: If Moyle wants those bills to pass, can he make it happen?

History says yes.

He can hold priority Senate bills. He can stall or reshape budgets. He can trade, negotiate, pressure, and maneuver better than anyone in the building. He has demonstrated — repeatedly — that he can force the Senate’s hand when he decides something matters enough.

We already know what he’s capable of.

The predictable defense will be that Moyle “did his job” by moving the bills through the House and sending them to the Senate.

But that’s a reductionist argument — and frankly, it undersells him.

Mike Moyle is not some passive participant in the legislative process. He is the most skilled dealmaker in Idaho politics. To judge him only by what one chamber does or doesn’t do ignores the power he has wielded time and again.

If these immigration bills die, it won’t be because Moyle lacked the ability to act.

It will be because he chose not to.

So the real question isn’t whether Mike Moyle can save the bills.

It’s whether he will.
