## ‘There is no funding’: City of Eagle has no budget for now to complete foothills shooting range

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Site-Photos_Willow-Creek_Overview-rotated.jpg)

Site of Eagle Shooting Sports Park. Photo: City of Eagle

If you were hoping to get some range time with your rifle in the foothills near Eagle anytime soon, you’re out of luck.

The City of Eagle, starting under former Mayor Jason Pierce, has talked about [plans for an 80-acre shooting sports park on a 300-acre piece of land](https://boisedev.com/news/2022/11/09/you-asked-what-is-the-status-of-the-two-shooting-ranges-in-eagle/) in the Eagle foothills for more than two years. The project was originally envisioned to have separate ranges for archery and 3D archery, ranges for rifles, shotguns and pistols as well as a separate locked law enforcement range for training at the city-owned and managed facility.

The city plans to complete its construction of the archery ranges next year, but beyond the more than half a million dollars needed for that portion of the project, there is no more money in the city budget to continue building the rest of the park for firearm practice.

![LONG Building - May 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/9dba9691-8b04-405d-8e90-9017ac05eb4b.jpg)

This is one of several large projects started under the Pierce administration, alongside the [Regional Sports Park along Highway 16](https://boisedev.com/news/2022/12/21/eagle-inks-deals-with-spring-valley-developer-for-10-million-sports-park-purchase-donated-land-for-gun-range/) currently under construction and the [purchase of the Jackson House for a yet-to-be-determined public use](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/06/07/jackson-eagle-repairs/). Other projects on the City of Eagle’s capital plan include the long-planned extension of Old Park Place in downtown Eagle, the extension of Aikens Street, safety upgrades to Eagle City Hall and an extension of Linder Road.

A $300,000 project to repair concrete at the Eagle Library branch was also recently added to the plan, at the expense of two other projects, due to “significant issues with liability and tripping hazards.”

Eagle City Council also wrestled with a budget deficit earlier this year after the city council opted both to not increase property taxes and add three new deputies to the Eagle Police Department, [leaving the city’s law enforcement costs outpacing revenues.](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/08/16/eagle-budget-police/)

## ‘There is no additional funding’

On Tuesday, Eagle City Council discussed its capital plan for infrastructure projects through fiscal year 2029. And beyond a final $100,000 needed to install signage and other finishing touches on the facility, there isn’t any more money available in the outer years to start on the bigger items of the park.

The conversation began with City Council President Helen Russell noting the “significant” public comments the city gathered on the plan and the high support for the shooting sports park getting finished. She asked city staff if there are plans to allocate more funds to finish the project soon. A memo from the city’s open house and public outreach on the capital plan showed 21 comments wanted to increase funding for the shooting sports park, while six marked it as a project they’d like removed from the capital plan.

Eagle’s Director of Long Range Planning Nichoel Baird Spencer said “there is no funding” beyond what is already planned.

“There is no additional funding in (fiscal year 2026 through 2029) which means the city has no intent to fund beyond that,” she said. “That doesn’t mean we can’t go after grants, but it will be harder without (funds to use for a match.) It is up to the council if that is a project you want to start saving for. This wouldn’t change this scenario while we are in a savings mode until there is funding appropriate for a scoped project.”

City Council Member Melissa Gindlersperger asked about potential options to dedicate more to the project, but Baird Spencer was frank about the high cost of the project and what the city had available.

“Phase 1 of the shooting sports park, which is the least amount of construction on the site was over $500,000,” she said, referencing the work to open the archery range. “Even if you set aside all of (those funds), you’d probably have a challenge moving forward.”
