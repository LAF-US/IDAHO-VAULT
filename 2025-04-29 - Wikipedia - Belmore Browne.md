**Belmore Browne** (1880 – May 2, 1954) was an American artist and explorer. He was known for his painted [dioramas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diorama "Diorama") in natural history museums.

## Early life

Belmore Browne was born in 1880 in [Tompkinsville, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tompkinsville,_New_York "Tompkinsville, New York"), to Ella Haskell and George Browne Jr. His father was a lieutenant colonel in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War").[^1] [^2] The following year, the family went to Europe and he spent six summers in Switzerland or the [Italian Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Lakes "Italian Lakes").[^3] At the age of eight, he traveled to [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") with his family.

In 1889, the family settled in [Tacoma, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma,_Washington "Tacoma, Washington"), and his father worked in the lumber business. He, along with his two brothers, attended private schools in the eastern United States. Following admission into college, he worked briefly for his father's lumber business. He studied at the [New York School of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_School_of_Art "New York School of Art") and [Académie Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_Julian "Académie Julian").[^2] [^3]

## Career

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7d/Outing_%281885%29_%2814773346311%29.jpg/250px-Outing_%281885%29_%2814773346311%29.jpg)

Illustration by Browne in The Outing Magazine (1917)

From 1901 to 1912, Browne was an illustrator for magazines.[^4] He worked briefly for a ranch east of the Cascades.[^3]

From 1901 to 1902, he returned to Alaska and the [Cassiar Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassiar_Country "Cassiar Country") as a member of [Andrew Jackson Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Jackson_Stone?action=edit&redlink=1 "Andrew Jackson Stone (page does not exist)") 's expeditions to collect large mammals for the [American Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Museum_of_Natural_History "American Museum of Natural History"). During these expeditions, he worked as an artist and hunter. He practiced drafting anatomical-correct drawings of animals.[^2] [^3] [^4]

In 1908, he was co-leader of the first group to ascend [Mount Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus_\(Washington\) "Mount Olympus (Washington)") in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_\(state\) "Washington (state)"). In 1906, 1910 and 1912, he was co-leader of the Parker-Browne Mount McKinley expedition with [Herschel Clifford Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_Clifford_Parker "Herschel Clifford Parker") to explore unmapped glacial regions in Alaska. He attempted to ascend [Denali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denali "Denali") (then Mount McKinley) three times.[^2] [^3] [^4] [^5]

From 1897 to 1898, Browne was a member of the [Washington National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_National_Guard "Washington National Guard").[^4] He served as a captain in [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") in the aviation section of the [Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Signal_Corps "United States Army Signal Corps").[^1] [^5]

Following the war, he moved to [Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta "Alberta"). According to his wife, this period was the most productive of his life. He would paint outdoors, even in the winter.[^2] His work was exhibited in a number of art museums throughout his lifetime, including the [National Academy of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Design "National Academy of Design"), [Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Academy_of_the_Fine_Arts "Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts"), [National Gallery of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art "National Gallery of Art"), the [Corcoran Gallery of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corcoran_Gallery_of_Art "Corcoran Gallery of Art") and the [California Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Academy_of_Sciences "California Academy of Sciences") in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco").[^4] [^6]

He painted dioramas for natural history museums later in his life. He and his son painted five wild animal groups, the [kodiak bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodiak_bear "Kodiak bear"), mountain goat, elk, caribou and mountain sheep, for the North American Hall of the [American Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Museum_of_Natural_History "American Museum of Natural History"). He also painted a diorama for the [California Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Academy_of_Sciences "California Academy of Sciences").[^3] [^5] Just before his death, he had completed a black bear group for the [Boston Museum of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Museum_of_Science "Boston Museum of Science").[^4] [^5] He died before starting a diorama of the Alaskan brown bear group for the [Peabody Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peabody_Museum_of_Natural_History "Peabody Museum of Natural History").[^4] [^6]

Browne would start spending his winters in [California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California "California") and was named director of [Santa Barbara School of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_School_of_the_Arts "Santa Barbara School of the Arts") in 1930.[^2] During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), he was a consultant on Arctic survival to the American and British Air Forces. He taught these courses in [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado "Colorado"). Following the war, he continued to teach in [McCall, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCall,_Idaho "McCall, Idaho").[^5] [^3]

Browne wrote *Conquest of Mt. McKinley* and several adventure books for boys. He was active in the [Boy Scouts of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America "Boy Scouts of America") and was a member of its [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_Scouts_of_America_National_Council "Boy Scouts of America National Council"). He wrote the first Boy Scout handbook. He was an honorary member of the [Camp Fire Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Fire_Club "Camp Fire Club") and was a member of [Marin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_County,_California "Marin County, California") 's Boy Scout executive board for fifteen years.[^4]

## Personal life

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c2/Browne_Residence%2C_Banff.jpg/250px-Browne_Residence%2C_Banff.jpg)

Browne's home in Banff, Alberta

Browne married Agnes E. Sibley of Philadelphia in 1913.[^3] [^6] They had a son and daughter, George and Evelyn.[^1] They had a home in [Banff, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banff,_Alberta "Banff, Alberta"). They moved to a summer house in [Seebe, Alberta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seebe,_Alberta "Seebe, Alberta"), in 1947. They had a winter house on Laguintas Road in [Ross, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross,_California "Ross, California"). They attended St. John's Episcopal Church in Ross.[^4] [^3]

Browne died on May 2, 1954, at the home of his cousin in [Rye, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rye,_New_York "Rye, New York").[^2] [^4]
