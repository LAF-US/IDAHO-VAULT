A boutique hospital chain whose business practices have [drawn criticism](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/business/article313755331.html) from Idaho’s largest health insurer is moving forward with plans to build a “micro-hospital” in the Treasure Valley.

An application request filed with the city of Meridian shows plans for a 24,000-square-foot micro-hospital on South Rackham Way, near the northeast corner of Eagle and Overland roads, south of Interstate 84.

The request was filed by limited-liability company AlignSphere Ventures and Boise-based engineering and design firm Kimley-Horn. But application materials indicate that the “concierge” level mini-hospital would be operated by Nutex Health, a Houston, Texas-based for-profit company with one Idaho hospital operating in Post Falls.

Nutex’s [Post Falls ER & Hospital](https://postfallserhospital.com/) has been the subject of allegations of excessive insurance claims and unfair business practices by Blue Cross of Idaho, the [Idaho Statesman reported](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/politics-government/state-politics/article314822914.html). Nutex has denied the allegations as [“categorically false.”](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/business/article314864340.html)

The new filings move the plan ahead to the city’s approvals process, where it is scheduled to make a stop at the Meridian Planning and Zoning Commission for a public hearing in August.

## 24,000-square-foot micro-hospital planned near I-84

According to its website, Nutex owns and operates small hospitals providing around-the-clock emergency care, plus some “specialty” services. The company boasts short wait times, one-on-one time with physicians and large, private rooms with baths and televisions, and catered meals, according to prior Statesman reporting.

“In comparison to the traditional hospital systems, patients will be met with the convenience of emergency care in the comfort of a relaxing environment, a short wait time, and ample one-on-one time with a board-certified physician and emergency trained staff,” Nutex Health said in a [news release](https://nutexhealth.com/nutex-health-opens-post-falls-er-hospital-its-first-hospital-in-idaho/) when it opened its Post Falls hospital in 2024.

In Meridian, the proposed Rackham micro-hospital is expected to include a “full emergency department” with 10 exam rooms, including one trauma room and one isolation room; an imaging center; and eight overnight patient beds, according to a project narrative letter. The hospital would have a main entrance and an ambulance entrance, as well as 57 parking spaces.

The company expects that the hospital would see 20 to 30 patrons on a typical day and 30 to 50 patrons on a busier one, the letter said. It would employ eight people.

AlignSphere Ventures is asking the city for a conditional-use permit to build the hospital, as well as a development-agreement modification and a preliminary plat. In addition to the micro-hospital, the preliminary plat would allow for two more lots — one intended for open space or storm-drain facilities and the other possibly for a future retail or restaurant development, the letter said.

The roughly 4.7-acre lot along Rackham Way is now vacant, application materials show.

Meridian’s Planning and Zoning Commission hearing on the proposed hospital is scheduled to take place at 6 p.m. on Thursday, Aug. 6, at City Hall. The public will have the opportunity to weigh in.
