## Seeing God in the flames: My story of the Old Trails Fire

### After losing his Spokane home to the Old Trails Fire, a survivor reflects on loss, faith, community support, arson, healing and rebuilding a stronger future.

*The views expressed in this opinion column are those of the author. They do not necessarily reflect the* [*views*](https://favs.news/about-favs-news/) *of* [*FāVS News.*](https://favs.news/)

Saturday, Aug. 1, a date that seemed like a normal summer day, would change my life forever. It was the day the Old Trails Fire took my home, my neighborhood, my treasures and more. It was also the days after that showed me how much love could be found amidst the ruins.

## A Saturday I’ll never forget

I remember everything that happened that morning. After texting with my fiance, I decided to go get McDonald’s breakfast. I brought it home and had my morning cup of coffee in my favorite Wenatchi Wear coffee mug while watching “The Adventures of Ichabod & Mr. Toad” on Disney+. Little did I know, it would be the last time I had breakfast there.

After a phone call with my brother, I was getting ready to go to Safeway to buy snacks for a get-together with my college friends I had planned to have that afternoon in Audubon Park. However, I stopped when I saw the Watch Duty notification about the fire. I wasn’t worried at first, but it was when the map showed the fire had jumped the river I started packing my belongings in my car.

As the evacuation notices came in and I grabbed what I could, I was talking to my brother and fiance on the phone trying to figure out where I should go. The moment I saw billowing smoke come from behind my neighbor’s house across the street, I knew I needed to get out.

I started driving down southeast on Indian Trail toward Francis to go to my fiance and friends, but the line of cars stretched far, and I knew I would be stuck there for a while. The moment that made me turn around and flee northwest to my parents’ house was when I saw flames had reached the sidewalk. If I had stayed, I’m not sure what would have happened.

I was at my parents’ house for barely an hour before they had to evacuate, too. With nowhere else to go, we fled with my sister toward Deer Park to seek refuge at my uncle’s house.

Later that evening, after several hours of driving at a snail’s pace with a line of cars heading north, we made it safely to Deer Park. It was there we learned more about the fires and felt the gravity of the situation.

## The death of prosperity

Sunday at 2 a.m., I struggled to sleep and decided to look at Facebook for any updates. I stumbled upon a [video](https://www.facebook.com/share/v/1FXrUN43JL/?mibextid=wwXIfr) of someone driving and thought I recognized part of the street as Indian Trail. At one point in the video, the person recording it turned to the right passenger side window and said, “All these houses are all burnt down.” My heart told me it was my neighborhood, but my brain told me it couldn’t be.

My dad, who struggled going to sleep as well, was up in the living room, and I showed him the video. At first he wasn’t sure if that was my neighborhood, but said we could go and see. We then made the drive back to Indian Trail.

As we drove through the thick darkness of the night that enveloped the street, we saw scenes of utter desolation. Beside the headlights of my car and the flashing lights of first responders, the only other light sources we had were the three flames we saw still burning next to the ruins of several homes.

My dad then told me to prepare myself.

We soon made it to Prosperity Lane. Or what was left of it.

Other than a few burnt out cars and a tree, it was impossible to make out anything else from the ash and concrete. Parts of the street were stained red with flame retardant. The only way we were able to tell which pile of smoldering ash was my home was a small green utility box out front near the road.

I didn’t know what to think. All I knew was my house was gone, along with everything inside. I had no words. The best words to describe my state of mind were shock, horror and devastation.

## Everything all at once

After driving away from the desolation and going toward my parents’ house to pick up some items left behind, I called my fiance to deliver the news.

As soon as I heard her answer, I collapsed. A cry of pure anguish was all my voice could produce as I fell to the living room floor, gently pounding my fist on the ground. My dad had to take the phone from me and tell her.

Before heading back to Deer Park, my dad and I stopped at a Safeway to get food for my sister and some breakfast for ourselves. I had already felt queasy driving up to the store. It intensified while we were parking and morning had arrived. I couldn’t stop it. Before my dad could lock the car I threw up in the parking lot.

My karate belts, mementos of my relatives, every video console I’ve had since I was a child, my collection of 1,100 movies, my Wenatchi Wear mug and other items I bought from Native-owned businesses, my books, my journals, my wolf blanket I’ve had since I was 5, a small telescope my maternal grandfather gave me, a stack of birthday and Christmas cards from my relatives, my high school yearbooks, letters I got from a high school retreat, art projects, my prom king crown, pocket watches my dad gave me, my fiance’s books and mementos that she brought over. It all hit me all at once.

I apologized. My dad responded with, “You don’t have to apologize about anything for the next year.”

## A blanket of love and care

As I mourned the loss of my home and the possessions too many to recount, I began receiving a tremendous outpouring of support from my community and started to see how much love surrounded me.

My editor, friend and mentor, Tracy Simmons started a [GoFundMe](https://www.gofundme.com/f/support-matthew-and-favs-writers-after-the-fire) for me and other FāVS News writers affected by the fires. Friends, family, people I’ve interviewed before, and so many others, shared and donated to the GoFundMe, sent me messages asking me if I would like a spare guitar, some clothes they had or what movies I need to rebuild my collection, and expressed more love and support than I could ever imagine.

One gift that still chokes me up is something I received from my friend Barry Moses. He reached out to me wanting to meet up and check in on me. When he arrived at my uncle’s house, we talked and I shared how I was doing before he said he had something for me. He went into his car and pulled out a blanket along with some money. He gave the blanket to me as a prayer for my new home, and it’s something I’ll cherish and respect for the rest of my life.

Since receiving it, I have slept better at night and feel safer when it’s wrapped around me. I feel that blanket also represents the incredible amount of love I have received in all of this. As soon as people heard I was in trouble, they surrounded me and made sure I was cared for and had a means of moving forward.

I may have lost so much in the fire, but in the last week, I got a better understanding of how much my community stands behind me. Grateful is too small of a word to describe how much I appreciate everyone who has reached out to me. God couldn’t save my house, but he made sure I knew I had the support needed to rebuild and that I would not face the desolation alone.

## The arsonist

In the days after the fire left a trail of destruction in its wake, it was found out that it was started on purpose. A man was arrested in connection to it and later admitted to it while in custody, along with setting 25 other fires in the past.

When I saw the news, I was mad, hurt and wondered why someone would start something that has turned so many lives upside down. However, after I expressed my feelings, I took time to refocus on mourning the loss of my home and remembering all the good that surrounds me.

I am in a lot of pain, but I don’t want that pain to dominate my heart. That would affect me in ways I don’t understand, and I don’t want hatred to blind me to all the good that God wants me to see.

One thing I learned from my faith and Immaculee Ilibagiza’s “ [Left to Tell: Discovering God Amidst the Rwandan Holocaust](https://www.amazon.com/Left-Tell-Discovering-Rwandan-Holocaust/dp/1401908977) ” is that forgiveness is a mighty tool for healing. That doesn’t mean I’ll excuse his actions, but rather, I will not let the pain of them take a lot of space in my heart. Someday I will forgive him, but until then I’m going to focus on the blessings God has brought into my life.

In [chapter four of Ephesians](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Ephesians%204:26-32&version=NIV) it is written, “‘In your anger do not sin’; Do not let the sun go down while you are still angry, and do not give the devil a foothold.” And, “Do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths, but only what is helpful for building others up according to their needs, that it may benefit those who listen.”

We need to express our anger, but make sure anger isn’t the only thing we feel right now. We can’t let it turn us into people we won’t recognize months from now or make us overlook those in need.

For the arsonist, I will not say his name or let it be recorded by my pen. Too often we immortalize the perpetrators of great tragedy and forget the names of those they hurt. Notoriety doesn’t tend the wounds of a rattled city.

Remember the actions of those who cause tremendous harm and their impacts so we can set safeguards for the future, but immortalize the names of the survivors and those who persevered. It’s the survivors who will bring healing, not the villains.

I hope that one day he finds the light and feels remorse for his crimes, but that is ultimately his choice. I don’t need an apology from him to heal and rebuild my life.

## What to do now

For those who need assistance with recovering from these fires, don’t be afraid to ask for help. The number of helpers who reside in Spokane is astonishing, and this is not something to try to deal with on your own.

For those from the outside looking in, find the GoFundMe’s and the organizations accepting donations. Every dollar, prayer and anything you can give is greatly appreciated. A spare shirt you have in the closet is just a shirt to you, but to someone who loses everything, it is a building block to a new beginning.

## Protecting the future

From this, Spokane will rebuild, and we will become stronger. In a [recent interview](https://www.cbc.ca/listen/live-radio/1-2-as-it-happens/clip/16230617-prediction-market-betting-clinical-trials-coming) with CBC, I was asked what justice would look like and I feel it involves several factors.

It includes addressing issues with pyromania, understanding its prevalence and the psychology behind it and helping those who let their destructive thoughts take hold.

We should also address the effects of climate change that set the stage for arsonists to light a spark that causes so much destruction by following the leadership of Indigenous communities. They know how to care for the land and what it needs to ensure future generations do not go through what so many families have experienced in this last week.

No one should have to see the rubble of their home in the dark of night.
