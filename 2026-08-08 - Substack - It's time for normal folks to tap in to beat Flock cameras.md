Perhaps politics isn’t your thing.

Maybe you’re too busy putting food on the table, spending time with your kids, and hitting the gym.

Or maybe you’re apathetic about politics, and you understand that citizens really have little influence on government.

Trust me, I get it.

Still, it’s time to tap in for the next few months. We need you.

Over the last few months, citizens and organizations nationwide have started sounding the alarm about Flock and Axon cameras, AI-powered systems that track your movements and keep a massive database containing your whereabouts, your characteristics, and even how many dents your car might have.

Civil libertarians on the right and left have rightly raised concerns about the overreaching nature of these cameras, pointing out that they violate First and Fourth Amendment protections.

The building of the surveillance state should motivate Americans to action. But even if that weren’t enough, consider how many law enforcement officials have abused the cameras to stalk their spouses and ex-lovers. [Dozens of cops have been charged](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/08/02/how-police-officers-used-vast-network-cameras-spy-their-exes/) with abusing these systems, and arrests will continue to come as they proliferate.

Even here in Idaho, the [Jerome County Sheriff used his Flock system to track his wife’s vehicle hundreds of times](https://www.kmvt.com/2026/03/31/jerome-county-sheriff-used-flock-cameras-search-wifes-vehicle-documents-show/). Of course, he was never charged with a crime. He opted to retire halfway through his four-year term.

And then there are the terrifying mistakes. A few days ago, police in Wisconsin pulled over a suspected murderer and pulled their guns on him in a filmed traffic stop.

Except they didn’t.

[Flock cameras triggered on the wrong car](https://www.fox6now.com/news/brookfield-police-stop-wrong-vehicle-flock), and an innocent American was subjected to an incredibly dangerous situation for no reason.

Men braver than I have started chopping down the cameras, with some even boasting about their heroic exploits on social media. Yet many of those heroes now face felonies, fines, and jail time because of their pushback against the growing surveillance state.

I’ve been in politics a long time, and I’ve dealt with serious, pressing issues throughout the years. Yet I believe this might be one of the defining issues of our time, and we need to handle it accordingly.

So many of us grew up reading the dystopian classics, books authored to warn us of a future day when the government’s reach and power would be nearly infinite. *1984*, for example, showed readers the coercive, soul-crushing power of an ever-watching Big Brother.

The days of Big Brother are upon us.

Consider that Wisconsin police recently used Flock cameras to arrest a man for weed possession, even though they had no real probable cause to do so. How did they make their case against the man?

Law enforcement used the man’s many, many trips to Michigan, where weed is available and legal to use and possess. [According to reports](https://gizmodo.com/wisconsin-cops-tracked-a-man-with-flock-cameras-to-arrest-him-for-weed-2000795424), Flock cameras tracked the man’s trips to Michigan, which gave police probable cause to make the stop—at least in their minds.

That’s terrifying. And if you’re inclined to agree with police on that one because you don’t like weed, that’s fine. But remember that we are only a few years removed from a global pandemic in which governments ordered people to stay home for weeks and months on end.

Imagine a pandemic powered by Flock. Is that terrifying enough for you?

It’s obvious why cops want Flock and Axon cameras. The systems make their jobs infinitely easier and likely speed the recovery of kidnapped persons. The positives are not lost on me.

I just don’t think those positives outweigh the constitutional abuses and cop stalking that occur when police have unfettered access to these all-seeing systems.

For its part, Flock has begun to push back. As citizen backlash has grown, more and more police agencies are crediting the video systems in press releases, praising Flock and Axon cameras for keeping people safe in their communities.

This is now a public relations and political war. It’s one we have to win.

That’s where you come in. Your voice — every voice — is needed on this issue. Your elected officials at the state and local levels need to hear from you. They need to know how much you oppose the construction and operation of this surveillance state.

Even if you don’t agree with me politically, the appeal should be obvious.

Democrats, imagine Republicans tracking you as you put up your pride flags, buy weed in Oregon or Washington, or buy certain books the GOP doesn’t like.

Republicans, imagine President Gavin Newsom’s FBI tapping into Meridian’s cameras to watch your trips to the gun store, your church gatherings, or your political meetings.

These scenarios aren’t unthinkable. I believe they’re likely if we don’t act.

It’s time to get in the game. First, [check here to see if Flock has cameras](https://thehill.com/policy/technology/6016998-flock-camera-map-how-many-are-in-your-neighborhood/) in your city and county.

Then attend city council meetings and state legislature hearings and force this issue. Make public officials hear you. Call them. Email them. Speak out during the public comment period. Sue them if you must and have the means to do so.

And don’t let officials or Flock hoodwink you. The only solution that protects your rights is the demise of these systems. Not more regulations. Not ending contracts. Cameras must be removed and networks disabled.

Keep in mind that the law enforcement lobby is incredibly powerful in Idaho and will accuse you of not backing the blue for opposing their surveillance state agenda. Be brave and oppose them anyway.

If you’d like to get educated on Flock, Axon, and other camera systems, I urge you to follow DeFlock Idaho on Facebook. Tyler Cain and his associates are doing amazing work and have had some success in their efforts.

Yet it’s not enough. It won’t be enough until Flock cameras are gone for good.
