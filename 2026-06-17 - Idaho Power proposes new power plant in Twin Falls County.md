TWIN FALLS, Idaho (KMVT/KSVT) — Idaho Power is looking to build the South Hills Power Plant near its existing Milner Hydroelectric Facility as energy demand rises across the Magic Valley.

[The proposed facility](https://www.idahopower.com/energy-environment/energy/energy-sources/natural-gas/south-hills-gas-plant/ "https://www.idahopower.com/energy-environment/energy/energy-sources/natural-gas/south-hills-gas-plant/") will include 12 natural gas-fired reciprocating engines on approximately 20 acres of land along the Snake River in Twin Falls County.

Construction could begin in 2027, with the plant expected to be operational by the summer of 2029.

“It will provide about 222 megawatts of service. That’s roughly the equivalent of powering about 180,000 homes,” said Eric Hackett with Idaho Power Production Engineering and Resource Development.

The company says the project is needed as Southern Idaho continues to grow, bringing more residential customers, irrigation needs and other industries.

“We’ve been in a state of growth in southern Idaho and eastern Oregon for many years, and we see that to be sustained over the next several years,” Hackett said. “As that load continues to grow, we have to plan for our future to ensure that we have reliable power, and part of the way we do that is by adding new resources.”

Hackett says the decision to place the plant in Twin Falls County came down to existing infrastructure. The location is near transmission lines, highway access and a natural gas supply line.

“All those things come together to not only a location that is feasible, but also that helps maintain that economic selection,” Hackett said.

Idaho Power says the plant would help provide electricity when demand is highest, especially during extreme weather.

The company adds that natural gas provides a resource that can be turned on when needed.

“Gas is a dispatchable resource, meaning it’s there when we need it. We have the ability to turn the power on or off as the load and our customers need electricity,” Hackett said.

The project still needs approval from the Idaho Public Utilities Commission before construction can move forward.

The commission is accepting public comments until July 31, giving residents a chance to weigh in on the future of energy in the Magic Valley.

“We believe this to be a benefit to the residents of Idaho and to Twin Falls to meet the growing demand,” Hackett said.
