BOISE, Idaho — A family has filed a civil lawsuit alleging they were attacked during a confrontation on the Payette River in August — an incident that, according to court records, involved a Boise County commissioner and left a teenage girl stabbed.

The lawsuit stems from an Aug. 23, 2025, incident on the Main Payette River near the Beehive Bend takeout.

"This is a Idaho. We love our Whitewater. We love rafting. This is a way to spend a Sunday afternoon, and now they're afraid to go on the river," the family's attorney, Terri Pickens, said.

Pickens said the family was rafting when they encountered a jet ski allegedly being operated recklessly. Pickens said the family’s daughter, identified in court documents as T.B., made a gesture toward the riders, who she claims had an open container of alcohol and children onboard without life jackets.

"They apparently got very upset about that and followed my clients to the takeout and attacked them," Pickens said.

Court records identify the jet ski riders as Lloyd Asher and Bo Fisher.

Pickens said the two men followed the family to the takeout and initiated a confrontation. She said one of the men asked “who wanted to fight,” prompting the mother to grab a river safety knife and demand they leave.

The men left the area but later returned with Darrell “Lindy” Lindstrom, according to the lawsuit. Lindstrom is a Boise County commissioner.

"It was a Boise County Commissioner, an active, sitting Boise County Commissioner," Pickens said.

Pickens said that during the second encounter, all three family members were physically "attacked." She said Lindstrom approached the mother from behind, forced her to the ground, took the knife and held it to her throat.

The daughter was injured while trying to intervene, suffering knife wounds, according to the complaint. Court records further allege Lindstrom stabbed the girl in the leg and threw the knife into the river. At the same time, the family’s son was allegedly assaulted and sustained significant facial injuries.

"We're talking about someone in a position of power just going bananas and attacking this poor family and landing all three of them in the hospital," Pickens said.

All three family members were transported to a hospital for treatment. Law enforcement responded to the scene, but no criminal charges have been filed.

“Here we are, eight months later, with no charges filed,” Pickens said. “So my clients were left with absolutely no choice but to file a civil suit for battery.”

The lawsuit seeks $10,000 in damages and alleges battery as well as intentional and negligent infliction of emotional distress.

Boise County Sheriff Scott Turner told KTVB investigators are working with the Boise County prosecutor’s office on a criminal investigation.

Pickens, however, criticized the process, calling it a conflict of interest for the sheriff’s office to investigate an elected county official. She said the case is supported by witness statements, police reports, medical records, photographs and video footage.

“When you have a minor who has been violently stabbed with substantial injuries that have been established, proven, documented and on video, the excuse that you're too busy doesn't fly,” Pickens said.

She added that the case raises broader concerns about accountability.

“It’s inexcusable for an elected official to sit idly by for eight months and have no consequences for this behavior,” Pickens said.

The investigation remains ongoing.

"I want to know that if me and my children are out on the main Payette, floating down the river, that we're safe. I want to know that there is an administration that is going to protect my family from an attack like this," Pickens said.
