Every year on July 24, Latter-day Saints celebrate Pioneer Day, a holiday that remembers the arrival of the first members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in the Salt Lake Valley in 1847.

After years of persecution and being driven from their homes in several states, these pioneers, led by Brigham Young, sought a place where they could freely worship God and build peaceful communities. Their journey across more than 1,000 miles of rugged frontier remains one of the most remarkable migrations in American history.

**A journey of faith**

For members of the Church today, Pioneer Day is about much more than remembering history. It is an opportunity to honor the faith, courage and determination of those early pioneers and to consider how those same qualities are needed in our own lives. As Latter-day Saints, we often speak of having a “pioneer spirit,” the willingness to move forward with faith even when the future is uncertain.

Many Latter-day Saint families celebrate Pioneer Day with picnics, parades, music, family history activities or service to others. In many areas, youth participate in pioneer treks, where they dress in period clothing, pull handcarts, and experience, in a small way, some of the physical and emotional challenges faced by the early pioneers.

![latter-day-saint](https://favs.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/DDFFFE0A-C984-445C-B634-4AA84B27AC89.png)

One family participating in a Pioneer Trek near Spokane in 2023. They are walking part of an 18-mile section in high temperatures over a three-day period, through a remote area southwest of Cheney, Washington (Photo courtesy of Camille Cranney).

These experiences are designed not to glorify hardship but to help participants appreciate sacrifice, strengthen friendships and deepen their faith in Jesus Christ.

## A personal connection

Personally, I celebrate Pioneer Day by reading the stories of my own pioneer ancestors, watching films that tell of the pioneer experience and attending a pioneer picnic with members of my congregation.

I am deeply grateful for my pioneer ancestors and the sacrifices they made so they could worship according to the dictates of their conscience. Their stories have become part of the foundation of my own life. They remind me that faith, perseverance and trust in God can help us overcome challenges in every generation.

![latter-day saint](https://favs.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/1A400901-8EA8-4655-B503-C65F95C53CB6.png)

Richard Ballantyne is the author’s third great grandfather. He was born in 1817 in Scotland and died in 1898 in Utah. He crossed the plains in Brigham Young’s company (Contributed by the author).

While Pioneer Day has its roots in the American West, its message is universal. Today, more than half of all Latter-day Saints live outside the United States, and many members speak of the “pioneers” in their own countries, those first individuals and families who introduced the restored gospel to their communities.

In that sense, every generation has pioneers who help establish faith for those who follow.

Ultimately, Pioneer Day points beyond the past. It reminds Latter-day Saints that discipleship often requires courage, perseverance and trust in God. The pioneers’ greatest legacy was not simply that they crossed the plains. It was that they kept their faith in Jesus Christ despite tremendous adversity. That enduring example continues to inspire Latter-day Saints around the world today.
