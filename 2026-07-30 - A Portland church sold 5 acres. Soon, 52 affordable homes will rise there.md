Facing a line of orange-vested volunteers, a construction crew member issued instructions for how to lift a wall into place.

Volunteers from Gov. Tina Kotek’s office and the state housing finance agency readied themselves as the crew member, with homebuilder Habitat for Humanity, ordered, “One, two, three, lift!”

As one, the state brass obeyed, hoisting the wall to separate two townhomes in Habitat’s largest Oregon development yet: 52 homes for 230 people in Southwest Portland’s leafy Hillsdale neighborhood.

When they open in phases over the next two years, the townhomes at 2374 S.W. Vermont St., called Gooseberry Trails, will be for Portlanders who can’t afford the median market-rate home, now priced at $564,900 according to the latest tally by the Regional Multiple Listing Service.

Habitat officials say they set prices so homeowners never pay more than 34% of their income on their monthly house payment.

Congregants of Greater Portland Bible Church have gotten used to seeing construction work in front of their church, which sold the five acres to Habitat for $1.63 million in 2021.

With enough preparatory sitework completed to start building houses, the project broke ground in March.

Even during the press-and-government event Thursday, contractors continued unobstructed: A nailgun popped over here, and over there concrete bubbled like gray lava into a dirt trench. Once it’s all done in 2028, kids will have a playground, and homeowners will have yards.

State officials were on hand Thursday to tout the millions in public subsidies that are bringing Gooseberry Trails to fruition.

Among other funding sources, Habitat leveraged about $9 million from the state and $5.4 million from the Metro affordable housing bond, awarded through the Portland Housing Bureau. According to its latest annual report, the Metro bond is mostly allocated and has helped create 64 new affordable housing projects with some 5,400 homes either completed or on the way.

After she and others raised the wall, Talia Kahn-Kravis, acting director of homeownership programs at Oregon Housing and Community Services, said: “It takes a village to make these projects affordable.”
