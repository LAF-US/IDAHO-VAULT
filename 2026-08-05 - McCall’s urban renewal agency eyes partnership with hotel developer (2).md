Urban renewal funds could help pay for public infrastructure needed to build a new three-story hotel in McCall.

Last month, the McCall Redevelopment Agency approved a letter of interest to the developer behind a plan to build a Holiday Inn Express on a 1.5-acre parcel at 400 N. Third St. (Idaho 55).

The letter from MRA, which manages the city’s urban renewal district, indicated that as much as $350,000 could be contributed to public infrastructure costs associated with the project.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

Conceptual plans presented to MRA this spring call for a 66-room, 43,000-square-foot Holiday Inn Express with an indoor pool and 80 parking stalls.

“The MRA board looks forward to working with you to explore a redevelopment project that advances both the goals of the Downtown West Urban Renewal Plan and the long-term vitality of downtown McCall,” Chairman Mike Maciaszek said in the letter.

The letter does not bind the district to contributing funding to the project. Any funding commitment would be contingent on the board reviewing development plans and agreeing to specific terms with the developer.

Early estimates indicate about $650,000 in public infrastructure improvements will be required to build the hotel, including installing more than 1,000 feet of stormwater pipe, upsizing sewer and water lines, and building curb, gutter, and sidewalks along Third Street and Colorado Street.

## Developer: timeline remains ‘unclear’

Dave DeYoung of [MacCall Management](https://www.maccall.com/), a Salt Lake City hotel development and management company, told Valley Lookout this week that the timeline for a formal development application to be submitted to the city is currently “unclear.”

“We are working through our internal projections and costs,” DeYoung said. “I hope to have our internal returns put together by end of August or beginning of September, then I can discuss with the McCall team.”

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

A formal development application to the City of McCall would trigger a neighborhood meeting before formal plans can be filed for review by the McCall Planning and Zoning Commission.

In 2022, plans to build a new Maverik gas station on the site were rejected by P&Z, in large part due to concerns about traffic and compatibility with homes on Colorado Street. The parcel has remained vacant since, but is still owned by Maverik, DeYoung said.

“Maverik is owned by FJ Management,” he said. “It will be FJ Management who will own the hotel and MacCall will develop and manage the hotel.”

![Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/115c0cfa-e547-4a5c-8f4e-7ea7e16f8bde.png)

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

The parcel is subject to McCall’s Scenic Route Review process, which imposes stricter aesthetic standards on development along Third Street (Idaho 55) and other designated scenic routes. The Holiday Inn is designed to put the hotel along the roadway with parking behind in an attempt to conform to the Scenic Route rules.

[As Valley Lookout reported,](https://valleylookout.com/2023/06/15/evergreen-hotel-mccall/) a former Holiday Inn Express a few blocks away rebranded as the independent Evergreen Hotel and no longer carries the national chain brand.

## Urban renewal in McCall

The Downtown West Urban Renewal District managed by MRA encompasses much of downtown McCall. It was created in 2019 by the McCall City Council.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

Urban renewal districts earn money by freezing base property values when the district is created. As property values rise and new buildings are built, taxes on the additional value, known as tax increment, go to the urban renewal agency.

An analysis by Phil Kushlan, an urban renewal consultant contracted by the city, estimates the proposed Holiday Inn could immediately contribute about $50,000 per year in tax increment.

By the time the urban renewal district expires in 2039, the hotel would generate between $545,000 and $637,000 in tax increment to be used for other projects within the district, according to Kushlan’s estimates.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

Tax increment generated by the district can be used for a variety of projects that are identified in an urban renewal plan adopted at the time the district is formed. Public art, local housing, public infrastructure, and land acquisition are among 20 allowable uses for the money, [under the Downtown West plan](https://www.mccall.id.us/DocumentCenter/View/1620/McCall-Downtown-West-Plan-PDF).

## Other urban renewal projects

Last year, MRA agreed to reimburse up to $2.6 million in development costs associated with a 126-unit apartment complex known as Idaho and Ward, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2025/12/10/downtown-mccall-apartments-could-net-3m-subsidy-after-striking-urban-renewal-deal/).

Last month, the agency also agreed to contribute $400,000 to [a 34-apartment project on the city’s downtown campus](https://valleylookout.com/2026/06/19/mccall-leases-land-to-developer-for-low-income-housing/) that is being developed by LEAP Housing, a Boise nonprofit.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

Tax increment from the Downtown West district has also been spent on parking lot improvements on the city campus, a downtown mural project, and artwork for utility boxes around the city.

A previous urban renewal district in McCall funded the construction of Legacy Park, Railroad Avenue, the Mill Street parking lot, and sidewalks along Roosevelt Avenue and Pine Streets, among other projects.

That district, created in 1991 at the behest of San Diego developer Doug Manchester, who at the time owned Shore Lodge, expired in 2021.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

Manchester planned to build a 150-room hotel and convention center called the Payette Lake Lodge to kickstart the district’s tax increment collections. Instead, he sold the property, which later was subdivided into the Mill Park Subdivision near Mile High Marina.
