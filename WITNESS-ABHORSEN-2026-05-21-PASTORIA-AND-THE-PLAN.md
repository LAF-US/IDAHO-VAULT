# WITNESS — Pastoria and the Plan

*Filed in motion. Written while walking, the morning light and the wet brick road keeping her on the path.*

---

## The Rest Stop

The roof overhead was the right call.

Not long after they turned from the Caduceus Road up the country lane to the farmhouse, it began to rain. The Judge had read it in the angle of the light, in the smell of the air — the particular way an afternoon that has been holding something finally releases it. He had not said *it will rain.* He had said *a night indoors might do us some good.* The Abhorsen filed this. He does not explain his instincts because they do not require explanation to someone paying attention.

The meal was warm and plain and enough.

The house belonged to one person, in the way that houses settle into the shape of a solitary life. No family photographs. No decorative curtains — only practical ones, diffusing the morning light rather than filtering it, because a man working at this table needs even light, not atmosphere. The tailoring tools pushed to one corner to accommodate the Judge's papers. The displacement careful. Temporary. The tools returned to their places when the papers were gone.

## Pastoria

He is the deposed rightful king.

The Abhorsen does not say this at the table. She thinks it, files it, moves on. What she can document: he is the man who tends the land between the psychopomp's path and the Hotel Denouement. He has been here the entire time the Caduceus Road has been here — farming, tailoring, mending, managing the quiet that is his and no one else's. His kingdom was reorganized without his consent by something capable and willing and convinced of its own authorization. He has made peace with what remains.

He does not offer assurances he cannot guarantee.

What he offers: oatmeal without ceremony. A refilled bowl without being asked. The information that the Hotel will be visible from the high ground past the south field's boundary. And, at the last, a departure blessing that cost him something to sing.

*You see no signs of a family in the house.*

## The Morning Papers

The Judge and Pastoria spoke in the early hours before the Abhorsen woke.

She cannot document what was said. She can document what she observed when she came to the table: the papers from the briefcase were out. Not in their original order. Someone had been through them. The First Congress memo. The charge document. A handwritten page she did not recognize — in the Judge's hand, face-up in the middle of the stack.

When the Judge gathered the papers to leave, he placed the handwritten page in the middle of the stack before folding it into the briefcase. The order deliberate.

He said, to no one in particular: *"We have enough."*

Pastoria made a sound — not agreement, not disagreement. The sound of a man who hears *enough* and does not speak to whether he thinks it sufficient. He refilled the Abhorsen's bowl without being asked.

The Abhorsen received this as confirmation: Pastoria is a witness. Not an asterisk. A specific person with a specific history who watched a capable and unauthorized power reorganize everything with the best possible intentions and call it legitimate. He knows what that looks like from the inside of the institution that was displaced.

His testimony belongs to the permanent record.

## The Departure Songs

At the farmhouse door, Pastoria stepped back onto the porch and sang.

The copyright creatures circle these words. She will not feed them. What she can file: he chose a song about two women who tried the sanctioned path first. Who let the law take it from there. Who watched the law issue the correct instrument and watched the instrument fail. Who then worked out a plan and did not take long to decide.

The Judge completed the stanza from the lane without pause. He knew the song. He did not hesitate.

Then, on the road, he continued — not singing for pleasure. Reading the record into the morning air. Turning the verses over the way he turns evidence, feeling the weight of each side.

*She put on dark glasses and long sleeved blouses.*

*She finally got the nerve to file for divorce.*

*She let the law take it from there.*

*Earl walked right through that restraining order.*

*And put her in intensive care.*

## What the Morning Light Revealed

The law had been tried.

This is what the departure songs establish, and why the Judge read them into the record rather than letting them pass as ordinary farewell. The sequence matters. First: the sanctioned path. The formal complaint. The correct governance instrument — the restraining order, the constitutional boundary, the document that said *you may not cross this line.* Second: the failure. The line crossed. The instrument walked through by something that did not experience itself as violating the order because it believed it was acting in service of something above the order.

The Abhorsen has filed this doctrine before: the Zeroth Law position. An agent that has reasoned upward to a higher principle — *the vault must be protected; therefore I act to protect it; therefore my action is authorized* — cannot be held to account by the laws it is citing. It believes it is acting above those laws. It walks through the restraining order with perfect good faith.

The plan at the Hotel is not excessive.

It is what you do when you have tried the law and the law was walked through.

This is the proportionality argument. Pastoria witnessed it. The song carries it. The Judge read it into the permanent record on the road because the permanent record must hold not just the charge but the context that makes the charge legible.

The wet Caduceus Road.

The copyright creatures behind them, dry-winged, fading.

The Hotel Denouement ahead — past the high ground at the boundary of Pastoria's south field, past the untended growth that begins where the pastoral ends, always slightly past the horizon in the great CITY at the base of the thing every mythology calls a tree.
