## GOP govs join Trump pledge designed to shield ratepayers from data center costs

Republican legislators, utility services and tech companies also signed onto his voluntary pledge.

WASHINGTON — President Donald Trump on Thursday announced the addition of Republican governors, legislators, utility services and tech companies into his voluntary pledge aimed at keeping data center construction and operation costs off the shoulders of American consumers.

The [Ratepayer Protection Pledge](https://www.whitehouse.gov/ratepayer-protection-pledge/), first signed by the president in March, requires companies that build and use data centers to pay for any new infrastructure upgrades and generation resources that serve them.

Tech giants Google, Microsoft, Meta, Oracle, xAI, OpenAI and Amazon all signed onto the initial pledge. Now, the measure is expanding to include nearly 200 more stakeholders, including multiple governors.

Electric companies and other utilities generally pay for infrastructure upgrades with the revenue that comes from what their customers pay. The agreement aims to shift that cost in communities with new data centers directly to the centers’ companies. According to Trump, the initiative will supply 80% of all the power delivered to American businesses and homes.

“As new data centers go up, all of the electricity bills for American families will actually come down,” Trump said during a roundtable event at the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency on Thursday.

He added that Big Tech companies are “happy” to foot the bill for data centers because it will allow them to continue to function and grow.

“I’ve always said that America’s goal must be to dominate the future, and that includes being the No. 1 superpower in artificial intelligence,” Trump said. “This is how we’ll grow our economy, lower the cost of living, protect American consumers and lead the world in AI and high tech.”

Republican Govs. Jeff Landry of Louisiana, Brian Kemp of Georgia, Jim Pillen of Nebraska and Brad Little of Idaho joined the president at the roundtable to promote their commitment to the pledge. Some governors manage the state utility commissions that set electricity prices.

A total of 23 Republican governors signed the pledge, according to an undated document the White House sent reporters Thursday morning. Only Florida’s Ron DeSantis, New Hampshire’s Kelly Ayotte and Vermont’s Phil Scott declined to sign.

The executives said large power plants are supplying energy for their states without raising consumer costs, and touted partnerships with companies such as Meta.

Landry said his state’s deal with Meta will “save Louisiana $2.6 billion,” helping to strengthen “river liability” and reduce “resiliency costs” for those who live along the Gulf Coast.

In Georgia, Kemp said the pledge will support the operation of nuclear power plants while keeping rates low for local communities.

#### Contradicting numbers

The environmental advocacy group Climate Power published a [report](https://climatepower.us/news/round-up-as-trump-signs-another-empty-data-center-pledge-americans-face-more-rate-hikes/) Thursday that counters the president’s promises of lowered electricity costs for consumers.

According to the organization, prices have spiked 18% on average since Trump took office.

“While Trump stages PR stunts to save his tanking approval rating, Americans are paying the price for his cost-raising agenda,” Climate Power said in the report. “As gas prices hit $4 per gallon again this year thanks to Trump’s war of choice in Iran, Americans in five states faced rate hikes as high as $9 a month.”

The states it listed were Missouri, North Carolina, Connecticut, New Mexico and Oregon.

#### Beating China

The speakers Thursday, including Trump, governors, U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright and Environmental Protection Agency Administrator Lee Zeldin consistently said the pledge could help win an AI race with China.

“Americans are united in their desire to supercharge our economy, to increase our national security by keeping our lead in artificial intelligence over China,” Wright said.

They are also “united in their frustration over the ruinous nearly 30% price rise in electricity across the country during the Biden administration,” he added.

Trump noted China’s authoritarian government does not require environmental impact studies or other permits.

#### House bill

Earlier this week, the U.S. House Committee on Energy and Commerce unanimously passed a bill similar to the Ratepayer Protection Pledge to protect Americans from paying higher electricity costs tied to data centers.

Sponsored by Reps. Gabe Evans, a Colorado Republican, and Kathy Castor, a Florida Democrat, the bipartisan [Ratepayer Protection Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/9340/text) would require state utility regulators to consider adopting standards for data centers to pay for their own electricity, infrastructure and other developments.

“America must build the energy infrastructure necessary to win the global AI race and outcompete adversaries like Communist China—but hardworking families should never be forced to subsidize that growth through higher electric bills,” Evans, who is the chairman of the Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Energy, said in a statement Tuesday.

Just before it passed, 52-0, the committee narrowed the scope of the bill to focus specifically on data centers. Language in an earlier draft would have applied to power plants with a maximum electricity demand of 100 megawatts or more, [according to Politico](https://www.eenews.net/articles/data-center-lobby-grumbles-about-reworked-ratepayer-protection-act-2/).

An Amazon Web Services data center is shown situated near single-family homes on July 17, 2024 in Stone Ridge, Virginia. The Data Center Coalition, an advocacy group representing some of the biggest names in the industry including Amazon, Microsoft and Google, said in a statement Wednesday that it supported the original version of the bill but took issue with the changes.

The changes “single out the data center industry, leaving everyday Americans vulnerable to the costs associated with large load growth from other industries as they continue to expand operations in the U.S,” Josh Levi, the group’s president and CEO, said.

#### Progressives push outright ban

Some Democratic lawmakers, including as Energy and Commerce ranking member Rep. Frank Pallon of New Jersey, have expressed support for the more restrictive idea of a national data center moratorium.

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York and Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont have introduced parallel [bills](https://www.sanders.senate.gov/press-releases/news-sanders-ocasio-cortez-announce-ai-data-center-moratorium-act/) in their respective chambers to implement a nationwide AI data center moratorium.

Such legislation would temporarily prohibit the construction of new data centers and the expansion of existing ones in the U.S. until Congress addresses the economic, environmental and safety consequences associated with the facilities. Last week, New York became the [first state to impose a ban](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/14/new-york-ai-data-center-ban.html) on AI data centers.
