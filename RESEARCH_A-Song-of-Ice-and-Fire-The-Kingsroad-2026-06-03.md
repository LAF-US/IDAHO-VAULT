# The Kingsroad

> How the realm is physically **bound together** — its principal overland artery, from Storm's End to the Wall. One of a triad with `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Small-Council-2026-06-03]]` (governance) and `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Citadel-2026-06-03]]` (knowledge).
>
> **Provenance.** Sourced 2026-06-03; ⚠️ awoiaf/Fandom **403'd on direct fetch** — claims via search-result extracts; figures vary by source. Tiers: **[canon]**, **[reading]**. Paraphrased.

## What it is & its route

- **[canon]** The **longest and best-maintained highway in Westeros** — the realm's principal **north–south** overland artery, wide enough for several wagons abreast. It runs from **Storm's End** in the south, through **King's Landing**, north through the North (near **Winterfell**) to the **Wall / Castle Black**.
- **[canon, ⚠️ figures vary]** Length is given as roughly **~2,000 miles** ("almost two thousand" to "over two thousand from Storm's End to the Wall"). *(The "1,000+ leagues" figure doesn't reconcile — ~2,000 mi ≈ ~670 leagues — so treat the mileage range as the better-attested number.)*

## Construction & history

- **[canon — correction]** Built **under King Jaehaerys I** (the Conciliator; reign 48–103 AC), begun **~62 AC** as the **first** of his great roads to bind the realm — **not by Aegon I**. (Same Jaehaerys/Barth golden age as the Doctrine of Exceptionalism and the codified small council.) Existing paths were reused where possible.
- **[canon]** Through **the Neck** it becomes **the causeway** — the **only safe route** through the bog (crannogmen know hidden ways), guarded at its northern edge by the ruins of **Moat Cailin**: the chokepoint that controls all north–south passage.
- **[canon]** It crosses the **Trident**; landmark inns sit along it — the **Inn at the Crossroads** (where the kingsroad meets the **River road** and the **High Road** into the Vale) and the **Inn of the Kneeling Man** (built where **Torrhen Stark bent the knee** to Aegon; once had a Trident ferry → the Conquest node).

## Quality & the road network

- **[canon]** Westerosi roads are generally **poor**; the kingsroad is the notable **well-built exception** — though even it varies (strongest **King's Landing → the Neck**, thick with inns; little more than dirt tracks just north of the capital).
- **[canon]** The network it anchors — *"all roads lead to King's Landing"*:
  - **Roseroad** — KL → Highgarden → **Oldtown** (second only to the kingsroad).
  - **Goldroad** — KL → Lannisport / Casterly Rock (also built under **Jaehaerys**, aided by Master of Coin Lord Martyn Tyrell).
  - **Ocean Road** — Lannisport/Casterly Rock → Highgarden.
  - **River road** — Casterly Rock → Riverrun → the Inn at the Crossroads.
  - **High Road** — the eastern climb into the Vale.

## Strategic importance

- **[canon]** The maintained road served **trade, governance, and military movement** alike (Jaehaerys made roadbuilding a primary initiative for exactly these reasons). **Controlling Moat Cailin / the causeway = controlling north–south passage** — which is why the North's chokepoint recurs in every war (→ the North node).

## Theme — the infrastructure of a unified realm

**[reading]** Where dragons *conquered* the realm and the small council *governs* it, the kingsroad **physically binds** it — Jaehaerys built it to knit the Seven Kingdoms into one body for trade, troops, and the king's word. It is the **Conciliator's** instrument the way the dragon was the Conqueror's: empire expressed not as fire but as **a road the crown maintains**. Real-world parallel: the Roman **cursus publicus** / imperial highways (⚠️ the explicit Roman-road inspiration in the wikis attaches to the **Valyrian roads**; the kingsroad parallel is functional analogy, not a sourced GRRM statement).
