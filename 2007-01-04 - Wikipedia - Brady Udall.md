**Brady Udall** is an American writer. In 2010, he was appointed [Writer-in-Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writer-in-Residence "Writer-in-Residence") of [[Idaho]], a position he held until 2013.[^1]

## Biography

Udall grew up in a large [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon "Mormon") family in [St. Johns, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Johns,_Arizona "St. Johns, Arizona").[^2] He graduated from [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University "Brigham Young University") and later attended the [Iowa Writers' Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Writers'_Workshop "Iowa Writers' Workshop") at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa "University of Iowa").[^3] He was formerly a faculty member of [Franklin & Marshall College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_&_Marshall_College "Franklin & Marshall College") [^4] starting in 1998,[^5] then [Southern Illinois University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Illinois_University "Southern Illinois University"),[^3] and now teaches writing at [Boise State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_State_University "Boise State University").[^6]

A collection of his short stories titled *Letting Loose the Hounds* was published in 1998,[^7] and his debut novel *The Miracle Life of Edgar Mint* was first published in 2001. The characterization and structure of the latter has been favorably compared to the work of [John Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Irving "John Irving").[^8] Thematically it has been compared to [Charles Dickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Dickens "Charles Dickens").[^3] [Michael Stipe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stipe "Michael Stipe") has [optioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(films\) "Option (films)") a film adaptation of *Miracle*, with [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists "United Artists") hiring [Michael Cuesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Cuesta "Michael Cuesta") to direct.[^8]

In July 2007, Udall appeared on an episode of *[This American Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_American_Life "This American Life")*.[^9]

Udall is the great-great-grandson of [David King Udall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_King_Udall "David King Udall") and his plural wife [Ida Hunt Udall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ida_Hunt_Udall "Ida Hunt Udall"). In an interview about his novel *The Lonely Polygamist*, he said this "strong family connection to polygamy" made it "only right" that he author a novel about a polygamist.[^10] Through this heritage, Udall is related to members of the [Udall family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udall_family "Udall family"), including former U.S. congressman and Interior Secretary [Stewart Udall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stewart_Udall "Stewart Udall"), and former congressman and presidential candidate [Morris Udall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Udall "Mo Udall").[^3]
