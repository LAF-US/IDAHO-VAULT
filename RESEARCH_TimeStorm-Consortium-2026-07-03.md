## 1. The project — `[research]`

**TIMESTORM** — *"Mind and Time: Investigation of the Temporal Traits of Human-Machine
Convergence."*

- **Funder / programme:** European Union, **Horizon 2020**, under the **FET (Future & Emerging
  Technologies)** strand.
- **Grant Agreement:** **641100**.
- **Duration:** **January 2015 – June 2018** (~3.5 years).
- **Aim:** endow robots and AI with **human-like time perception** — to study the role of *time*
  in human–robot collaboration so machines can perceive, represent, and **synchronize in time**
  the way people do (waiting, pacing, anticipating, ordering tasks).
- **Method signature:** deliberately interdisciplinary — the **psychology and neuroscience of
  time perception** fused with **cognitive robotics and cognitive systems**.

## 3. Significance — `[research — secondary]`

Per the EU write-ups, TIMESTORM took time perception in AI *"from an immature, poorly defined
subject into a promising new research strand,"* contributing on two fronts at once:

1. **Science:** helping identify the **brain circuits** underlying human time perception (the
   psychology/neuroscience partners).
2. **Engineering:** implementing those models in **robots with deep cognitive facilities**, so
   machines can **collaborate in temporal synchrony** with humans — the foundation, in their
   framing, for future time-aware robots.

## 4. Notable people — `[research]`

- **Murray Shanahan** (Imperial) — a prominent figure in AI and cognition (global-workspace
  theory, machine consciousness; science advisor on *Ex Machina*; later associated with DeepMind).
  His presence signals the project sat near the AI-and-mind conversation, not only applied
  robotics.
- **Sylvie Droit-Volet** (Bourgogne) — widely cited in the experimental psychology of time.
- **Tamim Asfour** (KIT) — known for the **ARMAR** humanoid robots.
- **Hedderik van Rijn** (Groningen) — cognitive modelling / temporal cognition.
- **Panos Trahanias** (FORTH) — computer vision and robotics; project coordinator.
