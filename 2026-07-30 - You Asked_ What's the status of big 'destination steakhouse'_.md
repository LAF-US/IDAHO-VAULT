Over a year and a half ago, [BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/01/24/prime-american-steakhouse/) a “destination steakhouse” was coming to Star.

Prime American, which was announced by the owners of the Anderson Reserve in Sweet in January 2025, promised a version of Anderson Reserve in the Treasure Valley with cigars, a butcher shop, a bar, and, of course, steaks.

“Our family has been in business since 1947. We are a little ways out there in Sweet,” Anderson told BoiseDev in 2025. “We do a lot during the holiday season, but our daily walk-in traffic is not that much. I’m hoping that we do a far larger business here in Star and can bring a high-quality butcher shop to the area.”

![Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/54fe6a7b-4e48-4c07-b9ad-1f79c34fb57e.gif)

Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power

A site plan shows a large open interior space with a horseshoe-shaped bar immediately behind the entrance. A set of semi-circle banquette-style booths sits on one side of the bar, which opens onto a large outdoor patio space. Behind the bar, a spiral staircase leads into a silo, which plans call “the cigar silo.” Anderson previously told BoiseDev the upstairs area will include a speakeasy-style bar and cigar lounge, and renderings show an additional outdoor balcony patio.

Now, with the goal opening date come and gone, BoiseDev looked into an update on the 15,000-square-foot, two-story project located at 10206 W. State Street.

When news about Prime American first broke, owner Paul Anderson said he hoped to have the restaurant open by June 2026. The project has since been delayed.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

The Prime American team posted on its Facebook account in May that it had passed its design review with Star.

“After nearly two years of planning, we’re excited to finally begin this next chapter. Star, Idaho. We’re coming!” the prospective restaurant wrote.

The restaurant promised to post a grand opening date when it had one. A BoiseDev review found that no building permits for the property are filed with the city yet. The restaurant invited veterans and people with disabilities to use its lot as a parking and viewing spot on July 4 for the Star Fourth of July Parade.

![Freeform - Invested in Boise - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/8d57412c-01ee-47ef-b704-9f71ec57d3ba.png)

Freeform - Invested in Boise - 2:1

According to planning documents provided by the City of Star, the parking has been reduced from 132 spaces to 130. Star Mayor Trevor Chadwick also told BoiseDev that the exterior of the property would have a few changes after the city’s design review, but mock-ups hadn’t been provided yet. Planning documents still show a bar, a spiral staircase in a silo, a butcher shop, dining space, a second floor, and a mezzanine.

Anderson did not return multiple requests for comment.
