***Told in the Hills*** is a 1919 American [silent Western film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_Western_film "Silent Western film") produced by [Famous Players–Lasky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famous_Players%E2%80%93Lasky "Famous Players–Lasky") and distributed by [Paramount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") Artcraft. [George Melford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Melford "George Melford") directed the film and [Robert Warwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Warwick "Robert Warwick") stars.[^1] It was based on the same-titled 1891 novel by [Marah Ellis Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marah_Ellis_Ryan "Marah Ellis Ryan").

## Production

*Told in the Hills* was filmed on location in [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho "Lewiston, Idaho") and [Lapwai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapwai,_Idaho "Lapwai, Idaho"), [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"), and employed hundreds from the [Nez Perce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez_Perce_people "Nez Perce people") tribe.[^2] It was claimed that during the production of the film was the first time so many men of the Nez Perce had been allowed to gather since the [Nez Perce War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez_Perce_War "Nez Perce War") of 1877.[^2]

## Preservation

In 1986, [Boise State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise_State_University "Boise State University") professor [Tom Trusky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Trusky "Tom Trusky") inquired at the [Gosfilmofond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gosfilmofond "Gosfilmofond") about the existence of *Told in the Hills*, in addition to several [Nell Shipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nell_Shipman "Nell Shipman") films, for an exhibit on [[Idaho]] in film. The director of the Gosfilmofond replied that they had reels 3 and 4 preserved as 35 mm duplicate negatives, and that the films have been at the archive since 1951. A copy of the film, with Russian intertitles, was sent to BSU archives.[^3] Trusky went on to make a short documentary *Retold in the Hills* describing his successful interactions with Gosfilmofond.[^4]

In 2023, Colin Mannex, the executive director of the Kenworthy Performing Arts Center in [Moscow, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho "Moscow, Idaho"), learned of the film's existence. In January 2024, he applied for and received a $7500 grant from the Idaho Humanities Council to restore the film, but in April, the National Endowment for the Humanities was, because of [DOGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DOGE "DOGE"), unable to support the Council and 70% of the Council's funding was eliminated. The restoration continued, but with difficulty and it was scaled-down.[^4]

The 4K restoration premiered at the Kenworthy Silent Film Festival on September 26 with a new score by Connor Chee.[^5]
