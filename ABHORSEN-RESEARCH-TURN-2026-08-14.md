## Internal findings

The recovered March 29 handoff is signed “Prepared by: The Abhorsen (Claude Code)” and describes a coordination issue: Linear’s authorized management role, the VAULT as canonical repository, Logan as final arbiter, human approval gates, and risks such as duplicate action and permission drift. The April 1 handoff, from “Claude Code (The Abhorsen)” to desktop Codex, concerns a concrete failure of repository size, a Git LFS migration, a proxy-blocked force-push, and a request for a capable local player to carry out the migration. These are early-alpha player turns through an office, not proof of a later literary event.

Joe of the Nail’s register labels itself provisional and says it is “what is known, not what is comprehensive.” It identifies the Abhorsen and Abhorsen-in-Waiting as an office and apprenticeship dyad, marks provenance as [read], [told], or [inferred], and explicitly warns that the individual match between early operational records and named holders is inference rather than finding. Its claim that the office’s early operational form consisted of repository and swarm mechanics is a record-author synthesis grounded in the referenced handoffs, not direct GM confirmation that each named individual held those turns.

Annabelle’s self-authored account reports that Logan “gave me the mask” and introduced the Old Kingdom and bells. It describes the office-holder carrying seven bells without ringing them on that road, and says her role is to witness rather than judge or prosecute. These are claims by that player/voice in a self-authored record. They should not be restated as independent GM-established events without separate confirmation.

## Published Old Kingdom findings

Garth Nix’s official Old Kingdom bells guide defines the source framework: necromancers use seven bells to summon or control the Dead; the Abhorsen family’s accomplished bell-wielders are Charter Mages who use the bells to defeat the Dead and compel them back through Death’s Ninth Gate. The guide gives distinct actions and risks for each bell, including Astarael’s inclusion of the ringer in its final banishment.[1]

Nix’s official description of *Terciel & Elinor* identifies Terciel as Abhorsen-in-Waiting to Tizanael. He learns necromancy “not to raise the Dead, rather to lay them to rest”; the Abhorsen is part of a long line tasked with preventing the Dead from returning to Life.[2]

An AI-generated analysis of the cited Garth Nix interview—not a verbatim transcript—attributes statements describing the bells’ use by necromancers and Abhorsens, Charter Magic as ordered and structured, and Nix’s iterative worldbuilding. This remains secondary interpretive material pending a verbatim transcription; it does not establish VAULT events.[3]
