The FCC on Thursday voted 2-1 to eliminate the national TV ownership cap rule that bars a single broadcaster from owning stations that reach more than 39% of U.S. TV households.

**Why it matters**: The party-line vote clears the way for local broadcasters to merge, giving single broadcast ownership groups far more reach than they previously could have.

- FCC chairman Brendan Carr has long argued that the cap was outdated and limits the ability of broadcasters to consolidate to compete with tech firms and streamers.

**Zoom in**: The FCC said in a [statement](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-424076A1.pdf) that deals would be evaluated on a case-by-case basis that authorizes deals that satisfy the agency's public review standard.

- "This will empower the FCC to approve deals that promote the public interest while allowing the agency to reject any deals that do not meet that standard," it said.

**Between the lines**: Nexstar is the clearest example of a beneficiary of the raised cap.

- Its $6.2 billion [takeover](https://www.axios.com/2025/08/19/nexstar-tegna-6-billion-broadcast-deal) of rival Tegna is currently on [pause](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/20/judge-orders-nexstar-tegna-to-pause-merger) as a federal judge considers an antitrust lawsuit filed by DirecTV and several state attorneys general.
- The FCC's Media Bureau approved Nexstar's application to transfer Tegna's broadcast licenses without a full, public commission vote in March.

**What they're saying**: In a statement, a Nexstar spokesperson said "For too long, local broadcasters were handcuffed from reaching the scale they needed to compete on a more level playing field."

- "Modernizing these rules will help ensure broadcasters can continue investing in local journalism and providing the free, trusted news and information that communities across America rely on every day."

**Reality check:** Thursday's vote to lift the cap doesn't resolve the antitrust lawsuit that put the deal on pause.

- That suit addresses issues outside of the FCC's cap vote.
- DirecTV says the deal would allow Nexstar to have pricing leverage over pay-TV distributors and those costs would be passed on to consumers.
- There's an ongoing debate over whether the FCC has the authority to lift the cap.

**Context**: FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez — the lone Democrat — voted in dissent, arguing Congress removed the FCC's authority to adjust the ownership cap after approving the current limit in 2004.

- The Biden-era FCC opposed lifting the cap, and the FCC [killed](https://link.axios.com/click/42429001.156/aHR0cHM6Ly93d3cuYXhpb3MuY29tL3Byby9tZWRpYS1kZWFscy8yMDIzLzA1LzIyL3N0YW5kYXJkLWdlbmVyYWwtdGVnbmEtZGVhbC1kZWFkP3V0bV9zb3VyY2U9bmV3c2xldHRlciZ1dG1fbWVkaXVtPWVtYWlsJnV0bV9jYW1wYWlnbj1uZXdzbGV0dGVyX21lZGlhdHJlbmRzX2V4ZWN1dGl2ZSZzdHJlYW09dG9w/5b97e02b3f92a471b92d70f9Bac844122) Tegna's $5.4 billion attempt to combine with Standard General in 2023.

**What's next**: The vote will likely be met with legal resistance by consumer advocacy groups.

- Already, Free Press, a media advocacy non-profit, [said](https://www.freepress.net/news/free-press-plans-take-carr-court-over-repeal-ownership-cap) it plans to appeal the vote.
