The project to replace and expand the failing Eckert Rd. Bridge in Boise hit a snag when the Boise Planning & Zoning Commission denied a river system application to build the new bridge.

[The project](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/12/09/failing-bridge-could-see-replacement-plus-roundabout-near-barber-park/), which includes two new bridges and a roundabout, requires the City of Boise to sign off on the impacts for the new Eckert Rd. Bridge to the Boise River within Boise City Limits.

The bridge connects Boise’s Barber Valley with southeast Boise. The road crosses the Boise River and Ridenbaugh Canal in quick succession, with access to Barber Park wedged between the two waterways. While the City of Boise controls the land on the north side of the river within its city limits, the south side of the river is in unincorporated Ada County, which includes Barber Park.

[As BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/03/10/eckert-road-bridge-reopens-boise/), the bridge was shut down on an emergency basis in early 2025.

Crews later made emergency repairs to reopen the link, accelerated the replacement project, and set it to start later this year.

## Final stop

The project was on the last phase of needed approvals, having won sign-off from the US Army Corps of Engineers, State of Idaho Department of Water Resources, Ada County, The Nampa-Meridian Irrigation District, Ada County Highway District and a recommendation of approval from City of Boise staff.

![Corwin Ford Nampa - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/02b5ccc2-e925-42c6-9e39-de8bd0223814.jpg)

Corwin Ford Nampa - July 2026 - 2:1

But after a nearly two-hour hearing, where no members of the public testified, the Planning & Zoning Commission narrowly voted to deny a river system permit for the project.

P&Z commission chair Chris Danley led a long and detailed set of questions for Cameron Waite of HDR Engineering and city staff about the project.

A large number of his questions related to the project’s impact on land in Barber Park, which is planned to be used as a construction yard to support the bridge’s construction. A number of old-growth trees could be removed in the park for the bridge construction, which would be replaced by new trees when construction is over.

![ICCU - August 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/1baecb3d-bed1-44ed-bff7-091052e1f1a0.jpg)

ICCU - August 2026 - 2:1

He focused on a rendering provided by ACHD that showed an area of trees that could be removed.

“There’s a map that we have in our packet, and I think I don’t know if I saw it on any of the pictures or renderings of either presentation, but it shows there is a cross-hatch area and it’s grayed out and shows all of the trees in that cross-hatch area are to be removed,” Danley said at one point.

“Has the public been shown anything about the changes that are going to occur in the near term for… construction staging?” he asked.

![Idaho Lottery - 90 MM - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/35da0207-4735-4f00-a726-8f11ae69e570.gif)

Idaho Lottery - 90 MM - 2:1

The area in question is not in the city limits, but in Barber Park.

“We were extremely surprised by the decision and discussion,” ACHD spokesperson Rachel Bjornestad said. “As such, our consultant (HDR) has submitted an appeal.”

## Safety

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/boise-pampz-puts-halt-to-eckert-bridge-replacement-330ce3b8661f0bb026ad.jpg)

Proposed design of the new Eckert Rd. bridge in Boise. Rendering: Via HDR

Danley also raised safety concerns about the bridge. He said the current bridge hadn’t had crashes in several years and questioned ACHD about why it opted for a wider bridge design with a center turn lane and shoulders.

![SERVPRO Boise - Jan25](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/5b2a85ae-933e-4c98-82a5-e207d65304e9.png)

SERVPRO Boise - Jan25

“If there have been no crashes on the existing bridge, then I’d love to know how we keep hearing that word safety throughout this in our packet,” he said to [Waite](https://www.linkedin.com/in/cameron-waite-5399158/). “How is safety being addressed if there are no crashes to fix?”

Waite told Danley that safety goes beyond the bridge itself, and factors in the driveway for Barber Park.

“The biggest issue with that is sight distance,” Waite said. “With the existing pathways and barriers, and especially on the Ridebnaugh Canal Bridge, the cage that goes over the top. Between the alignment and the bridge configuration, we’ve improved that sight distance to make it better, and added those left turns as well.”

![Valley Lookout house - generic](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/82689d5d-b8b7-4c7f-9d4c-b24100ffd632.gif)

Valley Lookout house - generic

Danley challenged the decision to make the bridge three lanes. He noted that Eckert Rd. is listed as a collector road on the Master Street Map, which calls for a two-lane configuration in residential areas.

Waite noted that for the bridge to be two lanes instead of three, it would have to taper down from the nearby roundabout, and again noted the presence of the Barber Park driveway as a complicating factor.

As the public hearing closed, Ricardo Calderon with ACHD noted this is the last step in the project, which is currently out for bid.

![Idaho Lottery - 90 MM - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/35da0207-4735-4f00-a726-8f11ae69e570.gif)

Idaho Lottery - 90 MM - 2:1

“We’re just kind of at the finish line,” he said. “You are one of the last permits we need to move forward with the bridge.”

Bjornestad later told BoiseDev that the bridge design was put together with the Barber Park entrance in mind, and echoed Waite‘s comments in the hearing.

“Improving safety and access at the Barber Park entrance is a goal of the bridge design, which is the reason for the turn lane at the park,” she said. “The bridge alignment will also provide greater visibility at the park entrance.”

## Deliberation

As the commission began to deliberate, two commissioners, Kati Stallings and Tony Torres, said they had not decided on a position. Commissioner Bob Schafer made a motion to approve the project.

“I think we have to remember there are multiple jurisdictions at play here that have almost higher-level scrutiny here as far as the environmental impact of this project,” he said in making the motion. “We have a staff report recommending approval; we have one, two, three, four, five different applications with the city — it’s gone through five different levels of scrutiny as far as staff. It’s gone through the Army Corps of Engineers, Idaho Department of Water Resources, City of Boise, Ada County, Nampa-Meridian Irrigation District, and ACHD will all be giving permits to construct this bridge.”

Danley pushed back, giving a nearly seven-minute response of his position.

![SERVPRO Boise - Jan25](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/5b2a85ae-933e-4c98-82a5-e207d65304e9.png)

SERVPRO Boise - Jan25

“It will not come as a surprise, I absolutely will not support this motion. We have an objective up here to be environmental stewards of our comprehensive plan. We have an obligation to our code that Class A lands, all of the water, everything, the noise, water, air quality are preserved,” he said. He claimed the city has “flat out authority” over ACHD.

The Planning & Zoning Commission is the deciding body on river system permits, but decisions can be appealed to the Boise City Council. Danley said he thought the council should ultimately decide.

“We are not the appropriate level of a decision,” Danley said. “I think that council is. Council are the ones who are elected. This is a very significant level of decision that rises above us. If they want to appeal to council, great, if that’s what the City of Boise wants to live with, great.”

![PCW - P3_ 2_1.png](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/c4722f34-be9c-413f-844a-ffd39cbbecde.png)

PCW - P3\_ 2\_1.png

After deliberation ended, Stallings and Torres were swayed to vote with Danley, and the motion to approve the bridge failed 4-3, with Danley, Stallings, Torres, and Mike Stefancic voting against approval. Schafer, Esther Ceja, and Jennifer Mohr voted to approve.

## Tense back-and-forth

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uuMlbopnsuc)

The commission then spent several minutes deciding what to do next. Finally, Shaffer spoke up.

“I’d like to encourage one of the previous no voters on my motion to make a motion to deny this application and move on,” he said. “That’s clearly where this is going, so someone step up and make the motion…. We’re sitting here… I read off the list of agencies that have signed off… and the Boise City Planning & Zoning Commission is going to get in the way of all of that. I want to be very clear over what we’re talking about right now… over a turn lane.”

![Corwin Ford - One Tank Trips - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/9934db1b-47e1-4fe3-ab77-78261066f89f.jpg)

Corwin Ford - One Tank Trips - 2:1

“I disagree with that contextual argument,” Danley said. “It’s not just a turn lane. It’s part of a project. The buffer space is part of a project. If this were a building, the existing building is about three stories; this is about ten stories, where four stories isn’t even going to be used. If this were a land use decision like that, how would we scrutinize that? We know we’ve doubled the footprint, and a majority of it has no bearing.”

“That’s where you and I have to disagree,” Shafer said. “We haven’t doubled the footprint; there are eight piers in the river now; they are going to go down to two. Yes, the bridge deck is wider. But if we think about what’s in the river right now, yes, we have to remove what’s there, but what they are putting back is less than what is there now. I think we have to make sure we’re talking big picture.”

Commissioner Torres then moved to deny the bridge.

The commission voted the same way, a 4-3 decision to deny the bridge project.

## ACHD appeals

ACHD and HDR appealed the decision Friday. In a letter to the city, the agency and its consultant said the decision was made with an incorrect use of Boise City Code, it was not supported by substantial evidence, and it was “arbitrary, capricious, or an abuse of discretion that was made without a rational basis and in disregard to the facts presented.”

“We firmly believe we have met all conditions for the permit,” Bjornestad said. “This project has been reviewed by the Army Corps of Engineers, the Idaho Department of Water Resources, Ada County, Nampa Meridian Irrigation District, and was recommended for approval from Boise City staff. We do not have a date yet, but we are working with city staff to bring it to the city council as soon as possible,” Bjornestad said. “HDR is also preparing a full report, so we will have much more specific information once that is finalized.”
