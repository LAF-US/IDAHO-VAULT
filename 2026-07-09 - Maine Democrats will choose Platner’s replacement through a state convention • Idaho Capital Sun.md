## Maine Democrats will choose Platner’s replacement through a state convention

US Senate candidate Graham Platner drops out of 2026 race against Susan Collins after he was accused of sexual assault

The Maine Democratic Party will hold a first-of-its-kind convention to choose Graham Platner’s replacement in the race for U.S. Senate, it announced Wednesday evening, around the same time Platner [dropped out](https://mainemorningstar.com/2026/07/08/graham-platner-drops-out-of-maine-u-s-senate-race/) after being accused of sexual assault.

During an emergency meeting, more than 100 state committee members voted to hold the convention after the party promised an open and transparent process to choose Platner’s successor. The party has not yet released details, but it has until July 27 to select a replacement.

The convention will include 500 delegates elected proportionally by county committees, along with the entire 100-person state committee, [according to the Bangor Daily News.](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2026/07/08/politics/elections/maine-democrats-want-convention-replace-graham-platner/) In Maine’s recent history, there has been no other instance of a nominee dropping out after winning a primary, that the Maine Secretary of State’s office is aware of.

“There is an unprecedented amount of energy and enthusiasm among Maine Democrats, driven in part by many of the dedicated volunteers and supporters who were inspired by Graham Platner’s campaign,” Maine Democratic Party leaders Charlie Dingman, Imke Schessler and Devon Murphy-Anderson said in a statement.

“We look forward to coming together and harnessing that energy around our new nominee as we work to defeat Susan Collins in November.”

The party also pledged transparency in the selection process going forward, though the meeting Wednesday was not open to the media.

## Troy Jackson announces bid for nomination

So far, at least one candidate — former Senate President and gubernatorial candidate Troy Jackson — has officially announced he is [seeking to replace](https://mainemorningstar.com/2026/07/07/who-will-replace-graham-platner-and-how-will-the-party-decide/) Platner. Several people, including other former Democratic gubernatorial candidates, have said they’d consider it, although Jackson is the only one who has filed paperwork with the Federal Election Commission to form a U.S. Senate exploratory committee.

“There is a powerful movement of working class people in the state of Maine, and millions more across America who are ready to send a progressive fighter to the Senate,” Jackson said in a statement on Wednesday evening. “I’ve been fighting for that movement my whole life — and I’m sure as hell not backing down now, when this fight is needed most.”

Jackson campaigned with Platner, embraced many of the same progressive priorities and was also endorsed by independent U.S. Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont.

Mark Brewer, chair of the political science department at University of Maine told Maine Morning Star Tuesday that Jackson might be the best candidate to step in to maintain the grassroots support Platner built.

Our Revolution, a progressive political action committee, seconded Jackson stepping in as Platner’s potential replacement.

“He has spent his life in the fight working people are asking for,” Executive Director Joseph Geevarghese said in a statement. “He is not arriving to it because a seat opened up.”

Other Democrats who have said they either would agree to be Platner’s replacement if chosen, or are strongly considering it include Secretary of State Shenna Bellows, Maine Beer Company owner Dan Kleban, and former Maine CDC Director Nirav Shah, among others

## Platner calls out establishment while ending his bid

The Maine Democratic Party was one of the first groups to call on Platner to withdraw on Monday evening following a [Politico report](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/07/06/graham-platner-sexual-assault-allegation-00987737) that a woman Platner had dated on and off for two years was accusing him of sexually assaulting her in 2021.The former nominee then [feuded with party leaders](https://mainemorningstar.com/2026/07/08/platner-campaign-polls-volunteers-as-dispute-over-replacement-intensifies/) on Tuesday and Wednesday after Murphy-Anderson accused his campaign of trying to influence the process for selecting his replacement. She said his team “would have no role” in determining the nominee.

Platner’s campaign pushed back, saying it had reached out only to understand what the process would look like, while adding that he wanted the voters and volunteers who selected him — not the party establishment — to decide his replacement.

In [the video](https://x.com/grahamformaine/status/2075009677495058687?s=46) announcing his withdrawal, Platner denied the allegations against him, saying he was dropping out because of the pressure from the Democratic establishment, which pulled its support from his campaign after Politico published the allegations. Since Monday evening, dozens of Democrats rescinded their endorsements of the oysterman, and political action committees withdrew their financial support.

Platner also addressed what he thinks should happen next.

“I’m not trying to dictate to anyone who it should be or how we get there, but I will say this: it needs to be open, transparent, and democratic,” he said.

“The ball is in the court of the Democratic establishment. The next Democratic senator for Maine needs to belong to the people of Maine. They need to reflect the will and the values of the people of this state.”
