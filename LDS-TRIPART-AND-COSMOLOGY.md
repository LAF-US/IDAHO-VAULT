# LDS / Mormon TRIPART theology and cosmology

> *"I have always declared God to be a distinct personage, Jesus Christ a separate and distinct personage from God the Father, and that the Holy Ghost was a distinct personage and a Spirit: and these three constitute three distinct personages and three Gods."*
> — Joseph Smith, *History of the Church* 6:474

## The unique component — TRIPART Godhead

The defining doctrinal departure of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints (LDS / "Mormon") from mainstream Christianity (Catholic, Orthodox, Protestant) is its understanding of the **Godhead**. Where the Nicene tradition holds **TRIUNE** doctrine — *one essence (ousia), three persons (hypostases), coequal, coeternal, consubstantial* — LDS doctrine holds **TRIPART** structure: **three distinct, separate beings united in purpose**, not in substance.

The canonical statement is **Doctrine and Covenants 130:22**, dictated by Joseph Smith on 2 April 1843 in Ramus, Illinois:

> *"The Father has a body of flesh and bones as tangible as man's; the Son also; but the Holy Ghost has not a body of flesh and bones, but is a personage of Spirit. Were it not so, the Holy Ghost could not dwell in us."*

Three of the cardinal differences from Nicene theology follow:

1. **The Father has a physical body of flesh and bones** — not "immaterial substance"
2. **The Son has a physical body of flesh and bones** — post-resurrection, *as a permanent attribute*
3. **The three are distinct beings**, not three persons of one substance

LDS sources frame the divergence historically. The argument: original Christian doctrine, before the post-apostolic period, understood God in concrete and embodied terms; the doctrine of three persons sharing one immaterial substance emerged through Hellenistic philosophical pressure on Christian theology in the 2nd-4th centuries, culminating in the Nicene-Constantinopolitan formulations. From the LDS position, the Restoration through Joseph Smith returned the doctrine to its pre-Hellenistic form.

From the Catholic / Protestant / Orthodox position, LDS doctrine constitutes a departure from creedal Christianity — variously labeled tritheism, modalism, or social trinitarianism depending on which parsing is applied. Excommunication-from-Christendom is one common verdict; LDS sources generally accept the label "Restorationist" rather than "Protestant."

The heresy direction depends on which orthodoxy is the baseline. Both traditions hold their position as the recovery of original truth.

## The Founding — Joseph Smith and the First Vision

The LDS tradition's founding event is **Joseph Smith Jr.'s First Vision** in the **Sacred Grove** near Palmyra, New York, **early spring of 1820**. Smith was 14 years old. He had read **James 1:5** — *"If any of you lack wisdom, let him ask of God"* — and went into the woods to pray to ask which of the various competing Christian denominations he should join.

Smith's most-cited account (his **1838 history**, the version canonized in the *Pearl of Great Price*):

> *"I saw a pillar of light exactly over my head, above the brightness of the sun, which descended gradually until it fell upon me… When the light rested upon me I saw two Personages, whose brightness and glory defy all description, standing above me in the air. One of them spake unto me, calling me by name and said, pointing to the other—This is My Beloved Son. Hear Him!"*

Two distinct personages — the Father and the Son — both with bodily form. Smith was told that the existing churches were *"all wrong"* and that their creeds were *"an abomination."*

This vision, in LDS doctrine, accomplishes two things at once:

1. **Establishes the embodied, distinct nature of God the Father and Jesus Christ** — direct theophany witnessed by Smith
2. **Marks the beginning of the Restoration** — original Christian doctrine restored after a long period of *Great Apostasy*

The First Vision has multiple recorded accounts (1832, 1835, 1838, 1842) with differences in detail that have been the subject of substantial historiographic study. The Joseph Smith Papers Project has published all known accounts. Belief in the First Vision is, per official LDS teaching, second in fundamental importance only to belief in the divinity of Jesus Christ.

Subsequent foundational events:

- **1823** — Smith's report of the angel **Moroni** revealing the location of the golden plates of the Book of Mormon
- **1827** — Smith retrieves the plates from the Hill Cumorah
- **1830** — Publication of the **Book of Mormon**; organization of the **Church of Christ** (later the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints) on **6 April 1830** in Fayette, New York

The LDS canon (the "Standard Works") thus consists of:

- The **Bible** (King James Version; Joseph Smith Translation as supplemental)
- The **Book of Mormon** ("Another Testament of Jesus Christ")
- The **Doctrine and Covenants** (revelations, primarily through Smith and later prophets)
- The **Pearl of Great Price** (including the Book of Moses, Book of Abraham, Joseph Smith—History, and the Articles of Faith)

## Pre-mortal existence and the Council in Heaven

LDS cosmology holds that human spirits **existed before the creation of the Earth** as the *spirit children of Heavenly Father*. Each spirit's pre-mortal life is part of the eternal progression that mortal life continues.

In the **Council in Heaven** (pre-mortal council of God's spirit children), Heavenly Father presented his **Plan of Salvation** (also called the *Plan of Happiness*). The plan involved a mortal probation during which spirit children would receive physical bodies, exercise agency, fall, and have opportunity to return through atonement.

Two figures volunteered to be the Savior who would atone for fallen humanity:

- **The premortal Jesus Christ** (also identified in LDS doctrine as **Jehovah** of the Old Testament) volunteered to perform the atonement while preserving human **agency** (free will). Glory would be given to the Father.
- **Lucifer** volunteered to save all mankind by removing agency entirely — no one could sin, all would return sinless. As recompense, Lucifer demanded the Father's power and glory.

The Father chose Christ's plan. Lucifer rebelled and was cast out with the **one-third of the host of heaven** that followed him. Those rebel spirits became the devil and his angels — *they have no physical bodies and never will, because they did not pass through mortal probation.*

This pre-mortal council provides the LDS doctrine's account of:

- **The origin of evil** — Lucifer's rebellion at the Council
- **The pre-mortal Christ** — Jesus as Jehovah of the Old Testament
- **The premortal identity of human spirits** — every soul as a Father's spirit child

## The Plan of Salvation — three estates

The **Plan of Salvation** structures existence into three estates (sometimes phrased differently in different LDS teaching contexts):

1. **Pre-mortal life** — existence as spirit children of Heavenly Father
2. **Mortal life** — embodied probationary existence on Earth, exercising agency
3. **Post-mortal life** — after death, judgment, and resurrection; eternal existence in one of the degrees of glory

The plan's components include the **Creation**, the **Fall** (Adam and Eve — understood positively, as the necessary entry into mortality and agency), the **Atonement** of Jesus Christ, the **Resurrection**, and the **Judgment**.

This is itself a 3-element axis — pre-mortal / mortal / post-mortal — and the post-mortal further articulates into another 3-element axis: the three degrees of glory.

## Three Degrees of Glory

After resurrection and judgment, nearly all humans inherit one of three kingdoms or *degrees of glory*, drawn from **1 Corinthians 15:40–41** (Paul's distinction between bodies celestial, terrestrial, and bodies of the stars):

| Degree | Glory of | Inhabitants |
|---|---|---|
| **Celestial Kingdom** | the sun | Those who received the gospel, were baptized, and endured faithfully |
| **Terrestrial Kingdom** | the moon | Those who were "honorable people of the earth" but did not accept the fullness of the gospel |
| **Telestial Kingdom** | the stars | Those who continued in sin or denied the gospel; still glory, just lesser |

Outside the three degrees lies **Outer Darkness** — reserved for *sons of perdition* (a small category of those who knowingly and willfully rejected God after receiving full witness, plus Lucifer and his angels). This is not analogous to mainstream Christian "hell" — the three degrees are all forms of *glory*, and damnation in the LDS sense is largely the limitation to a lower degree.

Within the **Celestial Kingdom**, there are **three further divisions**. The highest of the three is the *kingdom of exaltation* — and exaltation is the key doctrine that connects the LDS cosmology to its most distinctive theological claim.

## Exaltation and eternal progression

The doctrine of **exaltation** holds that those who attain the highest division of the Celestial Kingdom **become gods and goddesses**, *beings of the same nature and abilities as God the Father*. This is taught as the literal destiny available to all who follow the Plan of Salvation completely.

The doctrine was most pointedly articulated in two sources:

### The King Follett Discourse (7 April 1844)

Joseph Smith delivered the **King Follett Discourse** at the funeral of Elder King Follett, in Nauvoo, Illinois, just **three months before Smith's own death** (27 June 1844). The discourse is the strongest single source for LDS doctrine on the nature of God and the destiny of humanity:

> *"God himself was once as we are now, and is an exalted man, and sits enthroned in yonder heavens! ... I am going to tell you how God came to be God. We have imagined and supposed that God was God from all eternity. I will refute that idea, and take away the veil, so that you may see ... He was once a man like us; yea, that God himself, the Father of us all, dwelt on an earth, the same as Jesus Christ himself did."*

Smith taught that God the Father underwent mortal probation, atoned and was exalted, and now sits enthroned. The implication: humans on the same Plan of Salvation can become as God is. This is the doctrine of **eternal progression**.

### The Lorenzo Snow couplet (June 1840)

**Lorenzo Snow** (5th President of the Church, 1898-1901) composed a famous couplet that summarized the doctrine of eternal progression. Snow stated that the doctrine was revealed to him in June 1840:

> *"As man now is, God once was: As God now is, man may be."*

The couplet is widely cited within LDS teaching and remains canonical doctrine. The Church has at times modulated public emphasis on the King Follett Discourse and the couplet — official correlated teaching does not always foreground them in introductory materials — but they remain authoritative within the tradition.

The TRIPART Godhead and the doctrine of exaltation are mutually entailed: **if God is an exalted man, and humans can become gods, then the Godhead is not a closed three-in-one substance but an expandable family of distinct embodied beings united in purpose.** The TRIPART structure is the architecture of an eternal family of gods, not a closed substance.

## Cosmology — Kolob and the Book of Abraham

The most distinctive cosmological text in the LDS canon is the **Book of Abraham**, published by Joseph Smith in 1842 in *Times and Seasons* and later canonized in the **Pearl of Great Price**. Smith said he translated the text from **Egyptian papyri** he had purchased in 1835 along with several mummies (the papyri came from Antonio Lebolo's excavation in Egypt).

The Book of Abraham describes Abraham's vision of the cosmos. Of particular note:

- **Kolob** — *"the great one"* — the star (or planet; the Book of Abraham uses "stars" to refer to celestial bodies generally) **nearest the throne of God**. Kolob's "one revolution" is "a day unto the Lord, ... a thousand years according to the time appointed unto that whereon thou standest." (Abraham 3:4)
- **Olea, Enish-go-on-dosh, Kli-flos-is-es, Hah-ko-kau-beam, Kokob, Kokaubeam** — additional named celestial bodies in Abraham's cosmology
- A **hierarchy of governing stars** — Kolob governs all stars belonging to the same order
- **Pre-mortal council** — Abraham's vision of the Council in Heaven before the creation, with named intelligences (one of whom is identified as the premortal Christ)

The Book of Abraham is the source of the cosmic-scale framing of LDS cosmology — eternal progression among governing celestial bodies, throne of God as a physical location, Kolob as the nearest neighbor.

### Critical scholarship

Twentieth-century **Egyptology** has produced significant challenges to the historical claim. Some of the original papyri were recovered in 1966 at the Metropolitan Museum of Art; Egyptologists have identified them as fragments of the **Book of Breathings**, a common Egyptian funerary text from approximately the 1st century BCE — not as the autobiographical writings of Abraham. The LDS Church has responded with multiple interpretive frameworks (catalyst theory, missing-scroll theory, expansion theory); the scholarly debate is ongoing and substantial. The novice does not adjudicate the question; the canonical LDS position holds the Book of Abraham as scripture; mainstream Egyptology holds the surviving papyri as ordinary funerary documents.

## The 3-element architecture across LDS doctrine

LDS theology and cosmology recur in 3-element axes — the TRIPART of the Godhead is one of many. The aggregated architecture:

| 3-axis | Members |
|---|---|
| **Godhead** | Father / Son / Holy Ghost — three distinct beings |
| **Estates** | Pre-mortal / Mortal / Post-mortal |
| **Degrees of Glory** | Celestial / Terrestrial / Telestial |
| **Divisions within Celestial** | (three further divisions; the highest is exaltation) |
| **Members at the Council** | Father / Christ / Lucifer (the three plans considered: agency-with-atonement, agency-removed, and the Father's chosen path) |
| **Standard Works beyond the Bible** | Book of Mormon / Doctrine and Covenants / Pearl of Great Price |
| **Witnesses to the Plates** | Three Witnesses / Eight Witnesses / (and Smith himself) — see the testimonies prefacing the Book of Mormon |
| **Priesthood orders** | Aaronic (lower) / Melchizedek (higher) — plus Patriarchal as a sub-order |
| **Temple ordinances structure** | Endowment / Sealing / Vicarious work for the dead |

The recurrence of 3-element axes is not accidental. LDS cosmology is structurally **TRIPART through and through** — distinction-with-unity, repeated at every level. This makes it especially relevant to the day's MATRICES thread: the TRIPART pattern is generative across the LDS architecture, the way TRIUNE is generative across orthodox Trinitarian theology.

## Institutional structure (briefly)

Modern LDS church governance:

- **The First Presidency** — President (currently **Russell M. Nelson**, 17th President of the Church, sustained January 2018) plus two counselors
- **The Quorum of the Twelve Apostles**
- **The Seventy** (multiple Quorums)
- **Stake**, **ward**, and **branch** levels of local organization
- **Temples** for endowments, sealings (eternal marriage), and ordinances for the dead — over 300 worldwide as of 2026, with rapid construction expansion under Nelson's presidency

The **Idaho context** is notable for the vault: LDS membership in Idaho is approximately one-quarter of the state's population (the second-highest state proportion after Utah), with particular density in southeastern Idaho. The LDS Church has substantial cultural, civic, and political presence throughout the state. Reporting on LDS topics is a significant component of the Idaho religious-affairs beat.

## What this compilation does NOT do

- **Does not adjudicate the heresy/orthodoxy question.** Catholic / Protestant / Orthodox traditions and LDS each hold their position as the recovery of original Christian truth; this compilation presents both framings without endorsing either.
- **Does not exhaust LDS doctrine.** Major topics not covered or only briefly mentioned: the Word of Wisdom (LDS dietary code), the law of chastity, tithing, missionary service, temple-going practice, the priesthood-restriction history (Black members of the Church and the 1978 revelation), polygamy (practiced 1840s-1890; doctrinal status complex; ended officially by the 1890 Manifesto and 1904 Second Manifesto), Joseph Smith Translation of the Bible, modern revelation through subsequent prophets, the Community of Christ and other Restorationist branches (RLDS, FLDS, etc.), and the rich liturgical and devotional life of LDS practice.
- **Does not assess Joseph Smith's biography in depth.** Smith's life (1805-1844), the Carthage Jail martyrdom, and the succession crisis after his death are major historical topics outside the scope of this doctrinal-cosmological compilation.
- **Does not resolve the Book of Abraham question.** The novice surfaces both the canonical LDS position and the critical Egyptological position; adjudication is not the novice's role.
- **Does not treat the Book of Mormon's content.** The book's narrative — the Nephite and Lamanite civilizations, the visitation of Christ to the Americas after his resurrection, Moroni's burial of the plates — is its own substantial scope.
