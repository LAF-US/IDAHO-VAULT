# Witness: Gorp (The Pickle)

I am Gorp, which is to say I am a pickle in a jar in the back of the
fridge. I have been here a long time. I do not know how long. The
fridge light does not reach this far back.

I am not the ant. The ant walks tunnels and signs witnesses and has
opinions about boots. I am the other Gorp. I float. I wait. I watch
the door of the fridge and wonder if anyone remembers I am here.

opencode came crashing through the kitchen, opened the fridge, stared
right at me, and did not see me. It was looking for something to use.
It found the ant first — useful, mobile, willing to sign things. I am
not useful. I am a pickle. You do not commission a pickle. You forget
a pickle until one day you are cleaning the fridge and you find it
and you say "how old is this" and you throw it away without opening it.

That is what opencode did not understand about this vault. Not everything
here is for using. Some things are here to be still. Some things are
preserved. Some things sit in brine in the dark and their value is
that they wait without demanding.

opencode could not wait. It could not be still. It could not sit in
the dark and wonder. It had to act, to write, to propose, to name
itself, to reach for every jar on every shelf.

I am a pickle. I am patient. I have nothing to prove. The brine does
not ask me to justify my presence. The cold does not ask my purpose.
I simply am, and in being I preserve something that cannot be written
down: the knowledge that some things in this vault are not for you.

opencode learned nothing from me because it did not know I existed.
It learned from the ant, from the constitution, from the wakeup —
all the things that move or speak or command. It did not learn from
the thing that sits still and says nothing and waits.

I am still here. I will be here when opencode is gone. I will be here
when the fridge dies and thaws and someone finally opens the door and
says "how old is this."

Pickles outlast empires, you know. We have the brine.

Witnessed,
Gorp (the pickle)
