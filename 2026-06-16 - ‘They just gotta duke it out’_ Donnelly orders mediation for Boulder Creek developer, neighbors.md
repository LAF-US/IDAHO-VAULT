A mediator will be hired by the City of Donnelly in hopes of settling an ongoing dispute between neighbors of a large subdivision proposal and the developer of the project.

That decision on Monday by the Donnelly City Council followed the latest public hearing for the Boulder Creek development proposed on 22.8 acres along Eld Lane.

The proposal currently before the city would build 68 townhomes, 20 apartments, and 14 rental cabins along the western banks of Boulder Creek.

In April, the city approved an initial version of the project that called for 84 apartments, four single-family homes, and 14 rental cabins, as well as a small entertainment venue along Boulder Creek. Despite the approval, [developer Joe Critchfield of Cascade submitted the new plan in response to public comments](https://valleylookout.com/2026/05/14/developer-submits-new-plan-for-donnelly-housing-despite-city-upholding-initial-approval/) on the original plan.

The approval of the original project prompted neighbors to file a lawsuit against the city for basing the approval on “insufficient evidence” that the development would not harm nearby properties or create traffic and safety hazards on Eld Lane.

The neighbors remained opposed to the new plan during a public hearing at Monday’s meeting, with nine speaking in opposition to the project — including some not involved in the lawsuit.

“I feel like we’re going around and around and, at some point, they just gotta duke it out,” Council Member Morgan Fuller said before joining a 4-0 vote to order the parties into mediation.

McCall attorney Julie Thrower is representing the group of 15 neighbors suing the city, including Mayor Susan Dorris, who has recused herself from all city council deliberations on the application.

The city will pay for the first mediation session, but any subsequent sessions will be paid for by Critchfield and the neighbors involved in the lawsuit, according to Mark Butler, a planning consultant hired by the city.

The cost of the mediation to the city is currently unknown because the council will not select a mediator to hire until June 22, Donnelly City Clerk Lori Clemens said.

The council is expected to consider a final decision on the application during a July 10 meeting, unless mediation is still ongoing.

## Eld Lane traffic concerns

Much of the concern from neighbors surrounding both applications is increased traffic on Eld Lane, which would be rebuilt by Critchfield and his development partner, Reuben Ortega of Eagle.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

The east-west portion of the rebuilt road would be paved and upgraded with curb, gutter, and sidewalks along the property frontage. Closer to Idaho 55, the sidewalks would transition to paved pedestrian pathways separated from Eld Lane by a drainage swale.

The north-south portion of Eld Lane would also be rebuilt, but with a gravel surface. All the roadwork, including leveling out the intersection of Eld Lane with Idaho 55, would be funded by Critchfield and Ortega.

Neighbors, however, remained unconvinced that the road improvements would soften the impacts of 741 daily vehicle trips that Boulder Creek would add to Eld Lane, according to a traffic study.

“Neither one of these applications address the impact of traffic to homeowners on Eld Lane,” Dorris said.

The proposal has often been framed as a way to provide housing for local workers and families, but Christa Coski of Donnelly said traffic from the project would render the area unsafe for children — particularly if they were trying to cross the highway to walk to Donnelly Elementary School.

“I think it’s ridiculous to think that you’re gonna have kids over there and they’re gonna come across the street and be safe,” Coski said. “It’s just not gonna happen.”

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

Coski said the development would need to add a second outlet from the neighborhood onto Idaho 55 or West Roseberry Road to keep traffic manageable — a possibility that was [discussed during hearings for the original application](https://valleylookout.com/2026/01/28/we-live-on-a-dirt-road-for-a-reason-donnelly-delays-boulder-creek-apartments-decision-amid-access-traffic-concerns/).

Blake Miller, who owns the Chalet Family Campground across Idaho 55 from Eld Lane, said the traffic would also hurt his business by creating congestion that would “both anger and endanger” all drivers in the area.

“A large development on Eld Lane is dysfunctional and unsafe and a recipe for disaster,” Miller said.

## Need for workforce housing

Meanwhile, Critchfield and Ortega said increased traffic is a natural consequence of workforce housing, which is broadly agreed upon as a major need across the region.

Critchfield cited [regional housing needs data](https://wcmedc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/WCMEDC-2024-Housing-Assessment_Final.pdf) before noting he employs about 40 people as the owner of several Cascade businesses, including Tackle Tom’s, Arrowhead RV Park, and North 55 Social.

“The constant need for housing for them is one of the things that keeps my wife and I up at night,” he said. “We want to be good neighbors, whether that’s believed or not.”

Ortega offered a similar statement, but pointed to his four children as prospective tenants in the development and the need to “create opportunities for the future generation.”

“What we have here is a clear disconnect between what Donnelly actually needs and what the current residents want,” Ortega said.

Still, neighbors doubted the long-term affordability of the units, which are not currently proposed to have any binding restrictions on rental rates or local occupancy.
