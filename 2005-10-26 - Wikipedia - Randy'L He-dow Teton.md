**Randy'L He-dow Teton** (born 1976) is the [Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone "Shoshone") woman who posed as the model for the US [Sacagawea dollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacagawea_dollar "Sacagawea dollar") coin, first issued in 2000.

## Biography

Randy'L He-dow Teton is a [Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone "Shoshone") - [Bannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannock_\(tribe\) "Bannock (tribe)") / [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree "Cree") from the Lincoln Creek district of the [Fort Hall Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Hall_Reservation "Fort Hall Reservation") in southeastern [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"). She is the second oldest of five siblings and the daughter of Randy Leo Teton and Bonnie C. Wuttunee-Wadsworth (Shoshone-Cree), both members of the Shoshone-Bannock Tribe.

Her given middle name is He-dow, which is pronounced \[ˈhi:do:\] in the [Shoshoni language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshoni_language "Shoshoni language") and means " [meadowlark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_meadowlark "Western meadowlark") " (*Sturnella neglecta*), while in the [Northern Paiute language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Paiute_language "Northern Paiute language") it means "close to ground" and is pronounced \[ˈhai.ðo:\].[^1]

She graduated from the [University of New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_New_Mexico "University of New Mexico") at 24 with a B.A. in [Art History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_History "Art History") and a minor in [Native American Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_Studies "Native American Studies").

## Sacagawea dollar

In 1998, the [United States Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Mint "United States Mint") invited sculptor [Glenna Goodacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenna_Goodacre "Glenna Goodacre") to submit a design for the new [dollar coin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dollar_coin_\(United_States\) "Dollar coin (United States)") featuring [Sacagawea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacagawea "Sacagawea"), the [Shoshone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoshone "Shoshone") woman who acted as guide and interpreter for the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition "Lewis and Clark Expedition"). Goodacre went to the [Institute of American Indian Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_American_Indian_Arts "Institute of American Indian Arts") Museum in [Santa Fe, New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe,_New_Mexico "Santa Fe, New Mexico") to find a Shoshone woman to model Sacagawea, since no contemporary portraits exist. Goodacre chose Teton, the daughter of a museum employee, to be the face of Sacagawea.

## Career

Teton toured the country extensively to promote the new dollar coin's introduction and as a motivational speaker to encourage [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") education. Teton has stated that, "the image doesn't represent me, it represents all Native American women. All women have the dignity of the Golden Dollar's image." [^2]

Teton currently serves as the public information officer for the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes in southeastern [[Idaho]]. She continues to make public appearance as Sacagawea to bring attention to American Indian and Alaska Native issues and concerns.
