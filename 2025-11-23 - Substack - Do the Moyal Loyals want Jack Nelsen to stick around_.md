Jack Nelsen is one of the worst Republicans in the Idaho Capitol.

He’s been censured by two of his three county GOP committees, and his legislative scorecards show he’s barely a Republican.

![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!NUq1!,w_1456,c_limit,f_webp,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F2b0f2a2d-3a54-4321-b11f-7de390e2dada_2560x1707.jpeg)

Nelsen earned himself a 39.1% on the 2025 Freedom Index during a session when many moderate Republicans saw higher scores due solely to more conservative legislation running through the legislative process. He also notched a 0% on the Spending Index, meaning he voted to grow government spending with every single vote.

He also earned an F on the Idaho GOP platform scorecard, which measures how often a lawmaker votes with the GOP’s guiding policy document.

Suffice it to say, Nelsen has been a complete disaster.

Yet, a new dynamic has me wondering if the Moyle Loyals want Nelsen to stick around.

Over the weekend Young Americans for Liberty reportedly sent door-knockers into Nelsen’s district to share the news about his poor voting record. That set Nelsen off and earned YAL a silly post from the lawmaker.

*Just wanted to let you know there are paid political canvassers being paid from Young Americans for Liberty—YAL (a pro-drug legalization group from Texas) going door-to-door this weekend to try to discredit me and our Idaho values.*

*They are going to claim I have an anti-liberty voting record but my voting record is simple: I don’t answer to outside groups, political clubs, or activists from other states. I answer to the families, farmers, and small businesses right here in our community.*

Now, I’m pretty sure Idahoans don’t support Nelsen’s votes against grocery tax repeal, against school choice, and for more government spending, but I digress.

What’s striking in Nelsen’s clap back against YAL is that he uses, almost word for word, the same type of rhetoric flowing from the Moyle Loyals, a band of lawmakers and Twitter misfits who do Mike Moyle’s dirty work.

The chief troll, Sen. Brian Lenney, regularly smears YAL as a “pro-drug Libertarian PAC based in Austin.” Lenney, who was YAL’s Golden Boy and even spoke at their conferences, has intimated that YAL shouldn’t be doing any work in Idaho.

Keep in mind that no YAL-associated lawmaker has ever pushed a drug bill, and Lenney works for Cliff Maloney, who has advocated for full Oregon-style drug legalization in the past.

YAL, for all of its failing in legislative strategy and grassroots outreach, has been excellent in its work to expel moderate Republicans from the Statehouse. Lenney should know, as YAL helped unseat Jeff Agenbroad, Lenney’s predecessor.

All of this begs the question: Do the Moyle Loyalists want YAL to leave Jack Nelsen alone?

Do they really want YAL to exit the state, which would give the establishment an even bigger money advantage that it already has? Do these folks not see that YAL has been a valuable partner in making the Legislature more conservative every year?

I would love an answer from Lenney or the other YAL-haters, but I suspect they understand the implications of a full YAL withdrawal. I seriously doubt they are ready or willing to accept that YAL is ultimately a force for good, even if their legislative advocacy efforts are a bit ham-handed.

I hope some people wise up and realize that YAL can be a valuable ally in unseating people like Nelsen, Jim Guthrie, and Lori McCann.
