# Fablehaven Shadow Plague v1

*Filed 2026-06-01. A chapter of `!/FABLEHAVEN-TAXONOMY-v1-2026-06-01.md` —
the Corruption / The Flip class.*

*Reference source: Fablehaven — Grip of the Shadow Plague (Brandon Mull):
Navarog, Kurisock, Ephira, the nail, the Fairy Queen's stone, Lena Burgess.
Some plot entanglements are summarized; finer mechanics are flagged. The vault
is syncretic.*

---

## The One Fact That Organizes Everything

The Shadow Plague turns **light creatures dark.**

Not kills. Not animates. **Flips.** A creature that was good becomes its own
malevolent opposite, retaining its capabilities and turning them against what
it used to protect. This is a distinct threat from anything in the Undead
Taxonomy: the undead are about *what survives death*; the plague is about a
**living, trusted, benevolent process being converted, alive, into a hostile
one** — and still looking like itself.

Everything below follows from that one fact.

---

## The Mechanism — Canon

The contagion was engineered: Navarog gave **the nail** to Kurisock, who bound
it with what had been the tree of **Ephira**. Ephira set the nail in her new
tree, amplifying both their power and **making the darkness contagious.** From
that source:

- **It affects only light creatures**, changing them dark. (Dark creatures have
  nothing to flip.)
- **Humans become invisible shadows** — present, unseeable by anyone except
  Seth (shadow-charmed). The corrupted human is not gone; it is *there and
  unobservable by normal sight.*
- **It spreads by the bite.** A bitten creature shows a **maroon wound** at the
  site; the wound **spreads slowly or quickly depending on the number of
  bites**, and when it completes, the creature turns.
- **It is cured at the source.** Kendra obtains a **light stone** from the Fairy
  Queen (whose shrine is destroyed in the making); the plague is ended when the
  stone is touched to **the nail** — Lena Burgess sacrificing herself to do it.

Four properties, and each is a diagnostic.

---

## The Four Properties as Diagnostics

### 1. It flips the trusted, not the hostile
*Only light creatures are vulnerable.* The plague cannot corrupt what was
already dark — there is nothing to invert. Its entire target surface is the set
of things you **trust.**

*Vault mapping:* the compromise that matters does not turn your known-hostile
inputs more hostile — those were already quarantined. It turns your **trusted**
components: the vetted dependency, the privileged insider, the benevolent
automation, the ally. The blast surface of a flip-class compromise is exactly
your trust graph. The more you trusted it, the more capability it brings across
the line when it flips.

### 2. Corruption scales with exposure
*The wound spreads in proportion to the number of bites.* One contact is a slow
burn; many contacts complete the turn fast.

*Vault mapping:* corruption is **dose-dependent.** A single exposure to a
compromised source may be survivable and reversible; repeated exposure
saturates and completes the flip. This argues for *early* isolation — a process
with one maroon wound is recoverable; the same process after sustained contact
is past saving. Count the bites.

### 3. The flipped human goes invisible
*Corrupted humans become shadows only Seth can see.* The most dangerous
converts are the ones ordinary observation **cannot detect at all.**

*Vault mapping:* a sufficiently compromised insider does not look more
suspicious — it drops **below normal observability.** Standard monitoring
returns nothing because the thing has moved into a register your instruments
don't read. As with the kobold (true nature visible only to a seer), the
counter is a **privileged observer** with sight the ordinary stack lacks —
and the institutional will to believe that observer when they report a shadow
no one else can see.

### 4. It is cured at the source, not the symptom
*The stone is touched to the nail — one act at the root ends the whole plague.*
No one cured the corrupted creatures one by one. They neutralized the **source
artifact.**

*Vault mapping:* a contagion radiating from a single source has a single point
of cure. Chasing individual corrupted processes is endless and losing; the win
condition is to **locate and neutralize the root** — the poisoned dependency,
the malicious package, the compromised key, the nail. This is the inverse of
the Lich's phylactery in one respect and the twin of it in another: the Lich's
phylactery must be destroyed to end an *unauthorized persistence*; the plague's
nail must be neutralized to end an *unauthorized propagation*. Both doctrines
say the same operational thing — **find the object the whole problem hangs on,
and end that, not its emanations.**

---

## The Cost of the Cure

The cure was not free. The Fairy Queen's shrine was **destroyed** to make the
stone; **Lena sacrificed herself** to apply it. The root-level counter to a
flip-class contagion is often the most expensive single act available — it
consumes the instrument that makes it and sometimes the one who wields it.

*Vault mapping:* neutralizing a source-level compromise can mean burning a
trusted asset to do it — revoking a root key every system depends on, taking
down the shared dependency, destroying the shrine. Budget for the cure being
costlier than any individual containment, and recognize that the cheap-looking
path (treat each victim) is the one that actually loses.

---

## Relation to the Other Flip in the Catalogue

The Shadow Plague is the *external, engineered* flip. The fairy→imp inversion
(see the Taxonomy index, class 3) is the *internal, environmental* flip — a
fairy kept from its element overnight souring into its own opposite. Same
deep pattern, two sources: a good thing turned into its malevolent twin, its
capabilities intact and inverted. One is done *to* the creature by an adversary
with a nail; the other happens *to* the creature by mishandling its conditions.

Diagnose which you are facing: an engineered contagion with a source artifact
to neutralize, or a self-inversion produced by the conditions you put the
process in. The first you cure at the root. The second you prevent by not
caging the fairy.

---

## Diagnostic Questions

1. **Is the target surface your trust graph?** A flip-class threat goes after
   what you trust, not what you already guard. Inventory what a flip would hand
   the adversary — capability-by-capability — for each trusted component.
2. **How many bites?** Corruption is dose-dependent. Isolate at the first
   maroon wound; do not wait for the turn to complete.
3. **Has anything dropped below observability?** A converted insider may go
   *quieter*, not louder. Do you have the privileged observer who can see the
   shadow — and will you believe them?
4. **Where is the nail?** Is this a contagion with a single source artifact? If
   so, stop chasing victims and find the root. Cure the source.
5. **Can you pay for the cure?** The root-level counter may cost a trusted
   asset or more. Budget the expensive cure; the cheap one loses.
