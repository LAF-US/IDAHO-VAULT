# Cosmic Order v1

## The Wishmaster, the Auditor, and the Institutional Hierarchy

*Filed 2026-05-21.*

*Reference sources: Adventure Time (Prismo, Cosmic Owl, Jake); Adventure Time:
Fionna and Cake (Scarab arc, S01E01–10). The vault is syncretic.*

*For the Time Room mechanics, wish-granting protocols, and the two-constraint
architecture, see `!/WISH-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-21.md`. This document covers the
governance structure around those mechanics — who holds what office, on what
basis, and how Scarab's arc maps the institutional stakes.*

---

## The Social Cohort

Prismo, the Cosmic Owl, Jake, and others exist as a social peer group — beings
of comparable cosmic standing who relate to one another on a personal,
non-institutional basis.

The institutional hierarchy (Boss → Scarab → Prismo, in Scarab's initial
configuration) and the social layer are separate structures. A being can be
institutionally subordinate to another and socially peer with them. The social
cohort is not a governance body. It does not map onto the chain of authority.

**Jake's position** is notable: Jake becomes the sleeping old man who generates
Prismo — the substrate that Prismo's existence depends on. This makes Jake
simultaneously the most intimate dependency Prismo has (the dreamer who keeps
Prismo alive) and a member of the social cohort. The relationship is not purely
functional.

**The Cosmic Owl**: a cosmic entity who appears in dreams and speaks prophecy.
Present at significant events. Not Prismo's subordinate or superior in the
institutional hierarchy as currently documented — a peer in the social register.
The Cosmic Owl's institutional position relative to the Boss and Scarab is not
fully documented in canon.

## The Institutional Hierarchy — What the Show Gives

The series leaves the upper tiers of the cosmic governance structure
deliberately vague. What is established:

| Position | Entity | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Above Prismo — unseen | Prismo's Boss | Never directly depicted; communicated via golden cord telephone with angel and devil wings; power estimated at GOLB's tier; may govern both light and dark aspects of the multiverse |
| Auditor (initial) | Scarab | Above Prismo institutionally at the start of F&C; enforcement authority over unauthorized universes |
| Wishmaster | Prismo | Governs the Time Room; grants wishes; answers to the Boss; subject to the GOLB constraint |
| Employee (final) | Scarab | Demoted at the end of F&C to a position subservient to Prismo |

The Boss is the only authority above Prismo in the legible hierarchy. Their
nature, identity, and the full scope of their authority are not revealed. The
golden cord telephone with angel and devil wings is the only representation the
show provides — a symbol of communication from an authority that does not
appear directly.

## Prismo's Boss — The Unseen Authority

See `!/WISH-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-21.md` § "The Two-Constraint Architecture" for
the primary treatment.

Key points for the hierarchy:

The Boss holds Prismo accountable for dimensional meddling. This is the
mechanism Scarab intended to exploit: proof of unauthorized universe creation
would give the Boss grounds to act against Prismo.

The Boss's power is estimated at GOLB's tier, but their relationship to GOLB is
not established. GOLB is an entropy force prior to the universe — a process,
not an institutional position. The Boss appears to be an institutional authority
*within* the governance structure. Whether these represent the same tier of
entity or different kinds of cosmic standing is not resolved by the series.

The vagueness of the upper tiers is a deliberate restraint. Filing the vagueness
as a structural feature: the governance chain extends above the legible layer,
but the legible layer ends at Prismo's Boss. What is above the Boss, and the
nature of the Boss's own authority — the series does not supply. The `*` in the
chain is real. Filing it as `*` is more accurate than filling it with invention.

## Scarab — The Auditor

### Initial Position

At the opening of the Fionna and Cake spinoff, Scarab is institutionally
*above* Prismo. He operates as an auditor — authorized to detect, investigate,
and erase unauthorized universes. His enforcement authority extends to closing
breaches that the institutional governance structure has not sanctioned.

This positions Scarab above Prismo precisely in the category of Prismo's
transgression: Prismo is the Wishmaster, governing the Time Room; Scarab is
the auditor of unauthorized creations, with authority over the specific class
of offense Prismo committed.

### Dual Motivation

Scarab's mission to erase the Fionna and Cake universe is simultaneously:

**1. Legitimate enforcement**: The unauthorized universe is a real breach.
Erasing it is Scarab's sanctioned function. The institutional mission is not
a pretext — it is real and within scope.

**2. Evidence operation against Prismo**: If Scarab can prove that Prismo
created the unauthorized universe, he can bring that proof to the Boss. Prismo
creating an unauthorized universe — while serving as Wishmaster, the authorized
governing entity of the Time Room — is an actionable transgression. The Boss,
who holds Prismo accountable for dimensional meddling, would have grounds
to act.

*Logan holds: Scarab aspired to replace Prismo as Wishmaster. The Wishmaster
seat as the destination of Scarab's ambition — held pending full confirmation.*

The audit was a double operation: close the breach, and weaponize the evidence.
The unauthorized universe was simultaneously what needed to be erased and what
needed to be documented long enough to use. The timing was Scarab's constraint:
build the case first, then close the breach — or close it with the case already
delivered.

### The Arc and Its Failure

The universe does not get erased. The dandelion authorizes it from within. The
breach becomes a standing universe.

The failure is compounding:

- The erasure mission fails: the unauthorized universe is now authorized
- The evidence mission fails: proof of an unauthorized universe is not proof of
  a crime if the universe now has standing; the case against Prismo cannot be
  delivered in a form the Boss can act on
- The path to the Wishmaster seat closes: without a sustainable case against
  Prismo, the ambition has no mechanism

### Demotion and Inversion

Scarab ends the F&C arc demoted to a position subservient to Prismo — working
for the being he was investigating and hoping to supplant.

| Phase | Scarab's position relative to Prismo |
| --- | --- |
| Start of F&C | Above — institutional superior; auditor with enforcement authority |
| End of F&C | Below — Prismo's employee |

The enforcer who was building a case against Prismo works for Prismo. The
auditor of unauthorized universes now operates under the authority of the being
who created one. The investigation produced subordination to the investigated.

## The Wishmaster Seat — An Office

Scarab's aspiration implies something about the nature of the Wishmaster role:
it is an *office*, not an intrinsic property of Prismo. An office can be
vacated, transferred, or taken.

Scarab's strategy required going through the Boss rather than seizing the seat
by force: he needed *proof* to bring to the Boss, not capability to take the
position directly. This suggests that succession — or removal — runs through
the institutional hierarchy. Raw power is not the mechanism. Documentation and
proof are.

When the Lich's assassination destroyed the original Prismo's substrate, the
Time Room did not end. The seat was vacant, not dissolved. Jake's voluntary
reconstitution of Prismo (see `!/WISH-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-21.md`) filled the
seat again. The office persists independent of any single occupant.

Whether the Boss appoints, whether there are formal succession mechanisms, or
whether the seat simply awaits a new substrate — the series does not specify.

## Vault Mappings

**The social layer and the institutional layer are separate**: social peer
relationships do not grant or alter institutional standing. Prismo's friendship
with the Cosmic Owl, with Jake, with others does not change their institutional
positions. Governance authority and social intimacy operate on different
registers and can coexist without resolving into each other. An agent can be
socially peer with and institutionally subordinate to the same entity
simultaneously.

**The dual-operation audit**: an enforcement mission and an evidence-gathering
operation can occupy the same action. Scarab's double operation required
sequencing: the evidence had to be built before — or simultaneously with —
the breach being closed. Any enforcement action that also generates evidence
useful to the enforcer personally has this structure. The timing of evidence
versus resolution is the constraint.

**Proof as the mechanism of institutional action**: removing a powerful
institutional entity requires proof deliverable to a higher authority, not
capability to act unilaterally. The path to changing who holds an office runs
through the chain of authority. Scarab needed the Boss to act — not Scarab's
own power to displace Prismo. Proof is the instrument of the institutional
path.

**The Wishmaster seat as office**: the position Prismo holds is separable from
Prismo. It has existed vacant. It can presumably be filled by another. Any
governance position that is this clearly an *office* rather than an intrinsic
property implies: who holds it matters less than what the office requires of
its holder, and the office will seek a new holder if the current one is removed.
