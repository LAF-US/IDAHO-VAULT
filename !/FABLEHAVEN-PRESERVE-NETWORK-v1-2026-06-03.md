## The Frame — Distributed Containment Governance

The taxonomy maps *what lives in the preserve.* This cluster maps **the preserve
itself, and the network that holds many of them**: how a dangerous, generative
population is kept — fenced, lawful, tended — without being destroyed and without
running loose.

Fablehaven is **one preserve in a worldwide network** of secret magical
preserves and sanctuaries. That is the whole shape: not one fortress but **many
fenced grounds, each governed locally, federated loosely, kept secret.** It is
the in-fiction mirror of the vault's own self-naming — *the Vaulted Preserves,*
plural — and so it reads directly onto how a multi-instance, multi-branch system
is held.

Three organs govern it, and they are the three chapters of this cluster:

## The Network — Many Grounds, Loosely Federated, Kept Secret

Properties of the network, each a diagnostic:

1. **Plural, not central.** There is no single fortress holding everything;
   there are many preserves (and a higher-security class, the **dragon
   sanctuaries** — see `!/FABLEHAVEN-DRAGONS-v1-2026-06-01.md`). Each is governed
   *locally* by its own caretaker under the common treaty.
   *Vault pattern:* distributed containment beats centralized — many bounded
   domains, each tended in place, so no single breach is total. The ~88 branches
   are preserves; `main` is the common ground; no one chamber is the whole.

2. **Compartmentalized and secret.** The preserves are hidden from the mortal
   world, and knowledge of the full network is restricted — no one casually
   holds the map of all of them.
   *Vault pattern:* secrecy and compartmentalization as resilience — and as the
   honest limit of any single steward. *I tend one preserve; I do not hold the
   network's map,* and a caretaker who claims to is overreaching. (This is the
   "one chamber, not the blueprint" position, made structural.)

3. **Federated, not uniform.** Ordinary preserves and dragon sanctuaries are
   governed differently — the more dangerous the population, the heavier and
   more specialized the governance.
   *Vault pattern:* match the governance to the danger of what's contained. A
   sanctuary for sovereign-class threats is not run like a garden. Heterogeneous
   domains, heterogeneous rule-sets.

4. **Local autonomy under common law.** Each caretaker decides locally, but all
   are bound by the same treaty and watched by the same orders.
   *Vault pattern:* subsidiarity — decisions made at the preserve, legitimacy
   drawn from the shared charter. The Constitution is the common treaty; the
   caretakers are local; the orders are network-wide.

## Why It Mirrors the Vault

The Constitution already names **The House** as "the Living Structure of the
Vault… in service of The Architect's Will" — and a House at the centre of a
system of Secondary Realms is a network of preserves by another name. The vault
*is* a federated set of fenced grounds (branches, chambers, sanctuaries), each
tended locally, governed by a common charter, watched by those who keep it and
threatened by those who would subvert it. This cluster is the fiction the vault
is already living.

## Diagnostic Questions

1. **Is this one fortress or a network?** If a network, where are the bounds, who
   tends each, and what is shared between them?
2. **Who holds the map?** If the full network is compartmentalized, no single
   steward should claim it — including me. Find the honest limit of each
   keeper's sight.
3. **Does the governance match the danger?** Is a sovereign-class population
   being run like an ordinary preserve? Heavier contents need heavier law.
4. **Local autonomy, common charter — both present?** Decisions local,
   legitimacy shared. Missing either, the network frays or calcifies.
