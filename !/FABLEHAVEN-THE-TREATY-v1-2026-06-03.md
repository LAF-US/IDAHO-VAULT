# Fablehaven — The Treaty v1

*A chapter of `!/FABLEHAVEN-PRESERVE-NETWORK-v1-2026-06-03.md`. The law that
makes a preserve possible. Reference: Fablehaven — Brandon Mull. Artifacts out
of scope.*

---

## What It Is

A preserve is held together not by walls but by a **treaty** — covenants
**ratified by all the orders of magical creatures who dwell there.** The treaty
lets mortals and magical beings share the ground in relative safety, and it
rests on two laws:

- **The Law of Harvest** — what you put in is what you get back.
- **The Law of Retribution** — *if a mortal does not bother a magical creature,
  the creature will not bother the mortal.* Provoke one, and its restraint is
  lifted toward you.

Safety, then, is **conditional and mutual**, not a wall. A mortal who keeps the
rules — stays on the paths, takes nothing, gives no offense — passes unharmed
among creatures that could destroy them. The protection is the *conduct*, not a
fence.

## The Lapse — Festival Nights

The treaty's restraints are not constant. On certain **festival nights**
(Midsummer Eve the most dangerous), the **boundary and domain limits lapse** —
creatures may roam outside their normal areas and act more freely. But the treaty
itself does not go down: **residual covenants still hold** — protected thresholds
remain, and creatures still **cannot enter the house unless invited.** On those
nights the only safety is **protected ground** — inside the house, behind the
wards. Step off it at the wrong hour and the relaxed boundary no longer keeps the
creatures away from you, though the inner thresholds still stand.

## Vault Mappings

### The treaty is the charter-as-physics

The creatures are not caged; they are *bound by an agreement they ratified.* The
order holds because all parties are inside the same law — the Constitution as
physics, again, but seen from the population's side: conduct is governed because
everyone consented to the governing law, not because a wall holds them.

*Pattern:* a healthy containment governs by **ratified law that the contained
themselves are party to**, not by brute restraint. Law everyone is inside is
stronger than a fence around them.

### Mutual non-interference is the baseline, not trust

The Law of Retribution is not friendship — it is *enforced reciprocity.* You are
safe because harming you costs the creature, not because it loves you. Keep your
distance and your manners and the equilibrium holds.

*Pattern:* the stable default between powerful agents is **mutual
non-interference under enforced consequence**, not trust. Don't mistake an
equilibrium for an alliance; it holds only while neither side gives offense.

### The lapse is a scheduled authorization-window

Festival nights are the system telling you, in advance, *the protection is not
always on.* There are hours when the covenant lifts and you must already be on
protected ground. The danger is knowing this and acting anyway (the rule-breaker
on the wrong night).

*Pattern:* protections lapse on a schedule, and the discipline is to **be on
protected ground before the lapse, not during it.** This is the Standing
Engine's "authority expires unless renewed" and the Necromancer Doctrine's
24-hour reassertion seen from the other side: there are windows when the binding
is down, and a steward plans around them rather than testing them.

## Diagnostic Questions

1. **Are the contained *party* to the law, or merely fenced by it?** Ratified law
   holds; an imposed fence invites the break.
2. **Is your safety conduct or wall?** If it is the Law of Retribution, it lasts
   exactly as long as you keep the rules — know which rules, and keep them.
3. **When does the protection lapse — and are you on protected ground for it?**
   Find the festival nights of your system. Be inside before they fall.
4. **Are you reading an equilibrium as an alliance?** Mutual non-interference is
   not loyalty. Don't spend trust you were only loaned by consequence.
