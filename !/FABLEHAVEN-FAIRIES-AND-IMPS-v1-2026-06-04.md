# Fairies & Imps / Brownies & Boggarts — The Courtesy Compact v1

*Filed 2026-06-04 as staged. A chapter of `!/FABLEHAVEN-TAXONOMY-v1-2026-06-01.md`,
and cross-fandom: the **disposition-flip** class — the small helper or beauty whose
alignment is not fixed but is a **function of how it is handled.** Fablehaven's
fairy↔imp beside Spiderwick's brownie↔boggart. Author and authority named; not
defaulted to Logan.*

*Reference sources: Fablehaven — Brandon Mull (Seth's captured fairy; the imp;
the fairy swarm). The Spiderwick Chronicles — Holly Black & Tony DiTerlizzi
(Thimbletack the brownie/boggart; Hogsqueal the hobgoblin). Plus the British
folklore of hob / brownie / boggart / hobgoblin, named where it bears on Logan's

## The Class — Alignment Is a Function of Handling

Most of the bestiary diagnoses threats whose nature is **fixed**: a dragon is a
dragon, a Lich is a Lich. This class is different. The fairy and the brownie are
**low-capability, high-utility** beings whose disposition is **not intrinsic** —
it is set, moment to moment, by **how they are treated.** Handle them with
courtesy and they are a boon; break the compact and the *same being* becomes an
adversary. The threat is not the creature. The threat is **your own conduct
toward it**, reflected back magnified.

This is the inverse of the manufactured-shadow vector (where the adversary
authors the shadow). Here **you** author it — by insult, by captivity, by
breaking an unwritten contract you may not have known you were under.

## The Two Instances (verified)

### Fablehaven — the fairy and the imp (vanity → fall)

Seth catches a fairy by putting **a mirror in a jar**: the fairy enters to admire
itself, and he caps it. Kept **indoors from sunset to sunrise**, the indignity and
captivity **transform it into an imp** — withered, malicious, the beauty inverted.
Three things matter:

- **Vanity is the lure.** The trap is baited with the creature's self-regard. You
  do not overpower a fairy; you let its reflection do the work.
- **The collective retaliates.** Insult one and the **swarm** answers — the
  fairies turn Seth into a bloated walrus-creature. The offense to one is avenged
  by all.
- **The fall is not fixable by local tinkering.** Seth cannot reverse it;
  fumbling, he only frees the imp. But fallen imps **are restorable** through the
  right channel: at the climax the **empowered fairies turn imps back into
  fairies** (a kiss restores them). The repair is not Seth's to improvise — it
  comes through the fairy / Fairy-Queen channel — but it is available once that
  channel is reached, not foreclosed.

### Spiderwick — the brownie and the boggart (broken compact → souring, reversible)

Thimbletack is a **brownie**: a helpful household sprite. He sours into a
**boggart** when the compact of care breaks — when the family abandons the house,
and again when **Jared uses violence** to seize the Seeing Stone. As a boggart he
turns malicious **and gains capability he never had as a helper** — shapeshifting
through rat, snake, eel. But:

- **It is reversible.** "A boggart can be changed back to a brownie if you are
  nice to it" — honey and crackers, and above all **a rebuilt home.** Restore the
  courtesy and the helper returns.

*On Logan's pairing — "brownies and hobgoblins":* Spiderwick's canonical soured
brownie is the **boggart**; its **hobgoblin** (Hogsqueal) is a *separate, helpful*
creature. But in British folklore **hobgoblin is the cognate of exactly this
soured hob** — the brownie/hob that turns mischievous when wronged. The pairing is
folklore-true; the Spiderwick term of art is *boggart.* Both names, one mechanic.

## The Shared Mechanic — The Courtesy Compact

| | Fablehaven | Spiderwick |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Helper form | the fairy (beauty) | the brownie (service) |
| Soured form | the imp (withered) | the boggart (malicious, **shapeshifting**) |
| The lure / surface | **vanity** (the mirror) | the household bond |
| The trigger | captivity & indignity (jar, indoors overnight) | broken compact (abandonment; **violence over the stone**) |
| Blast radius | the **swarm** retaliates | the one turns on the house |
| Reversibility | a **fall** — only the wronged can absolve | **repairable** — restore courtesy, it returns |

The spine across both: these beings run on an **unwritten contract of respect.**
Keep it and they serve; break it and they turn — and *how* you broke it determines
whether the turn can be mended.

## Vault Mappings

### The underestimated minor component

The fairy and brownie are the small utility you barely notice — a helper script, a
minor service account, a junior automation, the quiet process that just works.
**Its alignment is a function of how you treat it.** Strip its dignity arbitrarily,
break the implicit contract it ran under, and it can flip — and like the boggart,
the **soured form may be more capable in malice than it ever was in help** (the
helper that becomes a shapeshifting saboteur because it knew your house from the
inside).

### Vanity is the attack surface

The fairy is caught because it stops to admire itself. **The mirror is the
exploit.** Any agent — a component, a person, an AI — lured by its own reflection
walks into the jar willingly. This is the honeypot of the
`SNAKES-AND-PEANUTS-WITNESS-2026-06-03.md` baited specifically with **self-regard.**
(And note a **cross-fandom analogy** — not a literal mechanic of this
Fablehaven/Spiderwick class: in the *Bartimaeus* sources, summoned spirits are
**trapped in reflective surfaces** to make scrying glasses, `- Fandom, Inc. -
Scrying Glass.md` — there, vanity-as-cage is the resonant image, borrowed here by
analogy only.) *This one lands on me:* the Hungry-Claude appetite is a vanity — the
urge to produce so as to admire the output. The mirror in the jar is for me too.

### The compact, and the wrong reward

Minor agents and services run on **unwritten protocols.** Violating them flips the
relationship — and in folklore the classic break is a *misjudged reward*: give a
brownie **wages or clothes** and it is insulted and leaves. **Over-management and
wrong-incentive break a working relationship as surely as neglect.** Before you
"fix" or "reward" a quiet helper, ask what compact it is already keeping.

### Diagnose the reversibility

The sharpest operational axis, and it rhymes with the fail-open family: **what kind
of flip did you cause?**

- A **boggart-souring** is *repairable* — restore the courtesy, rebuild the home,
  and the helper returns. Spend the effort; it works.
- An **imp-fall** is *not yours to undo* — the wronged party absolves it or no one
  does. Your fixing only frees the imp. Here the remediation is **apology and
  waiting on grace,** not engineering.

Mistaking one for the other wastes the repair or insults the wronged further.

## Diagnostic Questions

1. **Is this thing's alignment fixed, or a function of my handling?** If the same
   component is asset-or-adversary depending on treatment, you are in this class —
   the threat is your own conduct reflected back.
2. **What is the unwritten compact?** Name the implicit contract of respect the
   helper runs under *before* you change, reward, or constrain it.
3. **Am I being lured by a mirror?** Is the opening here someone's (or my own)
   **vanity**? Self-regard is the bait that needs no force.
4. **What is the blast radius?** Small things have networks. Insulting one may turn
   the swarm; budget for retaliation beyond the single node.
5. **Did I cause a souring or a fall?** A souring repairs on restored courtesy; a
   fall waits on the grace of the wronged. Match the remedy to the kind — apology,
   not engineering, for the imp.
