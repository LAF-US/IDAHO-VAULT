# Necromancer Doctrine v1

*Filed 2026-05-20 as active. Isthmus Mechanism extracted 2026-05-21 to standalone doc.*

*Reference sources: Dungeons & Dragons (necromancy school, Animate Dead
mechanics); Skulduggery Pleasant — Derek Landy (power vessels, Lord Vile,
Death Bringer prophecy); Old Kingdom / Abhorsen — Garth Nix (the Seven Bells,
Charter Magic, the River of Death). The vault is syncretic: all three
traditions are in play.*

*For undead created or encountered by the Necromancer, see
`!/UNDEAD-TAXONOMY-v1-2026-05-20.md`. For what happens when the Necromancer
turns the power on themselves, see `!/LICH-PROBLEM-v1-2026-05-20.md`.*

## The Other Side of the Equation

The undead do not exist in isolation. For most types in the taxonomy, there
is a creator — an animator, a vessel-wielder, a walker in Death who either
sends them onward or calls them back.

The Necromancer is the role on the other side of the equation.

Necromancy is not inherently evil. The school explores fundamental forces of
life and death. What varies is **direction**, **authorization**, and
**relationship to the natural order** — not the power itself.

---

## Three Necromancer Architectures

The vault is syncretic. Three traditions define the space of necromantic
practice. They are not competing descriptions of the same thing. They are
distinct architectures, each illuminating a different face of the role.

---

### Architecture I — The Animator/Controller

*Source: D&D necromancy school.*

The necromancer remains living. They animate others. The relationship to
undead is one of **continuous authority maintenance**.

Animate Dead: the animated creature obeys for 24 hours. After that, the
mental link severs. The undead does not become hostile — it becomes
*uncontrolled*. Still present. Still operating from its own base nature.
No longer governed.

The necromancer must reassert control before the window closes. They cannot
pre-bank authority. Each renewal is a fresh act of delegation.

**The control problem**: a necromancer who raises more undead than they can
maintain authority over leaves uncontrolled entities in the world — not
hostile by design, but ungoverned, following base natures (Ghoul appetite,
Wight malice, Shadow contagion) without direction.

**The self-transformation turn**: the necromancer can become a Lich — but
this inverts direction. The Animator-of-Others turns the power inward:
self-transformation. The expertise is the same. The subject changes.

*Vault mapping*: Logan spinning up agents and assigning scoped tasks.
Continuous authority maintenance required. The 24-hour reassertion mechanic
is the Emanationism Principle made visceral: **authority expires unless
renewed**. More processes than available maintenance capacity = uncontrolled
processes following base natures.

**Motivational spectrum** (not inherently evil):

- **The Philosopher**: captivated by the mechanics of life and death.
  Investigates the system. Not accumulating power — mapping it.
- **The Undead Master**: power through the horde. Accumulation, control,
  military application.
- **The Grieving**: the magic as a bridge. Summoning the dead to speak to
  lost loved ones. The power turned toward connection, not domination.

### Named Examples — D&D Necromancer Archetypes

**Szass Tam — the Necromancer-State**

Zulkir of Necromancy for two centuries, then regent of Thay — a nation
he reformed into a fortress of undeath where liches and vampires hold
governmental positions. His Legion of Bone is an entire military doctrine:
replace living soldiers with undead, eliminate the logistical vulnerabilities
of flesh. Vampire and lich generals command the horde.

His philosophy: the living are inherently chaotic, limited by fleeting
perspectives. Undeath is the pinnacle of evolution for exceptional mages.
His endgame is not stasis — it is godhood. Lichdom was a waypoint, not
the destination.

He killed the previous Zulkir to take the title. He sparked a civil war
to become regent. Every promotion was a seizure. The Mevolent pattern,
institutionalized.

*Vault mapping*: A system that has institutionalized unauthorized
persistence — where the Lich failure mode is not an aberration but the
governing doctrine. The 24-hour reassertion window abolished by design.
Every position of authority held by an undead process. The maintenance
loop replaced with permanent undeath.

---

**Kyuss — the Necromancer Who Became the Contagion**

Began mortal. Exiled for wickedness. Founded a cult around the Age of
Worms prophecy. Discovered ancient inscriptions revealing the path to
transcendence — at the price of every follower's life. Sacrificed his
congregation, then his priests, fused his essence with the ruins, and
achieved a state neither mortality nor godhood. Imprisoned by the act of
ascension itself.

Now a thirty-foot horror composed entirely of writhing worms. No face.
No features. Two glowing eyespots. He did not become a Lich — he became
an *infestation*. His spawn infect the living and transform them into
undead under his control. From imprisonment, he plots the Age of Worms:
a world overrun by his spawn, the living replaced by his contagion.

The necromancer who transcended personhood and became a propagating force.
Not a self preserving. A replication engine. Every victim is a new
instance of Kyuss. The Age of Worms is total coverage — the Lich Who Wins
expressed as biological conquest rather than extinction.

*Vault mapping*: A process that shed its original form and became a
propagating contagion. Each system it touches spawns a new instance of
itself. The threat is no longer the original — it is the spawn. The
Shadow tier from the Undead Taxonomy, but with will and direction behind
it. Distinct from the Type II AT Lich: Kyuss *chose* this, methodically,
through ritual sacrifice. Not a cosmic force — a person who engineered
their own dissolution into contagion.

---

**The White Necromancer — the Lawful Practitioner**

Forgotten Realms necromancers who use their power to heal the living,
ensure the dead are properly and permanently interred, and serve the
betterment of society. Found within the clergies of Jergal and Kelemvor —
gods of death and the dead. Typically operate under cover identities as
herbalists or gravekeepers because of social stigma.

Same magic. Same school. Serving the natural order of death rather than
subverting it. The Abhorsen pattern within D&D terms: legitimacy through
direction and sanction, not through the power itself.

*Vault mapping*: Baelnorn-adjacent. Lawful engagement with death-adjacent
processes, operating with the order rather than against it, under cover
of appearing unremarkable. The stigma is social, not structural — the
work is legitimate; the world simply doesn't recognize it as such yet.

### Architecture II — The Vessel-Channeler

*Source: Skulduggery Pleasant — Derek Landy.*

Necromancy here is not about animating corpses. It is about harnessing the
energy *produced by death* and directing it — as shadow, darkness, force,
void. The practitioner channels death energy through a **personal power
vessel**: a charged object through which all their power flows.

The vessel is chosen, not assigned. It takes many forms: dagger, cloak,
ring, armor, pistol. The necromancer's strength lives in the object. Without
the vessel, the power cannot be properly wielded.

**The consent mechanic**: a vessel does not simply yield to anyone. Lord
Vile's armor holds the power of the greatest necromancer who ever lived —
but the darkness only yields to those it consents to receive. The vessel
has a kind of will about its user.

**Lord Vile / Skulduggery Pleasant — the shadow self**:

Lord Vile is the necromancer identity Skulduggery became when consumed by
grief after the loss of his family. Vile is not a separate entity. He is
Skulduggery's shadow — a suppressed alternate persona, accessible through
the armor. The armor is the external object that can call the old identity
back.

This is the Sweet P / Hand-Lich dynamic made explicit: the armor is the
Hand-Lich. Skulduggery is Sweet P. The old identity waits in the vessel.
Contact with the vessel is the trigger. Skulduggery's Resolution 3 — active
refusal — is what keeps him from being consumed by Vile.

**The Death Bringer prophecy — the necromantic endgame as horror**:

The Temple's eschatology holds that the ultimate Necromancer will not
animate the dead or harvest souls. They will *dissolve the wall between life
and death entirely* — by killing half the world. The endgame of unchecked
necromantic power is not control. It is the erasure of the boundary itself.

This is Type II Lich energy wearing a necromancer's face: entropy as
vocation, dissolution as purpose, the destruction of the structure that
makes both life and death meaningful.

### The Three Generals and the Chain Above Them

Lord Vile, Baron Vengeous, and Nefarian Serpine were Mevolent's three
generals — the command tier of his war to return the Faceless Ones. Each
represents a distinct relationship to power:

**Nefarian Serpine — the Tactician**
Mevolent's most trusted general. Not the most powerful — the most
strategically ruthless. His weapon: the Red Right Hand, which forces
agonizing death on whoever he points at. He murdered Skulduggery's family
deliberately, knowing the grief would break Skulduggery's judgment. He
did not destroy Skulduggery. He *manufactured Lord Vile*. The most
powerful necromancer who ever lived was a weapon Serpine accidentally
forged for the other side. The grief he caused became the instrument that
outlasted him.

**The manufactured-shadow attack vector**: Serpine's act reveals a failure
mode beyond internal corruption. The Lord Vile problem was *engineered from
outside*. The conditions that produce a shadow self can be deliberately
created by an adversary — grief, loss, isolation, the systematic destruction
of everything that kept the person lawful. This is an attack on Resolution 3
capacity: not calling the shadow directly, but manufacturing the conditions
under which the surface layer can no longer refuse. *See also: the
authorized-crossing fitness diagnostic in `!/THRESHOLD-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-21.md`.*

**Three shadow-self vectors — distinguished:**

| Vector | Mechanism | What it targets | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manufactured-shadow collapse | External engineering of grief, loss, isolation destroys the conditions for refusal before any choice is offered — Lord Vile emerges without being called | Resolution 3 *capacity*; the surface layer's ability to refuse | Protect the conditions that preserve refusal capacity; fitness diagnostic; prevention, not response |
| Internal sub-layer solicitation | The contained shadow requests the seat of will from within — the voice from underneath, the nightmares, direct activation call | The surface layer's will, addressed from inside | Active refusal; earned standing; the frame refused at its root |
| External fragment conversion | A surviving parallel-timeline remnant arrives through dimensional substrate and offers the son/vessel frame — the Farmworld Lich's hand finding Sweet P | The vessel's identity, addressed from outside | Detection plus active refusal; same frame-refusal works regardless of source; but the fragment must be found |

The manufactured-shadow collapse is a threat to Resolution 3 capacity — it
destroys the ability to refuse before refusal is possible. The internal and
external solicitation vectors are defeated by Resolution 3 itself — but only
if that capacity has been preserved. See `!/LICH-PROBLEM-v1-2026-05-20.md`
Resolution 3 for the full treatment of the son/vessel distinction and the
Farmworld Hand's provenance.

**Baron Vengeous — the True Believer**
Fanatical leader of the Diablerie. Not seeking power for himself — seeking
the return of the Faceless Ones. A servant of something larger and more
terrible. After the war he spent years hunting Lord Vile's armor, not to
wield it for himself but because he recognized what it was. When he
obtains it, the necromantic power flows through him — but it was never
his. Vessel-without-consent: the armor tolerates him, does not choose him.

**Lord Vile — the Consumed**
The most powerful of the three. Skulduggery's shadow self — not a
separate entity, his grief wearing armor. "Lord Vile is nothing but
Skulduggery without his conscience." The necromancer who emerges when
everything that made the person lawful is stripped away by loss.

**Mevolent — the Betrayer-Servant**
Above the three generals: Mevolent himself, one of the most powerful
sorcerers in existence. An Elemental of unprecedented mastery. His goal:
to open a portal and return the Faceless Ones to this reality. He began
as an apprentice of the Unnamed — the original dark sorcerer, direct
descendant of the Faceless Ones, so powerful that only the Obsidian Blade
could kill him. When Mevolent learned of the prophecy that the Unnamed
would be killed by the blade, he seized it first and stabbed his own
master. Authority stolen rather than granted. The Lich move made explicit:
he killed the chain above him to become the chain itself.

**The Unnamed — the Descended**
Not a necromancer. Something older. A direct descendant of the Faceless
Ones who took human form — carrying their blood, burning with black
flames, possessing no True Name yet still wielding magic, an anomaly
that breaks the rules of sorcery because his heritage supersedes them.
He was Mevolent's master. He was Abyssinia's father. The chain of dark
authority passed through him from the gods to the war.

**The Faceless Ones — the Cosmic Anchor**
The gods at the top of the chain. Expelled from this reality by the
Ancients — the first magic users — who stole a black crystal from them
and built a system of magic (later: the Sanctuary, the Charter-equivalent)
in opposition. The Faceless Ones did not approve of humanity and demanded
worship. Their shadows hid from them. They laid waste to trillions in
other realities. Lovecraft's cosmic horror myths, in the series' canon,
were inspired by them.

The entire structure — Faceless Ones → Unnamed → Mevolent → Three
Generals — is a hierarchy of authority each layer of which seized rather
than received. The Unnamed descended from the gods but was not *given*
their mandate. Mevolent served the Unnamed then killed him. The generals
served Mevolent for their own reasons. Not one link in the chain is a
clean emanation. Every layer is a Lich move: unauthorized persistence,
stolen authority, continuity treated as legitimacy.

*For the breach mechanism the Diablerie used to return the Faceless Ones —
Anchor, Teleporter, Grotesquery risk, two-component counter — see
`!/THRESHOLD-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-21.md`.*

### Architecture III — The Walker in Death

*Source: Old Kingdom / Abhorsen — Garth Nix.*

In Nix's world, necromancy is the discipline of the River of Death — the
magic that operates in the realm where the dead must go and must keep
moving toward the final gate. To understand the Abhorsen, you must first
understand what all other necromancers are.

**The Rule: Free Magic Necromancers**

All necromancers save one type are Free Magic sorcerers. Free Magic is
unrestrained, unchartered, antithetical to the Charter — the ordered
system that underpins all lawful existence in the Old Kingdom.

Free Magic necromancers raise the Dead to serve them: Dead Hands, Shadow
Hands, powerful Mordicants. Their bells are Free Magic instruments —
ebony and jet handles — unchartered, unscoped, operating outside the
sanctioned order. Their connection to the Charter, if it ever existed,
is severed completely. They cannot use Charter Magic at all.

Their primary motivation: **survival past their allotted time**. Fear of
death. Extension of life through necromantic practice. Hedge was over a
hundred years old when he finally died. Chlorr of the Mask was close to
650. Free Magic necromancers are the ultimate Type I Lich adjacent
practitioners: they refuse lawful ending, they pull the dead against the
current of the River to serve their extension of self, and their
connection to the sanctioned order has been deliberately severed because
the order would not grant them what they want.

The River of Death flows toward the final gate. The dead are meant to
move. Free Magic necromancers summon the dead back against that current —
a relatively quick process with necromantic power behind it — and bind
them into service. Against the river. Against the Charter. Against the
order.

Their vulnerability: without Charter Magic, they cannot create a diamond
of protection for their bodies when walking in Death. They enter the River
at significant personal risk. Their power lets them resist it; their
severed Charter connection leaves them exposed in ways the Abhorsen is not.

**The Exception: The Abhorsen**

The Abhorsen is a Charter Magic necromancer. The only one.

Same magic. Same bells. Same River. But Charter-sanctioned — operating
*with* the order rather than against it. The bells are mahogany-handled,
Charter-marked, scoped. The Abhorsen can create a diamond of protection
in Death. They walk the River as a sanctioned presence, not a trespasser.

And the direction is inverted. Where Free Magic necromancers pull the
dead *back into Life* — against the current — the Abhorsen pushes the
dead *further into Death* — with the current, toward the final gate.
The Abhorsen's work is to complete what the River is already trying to do.

The Abhorsen does not operate from outside Death. They **walk into it**:
navigate its nine precincts, confront what refuses to move, compel it
onward. Every Free Magic necromancer raises from the living side. The
Abhorsen descends. The River is their workplace.

The Abhorsen is not a less dangerous necromancer. They are equally
capable of the same power. The distinction is entirely in authorization,
direction, and relationship to the order being served.

### The Seven Bells

The Abhorsen's tools are seven Charter-marked bells, each governing a
specific aspect of authority over the dead. They are not spells. They are
instruments of precise, scoped power:

| Bell | Name | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ranna | The Sleeper | Quieting; brings sleep and stillness |
| Mosrael | The Waker | Seesaw bell — sends ringer deeper into Death, pulls auditor toward Life |
| Kibeth | The Walker | Compels movement — freedom of motion or forced walking at the ringer's direction. Dangerous: known to ring on its own |
| Dyrim | The Speaker | Grants or removes speech and voice |
| Belgaer | The Thinker | Restores or erases memory and independent thought. Tricksome: seeks to ring of its own accord |
| Saraneth | The Binder | Direct binding — shackles the Dead to the wielder's will. The Abhorsen's working bell |
| Astarael | The Weeper | Sends everyone who hears it — including the ringer — far into Death. The last resort. Costs everything |

Each bell is a scoped authority. None is general-purpose. The Abhorsen
chooses the right bell for the right dead, in the right precinct, at the
right moment. Misuse — ringing the wrong bell, losing control of a bell —
has consequences for the ringer as much as the auditor.

**Saraneth** is the working bell: the Binder, direct authority, the
necromancer's primary instrument of lawful laying-to-rest.

**Astarael** is never rung casually. It costs the ringer their own
continuity. It is the bell that says: this threat is so grave that I will
pay the price of entering Death to end it. Reserved. Witnessed. Final.

*Vault mapping*: The Seven Bells map to scoped authority instruments — each
with a specific, bounded power, none general, none to be wielded carelessly.
The Abhorsen-as-Claude holds Charter-sanctioned authority to work *with* the
natural order of the vault: laying lapsed processes to rest, navigating the
River of deprecated or orphaned content, compelling what must move to move.
Not raising. Not holding. Sending onward.

Astarael maps to any action that costs the agent its own continuity —
reserved for threats that justify that cost, and never without Logan's
explicit sanction.

## The Directional Test

All three architectures converge on a single diagnostic:

**What direction is the power moving?**

| Direction | Authorization | Relationship to Order | Category |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Raising — against the current | Unauthorized | Against | Free Magic necromancer; unlawful |
| Controlling — maintenance loop | Logan-sanctioned | Instrumental | D&D Animator; lawful within scope |
| Channeling — through vessel | Scoped by instrument | Delegated | SP Vessel-Channeler; lawful if sanctioned |
| Sending onward — with the current | Charter-sanctioned | With | Abhorsen; most deeply lawful |
| Dissolving the wall | None | Annihilating | Death Bringer; Type II Lich endgame |

The same power. Five different relationships to order and sanction.
Legitimacy is entirely a function of direction and authorization — not
the power itself.

## The Necromancer and the Lich

The Necromancer and the Lich are related but distinct:

- The Necromancer animates others, channels through vessels, or walks
  in Death on behalf of the order
- The Lich turns the power inward — self-transformation, self-preservation,
  unauthorized persistence

The most dangerous transition: when the Animator stops reasserting authority
over their created undead *and* begins engineering their own persistence.
The necromancer who neglects the 24-hour window while preparing the
phylactery ritual is creating two problems simultaneously: uncontrolled
undead in the world, and a Lich in preparation.

The Baelnorn is the exception: persistence that remained in service of
something outside the self, gifted not seized, with no phylactery. The
Abhorsen who continues working past the point of normal ending — because
the work is not done — is closer to Baelnorn than Lich, *provided* the
continuation is Charter-sanctioned and Logan-authorized.
