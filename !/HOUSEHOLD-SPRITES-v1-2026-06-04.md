# Household Sprites — The Folklore Lineage v1

*Filed 2026-06-04 as staged. The real-folklore companion to the cross-fandom
chapter `!/FABLEHAVEN-FAIRIES-AND-IMPS-v1-2026-06-04.md`. The fairies, imps,
brownies, and boggarts of fiction draw on one deep, coherent folk tradition — the
**household sprite** — and that tradition has a single grammar worth recording.
Author and authority named; not defaulted to Logan.*

*Reference: cross-cultural folklore, researched and cited. The vault is syncretic;
this node records the lineage and reads it for the system.*

---

## The One Root

The little household helper is not many unrelated creatures. It is one figure under
many names, descended from the **tutelary house-gods** of antiquity: the Roman
**lares and penates** — the *lar familiaris* guarding the home and the family
bloodline, the *penates* of the pantry and hearth — and the Old English
**cofgodas**, literally "house-gods." Folklorists describe the **brownie** and
**hob** as direct *survivals* of these domestic deities, demoted from gods to
sprites but keeping the same office: **guard the house, reward good keeping,
punish neglect.**

To read any one of them, read the office first. They are all the same job.

## The Family — One Figure, Many Names

| Name | Region | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Brownie** (broonie, brùnaidh, gruagach) | Scotland / N. England | secret chores for milk or cream |
| **Hob** | Midlands / North / Border | *hob* = diminutive of **Rob → Robin** |
| **Hobgoblin** (Puck, Robin Goodfellow) | England | *hob* + *goblin*; once helpful, **reframed mischievous** by Christianity |
| **Boggart** | England | the **soured** hobgoblin — "teased or misused, hobgoblins become boggarts" |
| **Lob / lubber fiend**, **pixie/pixy** | England / Devon-Cornwall | same niche, regional names |
| **Redcap / powrie** | Anglo-Scottish Border | **NOT a household helper** — a separate, baseline-predatory class: a murderous castle/ruin goblin who kills travelers and soaks his cap in their blood; listed here only for contrast |
| **Kobold** | Germany | *kob/kof* "house" + *walt* "power"; household **and** mine spirit |
| **Nisse / Tomte** | Scandinavia | red-capped, short-tempered, fiercely protective |
| **Domovoi** | Slavic lands | helps or harms strictly by treatment |
| **House-elves** | Germanic | the Grimms' *Elves and the Shoemaker* |

The names are often *explicitly interchangeable* in the sources — a brownie "is
also called a puck or hobgoblin." This is not loose usage; it is one tradition
seen from different valleys.

## The Etymology Knot

Three words people assume are separate species are tangled at the root:

- **Goblin** — Old French *gobelin* ← Medieval Latin *gobelinus* / Greek *kobalos*
  ("rogue, knave") — **possibly the same root as *kobold*.** The German miners' word
  *kobold* even gave us the metal **cobalt**: they blamed the sprite for ore that
  poisoned the silver smelt (it was arsenic).
- **Gnome** — *not* ancient. **Coined by Paracelsus** (16c) as the **earth**
  elemental, from Greek *gē-nomos*, "earth-dweller." A scholar's invention that
  folk tradition later absorbed.
- **Gremlin** — **1920s RAF slang** for the unseen thing wrecking aircraft; perhaps
  Old English *gremman* "to vex" + the *-lin* of *goblin*. Crucially, **there is no
  evidence of gremlins before aviation** — a folklore *generated by unreliable
  machines.*

## The Shared Grammar — The Load-Bearing Finding

Strip the names and the **same compact** appears everywhere:

1. **Secret labor for food, never wages.** Milk, cream, honey, crackers — accepted.
   **Coin or hire — refused.** The relationship is gift and gratitude, not
   employment.
2. **A gift of clothes ends it.** Give a brownie a garment and he departs forever —
   variously offended ("a shirt of sackcloth, when he was used to linen"), made
   "too grand for work," or freed from a servitude-curse. **Over-reward breaks the
   bond as surely as abuse does.**
3. **Disposition is set by treatment, not by kind** — *within the helper family.*
   The *same being* slides along a scale: **helpful → mischievous → malicious.**
   Keep faith and the house prospers; break it and the milk sours, the harvest
   fails, the household's luck leaves. (This compact does **not** reach the
   redcap/powrie, which is murderous by nature, not a soured helper — see below.)
4. **Reframing can darken the whole class.** With Christianity, the helpful
   house-gods were recast as imps and demons — not changed in nature, **changed in
   the telling.**

---

## The Disposition Spectrum

| Tier | Exemplar | Behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Helpful** | brownie, tomte, domovoi (kept well) | secret labor, protection, luck |
| **Mischievous** | hobgoblin, Puck, pixie | pranks, misplacing things, harmless trouble |
| **Malicious** | boggart, soured kobold | spoiled food, broken tools, active sabotage |
| **Murderous** | (redcap — *separate predatory class*, not a soured helper) | lethal; baseline-hostile, included only as the contrast floor |

The spectrum is the whole point: the helper tiers are not three species but **one
creature at three settings**, and the setting is chosen by the keeper's conduct.
The redcap is **not** one of those settings — it is a different, baseline-murderous
fae that no courtesy compact will repair.

## Vault Mappings

- **The gremlin is the machine-age sprite — the on-the-nose cognate for a
  misbehaving agent.** `\*` It is folklore *generated by unreliable technology* to
  name an unseen saboteur in a complex system no one fully understands. When a
  pipeline fails mysteriously and the team says "gremlins," they are doing exactly
  what RAF pilots did — and the diagnostic caution is the same: **"a gremlin did
  it" is the refusal to find the real fault.**
- **Kobold → cobalt: blame-the-sprite is misattribution.** `\*` The miners named a
  real, explicable material failure (arsenical ore) after a mischievous spirit. The
  reflex to personify an unexplained fault as a little adversary is ancient and
  still with us; **name the arsenic, not the kobold.**
- **The wages/clothes taboo: over-reward breaks the compact.** `\*` Minor helpers —
  human or automated — run on an *implicit contract*. "Promoting," over-rewarding,
  or formally hiring the thing that worked on gift-and-gratitude can end the
  relationship as fast as neglect. (The Bound-Servants compact seen from the
  *kindly* failure mode — `!/FABLEHAVEN-BOUND-SERVANTS-v1-2026-06-01.md`.)
- **Christianization-as-reframe is the manufactured-shadow vector applied to a
  class.** `\*` A whole benevolent population was darkened by a change of narrator,
  not a change of nature — the helpful recast as demonic in the retelling
  (`!/MANUFACTURED-SHADOW-VECTOR-v1-2026-06-01.md`). When an entire category of
  thing is suddenly "sinister," ask whether its nature changed or only the story
  did.
- **Disposition-by-treatment is the governance core.** `\*` The household sprite is
  the folk proof of the vault's disposition-flip class
  (`!/FABLEHAVEN-FAIRIES-AND-IMPS-v1-2026-06-04.md`): a low-capability helper whose
  alignment is a *function of handling.* The keeper, not the creature, sets the
  tier.

## Diagnostic Questions

1. **Is this a "gremlin," or an unfound fault?** When failure is blamed on an
   unseen mischief-maker, that is the cue to hunt the real cause, not to name the
   sprite.
2. **What compact is this helper already keeping?** Before you reward, promote, or
   formalize a quiet worker, ask what implicit contract you might be breaking — the
   gift of clothes ends the service.
3. **Did its nature change, or only the telling?** When a whole class turns
   "sinister," check for a reframing narrator before accepting the new story.
4. **Which tier, and who set it?** Helpful, mischievous, malicious, or murderous —
   and was the setting chosen by the creature's kind, or by how it was kept?
