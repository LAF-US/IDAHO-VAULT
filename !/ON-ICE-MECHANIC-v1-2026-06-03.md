# The On-Ice Mechanic — Containment by Sustained Condition v1

*Filed 2026-06-03 as staged. The mechanic abstracted from its case study, the
Akhenaten precedent (`!/KERR-AKHENATEN-PRECEDENT-v1-2026-06-03.md`). The vessels
of the containment family hold a thing in a shell; **on-ice holds nothing — it
changes the conditions so the thing cannot act.** Author and authority named;
not defaulted to Logan.*

---

## Definition

**On-ice containment neutralizes a threat by sustaining an ambient condition
that switches its power off — without any vessel, wall, or binding.** The
prisoner is left **free and intact**; it is simply rendered inert for as long as
the condition holds.

The verified source is Kerr's djinn (prior turn): djinn power is **heat-fueled
and tied to the lifeline**, so **cold pacifies them**, and "freezing adult djinn
in ice" is a standing containment practice. Generalize off the heat specifics
and the mechanic is condition-agnostic: *find the ambient variable the threat's
power depends on, and hold it at the value where the power cannot run.*

Two modes, both attested in the precedent:

- **Storage** — hold the condition indefinitely to keep the threat inert (the
  djinn kept frozen).
- **Slow-field** — apply the condition briefly and *indiscriminately* to buy a
  window for a precise operation (the North-Pole extraction: chill everything,
  pull the ally out before the slowed adversary can escape). See the precedent
  node for the tangled-vessel doctrine this mode serves.

---

## The Five Properties

1. **Condition, not vessel.** Nothing is enclosed. The threat is at large and
   whole; only the *environment* denies it power. There is no shell to breach
   and no manifest to mislabel — and nothing to *reduce* the threat, either.
2. **Cheap and clean to apply.** No walls to build, no swap to arrange, no
   binding to negotiate. You change one variable. The fastest containment to
   reach for.
3. **Fully reversible and total on thaw.** Lift the condition and the threat
   returns **undiminished** — not weakened, the *whole* thing. On-ice is
   **suspension, never resolution** (the Quiet Box's lesson; the Treaty's
   festival-night lapse, `!/FABLEHAVEN-THE-TREATY-v1-2026-06-03.md`).
4. **Maintained-cost.** It holds only while you keep paying to sustain the
   condition. Stop sustaining it and the containment ends itself. (The
   Lectoblix's "keep feeding," `!/FABLEHAVEN-VAMPIRE-DIAGNOSTIC-v1-2026-06-01.md`,
   inverted into a defense.)
5. **Fail-open on lapse.** This is the danger that defines it. A wall fails
   *closed* (breach it and it's gone, but it was never depending on you). A
   condition fails **open**: power runs out, funding lapses, attention drifts,
   the freezer warms — and the threat wakes at full strength, *with no further
   act required from anyone.* On-ice fails toward release.

---

## Vault Mappings

On-ice is the family of **maintained-condition controls**: rate-limit held at
near-zero, a feature flag switched off, read-only / maintenance mode, a sandbox
or air-gap, a suspended-not-deleted account, a paused process. Each holds a
dangerous capability inert by *condition*, not by removal.

- **Use it for speed and reversibility.** When you must neutralize something
  *now* and may need it back whole, on-ice is right: cheap, fast, undoable.
- **Never mistake it for resolution.** The capability is intact; you have paused
  it, not handled it. A suspended account is a live account waiting for the
  condition to lapse.
- **Account for the upkeep, and the fail-open.** The control costs continuous
  attention/energy, and it releases the threat the moment that lapses — *without
  an attacker doing anything.* Budget the sustain, and alarm the lapse.
- **The slow-field is the surgical use.** When ally and threat are tangled (the
  Akhenaten case), an indiscriminate pause buys an extraction window — you need
  only a *relative* edge over the slowed threat, not to be unaffected yourself.

**Contrast within the containment family** (this is the on-ice entry):

| Type | Holds by | Upkeep | Failure mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sealed Evil** (founding seal) | a permanent binding | seal integrity / readiness to re-contain | **release on breach** — heavy to breach, but the threat is freed if the binding is undone (Bahumat/Muriel) |
| **The Knots** (Muriel) | a finite set of seals | none | spent open, one favor at a time |
| **The Quiet Box** | suspended isolation | none | blind release on swap |
| **On-Ice** | a **sustained condition** | **continuous** | **fails open on lapse** |

On-ice is the cheapest to reach for and the one that fails toward release **on
its own, from mere lapse of upkeep** — where the Sealed Evil releases only when
its binding is actively breached. Either way the danger is the same: remember it
is a pause, not a prison.

## Diagnostic Questions

1. **Did you contain it, or only pause it?** If the threat is intact and only the
   condition holds it, you are on-ice — suspension, not resolution.
2. **What variable is doing the work, and who sustains it?** Name the condition
   (the "cold") and the cost of keeping it. That cost is the containment.
3. **What happens on lapse?** On-ice fails *open* with no attacker required.
   Alarm the thaw; assume the threat returns whole.
4. **Speed-and-reversibility, or permanence?** If you need it back undiminished,
   on-ice is right. If it must never return, on-ice is the wrong tool — seal or
   resolve.
5. **Tangled with an ally?** Then on-ice as a slow-field may be the only safe
   move — pause all, extract the ally in the window, accept the relative edge.
