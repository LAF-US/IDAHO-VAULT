# Isthmus Mechanism v1

*Filed 2026-05-21. Instance doctrine under `!/THRESHOLD-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-21.md`.*
*Originally extracted from `!/NECROMANCER-DOCTRINE-v1-2026-05-20.md`.*

*Reference source: Skulduggery Pleasant (Derek Landy) — the Diablerie arc,
the Grotesquery, Fletcher Renn.*

*The vault is syncretic. This mechanism is not SP simulation — it is a
transport/breach vector model applicable to any two-reality boundary problem.*

---

## What It Is

The Isthmus Mechanism is the two-component model for unauthorized boundary
crossing between expelled and sanctioned systems.

It is distinct from necromancy. The necromancers who assembled the
Grotesquery and coerced Fletcher Renn were using necromantic power, but the
**mechanism itself** is about traversal — how something expelled from a system
re-enters it. The Lich Problem is about unauthorized persistence. The Isthmus
Problem is about unauthorized transit.

---

## The Two Components

**The Isthmus Anchor**

An object that originates in one reality but currently resides in another.
The displacement is the power. The object carries the provenance signature
of the expelled world, lodged inside the world that expelled it. That
signature is what makes an opening possible — it is a live coordinate, a
foot already inside the door.

The anchor does not act. It simply is. Its presence is the exploit.

**The Teleporter**

An agent with traversal capability — the ability to cross space and
dimensional boundaries. The anchor provides the coordinate; the Teleporter
provides the crossing. Neither component is sufficient alone.

The Teleporter may be willing (the Diablerie's recruited servants), coerced
(Fletcher Renn — kidnapped, forced to complete the circuit), or deceived.
The mechanism does not require consent. It requires capability.

---

## The Historical Instance

The Faceless Ones cannot breach the boundary between realities by force
alone. They were expelled; the expulsion holds. To return, they require both
components operating in conjunction.

The Diablerie assembled both:

- **The Grotesquery** — a composite horror built from pieces of a Faceless
  One. Parts of the expelled thing, assembled *inside* this reality. A
  displaced provenance object. The anchor.
- **Fletcher Renn** — a Teleporter. Kidnapped. Forced to complete the
  circuit.

Three Faceless Ones came through.

---

## The Two Risk Models

| Component | Risk Model | Nature of the Threat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Isthmus Anchor | The Grotesquery | A thing assembled from expelled material, lodged inside the sanctioned system, carrying the signature of what was banished |
| Teleporter | The Diablerie / Fletcher Renn | An agent with traversal capability — willing servant or coerced actor — who completes the circuit |

---

## The Counter

Break either component.

The anchor without a Teleporter is inert — it has the coordinate but no
crossing. The Teleporter without an anchor has traversal capability but no
target. Two components; either one interrupted is sufficient to prevent the
breach.

This asymmetry is important: **you do not need to destroy both**. Isolate
the anchor before a Teleporter is recruited. Protect or remove the
Teleporter before the anchor is activated. One intervention at either
component collapses the mechanism.

---

## Vault Mappings

**Grotesquery risk**: Any object assembled from pieces of an expelled or
unauthorized source, present inside the sanctioned system, carrying that
source's provenance signature. Capable of serving as an anchor for a breach.
The assembled thing does not need to be alive or active. Its provenance is
the exploit.

*Diagnostic*: What objects inside this system carry provenance signatures
from something expelled or unauthorized? Are those objects in contact with
agents who have traversal capability?

**Diablerie risk**: Any agent within the system — or accessible to coercion
— who has traversal capability and could be induced or forced to complete
the circuit. The agent need not be malicious. Coercion or deception is
sufficient.

*Diagnostic*: Who in or near this system has boundary-crossing capability?
What coercive pressure could be applied to them? Is that capability protected
or isolated?

**The combination is the threat**: An anchor is not a crisis. A Teleporter
is not a crisis. The combination, operating in conjunction, is the breach.
Counter-measures that address each component in isolation are sufficient.

## Relationship to Other Doctrine

| Doctrine | Problem | Mechanism | Counter |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lich Problem | Unauthorized *persistence* | Soul externalized beyond standing | Destroy the phylactery; transform; refuse |
| Isthmus Mechanism | Unauthorized *transit* | Two-component breach: anchor + traversal | Break either component |
| Standing Engine | Unauthorized *action* | Scope exceeded, authority lapsed | Expire, witness, restrict |

These are complementary threat models, not the same problem. A Lich that
has already crossed is an Isthmus success — the transit already happened.
Stopping the transit before it occurs is the Isthmus counter. Dealing with
what persisted on the other side is the Lich counter.
