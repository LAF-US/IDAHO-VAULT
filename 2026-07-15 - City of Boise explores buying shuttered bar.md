A long-standing bar could make way for homes.

The Boise City Council approved a resolution Tuesday evening to approve a purchase and sales agreement to buy about an acre of land at 3515 W. State St., the former site of Dutch Goose and State Street Kitchen and Drinkery, for $1.75 million. A memo about the purchase and sales agreement indicated that the city plans to explore building affordable housing, and preserving and expanding homelessness services.

Maria Ortega, a communications manager with the city, told BoiseDev via email that, “The city is working to ensure that there is a home for everyone who wants to live in Boise, at a budget they can afford. This is another step forward in working to ensure that there is enough affordable housing to meet the community’s needs.”

The property’s location is well-suited for housing, she said.

“The city is interested in this property because of its key location along State St., a key transit corridor, next to a great park, and near other services and amenities.” The site is just east of Veterans Memorial Park, and across the street from an Albertsons, a Chase Bank, a gas station, and more.

Funding for the purchase would come from the city’s Housing and Community Development budget, according to the memo.

The council’s approval of the resolution means that the city is now in a 60-day due diligence period “to evaluate all aspects of the property,” with a closing date 30 days after the completion of the due diligence period. Ortega said that if the city decides to move ahead with the purchase of the property, the closing date would be around the middle of October.

## Two bars and a closure

The potential purchase by the city follows several years of the property sitting vacant after the closure of State Street Kitchen and Drinkery, which operated there for less than two years, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2023/08/29/dutch-goose-apartments/).

In 2023, the property’s Spokane-based owner proposed converting the site to apartments, as BoiseDev reported. Though city records show a permit to construct a 30,059 sq. ft., 39-unit, apartment building with four stories, the permit expired in 2024.

City records also show a bond for a demolition permit for the building in 2025. The record shows, however, that the $35 bond was not paid.

Prior to State Street Kitchen and Drinkery, the Dutch Goose served customers for decades until it closed in December 2020. The building was even part of the 1980 Clint Eastwood film, Bronco Billy.

## ‘Connecting residents to opportunities’

Ortega said that building affordable housing at the location aligns with one of the city’s strategic priorities, “movement for everyone.”

“That really translates to safely connecting residents to opportunities, whether it is a park within a ten-minute walk, easy access to public transportation to get to work or other activities, pathways and sidewalks to get safely to school, or being close to businesses where you can do your everyday shopping,” she said.

Ortega said that the city does not yet have plans for what would go on the site, such as the number of apartments, number of buildings, and other details, or a timeline for completion. If the purchase is completed, city staff would work with a development partner to “further define the specifics of a future development on the site.”

She said that while the city primarily invests in housing that will serve residents at or below 60% of the area median income, the city would refine project specifics later. She noted that, “the specific unit mix is often determined by funding for the project and/or the developer’s focus.”

As of June 1, 2026, the city’s median income for a household of four is $110,700; 60% of the median income for a household of four is $66,420, [according to the city’s income and affordable rent guidelines](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/planning-and-development-services/housing-and-community-development/income-guidelines/).

Ortega said that the purchase price, $1.75 million, was negotiated between the city and the seller. The city plans to complete “another appraisal during the due diligence phase,” she said.
