**Malcolm MacKenzie Renfrew** (October 12, 1910 – October 12, 2013) was an American [polymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer "Polymer") [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist "Chemist"), inventor, and [professor emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professor_emeritus "Professor emeritus") at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") in [Moscow, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho "Moscow, Idaho").[^1] [^2] [^3] [^4] Renfrew Hall, the university's chemistry building, was named for him in 1985.[^1] [^5]

Renfrew is noted for his contribution to the development of [Teflon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teflon "Teflon"), including the first paper on the subject.[^3] [^6] In 1946 he spoke on behalf of [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") at the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") (ACS) meeting at which Teflon was announced.[^7] [^8] [^9]

## Early life

Born in [Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane,_Washington "Spokane, Washington"),[^10] Renfrew was the elder of two sons of Earl and Elsie MacKenzie Renfrew.[^11] [^12] Earl was an accountant and the family later moved to the [Palouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palouse "Palouse") at [Colfax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colfax,_Washington "Colfax, Washington"), and then across the [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho") border to nearby [Potlatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potlatch,_Idaho "Potlatch, Idaho") in 1923.

Renfrew graduated from Potlatch High School in 1928 and attended the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") in nearby [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho "Moscow, Idaho"), where he joined the [Phi Gamma Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Gamma_Delta "Phi Gamma Delta") [fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternities_and_sororities "Fraternities and sororities") and wrote for *[The Argonaut](https://www.uiargonaut.com/)*, the student newspaper.[^13] [^14] Originally interested in journalism, he switched his major to [chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry "Chemistry") [^13] and graduated with a [B.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in 1932 and an [M.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") in 1934.[^15] He then traveled to the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest "Midwest") to continue his studies and earned a [Ph.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ph.D. "Ph.D.") from the [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota "University of Minnesota") in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis") in 1938.[^1] [^16]

## Career

After gaining his doctorate, Renfrew worked for [DuPont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont "DuPont") in [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey"), where he produced a number of patents on [polymethyl methacrylate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymethyl_methacrylate "Polymethyl methacrylate"), including one on photopolymerization,[^17] material for tooth repair,[^18] as well as [epoxy resins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoxy_resins "Epoxy resins") [^19] and the first method of synthesis of poly(tetrafluoroethylene) in a form which was suitable for the commercial production of [Teflon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teflon "Teflon").[^20] It had been accidentally invented in 1938 by DuPont chemist [Roy Plunkett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_J._Plunkett "Roy J. Plunkett"), as a by-product of [chlorofluorocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorofluorocarbon "Chlorofluorocarbon") [refrigerant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerant "Refrigerant") research.[^21] [^22] [^23]

After further industrial experience with [General Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Mills "General Mills") Company back in Minneapolis and [Spencer Kellogg & Sons, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer_Kellogg_&_Sons,_Inc. "Spencer Kellogg & Sons, Inc.") in [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York "Buffalo, New York"), Renfrew returned west in 1959 to his [alma mater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alma_mater "Alma mater") in Moscow to head the UI Department of Physical Science.[^8] This was split into separate departments of physics and chemistry in 1967, with Renfrew as the Head of Chemistry, a position he retained until 1973; he retired in 1976 and became professor emeritus.[^15]

## Honors

In 1976, he was made a [Fellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellow "Fellow") of the [American Chemical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society"). Well known for his research, Renfrew was praised for his work on chemical safety and as an educator, both recognized in the [ACS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chemical_Society "American Chemical Society") 1985 Chemical Health and Safety Award for "his publications and column on Chemical Safety in the *[Journal of Chemical Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_Chemical_Education "Journal of Chemical Education")* ". In 2006 he received the Distinguished Science Communicator award.[^24]

Renfrew was also an artist: an exhibition of his paintings was held in Moscow City Hall in November–December 2010.[^25] His 100th birthday, October 12, 2010, was declared as "Malcolm M. Renfrew Day" in the state by [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Idaho "List of governors of Idaho") [Butch Otter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butch_Otter "Butch Otter").[^26] Part of the celebration was the rededication of Renfrew Hall, the chemistry building named for him a quarter century earlier. Opened 62 years ago in 1964 as the Physical Sciences Building ("Phy-Sci"),[^27] it was renamed "Renfrew Hall" in October 1985 for his 75th birthday.[^28]

## Personal

Shortly after gaining his doctorate, he married Carol Joy Campbell (September 19, 1913 – January 12, 2010), on June 26, 1938. A member of [Kappa Alpha Theta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kappa_Alpha_Theta "Kappa Alpha Theta") [sorority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternities_and_sororities "Fraternities and sororities"), she was a 1935 B.S. economics graduate of the University of [[Idaho]] from [Rosalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosalia,_Washington "Rosalia, Washington"), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_\(state\) "Washington (state)"). Born on a farm near [Fairfield, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairfield,_Washington "Fairfield, Washington"),[^29] at the time of her death at age 96, they had been married for over 71 years.[^24] [^30] [^31] [^32]

Renfrew was an [Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_\(Christianity\) "Elder (Christianity)") of the [Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(U.S.A.\) "Presbyterian Church (U.S.A.)"), a gifted [watercolorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolor_painting "Watercolor painting"), and played the [trombone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trombone "Trombone").[^1] [^33] He was a member of the [Idaho Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Vandals "Idaho Vandals") Non-Marching Pep Band [^34] and the "Hog Heaven [Big Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Band "Big Band")." [^35] ("Hog Heaven" was an early name for Moscow, due to plentiful [camas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camassia "Camassia") roots.) [^36] [^37] [^38]

Renfrew died at his residence in Moscow at Good Samaritan Village on October 12, 2013, his 103rd birthday.[^1] [^2] [^3] [^39] His mother Elsie (1889–1987) lived to age 98,[^12] and his younger brother Edgar (1915–2010), who also had a doctorate in chemistry, reached age 95.[^40]
