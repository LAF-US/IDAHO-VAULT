**Uniform Commercial Code Article 12 — Controllable Electronic Records.** Promulgated 2022 by the Uniform Law Commission and the American Law Institute as part of the 2022 Amendments to the UCC.

**Model law, not Idaho law.** Article 12 has force only where a legislature enacts it. Cited in `LICENSE` as a framework, expressly hedged there — *"asserted as controllable electronic records under this framework to the full extent applicable."*

## Idaho status — verified 2026-08-25

Idaho has **not** enacted Article 12 as an article of its commercial code. Checked against the Idaho Legislature's published statutes:

- Title 28's chapter index contains no controllable-electronic-records chapter.
- Idaho instead enacted its own **Digital Assets Act**, Title 28 Chapter 53 (§§ 28-5301–5306), added **2022, ch. 284** — the same year Article 12 was promulgated.

**The two diverge by design, and Idaho has not conformed since.** Article 12 creates a distinct controllable-electronic-record category governed by control-based rules. `Idaho Code §28-5304` instead keeps digital assets inside the existing Article 9 taxonomy, and splits them three ways: subsection (1) makes digital assets *intangible personal property* and *general intangibles*, and subsection (2) makes digital securities *intangible personal property* and *investment property* — both by reference to § 28-9-102 — while subsection (3) makes virtual currency *intangible personal property* and expressly **not** a security, notwithstanding § 30-14-102.

## The concept still has an Idaho hook

Non-adoption of Article 12 does **not** mean the term is absent from Idaho law. `Idaho Code §28-5303` uses it directly:

> (3) "Digital asset" means a representation of economic, proprietary, or access rights that is stored in a computer-readable format and includes an open blockchain token, digital commodity, digital security, virtual currency, and any other controllable electronic record.

and defines the operative concept of control:

> (2) "Control" means the ability to exclude others from the use of property

So "controllable electronic record" and control-as-right-to-exclude both appear in enacted Idaho law, by way of Chapter 53's own definitions rather than by adoption of Article 12.
