## Idaho has second-lowest error rate in nation for SNAP payments

A low error rate means Idaho will avoid paying more for the federal food assistance program

Idaho has continued its record of accurately paying out federal financial food assistance.

The Gem State last fiscal year had the [second-lowest rate in the nation](https://www.fna.usda.gov/snap/qc/per?utm_medium=email&utm_source=govdelivery) for payment errors in administering the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program, or SNAP, according to data released Wednesday. The federal fiscal year ran from Oct. 1, 2024, to Sept. 30, 2025.

Maintaining a low error rate for SNAP will be key to the state [avoiding having to pay significantly more](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/despite-idahos-low-snap-errors-state-legislature-wants-to-shore-up-verification/) for the program under new financial penalties set to go into effect next year.

“The department maintains a strong commitment to accuracy, accountability, and service delivery,” Idaho Department of Health and Welfare spokesperson AJ McWhorter said in an emailed statement. “A low SNAP payment error rate (2nd lowest in the USA) reflects the dedication of staff who work diligently to ensure that benefits are issued correctly and efficiently to eligible households.”

The U.S. Department of Agriculture runs and primarily funds SNAP at the federal level, and state agencies administer it to their participants.

Historically, the federal government has paid 100% of the cost of the food benefit and has split the administration costs 50/50 with states. However, under provisions of the federal One Big Beautiful Bill Act approved by Congress last July, states that have a payment error rate at or above 6% will have to contribute to the food benefit as well, increasing their costs for the program.

Those penalties, which would increase the state share based on how high the error rate is, will go into effect Oct. 1, 2027.

Idaho’s payment error rate was 3.85, according to the data released by the USDA. Only South Dakota had a lower rate, at 2.47. The national error rate was 10.62.

As of June 15, there are just over 123,000 Idahoans receiving food assistance through SNAP, according to the [Idaho Department of Health and Welfare.](https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/) Beneficiaries receive an average of $180 per person monthly.

Idaho was in the top three lowest rates for fiscal years 2022 through 2024. There is no data available for 2020 and 2021.

## More SNAP changes will be reflected in next fiscal year’s rates

The data released from fiscal year 2025 doesn’t reflect other [changes made to SNAP](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2026/01/02/changes-to-idaho-snap-food-program-coming-in-2026/) from the ‘big beautiful’ law, including expanded work requirements that went into effect in April. The state agency is also expected to increase the frequency of eligibility checks for the program in July. Previous changes in the law [removed eligibility from refugees](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2025/09/05/idaho-refugees-to-lose-access-to-food-health-benefits-under-big-beautiful-bill/) who have not yet obtained a green card.

The federal law also increased the state’s share of administration costs, which is expected to cost Idaho an additional [$6 million per year.](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2025/07/18/how-congresss-megabill-will-impact-idaho-cuts-to-taxes-medicaid-snap-and-more/) The Idaho Legislature this year passed [House Bill 730,](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/H0730/) which approved [$351,000](https://legislature.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/budget/JFAC/sessionrecord/2026/3.Health%20and%20Human%20Services/Health%20and%20Welfare,%20Department%20of/Welfare,%20Division%20of/~Budget%20Setting/March%2026,%202026/A.Motion.pdf?1782338050) in state general funds to create a new system interface to implement work requirements and other [new eligibility verification](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/despite-idahos-low-snap-errors-state-legislature-wants-to-shore-up-verification/) requirements.

Opponents of the bill argued it [could increase error rates](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/despite-idahos-low-snap-errors-state-legislature-wants-to-shore-up-verification/) by adding more administrative work, the Idaho Capital Sun previously reported.

McWhorter, the Health and Welfare spokesman, said the low rate shows that the agency’s processes are working.

“This achievement demonstrates effective program oversight, sound operational practices, and a continued focus on serving Idahoans with integrity,” McWhorter said. “Maintaining payment accuracy helps protect program resources while ensuring that individuals and families receive the assistance they need in a timely manner.”
