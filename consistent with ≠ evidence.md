# "consistent with" ≠ evidence

**"Consistent with" is not evidence.** It means only that the proposed explanation *could* account for the observed facts — not that it *does*, or that it is the *best* or *only* explanation.

When a source, official, or report says findings are "consistent with" some conclusion, that phrase is doing rhetorical work without doing evidentiary work. Any number of competing explanations may be equally consistent with the same facts.

## The distinction

| Phrase | What it actually claims |
|---|---|
| "consistent with" | The hypothesis is not ruled out by the data |
| "evidence of" | The data supports the hypothesis above alternatives |
| "proof of" | The hypothesis is established beyond reasonable doubt |

## Watch for it

- Medical reports: *"injuries consistent with a fall"* — does not establish a fall occurred
- Law enforcement: *"evidence consistent with foul play"* — does not establish foul play
- Expert assessments: *"findings consistent with fraud"* — does not establish fraud
- Official statements: *"results consistent with our policy goals"* — does not validate the policy

## The error

Treating "consistent with X" as confirmation of X is a form of **affirming the consequent** — a basic logical fallacy. If X were true, we'd expect to see Y. We see Y. Therefore X is true. *Wrong.*

## Reporting discipline

- Do not write "the evidence suggests X" when the source only said the evidence is "consistent with X."
- Do not use "consistent with" as a synonym for "supports" or "shows."
- Ask: *What would the data look like if the alternative explanation were true? Would it look the same?*
- Push sources to say what the evidence actually *shows*, not merely what it *doesn't rule out*.
