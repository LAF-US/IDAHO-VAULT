Teton Valley Health Care (TVHC) announced this past week it has received a $1.5 million donation from the Teton Ridge Ranch Foundation to cover hospital expenses including payroll, vendor payments and equipment costs — the latest in a series of donations from the foundation totaling more than $2 million to the valley’s only hospital.

The donation follows a $500,000 donation made by the foundation in October to replace the hospital’s failing HVAC system and install a dedicated power source for the operating room.

TVHC, an 87-year-old community hospital and the county’s second-largest employer, has faced mounting financial pressure over the past year. Federal reimbursement cuts, rising costs and Medicare and Medicaid cuts have weakened the hospital financially. A slow winter and late insurance repayments led the hospital to lay off 26 employees and close its infusion clinic in January. The infusion clinic provided chemotherapy, hydration therapy, and specialized nursing care to patients across the valley.

Hospital officials said the foundation’s donation will allow TVHC to remain open while leadership can work out a longer-term financial strategy.

“Every dollar we bring in right now buys time for thoughtful decision-making about this hospital’s future,” said CEO Leianne Everett in a press release. “We are focused on keeping essential services running for this community while we build toward something more sustainable.”

Ken Dunn, chair of the hospital’s board of directors, said the donation reflects a community obligation as much as a financial one. “Rural hospitals across the country are closing. We refuse to be one of them,” Dunn said. “Keeping these doors open isn’t just a financial challenge, it’s a community obligation.”

This donation is the latest in a series of donations from the Teton Ridge Ranch Foundation, which has given more than $2 million to Teton Valley organizations in recent years, including the Teton County Sheriff’s Foundation, Teton County Search and Rescue, the Community Foundation of Teton Valley and the Teton County Joint Housing Authority.

The foundation was established by Thomas Tull, a billionaire investor and landowner whose Teton Ridge Ranch surrounds a state trust parcel known as the Driggs 160 — a roughly 160-acre piece of land in central Teton Valley that state officials just recently announced auctioning.

Some in the valley have questioned whether Tull’s sweeping investments in these institutions — the hospital, the sheriff’s office, search and rescue — reflects pure generosity or a potential effort to demonstrate goodwill in a community where he holds significant land interests.

“Healthcare access is foundational to any community’s wellbeing,” the foundation said in a statement. “When a community institution needs support, you step up. That’s what neighbors do.”

TVHC has told Teton Valley News in the last few months it is continuing to explore potential partnerships with larger health systems. In a recent press release, TVHC said they are working towards resolving a lease agreement with Teton County.

However, in March, commissioners asked CEO Leianne Everett whether she was continuing to pursue a change in the lease arrangement, to which she said the matter was on hold.

Because Teton County owns the hospital’s buildings and land, TVHC cannot use the property as a cushion to pursue financing. Since 2013, when the hospital became a nonprofit, it has paid the county $70,000 annually in lease payments under a 99-year lease covering roughly an acre and a half in central Driggs. Hospital leadership has pushed to acquire the campus outright, arguing that county ownership blocks financing, donor support and the ability to expand.

County commissioners have been reluctant to give up the lease revenue given the county’s own budget constraints, and no agreement has been reached.

“Every dollar we bring in right now buys time for thoughtful decision-making about this hospital’s future,” Everett said in a press release. “We are focused on keeping essential services running for this community while we build toward something more sustainable.”

TVHC has told Teton Valley News it is not accepting interviews for the foreseeable future.
