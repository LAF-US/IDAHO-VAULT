Commercial businesses that occupy space in city parks fronting Payette Lake could soon be banned by the McCall City Council.

Earlier this month the council began reviewing a 2011 policy governing commercial agreements with businesses seeking to operate in some of the city’s most prime real estate – Legacy Park, Art Roberts Park, Rotary Park, and Brown Park.

“The question really is whether the approval process should be modernized after 15 years of growth, 15 years of expanded recreational tourism,” McCall Parks and Recreation Director Kurt Wolf said.

For years, the city’s concessionaire policy enabled rental jet skis to be staged on Legacy Beach, but that ended last year when Cheap Thrills Rentals chose not to renew a permit it had held for more than a decade. Another proposal for rental jet skis on Legacy Beach was received by the city last spring, but ultimately rejected by the council amid concerns about limited public beach space on Payette Lake, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2025/04/14/legacy-park-beach-belongs-to-everybody-mccall-rejects-summer-rental-proposal/).

Now, the council is doubling down on its stance and considering an outright ban on any commercial enterprise in city parks that requires space that could otherwise be used by the public at large.

“If somebody’s looking towards Rotary, Art Roberts, Brown or Legacy, if it’s going to occupy space that otherwise the public could use, that’s a defining characteristic that says ‘no,’” Council Member Mike Maciaszek said.

While Maciaszek favored an outright ban, Council Member Lyle Nelson suggested such proposals would have an “uphill battle” to gaining city approval.

McCall Mayor Colby Nielsen generally agreed with concerns raised by Maciaszek and Nelson, but suggested more flexibility could be needed.

“We want to have the guardrails up, but at the same time I feel pretty strongly about promoting local small business and entrepreneurship,” Nielsen said.

Wolf is expected to bring a revised policy for the council to consider at a future meeting.

## Sauna and cold plunge proposal

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/04/valleylookout.com-its-a-year-round-tourism-opportunity-mccall-gets-proposal-for-payette-lake-sauna-cold-plunge-img-0691.jpeg)

The southwest corner of Legacy Park was proposed as one possible site for a sauna and cold plunge proposal submitted to the McCall City Council this spring. Photo: Drew Dodson/Valley Lookout

The formal policy review follows a [sauna and cold plunge business that applied to operate in either Rotary Park or Legacy Park](https://valleylookout.com/2026/04/09/its-a-year-round-tourism-opportunity-payette-lake-sauna-cold-plunge-plan-divides-mccall-council/) earlier this year. That application was tabled by the council and has not yet returned for a final decision.

However, Maciaszek pointed to public comments the city received on the proposal to support his position on banning any businesses that require space in city parks.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“People don’t want to see our parks used for other people… to have a commercial use to make money. They can go get some private land and rent the property, and they can go make their money that way,” he said. “But public land is for the public good.”

An online survey conducted by the city in April garnered 536 responses, 70% of which were opposed to the sauna and cold plunge proposal.

An internal city memo summarizing the survey results noted that most respondents objected to allowing for-profit businesses to operate in public parks. Many worried that allowing one would give way to more, while other concerns about safety, liability, parking, and maintenance were also raised.

![Valley Lookout house - generic](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/82689d5d-b8b7-4c7f-9d4c-b24100ffd632.gif)

Valley Lookout house - generic

“Most people are not in favor of the proposal as it’s currently framed,” the memo said. “This isn’t a case of mixed or uncertain feedback. It’s pretty direct.”

The sauna and cold plunge proposal remains active, but it is currently up to the business to decide if it wants to return to the council for a final decision, McCall Public Affairs and Engagement Director Erin Greaves said.

## Financial benefit of concessionaire agreements

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/08/valleylookout.com-young-entrepreneur-starts-mobile-ice-cream-business-in-mccall-1000003368-large-young-entrepreneur-starts-mobile-ice-cream-business-in-mccall-1000003368-large.webp)

Fudgie Wudgie Founder Marshall Curtin. Photo: Courtesy Marshall Curtin

Despite the concerns, Wolf noted that there is a financial benefit to city parks for entering into concessionaire agreements with businesses. The money raised through the agreements helps fund maintenance and upgrades in the parks.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

“We just need to recognize too that if we don’t have some of these concessions or these opportunities coming in, we’ve got to get creative in finding other revenue streams to offset that with tax base or grants or whatever it might be,” he said.

The city currently has two active long-term concessionaire agreements in place.

One is for [Payette Pedal Party](https://valleylookout.com/2022/01/28/payette-pedal-party/), a cycle boat with capacity for up to 16 passengers that is based in Art Roberts Park. A new three-year agreement was signed by the city in January.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The other concession agreement is for [Fudgie Wudgie](https://valleylookout.com/2025/08/15/4380/), a bicycle cart ice cream business operated by a local high school student. That agreement started last year and was renewed for this summer.

Both agreements entitle the city to 3% of total sales made by each business. Rental jet ski concessionaire agreements previously in place netted the city $10,000 per summer, while the city also administratively issues short-term permits for concessionaires tied to events or certain weekends.

“It’s finding that balance of commercializing our public properties, but also trying to manage them well,” Wolf said.
