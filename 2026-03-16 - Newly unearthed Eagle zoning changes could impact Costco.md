## Costco a no go? Rule passed but never published could doom warehouse plan

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/its-official-costco-seeks-approval-for-new-store-along-idaho-55-with-roundabouts-Screenshot-2026-01-0.jpeg)

Rendering of a proposed Costco Wholesale store location in Eagle along Idaho 55. Rendering: Via KM Engineering

Five years ago, Eagle gave its blessing to start development of an upscale, walkable mixed-use development on Hill Road.

The project, called Quarry Village, [would have transformed the 27-acre former gravel quarry between Idaho Highway 55 and Horseshoe Bend Road](https://boisedev.com/news/2018/08/15/quarry-village-eagle-idaho/) into a development featuring entertainment, shopping, boutique retail, and housing packed into a single development. It was the vision of the heirs of the former quarry owners on the site.

After years of negotiations on a development agreement for the site, the City of Eagle approved it in July 2021. The Eagle City Council also approved a change to its comprehensive plan, which lays out the city’s vision for development in the area. This move changed the parcel’s designation from Business Park to Mixed-Use, which helped the project be approved and move forward.

![Washington Trust - Wilcox - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/dce5e549-15d3-4296-8c4b-239eb9eabc77.png)

Washington Trust - Wilcox - 2:1

Part of this change to the comprehensive plan included language saying “retail of no more than 120,000 square feet” should be allowed on the site. This change, which could be used to block major big box retailers, was in line with the vision for a mix of retailers at Quarry Village. But after the property went up for sale in 2022, and wholesale retailer [Costco is now eying development](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/01/07/costco-eagle-plan/) of the site, that could affect whether the large store is approved for construction.

The proposed Costco store is 166,000 square feet, [as BoiseDev reported.](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/01/07/costco-eagle-plan/)

This section of the comprehensive plan, approved in 2021 but not implemented until 2023, was not listed on the city’s comprehensive plan posted on its website. It was also not in the packet of proposed changes to the city’s comprehensive plan that the Eagle Planning & Zoning Commission considered in the summer of 2025 or earlier this year. However, it was included in the packet of changes before the Eagle City Council for consideration this week, as the city considers a sweeping rewrite of the comp plan to reduce density city-wide.

![Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/6f251bf1-00e6-48d7-8365-570820fe299b.gif)

Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow

When asked why the changes passed at the time weren’t updated in the city’s comprehensive plan, city spokesperson Laura Williams said these restrictions on the site aren’t new, even if they weren’t publicly available.

“The comprehensive plan is a document that serves as a guide and gets updated periodically, but between updates of the guide, all resolutions passed are no less valid or legally binding,” she wrote in an email. “The print document wasn’t updated since the resolution was approved, but we are working on that now.”

This change is part of the comprehensive plan package the [City of Eagle has been considering since at least last summer.](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/06/16/eagle-density-reduction/) During Tuesday’s public hearing, city council members heard from property owners who wanted specific changes to the zoning of projects they plan in the future, including a plea from developers eyeing a nearly 400-acre site on Beacon Light Road, and agreed they’d like to do a sweeping downzoning of the city. Mayor Brad Pike and council members also directed staff to further define mixed-use, so developers must add commercial uses to their projects instead of using the zone solely for dense residential projects.

The Eagle City Council concluded taking public testimony on the proposed comprehensive plan on Tuesday and will plan to vote on the final changes they discussed, including formally incorporating changes to the language governing development on the proposed Costco property, on April 14.
