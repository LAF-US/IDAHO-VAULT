The Idaho Potato Drop is considering moving to Expo Idaho this year.

During a quarterly update between Expo Idaho director Helen Baker and the Ada County Commission on Wednesday, Baker said Potato Drop organizers had toured the facility and asked about securing a contract for this year’s event.

She said the prospect is in the early stages for the event, which is traditionally held on New Year’s Eve. She said the event would use the outdoor grounds and a portion of one of the indoor expo buildings.

Idaho Wines - August 2026

Commissioner Rod Beck asked Baker if the event was considering the Expo because “they’ve been kicked out of downtown,” but Baker said she didn’t know if it was a “proactive move or a reactive move.” The drop has been held in Downtown Boise each year since it started in 2013, with the exception of a temporary relocation to the Idaho Center during the COVID-19 pandemic.

Potato Drop organizers Dylan Cline and Sandi Nahas indicated early on the morning of January 1 to a Boise Police Officer that they “were done” holding the event in the City of Boise after the 2025/2026 event. [BoiseDev obtained the body camera footage](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/03/09/idaho-potato-drop-boise/) for a story into a pattern of accidents, injuries, and friction between event organizers and the city dating back nearly a decade.

During the latest event, a [five-year-old girl was injured after a gasoline explosion](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/03/09/idaho-potato-drop-boise/) during a midnight fireworks display caused a pane of glass on the AT&T building to shatter, lacerating the girl’s face. Her parents [filed a tort claim](https://www.kivitv.com/downtown-boise/parents-of-injured-girl-files-tort-claim-over-idaho-potato-drop-fireworks-explosion) with the City of Boise seeking millions in damages for her injury. The tort claim said the city knew of “numerous concerns with the event planning and execution” done by Potato Drop organizers and “numerous instances of the setup of the event and resources not following the actual planning documents that were submitted to obtain the special events permit.”

![Washington Trust - OPA - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/641c4ef3-fc36-4367-8849-9d8e91d9f424.png)

A tort claim is a demand to a public agency for payment and can lead to a settlement, lawsuit, or no further action. BoiseDev can find no record that the girl’s parents have filed legal action against the Potato Drop organizers.

In addition to the girl’s injuries, the Borah post office building and Idaho Statehouse were damaged by the fireworks display. In past years, the event has led to a boy breaking his neck after falling on a ski aerial jump, as well as three other minor injuries and damage to a spectator’s jacket from fireworks fallout, [as BoiseDev reported.](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/03/09/idaho-potato-drop-boise/)

The City of Boise said the Potato Drop has not applied to return to the city this year.

BoiseDev asked Nahas by email if they were considering other locations for the event, and she did not respond by the deadline for this story. We also asked if the drop had obtained a renewal with the Idaho Potato Commission, the state agency that holds the trademark to use “Idaho Potato” commercially, and had a license agreement and sponsorship deal with the Idaho Potato Drop in past years.

For the event to move to Expo Idaho, Baker said it would be reviewed before it moved forward with a contract.

“An event of this magnitude would have review not only internally, but then with our risk management,” she said. “If you’re going to have fire permits, we’re close with fire and paramedics – so because we operate the largest events, we look at it from those angles.”

Baker said it would also be reviewed by the county’s legal staff. Finally, a contract would need to be signed off on by the Ada County Commission, which owns Expo Idaho.
