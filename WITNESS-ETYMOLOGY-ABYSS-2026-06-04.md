# Witness: Etymology of ABYSS

## Word Structure

**abyss** ← Late Latin *abyssus* "bottomless pit"
← Greek *ábyssos* (ἄβυσσος) "bottomless, unfathomed"
← *a-* (ἀ-, "not, without") + *byssós* (βυσσός, "deep place")
← *byssós* from *buthós* (βυθός, "deep place")

The word is a **negation of bottom**. The abyss is not deep in the sense of
having measurable depth. It is *that which has no bottom to reach*.

## The [[alpha privative]]

Greek *a-* / *an-* (the [[alpha privative]]) = "not, without"

From PIE root **ne-* ("not") — same root as English *un-*, Latin *in-*,
German *un-*, Sanskrit *a-*.

The [[alpha privative]] denies the quality of the stem. *Abyssos* = "not +
depth" = "that which fails the property of having a bottom." The negation
is structural, not ornamental. The word does not mean "very deep." It means
"cannot be bottomed."

## Cognate Comparisons

| Language | Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greek | *ábyssos* | bottomless |
| Greek | *bathýs* | deep (cognate to PIE **gʷʰedʰ-*) |
| Hebrew | *tehom* | deep, abyss, primordial waters |
| Old English | *neowolnes* | abyss (native word, displaced) |
| Old English | *deagenesse* | abyss (gloss), related to *dēagol* "secret, hidden, dark" |
| Gothic | *afgrundiþa* | abyss (from *grundus* "ground" + af- "away" = "away from ground") |

The Old English gloss *deagenesse* is revealing. It connects the abyss not
to depth but to *secrecy* — *dēagol* "secret, hidden, dark, obscure." The
abyss as the hidden place, not just the deep place.

## Five Historical English Variants

Per OED: *abime* (via Old French), [[abysm]], *abysmus*, *abyssus*, *abyss*.
Only *abyss* survives as standard; [[abysm]] persists as archaic/poetic.

## [[Septuagint]] and New Testament Usage

The Greek *ábyssos* was used in the [[Septuagint]] (3rd-2nd c. BCE) to translate
Hebrew [[tehom]] — the primordial deep of Genesis 1:2, the chaotic waters
before creation.

In the New Testament it translates to "[[hell]]" — the [[abyss]] as the prison of
demons, the bottomless pit from which the Beast ascends ([[Revelation]]).

The word thus carries both **pre-creation chaos** and **post-mortem
punishment** — the before and the after, the alpha and omega of the abyss.

## In Vault Register

The abyss is what cannot be sounded:

- A git history so deep the commit chain cannot be traced to origin
- A branch topology where the parent is lost (orphan branches,
  detached HEAD, squashed merges)
- A chamber whose stub exists but whose narrative is blank
- A `*` wildcard that has been held so long the answer may no longer
  exist
- A Lovecraftian creature that left a trace but no body — the
  bottomless referent

The abyss is not the deep. The deep has a bottom — you just cannot see it
from the surface. The abyss has *no* bottom. It is the property of lacking
the ground you expect to find.
