It could take nearly a decade for property taxes generated by a ‘new city’ proposed near McCall to fully fund public services for the development.

That is the conclusion drawn by an economic study of Red Ridge Village, a proposal by Texas-based DF Development for 722 homes and condos on 2,250 acres off West Mountain Road.

The study, completed by [BBC Research and Consulting](https://bbcresearch.com/) of Denver, was required by the Valley County Planning and Zoning Commission amid its review of a concept plan for the development, which would be built out in five phases spanning about 20 years.

The P&Z expects to hold another public hearing on the application on Aug. 27 at 6 p.m.

“Since our last public hearing, we’ve had three work sessions now,” Commissioner Heidi Schneider said. “So, I do think it’s time to loop the public back in with all this information that’s out.”

BBC’s study projects a long-term surplus in property tax funding generated by Red Ridge Village compared to its expected public service demands, but not until years after construction begins.

In total, Red Ridge would generate about $2.5 million each year in property taxes for EMS, fire, public schools, and county services. It would increase Valley County’s property tax collections by about 11.5% at full buildout.

It would also increase the total number of housing units in the county by about 5%, which is expected to drive an increase in demand for public services.

Red Ridge’s first phase, which calls for 93 single-family lots and 118 townhomes, would result in an annual property tax deficit of about $300,000 between the Valley County General Fund, the Valley Countywide EMS District, the McCall Fire Protection District, and the McCall-Donnelly School District, [according to the study](https://twistcms-shared.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/meetings/110/attachments/2e57824b294d4dd5a52ab0333c1fdaa7.pdf).

That funding deficit would turn into a surplus upon completion of phase 2, which would develop another 78 single-family lots, 83 condos, and 25 townhomes. Within two to four years, the surplus would make up for the initial deficit for EMS, fire, and county services.

The school district, however, would remain at a deficit serving Red Ridge until about six years after the third phase of the project is built — or until about 2041, under current phasing projections.

## Phasing key to withstanding ‘funding lag’

Public service providers would also see a delay between when Red Ridge needs service and when property taxes to pay for that service are actually collected.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“There’s a lag to when the change of the property value happens on the assessment rolls and then when those (property) taxes are collected,” said Kevin Williams, director emeritus at BBC. “I’ve been told that it can be up to a two or three-year lag.”

That funding delay, Williams said, makes it critical to phase the buildout of Red Ridge so that public service demands do not become overwhelming before tax funding arrives.

“Communication with the developer about the phasing and timing of when properties are going to come online and onto the tax revenues is pretty important,” he said.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

Despite the early funding shortfalls, at buildout Red Ridge would generate about twice as much property taxes as needed to fund the development’s service demands, according to the economic study.

“Our overall analysis indicates that at build out for all phases revenues would exceed expenditures,” Williams said.

## Other funding solutions

Williams suggested that early funding gaps could be closed by requiring DF Development to set money aside to pay for public service demands not met by property taxes from the development.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“The developer might put money into that fund that is then used to bridge that gap in those times when the development is ramping up,” Williams said.

The BBC study, however, did not factor capital expenses driven by Red Ridge, such as new schools or emergency facilities. Instead, the study focused on operational costs like personnel.

Capital expenses would need to be addressed through other funding sources to make taxing districts serving Red Ridge whole, Williams said.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

One strategy would be to build funding requirements into a development agreement that would bind DF Development to terms set by the county. The terms would be based on anticipated impacts and public service needs driven by the increased demand from Red Ridge.

Williams also advised that new impact fees could help ensure Red Ridge pays for capital expenses related to recreation infrastructure, roads, schools, and other public amenities.

“It’s really important to — depending on what your impact fees look like and what your capital needs look like — have that as up to date as possible,” he said.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

Valley County and the school district both do not currently have impact fees in place, but all three fire districts across the county do.

## Red Ridge Village background

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2025/03/valleylookout.com-red-ridge-village-plans-to-be-re-submitted-with-minimal-changes-screenshot-2025-03-07-at-4.07.56pm.jpeg)

A map of DF Development’s acreage subject to the Red Ridge Village proposal (shown in red). The City of McCall is shown in purple, while other DF lands are shown in orange. Map: Via GSBS Architects

DF is seeking county approval on conceptual plans for the planned community. The P&Z’s decision on the concept plan would be a recommendation to the Board of County Commissioners. The final design of each development phase would be subject to further county review.

As proposed, Red Ridge Village would include 302 lots for single-family homes, 170 townhomes, and 250 condos within the Village Center, which would include 50,000 square feet of commercial space.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The Village Center is also expected to include stations for the Valley County Sheriff’s Office and McCall Fire and EMS. The facilities and funding obligations would be determined in a development agreement binding DF to approval conditions stipulated by the county.

The village would be flanked by a 149-acre meadow, as well as a working farm that would provide produce to village restaurants and also buffer existing homes on West Mountain Road from residential development.

The [Red Ridge Village](https://dfdevelopmentllc.com/communities/red-ridge-village/) proposal is part of 30,920 contiguous acres of DF land that stretches about 12 miles from west of McCall along West Mountain Road to U.S. 95 west of New Meadows, near the Evergreen Forest Products sawmill. No development is currently proposed on the land in Adams County.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

[A traffic study](https://www.co.valley.id.us/media/Departments/PlanningZoning/PUD%2026-001%20Red%20Ridge%20Village%20Application/Appendix%20J%20Traffic%20Impact%20Study.pdf) estimates Red Ridge would create about 8,100 daily vehicle trips. The study recommends several roadway improvements that would be paid for by DF, including straightening out a series of sharp curves on West Mountain Road.

Improvements are expected to include two new stoplights at the intersections of West Valley Road and Boydstun Street, as well as Boydstun Street and Idaho 55.

A southbound turn lane would be added on Third Street (Idaho 55) at its intersection with Deinhard Lane. The timing of the existing stoplight could also be adjusted to account for Red Ridge traffic.
