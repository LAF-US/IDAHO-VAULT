**Thomas Edward Cheney** (1901–1993) was an American folklorist who made contributions to the field of [Mormon folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_folklore "Mormon folklore"). As one of the first Mormon folklorists, he collected [folk songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music "Folk music") in [Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah "Utah") and [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho") and authored books and articles on Utah, [[Idaho]], and Mormon folklore. He served as president of the Folklore Society of Utah from 1963 to 1964. Cheney also compiled songs for the [American Folklore Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Folklore_Society "American Folklore Society"), which were published in 1968. Along with books on Mormon Folklore, Cheney wrote a book about [J. Golden Kimball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Golden_Kimball "J. Golden Kimball"), which was considered controversial. He was also a professor of English at [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University "Brigham Young University") (BYU) and taught courses in [English Romantic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_literature_in_English "Romantic literature in English"), [ballads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad "Ballad"), and Mormon folklore.

## Early life

Thomas E. Cheney was born in 1901 in [Victor, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor,_Idaho "Victor, Idaho").[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 54">: 54</span> </sup> After graduating from high school, he attended summer school at Ricks College, now known as [Brigham Young University-Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University-Idaho "Brigham Young University-Idaho"). Immediately afterward, he began teaching at an elementary school. He earned his bachelor's degree in English at Utah Agricultural College (now Utah State University).[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 54">: 54</span> </sup> Cheney served a mission for the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints "Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints") (LDS church) in [Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California "Southern California") from 1924 to 1926.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 54">: 54</span></sup>

## Career

### Teaching career

Cheney resumed his teaching career after he returned from his mission and became a school principal. He was introduced to folklore during his master's program at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") in 1930. He completed his master's thesis on Mormon folk songs, attaining his master's degree in 1936.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 55">: 55</span> </sup> Cheney then became a professor of English at BYU, where he taught [English Romantic literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_literature_in_English "Romantic literature in English") and English, Scottish, and American ballads.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 55">: 55</span> </sup> [^2] He also taught graduate seminars in Mormon folklore. Additionally, Cheney led students through projects collecting their own folklore material in his Introduction to Folklore course.[^3]

### Folklore scholar

Cheney was one of the first Mormon folklore scholars.[^4] A pioneer folklore scholar of Idaho and Utah,[^5] he collected and transcribed Utah and [Intermountain Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermountain_West "Intermountain West") folk songs.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 54">: 54</span> </sup> [^6] [^7] He served as a folklorist consultant for the 1965 album *The Mormon Pioneers*, which included 17 folk songs that he helped contribute.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 58">: 58</span></sup>

## Memberships and affiliations

Cheney was a member of the Folklore Society of Utah, which he served as vice-president in 1958 and 1967.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 224–227">: 224–227</span> </sup> He was president of the Folklore Society of Utah from 1963 to 1964. During his presidency, he supervised the publication of five newsletters and organized a meeting in [St. George, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._George,_Utah "St. George, Utah") in December 1964, where a compilation of articles related to Utah folklore was planned.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 226, 227">: 226, 227</span> </sup> The compilation, titled *Lore of Faith and Folly*, was edited by Cheney and published in 1971.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 58, 227">: 58, 227</span> </sup> [^8]

## Publications

While teaching at BYU, Cheney continued to collect folk songs, eventually compiling 250 folk songs for the [American Folklore Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Folklore_Society "American Folklore Society"). These folk songs were published in *Mormon Songs from the Rocky Mountains* (originally titled *Songs of the Wasatch and Tetons*) in 1968.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 56">: 56</span> </sup> This book helped create a strong foundation of research for Mormon folklore.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 9">: 9</span></sup>

Cheney's subsequent book, *The Golden Legacy: A Folk History of J. Golden Kimball*, published in 1973, was controversial at BYU. This book revolved around the life of [J. Golden Kimball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Golden_Kimball "J. Golden Kimball"), an LDS Church leader and member of the [First Council of the Seventy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy_\(LDS_Church\) "Seventy (LDS Church)"), and contained a chapter with explicit language (which Kimball was known for using).[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 56">: 56</span> </sup> After 50 copies were sold from a print run of 6,000, the Brigham Young University Press withdrew the book at the request of the LDS Church.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 56, 57">: 56, 57</span> </sup> Peregrine Smith published the censored book in 1974.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 56, 57">: 56, 57</span> </sup> [^9]

Cheney's final book, *Voices from the Bottom of the Bowl: A Folk History of Teton Valley Idaho, 1823–1952*, was published in 1991.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 58">: 58</span></sup>

## Death

Cheney died in 1993 at the age of 92.[^1]<sup><span title="Page / location: 59">: 59</span></sup>
