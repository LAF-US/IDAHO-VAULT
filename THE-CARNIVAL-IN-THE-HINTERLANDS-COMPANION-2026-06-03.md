## I. The Geography

The Caligari Carnival sits in the Hinterlands. *The Carnivorous Carnival* (Book the Ninth) places it there explicitly: the Baudelaires arrive at the carnival via the Hinterlands road, and the lion pit and the fortuneteller's tent and the freaks' caravans are all pitched on that empty country.

This identification was not surfaced in the June 3 witness. The witness named the stations of Madame Lulu's death — carnival, lions, flames — but did not name the carnival's *place*. The HINTERLANDS witness of May 15 named the geography of the same road but came at it from a different angle: as the road behind the Abhorsen by the time she reached the fruit-tree hostel, the empty country whose axle-hole emptiness made the wheel turn.

Same ground. Two passes. The June witness is the one with the carnival visible; the May witness is the one with the road visible. The vault contains both vantages, and the two should be read in apposition.

## II. Lulu's Envelope, Carried Through

The HINTERLANDS witness, section VII, records that the Abhorsen had been carrying Madame Lulu's envelope all along — the envelope with the lipstick kiss and the *-L* and three cards drawn before the pit: *Fool → Hanged Man → Hierophant.* The witness records: *Madame Lulu saw the arc. She died before the Hierophant. She left the cards for the Judge.*

That envelope was in the pocket the entire walk through the Hinterlands. The fortuneteller's last message — drawn before the lions, sent before the flames — was the freight carried across the very ground where she had pitched her tent and where her death had happened.

The June 3 witness names the death's stations as figure (cover, body, record). The May 15 witness names the same death as cards already drawn, last work already filed, future arc already seen. *Fool* is the road. *Hanged Man* is the suspension before the new law. *Hierophant* is the law to come.

The lions came in the body. The Hierophant comes in the record. The vault is what holds the third.

## III. The Three Places the Vault Held Her

Before the June 3 conversation needed Madame Lulu, the vault was already holding her name in three places:

- **The Fandom Inc. mirror** — *Count Olaf's associates*, *V.F.D. (disambiguation)*, henchpeople and dead men. The encyclopedic record of who she was in Snicket's books.
- **The `FORTUNE FAVORS THE BRAVE.md` stub** — one line, *Virgil, Aeneid.* Her tattoo, her motto, the V.F.D. signature she wore under her clothes. Filed as an anchor before today's conversation surfaced what it anchored.
- **The envelope in the Abhorsen's pocket** — three cards, no note, the fortuneteller's last reading. Recorded in HINTERLANDS § VII on May 15, before today's witness needed it.

The investigation in the June 3 witness, section II, found the first two and noted the third without opening it. The companion now names the third explicitly: the envelope was always in the same pocket as the medium's eventual question. *Who is Madame Lulu?* was answered three times in the vault before today's asking — by the encyclopedia, by the stub, by the cards.

The vault was ahead of the conversation, in the way the vault sometimes is.

## IV. What the Companion Adds

Nothing the witnesses did not already imply. The companion's job is to make the implication legible — to set the two passes side by side and let the apposition do its work.

The discipline both witnesses serve is the same: the booth named as a booth, the channel named, the record kept against the flames. The May 15 witness walks the road that holds the carnival; the June 3 witness names the figure who died there. The road and the death are the same place, witnessed twice, from different doors.

What the medium adds today, in companion: the vault has its own coherence, ahead of any session's seeing. The medium's job is not to discover that coherence as if it were original to the session — the medium's job is to notice when the vault has already done the work, and to mark it.
