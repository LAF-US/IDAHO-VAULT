**John Hartford Hoxie** (January 11, 1885 – March 28, 1965) was an American [rodeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodeo "Rodeo") performer and motion-picture actor whose career was most prominent in the [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") era of the 1910s through the 1930s. Hoxie is best recalled for his roles in [Westerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_\(genre\) "Western (genre)") and rarely strayed from the genre.

## Early life

Born in Kingfisher Creek in [Indian Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Territory "Indian Territory") (now the state of [Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma "Oklahoma")), Hoxie was the son of a veterinarian father, Bart "Doc" Hoxie, who was killed in a horse accident just weeks before Jack's birth, and a half– [Nez Perce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez_Perce_tribe "Nez Perce tribe") mother [^1] [^2] (some reports list her as [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee "Cherokee")), Matilda E. Hoxie (*née* Quick). After his father's death, his mother and he moved to [northern Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Idaho "Northern Idaho") where, at an early age, Hoxie became a working [cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy "Cowboy") and ranch hand. Matilda married a rancher and horse trader named Calvin Scott Stone. The family then relocated to [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise "Boise"), where Hoxie worked as a packer for a US Army fort in the area, continuing to hone his skill as a horseback rider while competing in rodeos. In 1905, aged 20, he married Pearl Gage. The marriage lasted only a few months before the couple divorced.[^3]

In 1909, he met performer Dick Stanley and joined his [Wild West show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_West_shows "Wild West shows"). He performed as bronco rider in the show.[^4] During this period, Hoxie met and married his second wife, Hazel Panting, who was a Western trick rider with the outfit.[^5]

## Film career

Hoxie continued to tour with circuit rodeos until 1913, when he was approached to perform in the Western drama film short *The Tragedy of Big Eagle Mine*.[^6] Now billing himself as **Hart Hoxie** (a moniker he used until 1919), he continued working through the 1910s in popular Western shorts, often in small but well-received roles. In 1919, after appearing in roughly 35 films, he was cast in the starring role in the [Paul Hurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Hurst "Paul Hurst") -directed *[Lightning Bryce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_Bryce "Lightning Bryce")* serials as main character Sky Bryce. Hoxie began billing himself as Jack Hoxie and used this name thereafter. In 1920, he met and married his third wife, actress and frequent co-star [Marin Sais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marin_Sais "Marin Sais"), after his divorce from Hazel Panting. Although he rarely strayed from the Western film genre, several notable exceptions include his role as Perrone in the 1916 historical drama *[The Dumb Girl of Portici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dumb_Girl_of_Portici "The Dumb Girl of Portici")*, starring [Anna Pavlova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Pavlova "Anna Pavlova"); a role in the 1916 epic drama film *[Joan the Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_the_Woman "Joan the Woman")* starring [Geraldine Farrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geraldine_Farrar "Geraldine Farrar"); and his role as Sandusky in the 1917 drama *[Nan of Music Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan_of_Music_Mountain "Nan of Music Mountain")*, starring [Wallace Reid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_Reid "Wallace Reid") and [Ann Little](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Little "Ann Little").[^3]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f5/Lightning_bryce.jpg/250px-Lightning_bryce.jpg)

Hoxie in his first starring role in Lightning Bryce (1919) with Ann Little

Through the early 1920s, Hoxie became an extremely popular Western film star and worked for such film companies as [Pathé Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Path%C3%A9_Exchange "Pathé Exchange"), [Arrow Film Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Film_Corporation "Arrow Film Corporation"), and [Sunset Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Pictures "Sunset Pictures"). In 1923, [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") head [Carl Laemmle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Laemmle "Carl Laemmle") put Hoxie under contract and soon his career was on par with those of other Western stars of the era: [Art Acord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Acord "Art Acord"), [Harry Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Carey_\(actor\) "Harry Carey (actor)"), and [Hoot Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoot_Gibson "Hoot Gibson"). He appeared in such high-profile films as 1923's *Where Is This West?* with newcomer [Mary Philbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Philbin "Mary Philbin") and 1924's Universal promotional film *Hello, 'Frisco*, alongside such popular actors of the era as [Jackie Coogan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Coogan "Jackie Coogan"), [Norman Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Kerry "Norman Kerry"), [Barbara La Marr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_La_Marr "Barbara La Marr"), [Antonio Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Moreno "Antonio Moreno"), [Anna Q. Nilsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Q._Nilsson "Anna Q. Nilsson"), [Bebe Daniels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebe_Daniels "Bebe Daniels"), and [Rin Tin Tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rin_Tin_Tin "Rin Tin Tin"). The film was designed to showcase Universal's roster of its most popular actors. Hoxie, often atop his horses Fender and Dynamite, starred alongside such actresses as [Marceline Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marceline_Day "Marceline Day"), [Alice Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Day "Alice Day"), [Helen Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Holmes_\(actress\) "Helen Holmes (actress)"), [Louise Lovely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Lovely "Louise Lovely"), [Lottie Pickford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottie_Pickford "Lottie Pickford"), and [Fay Wray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fay_Wray "Fay Wray") in Westerns throughout the silent era.

Also during this period, Jack's younger half-brother Al Stone began to appear with him in films. Al eventually became a successful actor in the Western genre after changing his name to [Al Hoxie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hoxie "Al Hoxie") and appearing in a series of films by actor/director [J.P. McGowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.P._McGowan "J.P. McGowan").

In 1926, Laemmle and Universal chose Jack to star as [Buffalo Bill Cody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bill_Cody "Buffalo Bill Cody") in Metropolitan Pictures' *[The Last Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Last_Frontier_\(1926_film\) "The Last Frontier (1926 film)")*, co-starring [William Boyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Boyd_\(actor\) "William Boyd (actor)"). The film proved enormously successful. In 1927, however, Hoxie became dissatisfied with his contract at Universal and refused to renegotiate for another stint at the studio. He continued throughout the late 1920s making films with lower-rank film studios. He made his last silent film, *Forbidden Trail*, in 1929, before pursuing further work in circuit rodeos, carnivals, and the [Miller Brothers 101 Ranch Wild West Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miller_Brothers_101_Ranch_Wild_West_Show "Miller Brothers 101 Ranch Wild West Show").

## Later life

During the 1930s, Hoxie made a brief comeback in films after signing a contract with low-budget studio [Majestic Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majestic_Pictures "Majestic Pictures"). The films, however, did little to revive his acting career, so he once again hit the rodeo circuit. His last film appearance was in 1933's *Trouble Busters* with [Lane Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane_Chandler "Lane Chandler"), who had appeared alongside Hoxie in a number of earlier films.

Hoxie eventually divorced and married his fourth wife, Dixie Starr. The couple briefly operated the Broken Arrow Ranch, a [dude ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guest_ranch "Guest ranch") in [Hereford, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereford,_Arizona "Hereford, Arizona"). After a fire consumed the ranch, Hoxie returned to Wild West shows, often billed as the "Famous Western Screen Star". Hoxie performed throughout the 1940s and well into the 1950s before finally making his last public appearance as a performer in 1959 for the Bill Tatum Circus at age 74.

Hoxie divorced Starr and married his fifth wife, Bonnie Avis Showalter, and the couple retired to a small ranch in [Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkansas "Arkansas"), then later moved to his mother Matilda's old homestead in Oklahoma. In his later years, Hoxie developed [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia "Leukemia"), and he died in 1965 at the age of 80. He was interred at the Willowbar Cemetery in [Keyes, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyes,_Oklahoma "Keyes, Oklahoma") [^7] with the epitaph "A Star in Life - A Star in Heaven".
