August recess is an annual break in the congressional calendar that allows lawmakers to return home and meet with constituents. For public media supporters, it’s an opportunity to advocate for restoring federal funding closer to home.

[Congress has taken limited action on public media funding this summer, with final funding decisions still ahead.](https://protectmypublicmedia.org/blog/2026/08/12/where-local-public-media-station-funding-stands/)

Whether you have a few minutes or a few hours, you can help grow the Protect My Public Media network, contribute stories that demonstrate the value of local stations and build congressional support before final funding decisions are made.

### Ways to Strengthen the Campaign

### How to Strengthen Our Advocacy on the Ground

#### Attend a Congressional Town Hall

Congressional town halls allow constituents to ask lawmakers directly about issues affecting their communities. Here’s how to find an event and speak up for your local public media stations.

#### How Do I Find My Members of Congress?

Use the official congressional directories to identify the lawmakers who represent you:

- Find your representative by entering your ZIP code at [gov](https://www.house.gov/representatives/find-your-representative).
- Find your senators at [gov](https://www.senate.gov/senators/senators-contact.htm).

Congressional offices accept messages only from their constituents, so direct your outreach to the lawmakers who represent where you live.

#### How Do I Find a Congressional Town Hall?

Check your lawmakers’ official websites (.gov), email newsletters and social media accounts for announcements about town hall meetings (in-person, by telephone or virtually) or community office hours.

You can also call their district or state offices and ask about upcoming events.

#### What Should I Ask About Local Public Media Stations?

Introduce yourself as a constituent and ask the question below. If you can, name your local public media station or share a personal experience that shows why its service is important to you.

***Our local public media station is the heart of our community. They tell our local stories, keep us informed, provide critical information during emergencies and support learners throughout their lives. Americans overwhelmingly supported protecting federal funding, but Congress eliminated funding last summer. As a result, the local service stations provide is at risk, and some stations may go off-air. I am worried about what on the loss of federal funding will mean for our community and the local services we count on. Local public media stations are trying to find a way to bring back funding for their essential local services. Will you support restoring federal funding for our local public media stations?***

#### How Should I Participate in a Town Hall?

- Register in advance if required and follow the event’s rules and question-submission process.
- Ask one question rather than delivering a speech.
- Listen to the response without interrupting.
- Take notes and thank the lawmaker or staff member.

#### What Should I Do After the Town Hall?

Let Protect My Public Media know what the lawmaker said. Your notes will help us better understand their position and guide future outreach.
