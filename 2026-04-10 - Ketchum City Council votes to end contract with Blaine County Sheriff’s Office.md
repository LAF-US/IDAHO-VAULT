KETCHUM, Idaho (KMVT/KSVT) — The Ketchum City Council voted Thursday night to end its contract with the Blaine County Sheriff’s Office and establish its own police department, ending a 17-year partnership.

The council voted 3-1 to terminate the policing contract and finalize a transition plan.

“Councilman McGraw, aye, Councilman Cordovano, aye, Councilman Hutchinson, aye, Councilman Hall, no,” asked Mayor Pete Prekeges.

Ketchum residents filled the council chambers to hear from city leaders and Blaine County Sheriff Morgan Ballis ahead of the vote, with mixed reactions to the proposal.

“There are always unintended consequences and I think you all will find them in not only in the hiring and retention of the police force but you will also find it in the financial element of switching to a local Ketchum Police Department,” one resident said.

Another resident said, “I would like to see a Ketchum Police Department run by City Council and the Mayor as we did then.”

Prekeges pushed for the city to have its own police department, citing concerns over costs and control.

“We’re the outlier in Blaine County, Bellevue has its own, Hailey has its own, Sun Valley has its own, and we wanted to bring our police underneath governance of the City Council and Mayor,” Prekeges said.

Ballis raised concerns about ending the partnership, pointing to issues such as officer retention, the cost associated with a transition, and whether the city has the facilities to house its own police department.

“We have staffed them at 12 employees for this contract and they are already reducing down to one employee going down a full time detective to a part time detective which has its own risks in itself but there is a real possibility that by the time we get through this transition they may have half of that department or not have a department at all,” Ballis said.

Ballis also raised issue with how much the department will cost the city compared to contracting with the sheriff’s office.

Prekeges said the city has the money budgeted.

“We have the money budgeted, the same amount as we had budgeted for last year. We won’t be paying a fee to the Blaine County Sheriff’s office for management so that’s out and if we go down to 11 employees then we have that money,” Prekeges said.

Following the council’s vote, there is now a 120-day transition period where the sheriff’s office will assist Ketchum while the police department is established.

“If anyone thinks that policing is done the same that they’re, from first hand, they are incorrect. The City of Ketchum is the outlier in the county we are the only one with out our own police force,” Prekeges said.

Sheriff Ballis warned that ending the contract could impact public safety and limit resources available to the Ketchum Police Department.

“We have staffed them at 12 employees for this contract and they are already reducing down to one employee going down a full time detective to a part time detective which has its own risks in itself but there is a real possibility that by the time we get through this transition they may have half of that department or not have a department at all,” Ballis said.

The City of Ketchum and the Blaine County Sheriff’s Office have 120 days to complete the full transition.
