BOISE, Idaho — Crews are back to battling the [Claremont Fire](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/crews-respond-grass-fire-boise-foothills/277-c9a9e30f-d3be-44a8-85f2-39f48fe401f8) burning in the Boise Foothills after a stand-down order was issued earlier Monday for firefighter safety.

USWFS Idaho [on social media said](https://www.facebook.com/USWFSGreatBasinIdaho/posts/pfbid0oH6QmRWeL3cGMaM3YPo3nd3draF3QwmqkvdfNuKDKdrD99nKaubZ3PQWR63tKbhzl) that the Eastern Area Incident Management Team was alerted to safety concerns regarding the Unexploded Ordnance (UXO) area by the Army Corps of Engineers on the morning of July 13.

The engineers determined the heat being produced by the active fire front within the UXO areas was "more hazardous than previously explained." The USWFS said in order to prioritize firefighter safety, it is conducting a safety stand-down and that all firefighters on the Claremont Fire will stand down immediately.

Officials said crews are officially "re-engaged" on the Claremont Fire as of 3:30 p.m. Monday, July 13.

Credit: KTVB

During what the service described as a "tactical pause," fire crews were being moved away from hazard zones to a safe location.

In the post, USWFS said it was assessing plans to "ensure safe working conditions for all of the firefighters and keeping them informed on all new information and best safety practices."

As of July 13, the Claremont Fire has burned 6,805 acres and is 57% contained, according to USWFS Idaho.
