CALDWELL — Seven-term Republican Sen. Patti Anne Lodge has lived in Caldwell — outside her legislative district — for more than a year while she builds a new home on her property in Sunnyslope.

Two Canyon County Republicans question the validity of her temporary residence, but she said she still represents the people of her nearby district. And Tuesday, Lodge said she would put a mobile home on the property to live in because a few people are making a “political issue” out of the situation.

“The people that know us know that we’re not trying to do anything illegal,” Lodge said. “That’s where my bills come, that’s were I vote, that’s my home.”

Lodge, officially a Huston resident in District 11, has lived in a family home in District 10 in Caldwell since she had her home in Sunnyslope moved and donated. She said there have been numerous delays in the construction of her new home on the Sunnyslope property.

While Idaho’s Constitution requires senators and legislators to have lived in their districts for a year before their election, Idaho Code defines a qualified elector, which is a requirement for legislative office, to include someone who has left their home for “a temporary purpose.”

“That’s the kind of scenario we appear to be in” with Lodge, Idaho Secretary of State Ben Ysursa said. “But the final arbiter of that would be the State Senate. There’s quote-unquote ‘residency questions’ (in general). We’d love to have them be black and white. But sometimes they’re not.”

District 11 resident Maurice Clements ran against Lodge in the 2012 Republican primary.

“I’m puzzled that she is allowed to continue to live outside of the district she is supposedly representing,” Clements said.

Ethan Crisp, 23, Nampa, serves as chairman of the Canyon County Young Republicans.

“If you’re going to be able to represent a group of people, if you’re a state senator and you say you’re representing District 11, I find it very hard to believe you can represent them if you’re not living amongst them, if they’re not your neighbors,” Crisp said.

But Lodge said she’s still in close contact with her constituents. She visits her property, which she and her husband have owned for 47 years, in Sunnyslope daily to take care of livestock and perform other maintenance. And she said she welcomes Clements and Crisp to discuss the issue directly with her.

“I am amongst the people in my district, I’ve been amongst them for 47 years,” Lodge said. “I’ve been in Canyon County since I was 4. It’s not like I’m trying to hide anything.”

Lodge said she will explain her situation to fellow Republicans at the next Canyon County Republican Central Committee set for 7 p.m. Tuesday, Sept. 17, at the Canyon County Courthouse.
