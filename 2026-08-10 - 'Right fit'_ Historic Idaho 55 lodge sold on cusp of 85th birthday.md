A long-for-sale Valley County landmark has sold.

Cougar Mountain Lodge’s previous co-owner, Stacy Willis, announced that the lodge’s sale was official [via a social media post on Sunday](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1LZY7UNF7v/).

Co-owner Patrick Willis said he and Stacy feel relieved at the sale’s finalization.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

“It’s a fairly lengthy process selling a business and property like that, but we’ve got lots of really good memories, and have really turned it into a successful business,” Willis said by phone Monday.

Willis declined to share the sales price for the historic property, located in Smith’s Ferry. The owners originally listed the lodge for sale in 2024 for $1.6 million, but the price was later reduced to $875,000, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2026/04/23/cold-feet-deal-to-buy-historic-idaho-55-business-called-off/). [A buyer in April decided not to go through with their purchase](https://valleylookout.com/2026/04/23/cold-feet-deal-to-buy-historic-idaho-55-business-called-off/) due to concerns regarding the property’s conditional use permit, which limits the size of events and the number per year.

Willis said that the buyers are a family from McCall who “plan to keep it a family business, really similar to what it is now.”

“It was really a right fit for Cougar Mountain Lodge,” Willis said. He anticipates that once the buyers take over the lodge’s social media account, they will make a post introducing themselves. The new owners plan to open as the lodge’s operators on Thursday.

The new owners, who were not identified by name by Willis or real estate agents involved in the transaction, could not be reached for comment on Monday.

The deed transfer for the sale had also not yet been processed as of Monday, according to the Valley County Assessor’s Office.

## Expanded hours, lodging

The lodge has long served as a roadside staple for travelers seeking sustenance in the lodge’s restaurant or to browse its collection of antiques and artifacts.

Willis said that Cougar Mountain Lodge first opened in 1941. 2026 marks its 85th anniversary celebration. Willis said that he and Stacy decided to let the next owners choose how to mark that milestone.

Willis said that the buyers plan to continue operating the restaurant and would like to increase the number of items offered at the convenience store. This summer, the lodge has been closed on Tuesdays and Wednesdays. He said that the new owners are interested in expanding hours to seven days a week.

The new owners are also interested in restoring lodging. Willis said that the site offered lodging through the COVID-19 pandemic before pivoting to offering housing for employees. At the time, a surge in home prices made it difficult for people to afford housing locally, he said. Offering housing allowed the Willises to retain much of the same staff year to year because people could live in the Treasure Valley and commute up to the lodge to work on weekends.

The Willises plan to stay in Smith’s Ferry. Willis said he will continue to work for the Cascade Fire Department and that not managing the lodge will “free up a lot of time” to enjoy the region’s summer activities.

“Up here, summertime is the busy season, so we never get to go and do anything in the summertime,” Willis said. “So we’ll finally have a little bit of time to enjoy each other and where we live.”
