PENSACOLA, Fla. -- It's the end of an era for the local public broadcasting service. Effective July 1, Pensacola State College will end its affiliation with PBS.

PBS has been part of WSRE for decades and reaches more than one million viewers throughout the region.

Now the station is moving forward with a replacement.

"It's like you don't know what you have until it's gone," local mother Isabelle Wright said.

Isabelle Wright lives in a PBS household.

She and her kids tune in to the programming every week on WSRE.

Starting July 1, all their favorite shows are going away on the network.

"When you think about all of the other things that PBS, just as an entire organization, has done for communities across the country, it's heartbreaking," Wright said.

In 2025, President Donald Trump signed an executive order to defund all public broadcasting.

Pensacola State College lost $1.5 million in federal and state funding.

In September, trustee member Zack Smith commented on the costs.

"The current funding levels are unsustainable in many forms and fashions, and so that is why the board is being asked to re-evaluate what is best for WSRE, what is best for Pensacola State College, and what is best for us as a community," Smith said.

In April, we asked our viewers if federal funding should be restored for NPR and PBS.

51% of respondents said no.

Opinions were also split on our social media.

William commented, "I don't remember the part in the Constitution where the government has to pay for public radio."

Cody commented, "I absolutely support federal funding for both."

"By losing PBS, we're losing something else that helps keep people grounded and involved in their communities," Wright said.

The WSRE Foundation is in the middle of a lawsuit with PSC.

The foundation had $5.6 million in its account when PSC decided to end the affiliation in September.

Officials say that was enough money to keep PBS around for at least two more years without additional fundraising.

The college is blocking the foundation from accessing that money.

WSRE is replacing PBS with a new set of programming, including a feed from the Florida Channel and specials for America's 250th birthday.

PBS programming will still be available on streaming platforms.

Pensacola State College President Ed Meadows released a statement:

> Beginning July 1, 2026, a new programming schedule will be offered on WSRE-TV. A wide variety of programming, including educational, cultural, musical, and Pensacola State College content, along with some locally produced WSRE shows, will be airing on channel 23 starting July 1. The new lineup offers a slate of Fourth of July specials celebrating the nation's 250th anniversary. The programs include content from American Public Television, Telco Productions, The Florida Channel, WSRE-TV staff, and other sources.

For the full list of WSRE's new July programming, [click here](https://pensacolastate.edu/wsre).
