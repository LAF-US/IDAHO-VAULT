# OPINION: Idaho GOP’s Moon bets her future on Proposition 1 outcome

Commentary: Opinion of Steven L. Taggart

![Story image](https://public-assets-prod.pubgen.ai/brand_4fc3eb37-4c8a-4951-9324-365f6e4b53fd/asset_621c0423-d135-5438-a645-5957c741d069.jpg?w=3840)

Reelected [[Idaho]] State GOP Chairperson Dorothy Moon has laid down a marker. Her major goal this year is to defeat Proposition 1 in November. By doing so, she has linked her future to the measure.

Proposition 1 is the Reproductive Freedom and Privacy Act and is the only ballot initiative to make Idaho’s ballot this year. It specifies that every Idahoan has the right to reproductive freedom and privacy, including abortion, contraception and fertility treatments. If passed, at the point of fetal viability Idaho could regulate abortion except in cases of a so-called medical emergency.

In essence, the measure would overturn current Idaho’s abortion laws that were triggered by the U.S. Supreme Court’s Dobbs decision, which overturned Roe v. Wade. Idaho’s current laws are considered some of the strictest in the United States.

In her convention speech before being reelected Moon, committed to vigorously oppose and defeat Proposition 1. She claimed credit for defeating what was then designated as Proposition 1 in 2024, the open primary/ranked choice measure, that lost 70% to 30%.

Moon faces significant obstacles to match 2024’s result.

Boise State University’s annual Idaho Public Policy Survey released in January found that 61% of Idaho’s backed Proposition 1 while only 28% opposed. Word on the street is that more current polling reflects that there is roughly a 30-point lead for the measure.

Another challenge for Moon is financial. The Idaho Republican Party’s financial report shows only $121,000 raised this year with $210,000 total on hand. That war chest also has to cover party expenses, not just run a campaign against Proposition 1.

In contrast, the leading Proposition 1 advocacy group, Idahoans United for Women and Children, has raised just over $1 million this year and has $1.1 million on hand. In addition, in other state battles tied to abortion, the pro side has put millions of outside money into their state campaigns.

The Idaho GOP has put up a webpage on Proposition 1, which provides some of the arguments that Moon thinks will be persuasive with Idaho voters:

It asserts the measure will allow abortion through all nine months as long as approved by a physician.

It argues that passage would create “sexual libertinism” with no age limit.

Advertisement

It attacks that “medical emergency” depends on the doctor’s good faith, rather than an independent medical board’s review or a second opinion.

Part of the struggle for the opposition is the existing law.

Many Idaho health care providers have sounded the alarm, with particular emphasis on criminal penalties (up to five years in prison) that some believe might be imposed on medical personnel. There is data that shows that the current law has spooked Idaho doctors. Of Idaho’s 268 obstetricians, 114 left the Gem State after Dobbs through 2024, with only 20 new ones starting practice in Idaho. If Idaho’s doctors and nurses publicly back Proposition 1, that will provide a boon to supporters.

Also, Idaho’s law is considered by some as too tough on its exceptions, in particular those for pregnancies caused by rape and incest and where the line is drawn as to issues where the mother is herself at risk. Presumably proponents will highlight some of those areas.

Overall, Moon has put her credibility on the line. If Proposition 1 passes by a solid margin, Moon will be blamed. If she pulls off a defeat, she will receive some credit.

*Taggart is an attorney in Idaho Falls and has worked in Republican politics since his teens, both in campaigns and for elected officials, including running a congressional office.*
