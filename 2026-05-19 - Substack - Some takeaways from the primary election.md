I have a few thoughts following last night’s conservative massacre. Some are productive. Some are not.

It’s easy to look around and blame others for the losses sustained by conservatives last night. We can complain about dishonest ads. We can imagine elections were rigged. We can slam out-of-state money.

None of that helps retake the ground in 2028.

It’s time for a quick, honest evaluation of the conservative infrastructure to see where we lack. We can only control what we control, and it’s my judgment that what we control is severely lacking.

Here are my early takeaways from election night:

**We should have expected this:** One election does not a trend make. In 2024, conservatives ambushed the establishment and largely captured the Magic Valley. We all celebrated the Magic Valley Miracle. But, miracles are rare and ours ended last night.

Put simply, the conservatives took the establishment’s toy in 2024, and the establishment wanted it back. What did we expect? The area has been a stronghold for moderate Republicans for decades. Did we really think we would break their grip in an election cycle or two? If so, we were absolutely foolish to think that.

**Conservatives were outspent:** We won’t know the full picture for a few weeks, but it’s quite likely that establishment forces outspent conservatives by a $1 million or more. In 2024, Sens. Glenneda Zuiderveld, Tammy Nichols, and Brian Lenney showed us that steep spending gaps can be overcome. But, sometimes they just can’t. The establishment got nasty and spent big, and it worked.

Conservatives need to find new money here in Idaho. We don’t have a huge donor base, and things get tough when conservatives return to the same donors over and over. Finding new resources should be a high priority for anyone seeking to fight back in 2028 and 2030.

**Conservatives were outplayed:** A number of groups started attacking conservative lawmakers last September and October. For whatever reason, the conservative infrastructure didn’t respond. That won’t work moving forward. Big government brings bigger, longer, and more expensive elections. That’s the new reality, and we ignore that at our peril. Gone are the days where a candidate can file in March, raise a few bucks, shake a few hands, and win it all. Candidates and the conservative organizations that support them need to wage all-out war starting the September before a primary election.

**Principled lawmakers get attacked:** Sometimes, the bad guy wins in politics. That’s the way it goes. Sen. Dan Foreman, the guy who shilled for the teachers union, won his primary. Five of the Gang of 8 members, those who regularly opposed massive government budgets, did not. It’s not just. It’s not fair. But that’s how it goes sometimes. Taking principled stands can cost you.

I worry, of course, about the message remaining lawmakers will take away from last night. My sense is that more of them will fall in line on bloated budgets to avoid losing their seats. And, thanks to Foreman’s victory, more lawmakers will feel empowered to betray their voters when needed.

Ugh.

**Candidates weren’t ready:** I am told that some candidates didn’t have solid spending plans, nor did they have vendors ready to assist with campaign tasks. Many had no marketing plans until after the close of the 2026 legislative session.

That’s unacceptable. If you’re going to govern the state, you need to properly govern your campaign. Part of this, of course, speaks to the lack of conservative talent in the state. But still, candidates should have their vendors, budgets, and plans set almost immediately after jumping in. Dragging feet on the logistical stuff means less time in front of voters in the critical final weeks.

This is a wake up call for anyone and everyone who cares about turning Idaho into a conservative stronghold that actually does conservative things. We need to use this moment to get better. The stakes are only growing, and we are unprepared for what’s coming. I’ll write soon about the massive political shuffling that’s about to take place. I hope you’ll read that so you’ll understand why I’m so worried about last night’s results.

Change is coming and we have got to be better.
