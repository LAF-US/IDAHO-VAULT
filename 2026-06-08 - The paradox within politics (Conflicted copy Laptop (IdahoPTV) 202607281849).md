Before publishing his attack on me, I received an email from Larry LaRocco, who extended me the courtesy of letting me know he planned to publish an opinion piece about my race. Traditional political strategy would tell me to ignore the distraction and keep pushing forward. However, I think that the time for politics as usual has passed.

I have been involved in politics for several years. I learn something new every day. The paradox within politics has become very clear to me through this year’s campaign.

Establishment elites in [[Idaho]] say it’s important to recruit candidates who will run multiple times, build their own infrastructure, move the needle, and keep pushing until they succeed. They are not wrong; it is critical. As candidates, we’re told that we need to be willing to put it all on the line—more than once. We’re told to sacrifice our families, finances, and friendships. We’re told that if we are not willing to commit to running multiple times, resources would be better spent elsewhere. Everyone points to Rep. Steve Berch, who won his seat on his fifth attempt, as the epitome of what Idaho Democrats should be willing to do as candidates. However, in my case, elites like Mr. LaRocco and others are using my multiple runs as “proof” that I should give up. The establishment, rather than providing meaningful support, is happy to discard a candidate who no longer serves its purpose. We cast out our own and wonder why so many candidates run once, then never run again, and disengage altogether.

As rank-and-file Democrats, we call foul when we feel that those same elites are making the choices and trying to overrule the will of the voters in the democratic process. Look at the arguments still swirling over the last presidential election. Democrats are still angry that we had a nominee who didn’t go through a primary process. Look at what happened in 2016 when it came to light that the establishment had tipped the Presidential Primary in favor of their chosen candidate. We don’t understand how or why that would happen, but we fail to acknowledge that we allow that to happen.

Older Democratic operatives spin their wheels and wring their hands because we can’t figure out how to get young people to engage and care, especially with the Democratic Party. However, we refuse to listen to what they care about and are constantly telling them to wait their turn. We won’t acknowledge the challenges they face or offer any solutions. We seek compromise in everything, pander to an elusive “moderate center,” advance no meaningful policy solutions, and constantly place the burden on the young and the vulnerable to get on board.

We say we want fighters. We want people who will stick to their principles and values and stand up for us no matter what. Then, we blame the fighters for not fighting hard enough, while at the same time celebrating those who gave in on their values as “pragmatic politicians.”

We say that money in politics is the problem, not the solution. Yet, we use the amount of money a candidate raises as the primary barometer for success.

We exist in an endless cycle of disappointment, unable to understand why doing the same things always yields the same results.

When Richard Stallings first asked me to run for the U.S. Senate in 2022, we did not agree on everything. We had multiple discussions. In the end, he endorsed me for that race and my subsequent House race as well. When he told me he would support me, I asked him why, given all the things we did not agree on. He told me that the world of politics is full of politicians who are willing to abandon their beliefs in exchange for political advantage. He knew that I was in a position where I needed his support. He respected that, and even though I risked his support, I stood by what I believed to be true. Richard Stallings told me that he believed that in a time when people have lost faith in their leaders, we need people who will fight for their values—and for ordinary people. Incidentally, of our three living former Democratic congressmen at the time, he was the only one who sided with me over the Ada County moderate who challenged me in that primary.

I did not think that I was going to win that primary. We campaigned on issues that Democrats at the time avoided. We talked about immigration reform and DACA, we talked about abortion, we talked about LGBTQ rights, we took a position on assault rifles, and we talked about the challenges that everyday people (at least half the families in Idaho are just barely getting by) face. People told me that my focus on progressive issues would cost me the primary. It did not. I won by a large margin. In a race where I was outspent considerably by a candidate endorsed by establishment leaders such as Larry LaRocco.

If you think about it, the things that we were championing, the things that were too progressive, are actually common talking points for the protests and actions we are seeing today. And they are priorities that young people ask us to address.

In the general election, people told me once again to tone it down and try to appeal to moderates. I didn’t, and in fact, when HOAs started banning pride flags, we printed rainbow yard signs and handed them out because they were exempt from HOA rules. On election night, we didn’t win, but we did get the highest percentage any Democrat has ever gotten against Crapo.

The same held true for 2024. My campaign received the highest number of votes that any Democrat has ever received in Congressional District 2. We have achieved these milestones with less money, fewer resources, and fewer volunteers. People are looking for change. Together, we moved the needle. The same “practical” politicos who insist that we can’t take bold action on behalf of the people, and who demand that we accept incremental progress, are now using it as a bludgeon against me.

We just finished a primary, one with some of the largest Democratic turnout in years. There were three candidates in my race, all of whom I believe would have run this race until November. If Larry LaRocco or Betty Richardson wanted another Nebraska, they should have done what Nebraska did and run a candidate in the primary with a platform of stepping out of the way in favor of the independent. They certainly could have fielded a campaign against me; they have nothing positive to say about how I campaign. They chose not to because they knew they would lose and that it would definitively prove that their independent experiment was doomed to fail. At no point during the primary, or even leading up to the filing deadline, did I indicate a willingness to step aside. To be fair, I was often criticized for spending time criticizing Larry and Betty’s chosen candidate. On primary night? Almost as many people voted for me as voted in total in the 2022 primary.

After that strong showing, now Larry and Betty, as party “elites,” feel they have the right to override the will of the voters and choose which candidates should appear on the ballot. That type of backroom dealing has happened time and time again to cut out progressive choices across the country. The only difference is that the backroom of today is a Facebook group where Betty Richardson carefully curates activity, filtering out anything that does not support her worldview.

So, let’s talk about Todd. Those calling on me to support Todd must understand why I fundamentally disagree with him. There are the obvious reasons: his votes.

Todd was appointed to the legislature in 2024, after being recruited from the ranks of the moderate GOP. Between his two partial terms, he served roughly 20 weeks in active session before he quit. During his 20 weeks, Todd had multiple questionable actions and votes, but four really stand out:

- **HB1352, 2024, a law allowing mental health providers to deny services to the LGBTQ community.** Todd claims his vote for this law was an error and that he tried to change his vote. That may be true, but legislators have a duty to make sure that they get it right.
- **HB521, 2024, a tax cut bill primarily benefiting the wealthy.** Todd voted in favor of his first major tax cut, contributing to an ongoing trend resulting in an annual loss of revenue to the state of nearly $1 billion.
- **HB40, 2025, another tax cut bill primarily benefiting the wealthy.** During his 20 weeks in active session, Todd voted for tax cuts at the expense of critical programs 100% of the time.
- **SB1046, 2025, forcing a discredited fetal development curriculum in public schools.** This bill was loudly opposed by Planned Parenthood and other healthcare experts, but Todd voted for it anyway.

These four votes in 20 weeks may not seem like a big deal, but they demonstrate a pattern of a politician who says one thing and does another. Todd regularly claims that he is fighting the oligarchy and opposes tax cuts for the wealthy. However, his voting record shows that during his time in the legislature, he voted for those bills 100% of the time. Todd claims to want to help small businesses by reducing credit card fees, while also claiming that businesses should be primarily responsible for the cost of their employees’ health insurance. This is a person who can’t pick a party, let alone a position. His entire platform is nothing more than arguing that he will try to maintain the status quo.

Let’s talk about Risch. We all know that Risch has done nothing of substance for Idaho. He is a rubber stamp for Trump. The most focused he has been on Idaho during his time in the Senate involved trying to force the FAA to change flight patterns so planes wouldn’t fly over his house.

As for legislation, Risch does not seem to want to be bothered with developing solutions to the problems facing Idahoans. In fact, the link on his website to talk about healthcare goes to… well, nowhere, which pretty much sums up his plan to take on one of the largest financial burdens facing Americans today. He has no plan. He will likely fall in line with Trump, supporting an end to healthcare subsidies in favor of a $2,000 direct payment, which, when it comes to healthcare, will cover little more than the cost of a discount cremation.

On public lands, Risch has a failing record, regardless of how much he claims to value them. He has rubber-stamped the nominations of Trump officials who have indicated they have no interest in protecting public lands. He has supported legislation that would reduce protections for public lands across the board. Just another example of a politician who says one thing but votes differently.

Risch has failed to stand up for veterans when he voted against the PACT Act, which provides critical medical care for wounded veterans. He has also failed to exercise any oversight authority for the foreign wars that the administration has brought upon us, not only raising prices for consumers but endangering the lives of American military members with no clear need or objective.

Risch failed to stand up for farmers when he did not oppose the administration’s tariff overreach, which permanently cost many farmers and ranchers their livelihoods. In addition to the tariff and trade policy, Risch has avoided addressing critical immigration reform, which would ensure that farmers and ranchers have access to the labor they need. Instead, he has supported an immigration policy of terror, which has caused even documented workers to stay home.

We know that nearly half of Americans are not meeting their basic expenses. It is no different in Idaho. It has been shown that the chief factors driving this are Healthcare, Housing, and Childcare. Risch does not see fit to include any of these issues in his “priorities”.

We know that the majority of Americans are struggling right now. Risch will continue to stay the course if he is reelected. Todd will not be much better if he is elected, since his entire campaign revolves around staying in the middle and appealing to everyone’s sensibilities, while actually benefiting no one.

People are looking for someone who will fight for them, who will make a promise and stick to it, not simply try to spin an excuse after the fact. We have a long string of disappointments. House and Senate Democrats held the line on a shutdown for ACA subsidies only to give up and walk away with nothing. A Pennsylvania senator who suddenly changed his ideology after being elected. Governors in New Jersey, Colorado, and Virginia decided that the fight they promised to wage was simply not worth it.

Our campaign has always held to the core that our job is to make people’s lives better. That’s why we support Medicare for All, the only actual plan that will take on skyrocketing healthcare costs, improve access to care, protect rural healthcare, reduce poverty, and eliminate about 60-70% of personal bankruptcies in this country. It is a plan that every other major country in the world has adopted, and we have been kicking the can down the road on it since Truman was president. The time is now. Neither Todd Achilles nor Jim Risch has any interest in making it happen, however. Todd Achilles may talk a big game that sounds somewhat progressive, but his platform eschews those values in favor of upholding the status quo and excusing his past votes with platitudes.

We support immigration reform that recognizes the critical role immigrants play in building our economy. We absolutely want a path to citizenship for DACA individuals. These individuals have been contributing members of our society for decades, but have been living in limbo since the temporary DACA program was put into place over a decade ago. These people have built lives here; they have gone to school, followed the legal requirements, paid taxes, and built families here. We need to end the ridiculous immigration crackdowns that are sweeping our country, instilling fear even among those properly documented, destroying communities and families, and yes, even ending American lives.

We will fight for a tax system that stops handing out huge benefits to the wealthy and corporations while asking for nothing in return. There is a cost to living in and doing business in America, and everyone must contribute their fair share. Our current tax system incentivizes consolidation and transfer of wealth from our communities to individuals who seem immune to the consequences of their actions. Since Reagan first introduced his idea of trickle-down economics, nothing has trickled down, but wealth has certainly passed from the working class to the wealthy. This has to stop.

I have spent years working to bring more affordable housing options to my community and believe that home ownership is critical to a strong middle class. I believe that our expansion of housing cooperatives is a real solution for opening opportunities for home ownership that will be much more effective than longer mortgages and less regulation.

We need congressional reform. An end to Citizens United and the removal of dark money influence from politics. Our leaders should not be able to profit from their positions through insider trading. I support term limits and increasing the number of House members to improve representation and to follow the spirit of the design that House members should be the closest federal elected leaders to their constituents. This would reduce the ability to gerrymander, which should also be prohibited. It would also make it more realistic for House members to represent their districts. It seems crazy that even when we added two additional states, Hawaii and Alaska, we did not add more representatives. The number has been capped for nearly 100 years. The population has grown significantly since then.

I support term limits for the Supreme Court and believe that they should be subject to a binding code of ethics. No one in this country should be above the law, even those who rule on it.

I believe that public lands should remain in public hands. Legislation over the last few decades has made the PILT program less effective and has essentially created an artificial funding problem, leading to the idea that the only way to solve it is to sell the land.

I do not support foreign wars unless there is a clear and present danger to the United States. I believe that our military and defenses should be strong, but we do not have to put American military personnel in danger when the threat is not real.

I do not support the continued funding of the genocide in Gaza. Our tax dollars could be better spent building schools and hospitals here rather than blowing them up on the other side of the world.

I support the rights of the LGBTQ+ community. I am a member of that community and believe that the words in the Declaration of Independence, the document that started it all, still ring true. We all have the right to life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. There is no greater measure of happiness than the right to love yourself and choose whom you love.

I support the right of individuals to choose when it comes to reproductive health. The rights to an abortion, which were previously protected by Roe v. Wade, must be restored.

I have always been an advocate for organized labor. I support the PRO Act, and now, with the changes in our workforce due to AI and automation, I think the role organized labor will play in protecting workers’ rights is even more critical.

To me, these positions seem like common sense. I never actually saw myself as progressive or on the left until people continually pointed it out. I believe these are the directions we need to take if we are really going to address the issues Americans face every day.

One thing that we often hear is that we wish our politicians were more like us. We wish that the people in power would know what it is like to struggle down here on the ground. We wonder why so few of our leaders have shared our experiences. The short answer? It’s because we don’t vote/elect them. The system is set up to make it nearly impossible for anyone other than a wealthy older person to get elected or even campaign. It is extremely expensive. It is time-consuming. It is nearly impossible to work while campaigning. For the majority of us who are just trying to figure out how to pay today what we couldn’t afford to pay yesterday, the thought of making it here seems impossible. Very few everyday working people will ever receive a major party nomination to the US Senate.

The values I espouse and that people call “progressive” are actually held by a majority of Americans, especially younger Americans. When these policies are presented straightforwardly, without being labeled “Democratic” or “progressive,” they are very popular. Even so, few truly progressive candidates receive a federal nomination, because we’re told we’re unelectable and we must wait our turn as more “moderate” candidates make incremental progress. Even fewer candidates who are single parents or members of the LGBTQ+ community receive a nomination to federal office. Yet, I have achieved it three times. Representation matters. And people who represent regular folks are few and far between in campaigns and even less common in the halls of power.

I am not in this race because of ego. In fact, if I were concerned with ego, I would not be running as a Democrat in a state like Idaho. Have you read some of the comments that I receive? I have. They are not building me up. I am in this race because I am tired of sitting at my table trying to figure out how to manage the payments I am still making to the hospital for the portion of a necessary surgery that my insurance did not cover four years ago. I am in this race because I see my mother, who worked multiple jobs for 40 years, is still facing a retirement shortfall. If Social Security is cut, she will be unable to afford to live. I am in this race because I am constantly trying to juggle the numbers to figure out how to pay for the school activities my sons have that were free when I went to school. I am in this race because I see everything getting more expensive with no end in sight. I am in this race because I think that if you work full-time, you should be able to afford to live. Most of all, I am in this race because I do not feel that I am the only one facing these challenges, and I am tired of waiting for someone to offer a solution.

Today’s establishment Dems tell us to “vote blue no matter who,” but backtrack on that as soon as they have a progressive candidate who actually wants to get something done to move our country forward. They pretend that just getting out one useless and terrible legislator like Jim Risch will magically solve our problems. They act like a “moderate” who will likely vote more in line with the GOP than with Democrats is a solution to all our ills. People like Larry LaRocco want us to concede in advance. Progressives and others to the left of center are constantly told to wait until things get better before trying something bold. We are told that the first priority is to get rid of the incumbent, and that sometime in the future we can start to make some progress. That is what we have been doing in Idaho for 40 years. Supporting the moderates, those that we think are acceptable to voters of the other party, so that we can finally stabilize the status quo and then move forward. But what has that achieved in the past 40 years? The GOP is no longer recognizable as a conservative party, and an extremist agenda holds sway. We literally had sitting Democratic legislators knocking on doors for moderate Republicans in this past primary. And for what? What are we going to change by doing that? I can’t think of a better way to tell potential Democratic voters to give up and not bother than for us to start campaigning for Republicans who, when you look at “moderate” voting trends, capitulate to the Idaho Freedom Foundation within three sessions of getting elected. We have become so concerned with making ourselves attractive to Republican voters that we have completely ignored those who would actually choose to vote for us in the first place. Larry LaRocco and Betty Richardson are two people who helped craft this strategy. And now that they can see the absolute failure of their leadership, as made perfectly clear by our ever-dwindling footprint in the legislature and Democratic voter registration in an absolute freefall, their new solution is to just pretend there is no Democratic party and push for an “independent.”

Before you say I am putting party over people, understand this. We live in a two-party system. If we do not expend at least some effort in maintaining a political base and infrastructure, there is no opposition party. Many would argue that we are already past that point. Larry and Betty would have you believe that the only path forward is every man for themselves. The time that command is most often heard is just before the ship sinks. There is no recovery from that command. That is truly the point where there is no coming back. All hope is gone.

I am staying in this race because I believe that if we want to win an election, we have to stop trying to convince the other side’s voters that we aren’t that bad and instead look to the ever-growing crowd of people who have simply disengaged because they truly believe that nothing is going to change and that no one is even trying. We should be talking to them. We have not lost Democratic voters because the other side offered a better option; we have failed to gain voters because we ignored them and took them for granted. We haven’t looked at the non-voters and the disengaged and asked what might excite them. Instead, we return to the same “strategy” that has failed us for decades: remaking ourselves in the image we *think* the “center” wants to see.

By remaining in this race, I offer every progressive and left behind voter in this state someone to vote for. Maybe it won’t be enough. I can guarantee that we will never know if we don’t keep pushing.

For those of you who have said, “Take one for the team.” Let me be perfectly clear. If you believe pragmatism is more important than progress, if you are willing to allow your neighbors to continue to suffer under the burden of a system designed to crush them simply to maintain your own comfort, then we are not on the same team. This is best evidenced by LaRocco’s reply to a supporter who questioned his support for Todd. Larry’s statement, “Todd and I agree on most issues important to me and my family.” I am not sure if you know this, Larry, but you are a wealthy, retired, straight, white guy. Of course, you will be fine with Todd. To be honest, your family is probably doing just fine under Risch, too. If we were all in your situation, you are right, it probably would not matter. We are not all in your situation. What about us?
