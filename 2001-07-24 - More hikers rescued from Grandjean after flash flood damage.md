BOISE COUNTY, Idaho — Crews continued rescue operations Friday in the Grandjean area after flash flooding at the Wapiti burn scar prompted rescues and stranded hikers, officials said.

Boise County Sheriff Scott Turner told KTVB that search and rescue teams picked up another 12 people from the Sawtooth Lodge and transported them to Boise after they hiked out without a vehicle. Officials said there were additional people in the area, but all of them were able to get rides out.

Sheriff Turner previously said at least 14 people were removed from the area Thursday evening, including one man who had to hang onto a tree to stay safe as floodwaters and debris swept through the area.

Photos posted by the sheriff’s office showed vehicles buried under mud, logs and large rocks, and a camper trailer was also swept away in the mudslide. No injuries have been reported.

With assistance from Sawtooth Lodge, deputies said they continue monitoring for anyone else who may still be in the area.

The U.S. Forest Service has closed the Grandjean campground and trailhead in the Sawtooth National Forest for the foreseeable future because of debris flows. State Highway 21 remains closed from Grandjean to Banner Summit because of the mudslide, though Sawtooth Lodge remains accessible and is being used as a turnaround point for vehicles.
