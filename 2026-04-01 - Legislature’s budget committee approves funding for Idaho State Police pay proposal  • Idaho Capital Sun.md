## Legislature’s budget committee approves funding for Idaho State Police pay proposal

Legislators support the pay plan but questioned the process of shifting money around to pay for the raises

![close up of an Idaho State Police trooper's uniform patch](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/IMG_2450-2048x1365.jpg)

close up of an Idaho State Police trooper's uniform patch

The Idaho Legislature’s budget committee voted to move money around Wednesday to pay for a new $12.6 million pay plan for Idaho State Police commissioned officers. (Photo courtesy of the Idaho Governor's Office)

***EDITOR’S NOTE: This article was corrected April 3 to reflect funding for the pay plan is $6.5 million. Idaho State Police had originally requested $12.6 million from the Law Enforcement Project Choice Fund to implement the pay plan.***

The Idaho Legislature’s budget committee voted to move money around Wednesday to pay for a new $6.5 million pay plan for Idaho State Police commissioned officers.

While the Idaho Legislature’s Joint Finance-Appropriations Committee, or JFAC, voted 20-0 to approve a so-called trailer bill to fund the Idaho State Police pay increases, legislators from both major political parties complained that the specific way the JFAC would fund the pay increases is unsustainable and irresponsible.

The budget is considered a trailer bill because it follows behind other bills this year, including [House Bill 793](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/H0793/), [House Bill 967](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/H0967/) and [Senate Bill 1325](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/S1325/).

For several years, legislators have said ISP has difficulty recruiting and retaining officers because of low pay.

To pay for the Idaho State Police pay plan, the proposal redirects money from the beer excise tax, liquor account distributions and proceeds generated from sales of a new state license plate legislators approved earlier this year.

![Sen. Janie Ward-Engelking, D-Boise, speaks during a Joint Finance-Appropriations Committee meeting](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/011326JfacPSutphin029-2048x1388.jpg)

Sen. Janie Ward-Engelking, D-Boise, speaks during a Joint Finance-Appropriations Committee meeting on Jan. 13, 2026, at the State Capitol Building in Boise. (Photo by Pat Sutphin for the Idaho Capital Sun)

“It feels a little bit like we are doing a bake sale here to fund our state police, and I am hoping that going forward we have a better plan,” said Sen. Janie Ward-Engelking, D-Boise. “This is public safety; our public counts on these people. I would just say I hope that we find a better way to ensure that they have ongoing funding, and that we are not looking for pennies under the mattress.”

Senate Minority Leader Melissa Wintrow, D-Boise, expressed similar concerns.

“I probably will vote for this budget but am concerned that we are piecemealing the state police together,” Wintrow said. “I don’t think it’s a good practice to rob Peter to pay Paul here. Because we are impacting other accounts that we are going to have to come back and backfill anyway. We need a stable, predictable way to fund the police.”

Some of the money that is being redirected to the Idaho State Police pay plan would have instead gone to cities and counties.

![State Rep. Elaine Price, R-Coeur d'Alene](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/031626LegislaturePsutphin224-2048x1365.jpg)

State Rep. Elaine Price, R-Coeur d’Alene, asks a question during the March 16, 2026, Idaho House Local Government Committee meeting at the State Capitol Building in Boise. (Photo by Pat Sutphin for the Idaho Capital Sun)

“I will also vote for this because 967 passed the House floor so it’s the will of the body of the House, but this is a tax shift from our cities and our counties to the state police and my city and county are going to feel the impacts of this decision because we are a large resort city that gets a lot of taxes from liquor,” Rep. Elaine Price, R-Coeur d’Alene, said. “This is going to impact my community. It’s going to affect our property taxes because the city and the county are going to have to come after and replace these monies.”

The funding for the Idaho State Police pay plan still needs to be sent to the full Idaho House of Representatives and Idaho Senate for consideration. The proposal will be drafted into a trailer bill, assigned a bill number and publicly posted to [the Idaho Legislature’s website](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/) once it is read across the desk on the floor of the Idaho House or Idaho Senate. Routine policy bills are typically read across the desk and posted within about a day of being introduced by a committee. However, budget bills often take a little longer to post.
