# City Council to mull two versions of budget

## Mayor Dan Johnson’s budget passed first reading; council OKs staff to get 2nd version

Lewiston’s elected officials will have two options to consider for the city’s 2027 fiscal year budget.

One will be the $117.6 million budget written by Lewiston Mayor Dan Johnson. It contains, among other things, a 2.5% cost of living increase for city employees and would use $25.9 million in property tax revenue, $653,940 more than this year.

That budget passed its first reading at the Monday meeting of the Lewiston City Council and will go through two more readings before elected officials reach a final decision.

At the same time, the council voted to have city staff write another version of the budget that would limit the property tax increase to the $203,950 of revenue from new construction and annexation.

The idea was proposed by Councilor John Spickelmire, who said he disagreed with the amount that property taxes were raised for this year’s budget and what is proposed in the mayor’s budget for the upcoming year.

“We need to work harder,” Spickelmire said.

Councilor Matthew Wright agreed.

“It’s important when we’re raising the taxes, to raise it with the growth, if we do need to raise taxes,” Wright said. “That’s sustainable in my mind. I can see some situations where some event takes place that’s very unexpected, where you may need to raise taxes, but I haven’t seen that event.”

The votes on the proposed budget for the fiscal year that begins Oct. 1 followed a lengthy discussion of the budget with citizens raising a number of questions.

Johnson spent about 15 minutes listing ways the city has saved money such as using money from seizures in criminal cases to cover expenses, reducing training costs and designing a new ladder truck hundreds of thousands of dollars below the original estimate.

The police department used $524,000 in money from accounts belonging to an illegal massage parlor business to buy a new armored rescue vehicle and purchased new rifles.

Advertisement

The discussion included a number of questions from Lewiston residents, including one who wondered about the city’s investment in Bryden Canyon Golf Course.

The city is spending more on the course than it’s generating in revenue but the revenues are increasing each year.

Plus the golf course is encumbered by obligations going back to the 1970s when the city used state money for it that requires it to be used for recreation, said City Attorney Jennifer Tengono.

If the city divested the property, it would face a “hefty bill,” she said.

The budget will be before the city council again on Aug. 17 and Aug. 24.
