BOISE, Idaho — A popular highway in Idaho was partially shut down four weeks ago due [to a landslide](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/governor-little-secures-federal-aid-for-idaho-businesses-impacted-by-landslide-sba-ioem/277-62d36ab4-14f8-4140-a817-302daefcc6ac). Now, the Idaho Transportation Department (ITD) has opened U.S. Highway 95 for all travelers.

ITD has reopened Highway 95 between New Meadows and Council starting on Monday, April 28.

After heavy rainfall on March 16, part of the north-south highway sustained "significant damage," said a news release from ITD. Their crews have been working extended hours to accelerate the repair schedule. This expedited effort also included two shifts per day from ITD crews.

"US-95 is one of Idaho's most essential transportation routes, and from day one we knew we had to act swiftly and decisively to reopen in April," said Dan McElhinney, ITD Chief Deputy and Chief Operations Officer. "We deeply appreciate the public's patience and the continued collaboration among all city, county and local business partners throughout the closure."

Though the road is open, drivers should expect to see some crews in the area, setting up temporary lane closures if necessary.

"The expedited work by our engineers and contractor to reopen US-95 was focused on quality and community support," said Jason Brinkman, ITD District 3 Engineer.

The best way to get up-to-date information regarding road conditions and closures is to check [Idah511.gov](https://511.idaho.gov/#:Alerts).

[As previously reported by KTVB](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/governor-little-secures-federal-aid-for-idaho-businesses-impacted-by-landslide-sba-ioem/277-62d36ab4-14f8-4140-a817-302daefcc6ac), Idaho's Office for Emergency Management and the federal Small Business Administration (SBA) have issued an emergency declaration for businesses in the area, opening up an opportunity to apply for disaster relief loans from the SBA. Businesses in Ada, Adams, Boise, Canyon, Custer, Gem, Idaho, Lemhi, Payette, Valley and Washington counties – as well as the Oregon counties of Baker and Wallowa – are covered by this declaration.

Even if the business did not suffer any physical damage, it may be eligible for EIDLs, as EIDLs are for working capital needs caused by the landslide. The loan money may be used to pay fixed debts, payroll, accounts payable, and other bills unpaid due to this particular landslide.
