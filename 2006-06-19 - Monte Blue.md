**Gerard Montgomery Blue** ([né](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Bluefeather; January 11, 1887 – February 18, 1963) was an American film actor who began his career as a romantic lead in the [silent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film "Silent film") era; and for decades after the advent of sound, he continued to perform as a supporting player in a wide range of motion pictures.[^1]

## Early life

**Gerard Montgomery Bluefeather** [^2] was born in Indianapolis, Indiana to an Irish mother, Orphalena Lousetta Springer, while his father William Jackson Blue was believed to be half French and part [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_descent "Cherokee descent") and [Osage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osage_Nation "Osage Nation").[^3] He had three brothers; Charles Bertram, Leroy, and William Morris. His father was a [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") veteran, and served as a scout for [Buffalo Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bill "Buffalo Bill").

When his father died in a railroad collision, his mother could not rear four children alone, so Blue and one of his brothers were admitted to the [Indiana Soldiers' and Sailors' Children's Home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Soldiers'_and_Sailors'_Children's_Home "Indiana Soldiers' and Sailors' Children's Home"). He eventually worked his way through Purdue University in West Lafayette, Indiana.

Blue grew to a height of 6 feet 3 inches (1.91 m). He played football and worked as a fireman, [boilermaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_worker "Railroad worker"), coal miner, [cowpuncher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowpuncher "Cowpuncher"), ranch hand, circus rider, lumberjack, and day laborer at the studios of [D.W. Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.W._Griffith "D.W. Griffith").

## Career

Blue had no theatrical experience when he came to the screen. His first movie was *[The Birth of a Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birth_of_a_Nation "The Birth of a Nation")* (1915), in which he was a stuntman and an extra. Next, he played another small part in *[Intolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intolerance_\(film\) "Intolerance (film)")* (1916). He also was a stuntman or stand-in for [Sir Herbert Beerbohm Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Beerbohm_Tree "Herbert Beerbohm Tree") during the making of *[Macbeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macbeth_\(1916_film\) "Macbeth (1916 film)")* (1916). Gradually moving to supporting roles for both D.W. Griffith and [Cecil B. DeMille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_B._DeMille "Cecil B. DeMille"), Blue earned his breakthrough role as Danton in *[Orphans of the Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orphans_of_the_Storm "Orphans of the Storm")*, starring sisters [Lillian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillian_Gish "Lillian Gish") and [Dorothy Gish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Gish "Dorothy Gish"). Then, he rose to stardom as a rugged romantic lead along with top leading actresses such as [Clara Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Bow "Clara Bow"), [Gloria Swanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Swanson "Gloria Swanson"), and [Norma Shearer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norma_Shearer "Norma Shearer"). He most often acted with [Marie Prevost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Prevost "Marie Prevost"), with whom he made several films in the mid-1920s at [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros.") Blue portrayed the alcoholic doctor who finds paradise in [MGM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM "MGM") 's *[White Shadows in the South Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Shadows_in_the_South_Seas "White Shadows in the South Seas")* (1928). Blue became one of the few silent stars to survive the sound revolution; however, he lost his investments in the [stock market crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_crash_of_1929 "Stock market crash of 1929").

He rebuilt his career as a character actor, working until his retirement from films in 1954, and he played character roles in various television series until 1960, mostly [Westerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_\(genre\) "Western (genre)"), such as *[Annie Oakley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Oakley_\(TV_series\) "Annie Oakley (TV series)")*. From the mid-1930s, he was a contract player at [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros."), working in character parts and as an extra.[^4]

One of his more memorable roles was as the sheriff in *[Key Largo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Largo_\(film\) "Key Largo (film)")* opposite [Lionel Barrymore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Barrymore "Lionel Barrymore").

For his contributions to the motion pictures industry, Monte Blue received a [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_actors_with_Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame_motion_picture_stars "List of actors with Hollywood Walk of Fame motion picture stars") on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame") at 6290 Hollywood Boulevard on February 8, 1960.[^5] [^6]

## Personal life

Blue divorced his first wife in 1923 and married Tova Jansen the following year. He had two children, Barbara Ann and Richard Monte. During the later part of his life, Blue was an active [Mason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masonic_lodge "Masonic lodge") and served as the advance man for the Hamid-Morton Shrine Circus. In 1963, while on business in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, he died after suffering a heart attack attributed to complications from influenza.[^7] He is interred next to his mother-in-law, actress [Bodil Rosing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodil_Rosing "Bodil Rosing"), at [Forest Lawn Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Lawn_Memorial_Park_\(Glendale\) "Forest Lawn Memorial Park (Glendale)") in Glendale, California.[^8] [^9]
