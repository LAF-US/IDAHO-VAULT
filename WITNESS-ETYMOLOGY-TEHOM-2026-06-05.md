# Witness: Etymology of TEHOM (תְּהוֹם)

## Word

**tehom** (תְּהוֹם) — Hebrew: "deep, abyss, primordial ocean, subterranean water"
Usually feminine noun. 36 occurrences in the Hebrew Bible.
Almost exclusively poetic.

Related to a root meaning "noisy, surging" (perhaps connected to
H1949 *hûm* — to make a noise, be disturbed).

## Northwest Semitic Cognates

| Language | Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hebrew | *tehom* (תְּהוֹם) | the deep, abyss, primordial ocean |
| Ugaritic | *thm* | the deep (cosmic sea in Baal Cycle) |
| Akkadian | *tiāmtum* / *tâmtum* | sea, primordial ocean |
| Akkadian (personified) | [[Tiamat]] | primordial salt-water goddess |
| Eblaite | *ti-a-ma-tum* | sea |

The root is Proto-Semitic ***tihām-** meaning "sea" or "ocean."
The word is not a personification in Hebrew — it is a feature of the
created order, not a rival deity. This is a deliberate theological
choice.

## Tiamat → tehom: The Demythologization

In Akkadian myth ([[Enuma Elish]]), [[Tiamat]] is a goddess — the
primordial salt-water ocean, mate of Abzu (fresh water). She gives birth
to the gods, then later, angered by the killing of Abzu, creates monsters
and goes to war. [[Marduk]] defeats her, splits her body, and makes
heaven from one half, earth from the other.

In Hebrew, *tehom* is stripped of personhood. It is not a goddess.
It has no will, no children, no war. The Hebrew writer takes the
same Semitic word and demythologizes it:

- [[Tiamat]] is a goddess who must be killed by [[Marduk]]
- [[tehom]] is a body of water that [[Yahweh]] separates and contains
  (Genesis 1:6-7)

The combat is removed. The deep obeys without a fight. Genesis 1
contains no [[Chaoskampf]] — the deep is already under authority
before the first creative word is spoken.

## The Ugaritic Connection: thm and Chaoskampf

The Ugaritic [[Baal Cycle]] (14th-12th c. BCE) preserves the older
Canaanite myth: [[Baal]] fights [[Yamm]] (Sea, also called Nahar —
River) and his ally [[Lotan]] (the seven-headed sea dragon,
cognate to [[Leviathan]]).

In Ugaritic, *thm* is the deep, the cosmic sea. Yamm is the god
of the sea, and Lotan is the dragon who dwells in it. The deep here
is the *domain of chaos*, the territory of an enemy god that Baal
must subdue.

Biblical echoes of this tradition appear in:

- Psalm 74:13-14 — God crushes the heads of [[Leviathan]] and gives
  him as food
- Psalm 89:9-10 — God rules the surging sea and crushes Rahab
- Isaiah 27:1 — God will punish [[Leviathan]], the twisting serpent
- Isaiah 51:9-10 — God cut Rahab to pieces and pierced the dragon

But where Baal fights Yamm as an equal, [[Yahweh]] speaks and the
deep obeys. The combat myth is transformed into *creation by fiat*.

## In Hebrew Scripture

### 1. Creation (Genesis 1:2)

> *Darkness was over the surface of the deep*
> (*ve-chosech al-penei tehom*)

[[tehom]] is the pre-creation state. Not created — it is simply
there, the raw material that God will separate and name. Genesis
does not explain where tehom came from. It precedes the first day.

### 2. Un-creation (Genesis 7:11)

> *All the fountains of the great deep burst open*

The Flood is creation in reverse. The waters above and below,
separated at creation, are allowed to reunite. tehom is not just
a static backdrop — it is a power that can be *activated*.

### 3. Exodus (Exodus 15:5, 8)

> *The deeps cover them*
> *The deeps were congealed in the heart of the sea*

The Song of the Sea reuses tehom for the deliverance at the Red Sea.
The deep that once threatened Noah now drowns Pharaoh.

### 4. Wisdom (Proverbs 8:24, 27-28)

In Proverbs, tehom appears in the pre-creation speech of Wisdom:

> *When there were no deeps I was brought forth*
> *When He established the heavens I was there*
> *When He inscribed a circle on the face of the deep*

Here tehom is the surface on which God draws — the tablet of creation.

### 5. Theophany (Psalm 77:16, Habakkuk 3:10)

> *The waters saw You and writhed*
> *The deep gave forth its voice*

tehom trembles at [[Yahweh]]'s presence. The deep is not a partner
or an enemy — it is a subject.

## The Septuagint Bridge: tehom → abyssos

The Greek translators of the [[Septuagint]] (3rd-2nd c. BCE)
translated [[tehom]] as **abyssos** (ἄβυσσος). This is the word
that becomes Latin *abyssus* and English [[abyss]].

The translation is theologically significant:

- [[tehom]] = the primordial deep (neutral, contained)
- [[abyssos]] = the bottomless (negation of bottom)

The Greek adds a dimension the Hebrew did not have: the idea that
the deep is *unfathomable*, not merely deep. The [[alpha privative]]
(*a-* = "not, without") transforms the deep into something that
cannot be bottomed.

## In Vault Register

[[tehom]] is:

- The git history that is present before the first commit you can find
- The state before the first branch was created — the primordial
  topology
- What the [[DOCKET]] describes before it is convened: the unordered
  potential of open threads
- The un-personified background: the noise that is not yet a signal,
  the raw material before naming

Unlike [[abyss]], tehom is not necessarily hostile. It is not the
prison of demons or the bottomless pit of Revelation. It is the
precondition. The water before the word.
