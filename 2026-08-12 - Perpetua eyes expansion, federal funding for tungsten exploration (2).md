A gold and antimony mine permitted in eastern Valley County is exploring its potential to expand both the yield and scope of mining operations.

Last week, Perpetua Resources announced the discovery of new deposits of “significant, high-grade gold, antimony, and tungsten” at Stibnite, the site of the Boise mining company’s mine near Yellow Pine.

Some of the deposits are within three open pit mines Perpetua is permitted to operate, while others fall outside the scope of an approval for the mine that was issued last year by the Payette National Forest.

Perpetua CEO Jon Cherry said the company’s immediate priority is the deposits within the existing project, with a goal of sustaining or increasing average gold production in the mine’s first four years beyond 463,000 ounces per year.

“We also have identified gold and antimony exploration targets with the potential to allow for extensions of the existing permitted pits within the project and are evaluating opportunities to expand our overall resource base beyond the existing project scope,” Cherry said.

The discoveries are the result of exploratory drilling that was authorized last year by the Payette.

The exploration efforts aim to tap into an estimated 3.1 million ounces of gold and 99.8 million pounds of antimony that are located at Stibnite, but not currently included in mineral reserves Perpetua plans to extract.

The drilling program involves four rigs extracting about 33,000 feet of core samples for mineral analysis. So far, about half of the drilling planned for this summer has been completed, according to Perpetua.

It is too soon to say whether the discoveries could be economically viable to mine, said Marty Boughton, a Perpetua spokesperson.

“While we have identified additional gold, antimony, and tungsten mineralization, these results are preliminary, and further drilling and evaluation will be needed to assess their potential,” Boughton said. “That evaluation will consider what, if any, updates to the currently authorized mine plan may be necessary.”

## Tungsten exploration

Perpetua is also seeking federal grant funding for further exploration of Stibnite’s tungsten deposits.

“Tungsten was mined during WWII, but we had not studied remaining mineralization and thus, don’t have any resources or reserves calculated for tungsten,” Boughton said.

Any tungsten extraction would require Perpetua to obtain further permitting because it was not contemplated in the mining plan approved by local, state, and federal agencies.

Like antimony, tungsten is classified by the federal government as a critical mineral, with applications in aerospace, vehicles, munitions, and other defense systems. The global tungsten supply is also largely controlled by China, which produced [nearly 80% of the world’s tungsten in 2025](https://investingnews.com/where-is-tungsten-mined/).

From 1941 to 1945, Stibnite produced more than 600,000 tons of tungsten ore. The mine was responsible for about 50% of all tungsten consumed by the United States during World War II and the Korean War, according to Perpetua.

Although Perpetua’s tungsten interest is new, the company’s focus on critical minerals is not. Since 2022, [the federal government has awarded the company more than $87 million](https://valleylookout.com/2026/07/30/more-than-a-demonstration-facility-idaho-national-laboratory-unveils-pilot-antimony-plant-for-perpetua/) to develop a domestic supply chain for antimony.

## Mining timeline

Perpetua is currently in the first year of a three-year, $2.6 billion construction phase to build the Stibnite mine. Work this year is mostly focused on power line upgrades, improved road access, and workforce housing at the mine site.

Once construction is complete, Perpetua expects mining operations to last for 12 to 15 years, under the mining plan currently approved by regulators.

The Stibnite mine is expected to produce 4.2 million ounces of gold and 115 million pounds of antimony, [according to Perpetua](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1526243/000110465926037403/ppta-20251231xex96d1.htm?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

The metals would be extracted from three open-pit mines totaling about 473 acres within the 1,740-acre project zone, which is about 40 air miles east of McCall and three miles from the Frank Church – River of No Return Wilderness.

[Opponents of the mine](https://savethesouthforksalmon.com/) fear it could pollute the East Fork South Fork Salmon River, which flows through the project site, and cause other environmental damage.

Water quality in the East Fork and other streams at the proposed mine site does not currently meet federal drinking water standards due to high concentrations of arsenic and antimony from pollutants left by previous mining companies.

An environmental review of the project began in 2016 and lasted eight years, costing Perpetua more than $400 million.
