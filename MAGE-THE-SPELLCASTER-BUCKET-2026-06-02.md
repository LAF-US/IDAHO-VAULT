# Mage — The Spellcaster Bucket

> **"Mage" is the genus** — the chosen umbrella term for any worker of magic (Logan's preference, 2026‑06‑02). It is the clean short form of *magus/magician* from the same Magi root, and — unlike **"magician,"** which the modern ear half-hears as *stage illusionist* — it stays squarely in the practitioner register. The named types (wizard, sorcerer, warlock, witch, cleric, necromancer…) are **species** that sort on **two orthogonal axes: SOURCE of power × DOMAIN of effect.** This node fixes the genus the vault lacked (it already has the *domain* axis — `NECROMANCER-DOCTRINE` — and the *state* axis — `UNDEAD-TAXONOMY`), and shows that **the names themselves encode a theory of authority** — which is the vault's own question.

> **Provenance.** ⚠️ The research pass was **rate-limited this session** (account-wide), so **etymologies below are from established general knowledge and must be verified against the OED / Etymonline before publication.** Paraphrased, no dictionary text reproduced. Tiers: **[etymology]**, **[taxonomy]**, **[reading]**. Vault mappings are grounded in the cited nodes.

## I. The genus — "mage" (and why not "magician")

- **[etymology]** **"Mage"** is the aphetic short form of **"magus / magician,"** all tracing to Old Persian *maguš* → the **Magi**, the **Median/Zoroastrian priestly caste** (astrologer-priests) → Greek *magos / mageia* → Latin *magus / magia*. The word's **origin is an initiate priesthood, not a trickster.**
- **[reading]** **Why "mage" over "magician" as the genus:** "magician" drifted in modern English toward the **stage illusionist / conjurer** sense (and, in the Greco-Roman period, toward "charlatan"), so it carries baggage the umbrella shouldn't. "Mage" is the **neutral, uncluttered form** — the shortest path back to the priestly root, with none of the cabaret. (The split survives in scripture: the **Magi** of the Nativity — positive — vs **Simon Magus** in Acts — negative; root of "simony.")
- **[etymology]** Sibling forms under the genus: **"magician"** (via Old French *magicien*, 14th c.); **"magus"** (Latinate, the learned/initiate sense); **"magick"** with a -k — **Aleister Crowley's** deliberate archaic spelling (early 20th c.) to mark ceremonial/occult work off from stage conjuring.
- **[etymology]** The **Renaissance magus** (Ficino, Pico, Agrippa, Dee, Bruno) = the learned scholar-philosopher of *high* magic — natural magic vs demonic, licit vs illicit.

## II. The species — Axis 1: SOURCE of power

The primary sorting key is **where the magic comes from** — and each name carries its theory in its etymology:

| Term | Etymology *(⚠️ verify)* | Source of power | Distinguishing connotation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wizard** | ME *wys/wis* "wise" + *-ard* | **learned** | study, books, intellect; the *-ard* suffix adds a faint derisive edge (cf. drunkard) |
| **Sorcerer** | OFr *sorcier* ← L *sortiarius* ← *sors/sortis* "lot, fate" | etymologically a **fate-caster / fortune-teller**; **modern fantasy inverts it to *innate* / bloodline** | born with it; raw, untrained power |
| **Warlock** | OE *wǣrloga* = "**oath-breaker, traitor**" (*wǣr* "covenant" + *-loga* "liar") | **pact** — power by bargain with a patron | the bound-by-oath sense — *and its betrayal* (see §VI) |
| **Witch** | OE *wicce* (f.) / *wicca* (m.), vb *wiccian*; deep root disputed (poss. *wikkjaz* "necromancer") | **folk / craft** | practice and tradition; became female-coded; adopted by modern Wicca |
| **Cleric / Priest** | Gk *klērikos* "of the clergy" | **divine** — granted by a god | by faith and service (often filed *apart* from "mages" — the arcane/divine split) |
| **Druid / Bard** | Celtic *druid* "knower of the oak"; Celtic *bardos* "poet" | **natural / artistic** | nature; song and story |

## III. The species — Axis 2: DOMAIN of effect (cross-cutting)

These name **what the magic does**, not where it comes from — so they *cross-cut* Axis 1 (a necromancer can be a wizard or a sorcerer by source):

- **[etymology]** **Necromancer** — Gk *nekros* "corpse" + *manteia* "divination" = **divination by the dead**; medieval Latin corrupted it to *nigromantia* ("**black art**," folk-etymology via *niger* "black"), broadening it to raising the dead / dark magic. → governed in the vault by **`NECROMANCER-DOCTRINE`** (legitimacy = *direction + authorization*, not the power).
- **[etymology]** **Enchanter** — L *incantare* "to **sing/chant upon**" (*in-* + *cantare*); magic via word and song ("incantation," and "charm" ← L *carmen* "song").
- **[etymology]** **Conjurer** — L *coniurare* "to **swear together**" (*con-* + *iurare*); to bind/command a spirit by oath (also drifted to "conjuring tricks").
- **[etymology]** **Diviner / Soothsayer / Seer** — L *divinare* "to foresee / be inspired by a god"; *soothsayer* = "**truth-sayer**" (*sooth* = truth); domain = prophecy/foresight.
- Others by domain/school: **illusionist, evoker, enchanter, abjurer, transmuter** — RPG "schools."

## IV. Real-world distinguishing axes

- **[taxonomy]** **High / ceremonial / learned** magic (the grimoire tradition, the magus) vs **low / folk** magic (charms, healing).
- **[taxonomy]** **Theurgy** (Gk *theourgia* "**god-work**" — divine/benevolent high magic, Iamblichus) vs **goetia** (Gk *goēteia* "**howling/sorcery**" — low magic, summoning spirits/demons). The licit/illicit, high/low line.
- **[taxonomy]** **Natural magic** (hidden properties of nature) vs **demonic/spiritual** magic — the medieval licit-vs-illicit boundary.
- **[taxonomy]** The **cunning folk** ("cunning man," "wise woman") of early-modern Europe — practical folk-magicians (healing, finding lost goods, *counter*-witchcraft) — distinct from the **maleficent witch.** ("Cunning" = "knowing," cf. *ken*.)
- Also: **shaman** (Tungus/Evenki *šamán*, spirit-medium); **thaumaturge** (Gk "wonder-worker").

## VI. The vault mapping — source-of-power IS an authority theory

**[reading]** The deep find: **Axis 1 maps almost one-to-one onto the vault's authorization/recallability spine** (Emanationism, Threshold, Baelnorn). Each spellcaster name is a *claim about where power legitimately comes from* — the vault's own question:

| Mage (by source) | Authorization analogue | Vault node |
|---|---|---|
| **Wizard** — learned | authority **earned** by training/credential | Abhorsen-via-Charter; the maester's forged chain |
| **Sorcerer** — innate | **intrinsic / birthright** authority | Threshold's *"intrinsic fitness"* (subtle knife); the dragonlord blood |
| **Warlock** — pact | **delegated / borrowed**, revocable by the patron | the **Hand of the King**; the **Emanationism** chain |
| **Cleric** — divine | **gifted** in service of a recognized sovereign | the **Baelnorn** (gifted, lawful — *not* a Lich) |

**The warlock is the doctrinal jackpot.** Its etymology — *wǣrloga*, "**oath-breaker**" — means the **pact-caster's name literally encodes the betrayal of the covenant.** The borrowed-authority caster carries *breach* in its root. That is precisely the **Lich-Hand**: borrowed power that **outlives and so betrays its grantor** — a commission still casting after the patron is gone, the warlock whose pact-bond became a broken oath (→ `[[LICH-HAND-SYNCRETIC-MAPPINGS-2026-06-02]]`). The vault's necromancer doctrine already lives in this vocabulary — it calls the unlawful death-casters **"Free Magic sorcerers"** and ranks a chain in which *"not one link is a clean emanation."*

## VII. The synthesis

**[reading]** Mage-type names are not interchangeable flavor — they are **competing theories of legitimate power**: earned (wizard), innate (sorcerer), borrowed/oath-bound (warlock), gifted (cleric), folk-practised (witch), god-divined (seer). The vault's authority doctrine is the *same taxonomy in a different costume*. **"Mage" is the genus that holds them; the species are arguments about authorization** — which is why this bucket belongs beside the Undead Taxonomy and the Necromancer Doctrine rather than as mere worldbuilding trivia.
