One of the mysteries of the last 10 years is how a controversial New York City developer managed to convince Republican primary voters to vote for him in the 2016 primaries over more qualified and respectable Republican candidates. This conman was outed just a month before the November election for who he really was by a video of his remarks bragging about how he could grab and assault women and pay no price for it. That should have been a barn-burning end to his candidacy.

It’s those Republican primary voters who often attempted to seize the moral high ground on issues like abortion or prayer in schools who failed the test of their party’s moral standards when they turned their souls away from their righteousness and voted for the conman no matter what he said or did.

Political junkies can debate whether Hillary Clinton was the right Democratic candidate for the 2016 presidential election, but she had to overcome more than the baggage of her husband, Bill. FBI Director James Comey gave Trump considerable advantage when days before the November election, he publicly disclosed he had reopened the probe into Clinton’s email use as secretary of state. It’s hard to believe that didn’t play a key role in Trump’s victory in 2016 with months of news coverage about her use of a personal email server.

(It also seems quaint compared to today’s avalanche of corruption in the Trump family).

Then there was Putin who deserves an Oscar for his supporting role in Trump’s victory as he undermined Clinton and the Democratic Party with cyber-hacking and social media disinformation that came to Trump’s rescue.

Following the Jan. 6, 2021 attack on the Capitol and Trump’s role in encouraging it, Sen. Mitch McConnell blew an opportunity to convict Trump after the U.S. House of Representatives impeached him. That could have been a new day for America, devoid of the crude, corrupt and disgraceful behavior that has come to characterize Trump’s presidency.

Trump also benefited in the 2024 election when President Joe Biden ran for a second term when it was clear he was not up for the job. He eventually handed the nomination to Vice President Kamala Harris, in what turned out to be an unsuccessful campaign effort.

Here we are in Trump 2.0 with an increasingly unstable president who seems confounded over how to bring his own personal war against Iran to a close. But he has no problem turning his back on Ukraine, just recently reversing his decision to give Ukraine the license to build Patriot missiles. The Patriots that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy needs so desperately at this moment are Ukraine’s only hope of intercepting missiles that kill innocent civilians in Ukraine’s cities.

Trump lamented that he wants the war in Ukraine ended and says he’s “not looking for missiles” but peace. Not a word about the unprovoked invasion of Ukraine by Putin and the Russian missiles and drones that have killed hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians in their homes, hospitals, schools and on the streets. Instead, he treats Putin’s war against Ukraine as a feud between two nations with little or no acknowledgement of the war criminal Putin’s role.

Such refusal to aim responsibility at Putin and Putin alone suggests that those playboy days in Russia promoting and building his businesses apparently compromise his ability to help a democratic Ukraine defend itself against Putin’s ruthless missile and drone strikes. Are there photos or videos in Putin’s files that reveal the man who was Jeffrey Epstein pal and the man who lost a lawsuit to a woman who claimed he sexually assaulted her?

There’s another reason for Trump’s subservience to Putin. Trump likes power. Trump likes winners. Trump likes winners who seize the day like Putin did on February 22, 2022, when he took the offensive and invaded Ukraine. Putin sees some messianic vision of a reconstituted Soviet Union which he served as a KGB agent in East Germany during the Cold War. You don’t have to listen to Trump for long to pick up a similar maniacal claim that he is the greatest president in American history. Two peas in a pod.

What’s the point of rehashing all of this, going back to 2016? It’s a reminder of just how unpredictable politics can be, how the American presidency can be determined by accidents of history, unforeseen moments in our politics when quirks of fate make a huge difference as they did in 2016 and 2024.

Those hare-core MAGA types often get credit for Trump’s victory and that sure is the case in Republican primaries, but it is not the case in a general election for president. Moderate Republicans, independents, Democrats and even some disenchanted MAGA voters who now see the futility in a Trump presidency can make a difference in the 2026 midterms and certainly in 2028.

Even here in bright red Idaho, independent Todd Achilles is gaining ground on one of the most useless chairs of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee, a decided contrast to Idaho’s Senator Frank Church. It’s the perfect storm for non-performing Sena. Jim Risch that Achilles is successfully navigating an odyssey of his own this year, especially now that it’s a two-man race.

It’s a moment when we must recognize just how accidental Trump’s presidency is. Subtract just a few “one-offs” and the course of American politics might look nothing like it does today. This is no time to lose interest in the political process or, worse yet, feel as though there is no return from Trump’s degradation of American democracy. That is the case in Russia where Andrei Kolesnikov in his book, “The Closing of the Russian Mind,” laments the “learned indifference” of the Russian people, who live under a repressive regime that offers no hope of removing Putin from office. Unlike Russia, we still have an electoral process that can work in 2026 and 2028. Consider Trump a detour from politics as usual rather than some preordained future over which we have no control.

As we approach the midterms and here in Idaho have an opportunity to send an independent to the United States Senate, this should be a moment of hope when Americans can make a difference and take back America. Unlike the repression Trump’s pal, Vladimir Putin, practices on his people, we can still take to the streets as we did with the No Kings protests. We can still clog the offices of our Senators and Congressmen with phone calls and emails objecting to their fealty to Trump. We can contribute to candidates of our choice and help register voters for the midterm elections.

Now is the time to take back America.

*Bob Kustra served as president of Boise State University from 2003 to 2018. He is host of Readers Corner on Boise State Public Radio, a regular columnist for the Idaho Statesman and a contributing columnist for the Chicago Tribune. He served two terms as Illinois lieutenant governor and 10 years as a state legislator.*
