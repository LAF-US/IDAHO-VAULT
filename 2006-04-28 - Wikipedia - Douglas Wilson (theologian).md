**Douglas James Wilson** (born 1953) is an American [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_right "Christian right") [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism "Calvinism") and [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism "Evangelicalism") Christian [theologian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theology "Theology") and [pastor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastor "Pastor").[^1] Wilson leads [Christ Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church_\(Moscow,_Idaho\) "Christ Church (Moscow, Idaho)") in [Moscow, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho "Moscow, Idaho"), in which capacity he has taken leading roles in the [Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communion_of_Reformed_Evangelical_Churches "Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches") and the [classical Christian education movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_education_movement "Classical education movement").[^2] He is a public proponent of [postmillennialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmillennialism "Postmillennialism"), [Christian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism "Christian nationalism"), [covenant theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_theology "Covenant theology"), and [biblical patriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_patriarchy "Biblical patriarchy"). Wilson, a self-described ["paleo-Confederate"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Cause_of_the_Confederacy#From_the_twentieth_century_to_the_present "Lost Cause of the Confederacy"), first garnered extensive media coverage in 2004, when he held a conference which publicized his controversial stance on [American slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States "Slavery in the United States").[^3] [^4]

## Biography

Douglas Wilson was born in 1953. In 1958 his family moved to [Annapolis, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapolis,_Maryland "Annapolis, Maryland"), where he spent most of his childhood.[^5] His father, Jim Wilson, was a full-time [evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism "Evangelism") who worked with the [Officers' Christian Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officers'_Christian_Fellowship "Officers' Christian Fellowship"). His father had become a Christian in the [Naval Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Naval_Academy "United States Naval Academy") and worked in Christian literature ministry both in Annapolis and later in Idaho.[^6] His father moved to the [Moscow, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho "Moscow, Idaho"), area after retiring from the Navy to start a Christian bookstore on the [Washington State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University "Washington State University") campus.[^7]

Upon graduating high school, he enlisted into the submarine service, serving on the [USS *Tusk*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Tusk "USS Tusk") and the [USS *Ray*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Ray_\(SSN-653\) "USS Ray (SSN-653)").[^8] He graduated from the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho"),[^9] where he met his wife to be, Nancy, whom he married in 1975.[^10]

In 1977, Wilson began preaching at an [Evangelical Free Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Free_Church_of_America "Evangelical Free Church of America") [plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_planting "Church planting") in [Pullman, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman,_Washington "Pullman, Washington"), where he espoused a [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists "Baptists") theological framework inspired by the charismatic [Jesus People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesus_movement "Jesus movement") movement. He would eventually move the congregation to nearby Moscow, where it became [Christ Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church_\(Moscow,_Idaho\) "Christ Church (Moscow, Idaho)").[^9] In 1988, the controversy surrounding [openness theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_theism "Open theism") inspired Wilson to take a more conservative, Reformed stance. In 1993, he began [baptizing infants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_baptism "Infant baptism") and espousing a [presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_polity "Presbyterian polity") model of church governance. In 1998, he began relationships between Christ Church and two other nearby congregations in Washington, marking the establishment of the [Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communion_of_Reformed_Evangelical_Churches "Communion of Reformed Evangelical Churches") (CREC).

Parallel to his pastoral roles, Wilson has established a number of [classical Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Christian_education "Classical Christian education") educational institutions connected to Christ Church. [Logos Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logos_School "Logos School"), one of the first schools associated with the classical Christian education movement, was founded in 1981. He also played a role in establishing [New Saint Andrews College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Saint_Andrews_College "New Saint Andrews College"), a liberal arts college, and Greyfriars Hall, a ministry training program; Wilson teaches at both institutions.[^11] [^12] [^13] He also established [Canon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Press "Canon Press"), a Christian publishing house, and cofounded the Reformed cultural and theological journal *[Credenda/Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credenda/Agenda "Credenda/Agenda")*.[^14] [^4]

Wilson came to national prominence after organizing a conference at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") in early 2004, for which the promotional material included Wilson's tract *Southern Slavery as it Was.* Wilson's views on slavery caused significant controversy (detailed below) and increased his prominence as an [apologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_apologetics "Christian apologetics"). In 2009, he was featured in the documentary film *[Collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collision_\(2009_film\) "Collision (2009 film)"),* which documented his debates with [New Atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Atheism "New Atheism") author [Christopher Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hitchens "Christopher Hitchens") on his promotional tour for the book *Is Christianity Good for the World?*

Wilson has authored books on theology and culture, as well as children's and poetry books. He blogs and is a frequent guest at conferences and on podcasts.[^15]

Wilson again attracted attention in October 2020 for leading a maskless worship service at Moscow City Hall, in the midst of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19 "COVID-19"). In the 2020s, especially after the second election of Donald Trump, Wilson has been a subject of frequent press coverage as one of the self-professed leading figures of the Christian nationalist movement. He has been received sympathetically by prominent conservatives including [Tucker Carlson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Carlson "Tucker Carlson") and [Pete Hegseth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Hegseth "Pete Hegseth"), the latter of whom has attended a DC plant of Christ Church.[^7] [^16]

In February 2026, Wilson was featured as a guest pastor for [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon "The Pentagon") 's newly-instituted monthly Christian worship service, where he prayed alongside Hegseth.[^17] The Pentagon defended Wilson's appearance.[^18]

## Classical Christian education

Wilson has been an advocate for [classical Christian education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Christian_education "Classical Christian education"), laying out his vision in several books and pamphlets, including *Recovering the Lost Tools of Learning* and *The Case for Classical Christian Education*. He has also critiqued the American public school system by urging Christian parents to seek other educational options in *Excused Absence: Should Christian Kids Leave Public Schools?*. He argues that American [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(government_funded\) "Public school (government funded)") are failing to educate students and proposes a Christian approach to education based on the medieval [trivium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivium_\(education\) "Trivium (education)"), a philosophy of education with origins in classical antiquity that emphasizes grammar, logic, and rhetoric and advocates wide exposure to the [liberal arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_arts "Liberal arts"), including classical [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_culture "Western culture") languages such as [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin") and [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language "Greek language"). The model has been adopted by a number of Christian [private schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_school "Private school").[^19] and [homeschoolers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling "Homeschooling").[^20] Wilson founded the [Logos School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logos_School "Logos School") in Moscow in 1981 to fulfill his vision.[^21] He helped found the [Association of Classical Christian Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Classical_Christian_Schools "Association of Classical Christian Schools") (ACCS) in 1993.[^22] [^23] Their website lists 532 member schools (as of 2024). [^24]

Wilson received the inaugural Boniface Award from the ACCS in 2019. The award is given to recognize "a public figure who has stood faithfully for Christian truth, beauty, and goodness with grace." [^25]

## Theology

Wilson has written on numerous theological subjects and produced several biblical commentaries. He advocates [Van Tillian presuppositional apologetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presuppositional_apologetics#Van_Tillian_presuppositionalism "Presuppositional apologetics").[^26] He has written extensively in defense of [covenant theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_theology "Covenant theology"), [infant baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_baptism "Infant baptism"), and [Calvinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_theology "Reformed theology") in works such as *The Covenant Household*, *Knowledge, Foreknowledge, and the Gospel*, and *To a Thousand Generations: Infant Baptism*.

Wilson has engaged in extensive critique and debate with prominent [New Atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Atheists "New Atheists"). In May 2007, Wilson debated [Christopher Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hitchens "Christopher Hitchens") in a six-part series published first in *[Christianity Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_Today "Christianity Today")*,[^27] and subsequently as a book entitled *Is Christianity Good for the World?* with a foreword by [Jonah Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonah_Goldberg "Jonah Goldberg"). His book *Letter from a Christian Citizen* was Wilson's response to atheist [Sam Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Harris_\(author\) "Sam Harris (author)") 's *[Letter to a Christian Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letter_to_a_Christian_Nation "Letter to a Christian Nation")*, and his book *The Deluded Atheist* was his response to [Richard Dawkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dawkins "Richard Dawkins") 's *[The God Delusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_God_Delusion "The God Delusion")*.

Wilson holds to a view of [Christian eschatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_eschatology "Christian eschatology") known as [postmillennialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmillennialism "Postmillennialism").[^28] He has set forth his position in *Heaven Misplaced: Christ's Kingdom on Earth*, in his commentary on Revelation, *When the Man Comes Around*, and his commentary on [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Thessalonians "First Thessalonians") and [Second Thessalonians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Thessalonians "Second Thessalonians"), *Mines of Difficulty*. He has spoken and written in defense of the view, participating in a dialogue about eschatology with other evangelical ministers [John Piper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Piper_\(theologian\) "John Piper (theologian)"), [Sam Storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Storms "Sam Storms"), and Jim Hamilton as the representative of the postmillennial position.[^29]

Wilson's views on [covenant theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_theology "Covenant theology") have caused controversy as part of the [Federal Vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Vision "Federal Vision") theology, partly because of its perceived similarity to the [New Perspective on Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Perspective_on_Paul "New Perspective on Paul"), which Wilson does not fully endorse, though he has praised some of its tenets.[^30] The [Reformed Presbyterian Church in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformed_Presbyterian_Church_in_the_United_States "Reformed Presbyterian Church in the United States") 's Covenant Presbytery declared his views on the subject to have "the effect of destroying the Reformed Faith" and found his teachings to be heretical.[^31] [^32]

Wilson is an adherent of [biblical patriarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biblical_patriarchy "Biblical patriarchy"). He believes that wives should submit to their husbands and that leadership roles in the church should be restricted to men. In his 1999 book, *Federal Husband*, Wilson argued that a husband as "federal head" assumes responsibility for his wife's spiritual condition. Wilson would like to see the [Nineteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Nineteenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") which gave women [^33] the right to vote repealed,[^16] though he is not calling for its immediate repeal. Instead, he would prefer [household voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household_voting "Household voting").[^34] Wilson has praised the [1969 Stonewall raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_riots "Stonewall riots"), and called for restoring [sodomy laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodomy_laws_in_the_United_States "Sodomy laws in the United States"), though not "aggressively enforced".[^34]

In a 2025 CNN interview, Wilson said he has embraced the term [Christian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_nationalism_in_the_United_States "Christian nationalism in the United States") because "it's better than the other names he gets called", stating, "I'm not a [White nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism "White nationalism"). I'm not a [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_\(insult\) "Fascist (insult)"). I'm not a [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism "Racism"). I'm not a [misogynist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misogynist "Misogynist"), and those are the names that usually get thrown at me... and then when someone says, well, that's Christian nationalism, I can — well, I can work with that." [^16] Wilson calls the United States a " [backslidden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backsliding "Backsliding") [Christian republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_republic "Christian republic")." [^35]

Wilson's views have received attention from European media. In September 2025, he was depicted on the cover of *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel "Der Spiegel")* as one of the " [archconservative Bible fanatics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_extremism "Christian extremism") " (*Erzkonservative Bibelfanatiker*) surrounding [Donald Trump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Trump "Donald Trump").[^36] In February 2026, *[Wysokie Obcasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wysokie_Obcasy "Wysokie Obcasy")* stated that Wilson was "known for his radical views on the place of women in family life".[^37]

Wilson is a vocal opponent of [anti-semitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-semitism "Anti-semitism").[^38]

### Southern slavery

In his pamphlet *Southern Slavery, As It Was*, which he cowrote with Christian minister [J. Steven Wilkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Steven_Wilkins "J. Steven Wilkins"), Wilkins wrote that "slavery produced in the South a genuine affection between the races that we believe we can say has never existed in any nation before [the War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") or since." [^39] [Louis Markos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Markos "Louis Markos") notes that "though the pamphlet condemned racism and said the practice of Southern slavery was unbiblical, critics were troubled that it argued U.S. slavery was more benign than is usually presented in history texts." [^40] Some historians, such as [Peter H. Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_H._Wood "Peter H. Wood"), [Clayborne Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clayborne_Carson "Clayborne Carson"), and [Ira Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Berlin "Ira Berlin"), condemned the pamphlet's arguments, with Wood calling them "as spurious as [Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial "Holocaust denial") ".[^41]

Wilson held a conference at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") in 2004 for those who supported his ideas. The university published a disclaimer distancing itself from the event, and numerous anti-conference protests took place. Wilson described critical attacks as " [abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionist "Abolitionist") propaganda".[^41] He also has repeatedly denied any racist leanings. He has said his "long war" is not on behalf of [white supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_supremacy "White supremacy"); rather, Wilson claims to seek restoration of a prior era, during which he says faith and reason seemed at one and when family, church, and community were more powerful than the state.[^3]

The [Southern Poverty Law Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Poverty_Law_Center "Southern Poverty Law Center") connects Wilson's views to the [neo-Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confederate "Neo-Confederate") and [Christian Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Reconstruction "Christian Reconstruction") movements influenced by [R. J. Rushdoony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R._J._Rushdoony "R. J. Rushdoony"), concluding, "Wilson's theology is in most ways indistinguishable from basic tenets of \[Christian\] Reconstruction." [^42] Although he is categorized by some as a "neo-Confederate",[^43] he rejects that term and calls himself a "paleo-confederate" instead.[^44] Wilson describes his politics as "slightly to the right" of Confederate general [J.E.B. Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._E._B._Stuart "J. E. B. Stuart") on his blog.[^1] [^18]

[Canon Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_Press "Canon Press") ceased publication of *Southern Slavery, As It Was* when it became aware of serious citation errors in 24 passages authored by Wilkins, wherein quotations, some of them lengthy, from the 1974 book *Time on the Cross: The Economics of American Negro Slavery* by [Robert William Fogel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_William_Fogel "Robert William Fogel") and [Stanley L. Engerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_L._Engerman "Stanley L. Engerman") were not cited.[^45] Robert McKenzie, the history professor who first noticed the citation problems, described the authors as being "sloppy" rather than "malevolent", while also pointing out that he had reached out to Wilson several years earlier. According to the [Southern Poverty Law Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Poverty_Law_Center "Southern Poverty Law Center"), "He described the lifted passages as simply reflecting a citation problem, and attributed the latest uproar to 'some of our local [Banshees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banshee "Banshee") \[who\] have got wind of all this and raised the cry of plagiarism (between intermittent sobs of outrage).'" [^46] Wilson reworked and redacted the arguments and published (without Wilkins) a new set of essays under the name *Black & Tan* [^47] after consulting with historian [Eugene Genovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Genovese "Eugene Genovese").[^48]

Concerns about Wilson's personal safety due to his comments on slavery, as well as criticisms from both liberals and conservatives, led to the Visão Nacional para a Consciência Cristã's rescinding his invitation to speak at a large Reformed theological gathering in Brazil in February 2024.[^49]

In a 2025 interview with [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN"), Wilson stated "Slavery was overseen and conducted by fallen human beings, and there were horrendous abuses and there were also people who owned slaves who were decent human beings and didn't mistreat them. I think that system of chattel slavery was an unbiblical system, and I’m grateful it's gone." [^16]

## Personal life

Wilson and his wife, Nancy, married on New Year's Eve in 1975 and have three children and many grandchildren.[^10] His son [Nathan Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._D._Wilson "N. D. Wilson") is a writer of [young adult literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_adult_literature "Young adult literature").[^50]

In 2018, Wilson announced on his blog that he had been diagnosed with a cancerous tumor in his [jaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_cancer "Oral cancer").[^51] He wrote in response to the news:

> Scripture teaches us that we are to give thanks in everything (1 Thess. 5:18), and for everything (Eph. 5:20). God really is sovereign in every detail of every life. So we have thanked the Lord for this cancer, and we intend to continue to thank Him for it. We don't know what good purpose God has for it, but we are assured that the One who counts both hairs and sparrows is also the One who controls the behavior of every cancer cell.

Later that year, Wilson had a successful operation removing the tumor, followed by a successful recovery.[^52]
