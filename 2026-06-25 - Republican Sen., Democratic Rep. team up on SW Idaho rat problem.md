A pair of Idaho legislators is urging local governments to direct resources to address a rapidly spreading pest.

Idaho Sen. Tammy Nichols and Rep. John Gannon authored a letter, dated June 24, addressed to local officials calling on them to take swift action to curb the spread of invasive rats in the Treasure Valley.

“This is a serious public health, safety, and property concern, and it will require cooperation from state and local governments to help control and eliminate the problem before it gets further out of hand,” the letter said.

The rats are believed to have arrived in 2024 as stowaways in shipping containers, [as BoiseDev reported.](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/02/10/rat-nuisance-bill/) During this year’s legislative session, legislators considered several bills to provide local governments with the authority to mobilize against the issue. One bill, [Senate 1271](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/S1271/), failed by just 6 votes in the Idaho House, with lawmakers citing concerns that the bill would direct the Idaho State Department of Agriculture to take measures against the spread of rats without providing any additional funding for such efforts, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/03/27/rats-local-gannon/). ISDA directs Idaho’s invasive species program, which tackles issues such as the spread of quagga mussels in the Snake River.

## Cities, counties, irrigation districts, state departments

Nichols and Gannon’s letter said that even though the legislature has yet to declare rats an invasive species or require ISDA to take action, “that does not mean local governments and public agencies are without responsibility.”

It noted an array of entities that manage areas that could become prime rat habitats, including cities that manage land along the Boise River, irrigation districts that oversee canals and waterways, and drainage systems operated by highway districts.

Gannon told BoiseDev via email that the letter has been sent to the Star, the City of Garden City, the City of Boise, the City of Meridian, the Ada County Board of Commissioners, the Idaho Transportation Department, and the Idaho Department of Parks and Recreation. He said they are attempting to send the letter to irrigation districts, though he said they can be “more difficult to contact.”

“We understand that local governments are often concerned about taking on new financial burdens, and that is not the goal of this request,” the letter said. “The goal is to stay ahead of the problem, remain in control of the rat population, and prevent a much larger and more expensive crisis later. A coordinated, proactive response now may help avoid greater costs for local governments, taxpayers, and property owners down the road.”

## A joint website, new legislation

Gannon told BoiseDev via email that he would like to see the entities take action as soon as possible.

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

Watkins Carter - 2:1

“All need to police their properties, and there needs to be rat control information on a joint website,” Gannon said. “ … While local authority is limited, they certainly have authority over their own properties.”

The letter asks local governments “to consider designating an employee to spend a few hours each week helping develop and coordinate a local response plan. That plan could include public education, identifying problem areas, coordinating with other jurisdictions, and taking appropriate action to reduce rat habitats and infestations.” Larger governments or agencies may need to direct additional time and resources depending on the size of the problem in their area, it said.

Gannon said urgent action is required because the rats “multiply as we speak!”

![Twilight Crit 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/7a9153dd-9b20-425c-8428-1affff0ec02d.png)

The letter emphasized the importance of local governments taking action together.

“This is not the time for finger-pointing or saying, ‘it’s not in our jurisdiction,’” the letter said. “Idahoans want solutions, not blame. If we act now and work together, we have a much better chance of keeping this problem from spreading further and becoming even more costly to address.”

The state still needs legislation on the issue as well, Gannon said. He said that he is looking into what funds are spent on controlling quagga mussels and Mormon crickets. Mormon crickets are not invasive, but ISDA does have a control program for them.

## How can property owners slow the spread of rats?

The two types of rats in Idaho are the Norway rat and the roof rat, according to a post from the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare included with the letter.

Rats can spread diseases such as plague, hemorrhagic fever, and flea-borne typhus, the post said.

It also lists tips to prevent the spread of rats:

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

Watkins Carter - 2:1

- Keep garbage in tightly sealed containers and avoid leaving pet food or bird seed outside
- Clean up fallen fruit and other food sources in your yard
- Trim trees and vegetation away from roofs and structures
- Seal cracks, gaps, and and openings around doors, windows, vents, and crawl spaces
- Cover vents and rooftop plumbing vent pipes more than 2 inches in diameter with wire mesh

If you do discover evidence of rats in your home or on your property, the agency recommends installing traps and bait stations where children and pets can’t reach them, and potentially calling a professional exterminator. It also recommends following [the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s protocol for cleaning up rat droppings](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-pets/rodent-control/clean-up.html).

For more information, visit the [Idaho Department of Health and Welfare’s website](https://healthandwelfare.idaho.gov/dhw-voice/rats-more-just-nuisance-they-can-pose-risks-your-health) or the [City of Boise’s rodent prevention resources page](https://www.cityofboise.org/residents/rodent-prevention/).
