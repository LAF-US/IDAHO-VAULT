# The Wall and the Watch

> The realm's northern edge, and the sworn brotherhood that mans it — a 700-foot ward of ice and a dwindling order guarding against a threat the rest of Westeros has filed under nursery tale. One of a trilogy with `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-Beyond-the-Wall-2026-06-02]]` and `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Others-and-the-Long-Night-2026-06-02]]`.
>
> **Provenance.** Canon from this session's geography/institutions research (search-extracted A Wiki of Ice and Fire) + established lore. Tiers flagged: **[canon]**, **[legend]** (in-world myth), **[theory]**, **[show]**, **[reading]**. ⚠️ Verify specifics before publication; the Night's Watch oath is paraphrased, not reproduced.

## The Wall

- **[canon]** A colossal barrier of **ice ~700 feet tall and ~300 miles (100 leagues) long**, spanning Westeros from the Bay of Seals in the east to the Gorge in the west — the realm's northern boundary.
- **[legend]** Raised some **~8,000 years ago**, after the **Long Night**, by **Brandon the Builder** — said to have had the help of **giants and the children of the forest**, with **spells woven into the ice.** **[theory]** The recurring implication is that the Wall is a **magical ward** the Others cannot cross — its power as much sorcery as engineering.
- **[canon]** The Watch built **19 castles** along it but never manned more than 17 at once. At the saga's start only **three are garrisoned**: **Castle Black** (~600 men, the headquarters, with its great ice-elevator), the **Shadow Tower** (~200, the western end), and **Eastwatch-by-the-Sea** (the eastern end, on the coast). A handful of gates tunnel through the ice; most castle gates are sealed with ice and stone.
- **[canon/legend]** The **Nightfort** — the oldest, largest, now-abandoned castle — holds the **Black Gate**, an ancient weirwood door, and is the setting of the realm's darkest Wall-legend (below).

## The Night's Watch

- **[canon]** A **sworn brotherhood** that guards the Wall and the realms of men against threats from the north. The vow is **for life**: a brother renounces **wife, lands, children, and any crown or claim**, and takes **no part in the wars and politics of the realm** ("the Watch takes no side"). The oath casts the brother as *the sword in the darkness* and *the shield that guards the realms of men.* (Paraphrased — the full vow is in the books.)
- **[canon]** Three orders, each under its own officer, all beneath the **Lord Commander** (elected for life by the brothers):
  - **Rangers** (the **First Ranger**) — patrol and fight beyond the Wall; the most dangerous duty.
  - **Builders** (the **First Builder**) — maintain the Wall, castles, and roads.
  - **Stewards** (the **Lord Steward**) — supply, cook, tend, and keep the order running (and, like Sam, keep its records).
- **[canon]** Brothers wear all **black** ("black brothers," "crows" to the free folk); a **maester** serves the Watch (the blind **Aemon Targaryen** at Castle Black). To **"take the black"** is for life — and, increasingly, an alternative to a dungeon or the headsman: the Watch has decayed from an honored calling into a penal posting of **~hundreds of men, many of them criminals**, to hold a 300-mile wall.
- **[canon]** Notable: **Jeor Mormont** ("the Old Bear," Lord Commander), **Jon Snow**, **Maester Aemon**, **Qhorin Halfhand**, **Samwell Tarly**; **Mance Rayder** is a *former* brother who deserted to become King-Beyond-the-Wall (→ Beyond the Wall node).

## The Night's King *(legend)*

- **[legend]** The **thirteenth Lord Commander** is said to have taken a woman with cold blue eyes (an Other, or corpse-queen) as his bride, declared himself "the Night's King," and ruled the Nightfort in horror for thirteen years — sacrificing to the Others — until the King in the North (a Stark) and a King-Beyond-the-Wall joined to bring him down, and his very name was struck from all records. ⚠️ **[show]** Distinct from the show's "Night King" (a created leader of the Others) — the books' Night's King is a fallen *human* Lord Commander of legend, name erased.

## Theme — the watch the realm forgot

**[reading]** The Wall and the Watch are the saga's great irony: the **one institution dedicated to the only threat that can end the world**, left to rot while the kingdoms bleed each other over the Iron Throne. The realm starves it of men, mocks its warnings as **"grumkins and snarks,"** and posts its criminals there to die. It is the **forgotten shield** — and its decay is the exact cost of the curated record (→ the Maesters node, the Empty Office note): a civilization that filed its existential threat under legend also stopped paying to defend against it. The erased name of the Night's King and the erased memory of the Long Night are the same act — *power forgetting what it would rather not face.*
