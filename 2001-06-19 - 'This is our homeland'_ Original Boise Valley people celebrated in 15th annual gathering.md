BOISE, Idaho — Descendants of the original Boise Valley inhabitants gathered Saturday at Eagle Rock Park for the 15th annual Return of the Boise Valley People.

Organizers said five tribes from Idaho, Nevada and Oregon trace their ancestry to the Boise Valley. Representatives from across the area gathered, including people from the Shoshone-Bannock tribes, the Burns Paiute tribe, the Fort McDermitt Paiute-Shoshone tribe and the Confederated Tribes of Warm Springs. Those communities were forcibly removed from the area in the late 1800s following the discovery of gold and silver.

The event followed a ceremony Friday night at Boise City Hall, where the Boise City Council president welcomed tribal members with a formal proclamation. Mayor Lauren McLean was at a prior engagement, according to a news release.

'This is our homeland," Louise Dixey, cultural resource director for the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes, told KTVB at Friday's proclamation. "We'll continue to return here and educate people in many different ways."

The city has partnered with Indigenous tribes to develop informative signs throughout Boise so Idahoans can learn more about the Boise Valley people, Dixey said.

Saturday’s gathering included traditional foods, storytelling and a land back presentation at Eagle Rock Park. Eagle Rock is a spiritual gathering place for many people, according to the city of Boise.

Susan Avila Dixey of the Shoshone-Bannock Tribes said the event is about preserving history and identity.

“It’s really important to know where you come from,” Dixey said. “Growing up, I was always taught to learn about your ancestors and your background so no one can take that knowledge away from you.”

Organizers described the return as a spiritual homecoming for many descendants, noting that some ancestors are buried at Eagle Rock Park. They said the long-term goal is to build a cultural center in Boise to share the history of the area’s first inhabitants.

The Return of the Boise Valley People continues through Sunday, June 21 at Eagle Rock Park and Gowen Field.
