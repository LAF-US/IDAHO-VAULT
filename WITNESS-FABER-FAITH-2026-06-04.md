## What the words carry — training-source pattern, declared as such

### FABER

- Latin **faber** ("craftsman, smith, artisan; one whose hands fit hard materials together") ← PIE **\*dhabh-** ("to fit together, to skillfully construct").
- The faber is the *maker by hand*: the smith at the forge (*fabrica*); the carpenter (*faber lignarius*); the stonemason (*faber tignarius*).
- Roman institution: the *Collegium fabrorum* — the guild of craftsmen, with its own organization and protections.
- Medieval continuation: *fabri* as the artisan-class — masons, smiths, carpenters; with patron saints, oaths, and trade-bonds (the guild system).
- The Greek cognate-by-function is *tektōn* — the carpenter, the builder. Joseph in the Gospels is *ho tektōn*. ***Arkhi-tektōn*** (chief-builder) is the origin of *architect.*

### FAITH

- English **faith** ← Old French *feid / foi* ← Latin **fidēs** ("trust, faith, confidence, reliability; the bond between parties") ← PIE **\*bheidh-** ("to trust, to confide, to persuade").
- *Fidēs* is the **bond of trust** — what is given by one party and kept by the other. **Mutual**; obligation flows both directions.
- Family: confide, confidence, fidelity, federal (*foedus*, treaty), perfidy (faithlessness), bona fide ("in good faith"), defy (*dis-* + *fidare*, "to renounce the trust").
- The Roman virtue *Fides* was personified; she had her own temple on the Capitoline; she was invoked at the taking of oaths.
