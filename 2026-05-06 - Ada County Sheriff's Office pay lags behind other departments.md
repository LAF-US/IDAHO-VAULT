## Data: Ada County Sheriff’s Office pay lags behind nearby departments, peer cities

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/data-ada-county-sheriffs-office-pay-lags-behind-nearby-departments-peer-cities-Screenshot-2026-04-0.jpeg)

An Ada County Sheriff's Office vehicle. Courtesy of Ada County Sheriff's Office

Deputies at the Ada County Sheriff’s Office take home far smaller paychecks than other Treasure Valley police departments or similar cities in the Mountain West.

Data obtained by BoiseDev through the Idaho Public Records Act found ACSO employees’ take-home pay ranked behind four other Treasure Valley departments and two others across the Mountain West region, and was nearly tied with a third in the last fiscal year. When you factored in the more than 1,000 of ACSO’s employees working in court services, the Ada County Jail, the emergency dispatch center, administration, department executive leadership, and the patrol division, the median pay came in at $68,849 per year in fiscal year 2025. Employees on patrol alone took in an average of $96,946 during the last fiscal year.

This ranks behind the same metrics gathered for the police departments in Nampa, Meridian and Boise. Nampa’s median pay for the same period outpaced ACSO by roughly ten thousand, with roughly $107,000 for its nearly 200 employees. The larger departments of Meridian and Boise were nearly tied with roughly $113,000 and $116,000, respectively.

ACSO was even further behind departments in similar metro areas elsewhere in the Mountain West. Officers in the Reno Police Department had the highest take-home pay of the cities whose pay data BoiseDev reviewed during the last fiscal year, with a median of roughly $162,000. The Spokane Police Department also came out tens of thousands ahead of ACSO and other Treasure Valley Departments with a median take-home pay of roughly $145,000 during this period. The Salt Lake City Police Department had a median of roughly $92,000, which came in over the overall median for ACSO, but under the average pay for only patrol officers, instead of factoring in the lower compensation for administrative staff and 911 dispatchers.

BoiseDev’s analysis compared the gross pay each officer received during the 2025 fiscal year, instead of just their flat salary or hourly rate. This takes into account what officers made on overtime, extra pay bonuses for special assignments like K-9 units or SWAT teams, or if they were only paid for part of the year because they did not work for the department the entirety of the year-long period. BoiseDev also chose to mostly use the median pay of the departments due to the existence of outliers, like employees who worked only a few days in the fiscal year or top-paid department leadership.

Ada County Sheriff Matt Clifford doesn’t set the department’s budget or his employees’ salaries; the Ada County Commissioners do. Last year, Clifford asked for an additional 4.2% raise on top of what was also given to employees across the county, but [the commissioners opted to say no to the request](https://boisedev.com/2025/06/21/ada-2026-budget/) and allow him to add new positions instead. When asked to comment on the findings of this analysis, Clifford said the department isn’t currently struggling with high turnover, but waiting to address problems with pay until turnover levels rise would put ACSO “at an extreme disadvantage.”

He said the Ada County Human Resources Department is currently doing a market analysis comparing ACSO’s pay to other departments for the upcoming 2027 fiscal year, and he plans to keep pushing for competitive wages from the commissioners.

“We want our pay scale to stay competitive with neighboring agencies,” he wrote in a statement. “Competitive wages are essential to both retaining the employees we have and attracting prospective new hires. It’s important that we proactively compensate our employees in line with nearby agencies and the value of the work they provide.”

When asked about these findings, the Ada County Board of Commissioners said this style of analysis, examining take-home pay for officers instead of salaries, doesn’t take into account employees only working part of the year, officers being promoted or changing positions mid-year, or other complications of law enforcement compensation. County Chief Operating Officer Steve Rutherford said the board follows a “defined and detailed” process for raises at the county, including at ACSO, and also noted the ongoing HR study for the sheriff’s office compensation that is ongoing.

“Finally, the board has been thoughtful on the issue of employee compensation for the last several years, understanding the importance of balancing the burden on property taxpayers with the need to hire and retain quality employees,” Rutherford wrote. “Accordingly, the board closely monitors Ada County’s turnover data, as that metric is an important indicator of compensation issues. Again, Ada County uses a detailed process that provides the Board with comprehensive information, so they are able to balance business, budgetary, and other administrative needs of the county.”

## How does the county’s health plan stack up?

BoiseDev also requested the details of each of the department’s health plans to compare, in addition to our look at compensation. Our analysis of benefits offered shows ACSO also offers costlier health insurance plans than most of the other departments reviewed for this story.

According to the benefits package for Ada County, deputies who have a PPO health plan for themselves and a spouse pay a total of $2,844 in premiums for an entire year, which includes an out-of-pocket maximum of $7,000 for a family plan. A PPO plan at the Meridian Police Department for an officer and their spouse costs $2,119.56 in premium payments for the year and has an out-of-pocket maximum of $7,500 for a family plan. The benefits package for the Boise Police Department does not list a monthly premium, but the city’s PPO plan has a $400 deductible and an out-of-pocket maximum of $1,400 for a family plan.

The Nampa Police Department requires an annual premium of $1,510, plus an out-of-pocket maximum ranging from $5,000 to $7,000, depending on whether a service is in or out of network.

The Spokane Police Department offers four different plan options, ranging from a yearly premium payment of $1,260 to $1,620, depending on the plan. Out-of-pocket maximums on a family plan at SPD range from $1,875 to $4,000. Reno’s most recent health benefits package does not list the information for what employees pay for their yearly premium, but a PPO plan has a deductible between $600 and $1,800, depending on whether a service is in or out of network for a family plan. The out-of-pocket maximum for a family plan at RPD ranges between $6,000 and $12,000, depending on whether a service is in or out of network.

The City of Salt Lake City’s benefits package does not list a yearly premium amount, but the deductible for a family plan is $4,000 for in-network providers and $8,000 for out-of-network, compared to a $700 annual deductible for a family plan at ACSO. The out-of-pocket maximum is $8,000 for in-network services and $16,000 for out-of-network.
