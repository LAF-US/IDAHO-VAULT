# Hermeticism

**Hermeticism** is the philosophical and religious tradition built on writings
attributed to **Hermes Trismegistus** ("thrice-greatest Hermes") — a syncretic
sage fusing the Greek god **Hermes** with the Egyptian god of writing and
wisdom, **Thoth**. It is the doctrinal root beneath [[Alchemy]]: the single
axiom that the alchemical Work rests on — *"as above, so below"* — is a
Hermetic sentence. This node sits **upstream** of the vault's symbolic-language
cluster, supplying the cosmology that the glyphs and operations of alchemy
encode.

This is an original synthesis grounded in the sources listed at the end, not a
verbatim copy.

## What Hermeticism claims

At its center is the doctrine of **correspondence**: the cosmos is a single,
living, interconnected whole, and every level mirrors every other. The
macrocosm (the universe, the heavens) and the microcosm (the human being,
matter) are reflections of one another, so that to read one is to read the
other. Knowledge therefore proceeds by analogy and resonance rather than by
isolating parts — and the divine, the cosmic, and the human are continuous, not
sealed off from each other.

The Hermetica traditionally divide wisdom into **three parts** — sometimes
called *the three parts of the wisdom of the whole universe*:

- **Alchemy** — the operation of the *Sun*: the perfection of matter (and self).
- **Astrology** — the operation of the *stars*: their influence, and the work of
  rising above it.
- **Theurgy** — the operation of the *gods*: ritual union with the divine.

This triad is why the vault's [[Alchemy]] node keeps brushing against astrology
(the planetary metals) and against the theurgy/thaumaturgy distinction: all
three are branches of one Hermetic trunk.

## The texts

**The Corpus Hermeticum.** The core philosophical writings are a set of Greek
treatises (dialogues and discourses, e.g. the *Poimandres*) produced in the
Hellenistic Egyptian milieu, conventionally dated to roughly the **2nd–3rd
centuries CE**. They are religious-philosophical rather than operational —
concerned with the soul, rebirth, divine mind (*nous*), and ascent.

**The Emerald Tablet (*Tabula Smaragdina*).** A short, cryptic text — the single
most influential Hermetic document for alchemy. It first surfaces in **Arabic
sources of the late 8th / early 9th century** (embedded in the
*Kitāb sirr al-khalīqa*, the "Book of the Secret of Creation"), and was
translated into **Latin in the 12th–13th centuries**, after which it became the
foundational charter of European alchemy. Its opening is the most quoted line in
all of Western esotericism:

> *That which is below is like that which is above, and that which is above is
> like that which is below, to accomplish the miracle of the one thing.*

This is the sentence the [[Alchemy]] node names as its fourth first principle —
the doctrine of correspondence in its rawest form, and the reason the metals are
bound to the planets and the bench-work is also soul-work.

## The Renaissance revival

For a thousand years the Greek Hermetica were largely lost to the Latin West.
Then in **1460** a manuscript reached Florence; **Cosimo de' Medici** judged it
so important that he had **Marsilio Ficino** *interrupt his translation of Plato*
to render it first. Ficino's Latin **Corpus Hermeticum (1463)** ignited a
century of Hermetic enthusiasm. Crucially, Renaissance readers believed Hermes
Trismegistus was a near-contemporary of Moses — an ancient Egyptian sage holding
a *prisca theologia* ("primordial theology"), a stream of divine wisdom older
than and parallel to scripture. This supposed antiquity is what gave Hermeticism
its enormous authority and licensed figures like Pico della Mirandola, Giordano
Bruno, and the wider magical-philosophical current of the period.

## The skeptical correction

As with the [[Language of flowers]] (where Beverly Seaton showed the "ancient
cipher" was largely a 19th-century publishing genre), the popular picture of
Hermeticism needs a scholarly check on two fronts:

- **Casaubon's redating (1614).** The classicist **Isaac Casaubon** applied
  philological analysis to the Corpus Hermeticum and demonstrated it was *not*
  primordial: its post-classical Greek, its echoes of Hellenistic authors, and
  its indirect knowledge of Christian ideas placed it firmly in the early
  centuries CE, not the age of Moses. This demolished the *prisca theologia*
  claim and is a landmark in the history of textual criticism — meaning is
  dated by its language, not by its self-advertisement.
- **The *Kybalion* is modern.** The widely sold *Kybalion* (1908), published by
  "Three Initiates" and generally attributed to **William Walker Atkinson**,
  presents "seven Hermetic principles" as ancient Egyptian-Greek doctrine. It is
  in fact an early-20th-century **New Thought** work; its seven principles are
  largely modern. Treat it as a *reception* of Hermeticism, not a source for it —
  the same caution the cluster applies to Victorian flower dictionaries and to
  Jung's reading of alchemy.

## The afterlife

Hermetic correspondence runs straight into the modern psychological reading of
the tradition: **Carl Jung** treated "as above, so below" as an image of the
relationship between conscious and unconscious, and read alchemical and Hermetic
symbolism as projections of the psyche (see [[Jung and alchemy]]). The tradition
also feeds Rosicrucianism, the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, Tarot
symbolism, and the general vocabulary of Western esotericism.

## Relationship to the cluster

Hermeticism is the **keystone**: it supplies the doctrine of correspondence that
[[Alchemy]] turns into operations, that [[Alchemical symbols]] compress into
glyphs, and that [[Jung and alchemy]] re-reads as psychology. Where the
[[Language of flowers]] is a *sibling* symbolic language, Hermeticism is the
cluster's *ancestor* — the philosophy that makes "the object is the message"
a claim about the structure of reality and not merely a code: if every level
mirrors every other, then *everything* is a sign of everything else, and
reading the world is the whole of the work.
