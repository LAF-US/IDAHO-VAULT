## Information

Coords: 46.4122, -117.0258

Located on the campus of Lewis-Clark State College in Lewiston, [[Idaho]], *Hospitality of the Nez Perce* is a powerful public sculpture that commemorates one of the most important relationships forged during the Lewis and Clark Expedition. Created by renowned Nez Perce artist Douglas Hyde, the installation honors the assistance, guidance, and generosity shown by the Nez Perce people to the Corps of Discovery during a critical phase of its journey through the Pacific Northwest.

The sculpture portrays a significant moment of interaction between expedition leaders Meriwether Lewis and William Clark and the Nez Perce people. Central figures include Lewis, depicted in his distinctive tricorner hat, and the Nez Perce leader Walamottinin, commonly known as Twisted Hair, who played an important role in helping the expedition navigate unfamiliar territory. Clark, wearing a fur cap, stands nearby as part of the scene. Additional sculptural elements are positioned throughout the landscaped setting, encouraging visitors to explore the artwork from multiple perspectives and reflect on the cultural exchange that took place more than two centuries ago.

The Nez Perce people’s assistance proved essential to the success of the expedition. After crossing the Bitterroot Mountains in 1805, the Corps of Discovery arrived exhausted and in need of aid. Members of the Nez Perce Nation provided food, shelter, guidance, and assistance in constructing canoes, helping the expedition continue its journey westward to the Pacific Ocean.

Installed in 1993 as part of the centennial celebration of Lewis-Clark State College, the sculpture reflects the college’s connection to regional history and the enduring significance of the Lewis and Clark story in Idaho. A second casting of the sculpture was later installed in Boise near the Idaho State Capitol, expanding its reach and educational impact.

Visitors can find the sculpture on the college campus among landscaped grounds near the library and academic buildings. The setting provides a thoughtful space for learning about the expedition while recognizing the indispensable contributions of the Nez Perce people and the importance of intercultural cooperation in the history of the American West.

For those following the Lewis and Clark Trail, *Hospitality of the Nez Perce* offers a meaningful opportunity to reflect on one of the expedition’s most significant partnerships and the lasting legacy of the Nez Perce Nation.
