**Solon Hannibal de la Mothe Borglum** (December 22, 1868 – January 31, 1922) [^1] was an American [sculptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture "Sculpture"). He is most noted for his depiction of frontier life, and especially his experience with cowboys and native Americans.

He was awarded the [Croix de Guerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croix_de_Guerre "Croix de Guerre") by France [^2] for his work with *Les Foyers du Soldat* service clubs during [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I").[^3]

## Early life

Born in [Ogden, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogden,_Utah "Ogden, Utah"), Borglum was the younger brother of [Gutzon Borglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gutzon_Borglum "Gutzon Borglum") and uncle of [Lincoln Borglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Borglum "Lincoln Borglum"), the two men most responsible for the creation of the carvings at [Mount Rushmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rushmore "Mount Rushmore"). Solon's [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danes "Danes") immigrant father James Borglum was a [Mormon polygamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism_and_polygamy "Mormonism and polygamy"), being married to two sisters, Ida and Christina Mikkelsen. When the family – each wife had two children – moved to Nebraska they could no longer openly be husband and wives, so Solon and Gutzon's mother Christina was listed as the family servant. When the father moved the family again to [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri "St. Louis, Missouri") in 1871, so that he could attend medical school, the decision was made to leave Christina behind. The children were told to never talk about her again. Solon was about three years old at the time.[^4] Solon grew up in [Fremont, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont,_Nebraska "Fremont, Nebraska") and [Omaha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha "Omaha") [^5] and spent his early years as a [rancher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancher "Rancher") in western [Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska "Nebraska").[^6]

Solon’s father was a physician but had worked as a wood-carver, which almost certainly influenced Solon’s older brother, Gutzon, to pursue a career as an artist. Having shown little interest in formal schooling, the younger son spent his teens working on his father’s ranch near Fremont, Nebraska. He showed a talent for drawing horses, and his careful studies of their movements prompted Gutzon to encourage Solon to pursue art as a profession.

## Education

In 1893 Solon went to Omaha to study with [J. Laurie Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Laurie_Wallace "J. Laurie Wallace"), a former pupil of [Thomas Eakins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Eakins "Thomas Eakins"). Following this early, and evidently brief, formal training, he joined his brother Gutzon at his home in the Sierra Madre mountains. A personality clash with Gutzon’s first wife Lisa however, forced Solon to move on; he went to Los Angeles, where he painted portraits and to [Santa Ana, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California "Santa Ana, California"), where he taught art privately. He had little success, however, and in November 1895 he traveled to [Cincinnati, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati,_Ohio "Cincinnati, Ohio"), where he entered the Cincinnati Art Academy. One of his instructors, the sculptor [Louis Rebisso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Rebisso "Louis Rebisso"), encouraged him to try sculpting. His first effort was a sculpture of a group of horses based on observations and drawings he had made at the U.S. Mail stables in Cincinnati.[^7]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/70/Solon_Borglum_1902.jpg/250px-Solon_Borglum_1902.jpg)

Borglum working, 1902

In 1898 the Art Academy awarded Borglum a scholarship that allowed him to go to Paris, where he matriculated at the [Académie Julian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_Julian "Académie Julian") as a student of [Denys Puech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denys_Puech "Denys Puech"). He met leading sculptors [Emmanuel Fremiet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Fremiet "Emmanuel Fremiet") and [Augustus Saint-Gaudens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_Saint-Gaudens "Augustus Saint-Gaudens"), who gave him further encouragement. Borglum received a silver medal at the [Exposition Universelle (1900)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_\(1900\) "Exposition Universelle (1900)") and another at the [Pan-American Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Exposition "Pan-American Exposition") in [Buffalo, NY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_NY "Buffalo, NY") [^8]

## Later life

In 1898, Borglum married, and Solon and his wife, Emma (née Vignal),[^9] spent the summer of 1899 at the [Crow Creek Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crow_Creek_Reservation "Crow Creek Reservation") in [South Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Dakota "South Dakota"). Though he later lived in [Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris "Paris") and [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City") and achieved a reputation as one of America's notable sculptors, it was his depictions of [frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontier "Frontier") life, and especially his experience with [cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy "Cowboy") and [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States "Native Americans in the United States") peoples, which was the basis of his reputation.[^10]

In 1901, Solon and his wife, Emma had a son, Paul Arnold Borglum.[^11] [^12]

On 9 December 1903, Solon and his wife, Emma had a daughter, Monica (née Borglum) Davies.[^13] [^14]

In 1906, Borglum moved to the [Silvermine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvermine,_Connecticut "Silvermine, Connecticut") neighborhood of [New Canaan, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Canaan,_Connecticut "New Canaan, Connecticut"), where he helped found the " [Knockers Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvermine,_Connecticut#Silvermine_Art_Guild "Silvermine, Connecticut") " of artists. His brother, Gutzon, lived in nearby [Stamford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford,_Connecticut "Stamford, Connecticut") from 1910 to 1920.[^15]

In 1911, Borglum was elected into the [National Academy of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Design "National Academy of Design") as an Associate member.[^16]

During [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Borglum was in France, serving as secretary of the YMCA, and then taught sculpture at the [American Expeditionary Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Expeditionary_Forces "American Expeditionary Forces") Art Training Center in [Bellevue (Hauts-de-Seine)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue_\(Hauts-de-Seine\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "Bellevue (Hauts-de-Seine) (page does not exist)"), [Seine-et-Oise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-et-Oise "Seine-et-Oise"),[^17] outside Paris.[^18]

Circa 1918, in New York City, he opened a second [^19] studio [^20] and established the [American School of Sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_School_of_Sculpture "American School of Sculpture").[^21] He ran the school and gave many lectures on art until his death after an [appendectomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appendectomy "Appendectomy") complicated by his war wounds [^22] in January 1922.[^23] His legacy was carried on by his wife Emma until her death in 1934, at which point his daughter Monica and her husband, A. Mervyn Davies,[^24] oversaw the exhibition of his artwork. In 1974 they published his biography *Solon H. Borglum: A Man Who Stands Alone*.

Borglum's papers are held at the [Archives of American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archives_of_American_Art "Archives of American Art"),[^25] and the Library of Congress.[^26]

## Works

Borglum created several animal groups while in Paris, including *Lassoing Wild Horses* and *The Stampede of Wild Horses*, which were shown at the [Paris Salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_\(Paris\) "Salon (Paris)") in 1898 and 1899, respectively.

The year 1903 was a banner one for the artist. He had a one-man show of thirty-two small sculptures at the Keppel Gallery, New York. In his ground-breaking *History of American Sculpture* published that year, [Lorado Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorado_Taft "Lorado Taft") devoted several pages to Borglum,[^27] and he was the subject of an entire chapter in [Charles Caffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Henry_Caffin "Charles Henry Caffin") ’s 1903 book *American Masters of Sculpture*.[^28] In 1904 Borglum won the gold medal at the [Louisiana Purchase Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase_Exposition "Louisiana Purchase Exposition") held in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri "St. Louis, Missouri").

Borglum received several major public commissions, including an equestrian monument of General [John Brown Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Brown_Gordon "John Brown Gordon") for the grounds of the [Georgia State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_State_Capitol "Georgia State Capitol") in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta "Atlanta") (1907), one of [Rough Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rough_Riders "Rough Riders") [Buckey O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buckey_O'Neill "Buckey O'Neill") for the plaza in front of the courthouse in [Prescott, Arizona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescott,_Arizona "Prescott, Arizona") (1907), and *[The Pioneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pioneer_\(Visalia,_California\) "The Pioneer (Visalia, California)")*, which was erected in the Court of Honor at the [Panama–Pacific International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93Pacific_International_Exposition "Panama–Pacific International Exposition") in [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco") (1915).

Two of his works are located in [Jersey City, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey "Jersey City, New Jersey"). His sculpture *Buffalo and Bears* is in Leonard Gordon Park in the city's Heights section [^29]

In 1974 a group of the sculptor's descendants gave twenty bronzes, marbles, original plasters, portfolios of drawings and paintings to the [New Britain Museum of American Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Britain_Museum_of_American_Art "New Britain Museum of American Art"). Today the Museum houses the largest repository of Borglum's works.

Borglum sculpted a larger than life bronze [equestrian statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue "Equestrian statue") for the *[Bucky O'Neill Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucky_O'Neill_Monument "Bucky O'Neill Monument"), Rough Rider* at the [Yavapai County Court House Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yavapai_County,_Arizona "Yavapai County, Arizona") in Prescott, Arizona.[^30] [Teddy Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Roosevelt "Teddy Roosevelt") had persuaded Buckey O'Neill to join the Rough Riders and he was killed at the [Battle of San Juan Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Juan_Hill "Battle of San Juan Hill"). Borglum's statue *Cowboy at Rest* is also located on the grounds of the Yavapai County Court House in Prescott, Arizona.[^31]

Borglum's pieces can be found at the [Buffalo Bill Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo_Bill_Museum "Buffalo Bill Museum") in [Cody, Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cody,_Wyoming "Cody, Wyoming"), including *Evening*, a depiction of a cowboy leaning against his unsaddled horse at the end of the day.

Two of Borglum's sculptures, *Inspiration* and *Aspiration*, which depict Native American men, stand in the front courtyard of [St. Mark's Church in-the-Bowery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mark's_Church_in-the-Bowery "St. Mark's Church in-the-Bowery"), in the [East Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Village,_Manhattan "East Village, Manhattan") neighborhood of [Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan "Manhattan") in [New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City "New York City"), flanking the front gate.

Black and white photos of *Cowboy Mounting*, *Lost in a Blizzard* (in marble), and *Tamed* can be found in Caffin's book.[^32]
