Holy freaking airball, as the kids say.

Idaho’s DOGE Task Force, a committee dedicated to cutting waste and duplication in state government, might ignore the easiest spending reduction lawmakers could make next year: Idaho Launch.

With one bill, lawmakers could save Idaho taxpayers $50 million annually. Yet the DOGE Task Force appears unwilling to suggest that cut to help fix a looming budget gap.

It’s a layup for cost-conscious lawmakers. Yet, the task force appears committed to an airball.

Launch is a state program that gives Idaho high school students who remain in the state for higher education up to $8,000 to cover tuition and fees, an amount designed to cover 80% of the direct costs of attending college in the Gem State.

It’s Gov. Brad Little’s signature program, and he touts it at every turn. Recently, he trekked to Washington, D.C., to sell the program to President Donald Trump.

After the meeting, Little touted Trump’s support for the program.

> “The Trump administration’s recognition of Idaho LAUNCH this year was a defining moment. Idaho LAUNCH fits perfectly into President Trump’s vision for the American worker. With LAUNCH, Idaho is leading the nation in preparing our young people for rewarding careers in a dynamic workforce environment,” Little said in a press release.

Trump’s endorsement doesn’t change Launch’s true nature: It’s a redistributive giveaway and market engineering by a state government that should know better.

Beyond that, the program makes it harder for middle-class families to put food on the table, contribute to retirement plans, and save for their own kids’ post–high school plans. Unnecessary spending keeps the state’s tax burden higher than it needs to be, taking more money from taxpayers’ pockets.

It’s baffling, then, that a Republican-led task force in a supposed red state would ignore such a simple reduction that would provide significant bang for the buck.

The DOGE Task Force, which isn’t time-limited like its federal counterpart was, briefed budget committee members on its progress last week. It provided budget writers with a list of 70 agencies, departments, or commissions to reduce, realign, or cut altogether.

Launch was nowhere to be found on the list of possible cuts.

Now, caution is appropriate. The committee has at least one more meeting before lawmakers open the 2026 session in January. Members could still add Launch repeal or defunding to whatever work product the task force sends to lawmakers next year.

That appears unlikely at this point. Maybe lawmakers will surprise us.

Should task force members ignore Launch in their final recommendations, it would fly directly in the face of the Idaho Republican Party’s platform. Convention delegates approved language that many saw as opposing Launch at the party’s convention last summer.

> “We strongly support professional, technical, and continuing education programs that provide career readiness and college preparation, but do not support using taxpayer funding for programs beyond high school,” Section 6 of the Education portion reads after last year’s revision.

State Rep. Josh Tanner, the assistant majority leader and a close ally of Speaker Mike Moyle, introduced legislation in the 2025 session to accelerate Launch’s repeal, but the measure never received a committee hearing. The bill would have ended the program in 2026 instead of the scheduled 2029 sunset. It earned a +3 rating on the Idaho Freedom Foundation Freedom Index, which rewards policies that limit the size and scope of government.

It’s unclear why the bill never received a committee hearing. It’s worth noting that the committee’s chairman, Rep. James Holtzclaw of Meridian, voted for the original Launch authorization bill in 2023.

Idahoans will be watching the rest of the DOGE Task Force process closely. If lawmakers are serious about cutting spending to reduce budget gaps, they’ll suggest cutting Launch next session. If they choose to keep the program, they’ll show the committee is little more than political posturing ahead of an election year.
