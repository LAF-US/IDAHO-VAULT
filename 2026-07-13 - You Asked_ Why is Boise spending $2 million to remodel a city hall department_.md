The City of Boise plans to spend more than $2 million to remodel the second floor of Boise City Hall’s main building, which houses the city’s planning and development services office.

It’s one of the largest single-year expenditures outlined in the city’s 2027 budget.

The city says the funds for the project will be paid for by funds collected from developers for building permits, plan check reviews, and the like — and won’t come from tax funding. But the fund being used for the remodel is ultimately backstopped by tax dollars, and the city says without an increase in developer fees, that fund could run dry in a few years — and a $2 million drawdown would make it happen more quickly.

## What the remodel entails

Maria Ortega, a city spokesperson, said the current setup of the second floor isn’t sufficient, “particularly in terms of accessibility, customer flow, and the need for a clearer and more secure separation between publicly accessible areas and staff workspaces. The project is intended to improve how the space functions for both the public and staff by enhancing customer service, accessibility, safety, and overall operational efficiency.”

Ortega said the remodel would update the lobby, staff workspaces, conference rooms, and restrooms. Ortega said the update was needed because the way the department works has evolved.

“The current space was designed around an older, paper-based service model, with filing cabinets and storage areas that are no longer used in the same way as records and processes have become more digital over the years,” she said. “As a result, portions of the floor are underutilized, while public-facing areas are not as clear, accessible, or efficient as they should be.”

Though the remodel would come from the city’s development fee fund, scrutiny on taxes at all levels of government has been heightened in recent years. The city has proposed to raise property taxes on residents, though it [stepped back from an initial plan](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/06/09/with-good-break-of-news-mclean-to-lower-proposal-for-tax-increase/) that would have raised them even more.

“We recognize residents care deeply about how public dollars are spent,” Ortega said. “This project is being considered because it directly affects how people access city services every day, and it focuses on making better use of space the city already has rather than expanding into new space.”

## Special fund, thanks to Micron

Ortega told BoiseDev that “the city is proposing to use available excess development revenue to fund the work.”

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

Watkins Carter - 2:1

Currently, the city has $8 million in its Development Fee Reserve, which the city started in 2025, according to city spokesperson Emilee Ayers. The remodel would come from those funds. The city has seen a large increase in the development fees it collects in recent years, buoyed by large-scale projects by Micron Technology and St. Luke’s Health System.

Micron pays a big discount on development fees versus what it would typically pay for a project of its size, with Boise Mayor Lauren McLean telling BoiseDev in 2022 that the city had worked to “right-size” the fees for the technology giant. Even still, Micron is paying millions in fees for its nearly $50 billion in announced projects.

City of Boise Budget Division Senior Manager Alisha McAndrews said the city is conducting a “cost-of-service study” for the Boise City Council, which could decide to raise development fees to ensure tax dollars from the general fund aren’t needed to help pay for planning and development services. If the remodel project moves forward, the reserve will have $2 million fewer dollars in it than it might otherwise.

“Based on today’s budget and financial outlook, restricted development fee revenues are sufficient to fund eligible development-related operating costs, the remodel, and maintain a substantial reserve,” McAndrews said. “The remodel is funded entirely with restricted development fee revenue — not unrestricted general fund.”

The city will consider its budget with a public hearing at Boise City Hall Tuesday at 6 p.m.
