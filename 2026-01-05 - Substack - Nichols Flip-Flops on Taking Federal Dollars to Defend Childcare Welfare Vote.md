Idaho Sen. Tammy Nichols has flip-flopped on her stance regarding federal dollars for Idaho programs as she defends her vote for a socialist childcare welfare program that critics say is ripe for Somali-style abuse.

During the 2025 legislative session, Nichols—who co-chairs the Idaho Freedom Caucus—voted for Senate Bill 1206, a spending bill that allowed $14 million in federal dollars to flow through the state to childcare providers in Idaho. That arrangement is similar to a Minnesota program that was allegedly taken advantage of by Somali fraudsters.

Conservatives across social media have hammered Nichols for her vote, questioning why a freedom-minded lawmaker would support additional federal dollars for welfare programs.

Nichols has taken to social media to defend her vote, arguing that it was the sensible option.

“But a ‘no’ vote wouldn’t have stopped the program of lowered taxes,” Nichols wrote in a Facebook comment. “It would have just sent Idaho taxpayer money to other states and taken Idaho out of the loop. I’m not interested in symbolic votes that change nothing. I voted based on what actually happens in the real world.”

That comment—suggesting Nichols supports bringing more federal dollars into the state—stands in sharp contrast to her prior statements on the issue.

In a recent candidate questionnaire for the Idaho Republican Party, Nichols expressed interest in reducing Idaho’s dependence on federal dollars.

“Reclaiming sovereignty is essential for preserving Idaho’s distinct identity and values, ensuring decisions reflect the will of its citizens,” [Nichols wrote](https://idgop.org/candidate/tammy-nichols-3/). “It also allows Idaho to distance itself from reliance on federal debt dollars, promoting fiscal responsibility and economic independence.”

It remains unclear when or why Nichols changed her position on bringing additional federal dollars into Idaho.

Her new stance also ignores how much Idaho already receives from the federal government. While she argues it is her duty to bring Idahoans’ tax dollars back to the state, data suggests Idaho residents receive $1.15 for every $1 they pay in federal taxes.

In short, Idahoans already get back more than they pay.

Other lawmakers have offered different views on accepting federal funds.

State Sen. Christy Zito told social media followers that lawmakers are not required to take federal cash.

“We do not have to accept the money and continue a federal program in our state,” Zito wrote last week. “Saying, ‘If we don’t take federal dollars, they’ll just go to another state,’ is like having your child come home and say he’s going to buy meth from the drug dealer on the corner because if he doesn’t take it, the drug dealer will just sell it to the kid in the next house.”

State Sen. Brian Lenney called Senate Bill 1206 “a massive expansion of the nanny state.”
