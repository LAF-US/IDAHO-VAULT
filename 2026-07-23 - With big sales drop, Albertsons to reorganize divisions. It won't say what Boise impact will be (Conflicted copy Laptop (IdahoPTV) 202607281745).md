Boise-based Albertsons saw its stock drop rapidly Thursday after it said a key sales figure dropped in the most recent quarter and that it expected the rest of the year to be tougher than expected.

The company also said it would make several changes, including the retirement of its chief financial officer and consolidation of 11 regional offices into just four divisions.

“While these results did not meet our expectations, they underscored the need to move faster,” Albertsons CEO Susan Morris said. “…we are accelerating change to strengthen our competitiveness and improve execution across the business.”

Albertsons said it would take its regional offices across the country and combine them into the four regional hubs. The former Mountain West, Portland and Seattle markets would become the “West Region,” which would cover [[Idaho]]. The current Mountain West office was located in a building next to Albertsons’ main headquarters in Boise, but that [building is now available for lease](https://www.loopnet.com/Listing/500-E-Baybrook-Ct-Boise-ID/39433468/).

[Just last year](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/02/05/albertsons-realighment/), Albertsons combined its Boise and Denver divisions to form the Mountain West group, and will now push the consolidation further.

Albertsons did not answer BoiseDev’s repeated questions about where the four divisions would be based, and it did not provide specifics about large-scale layoffs or relocations, if any. An unnamed company spokesperson using a general email box did say that they would eliminate the division president roles across the company. Morris said they had put top leaders in charge of the four new regions.

According to a memo obtained by BoiseDev, the new West division will be headed by Brad Street, who is currently based in Boise and oversees the current Mountain West region.

Morris also said Albertsons would execute the changes “very carefully” and noted it involves risk. She said that the company’s emphasis is on “better, simpler store execution (and) clarity in accountability and ownership.”

## Centralization

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/IMG_5636.jpg)

Albertsons in Star. Photo: Don Day/BoiseDev

Albertsons, which over the last decade has emphasized a model it calls “locally great, nationally strong,” has long touted its work to push decision-making closer to stores. It will now move in the opposite direction. It calls the initiative “ACI Edge.”

Albertsons CFO Sharon McCollam, who joined the company in 2021, will retire, but will “remain in her role until a successor is named.” McCollam was hired under former CEO Vivek Sankaran and continued in the role when Morris took the top job last year.

On an earnings call, McCollam thanked the company and her teammates, then pivoted to Albertsons’ results.

“Our performance fell short of our expectations,” McCollam said. “We have moved quickly to address those issues,” noting the division changes and another concept the company calls “Merch United.”

Merch United will also move buying decisions and other functions out from the divisions to the national level for items in the center of stores, “bringing customer insights, supplier relationships, strategy, product, placement, promotion and price under a single enterprise team while continuing to combine national scale with local expertise. Merch United combines the buying power, data and analytics of a national retailer with the customer focus and local insights of Albertsons Cos.’ market teams.”

Albertsons operates under more than 20 brands around the country, including Safeway, Carrs, Vonns, MarketStreet and others. It has more than 2,200 stores.

## Financial results

McCollam said the company’s “lower income” customers are contributing to “softness” in Albertsons’ earnings. The company’s “identical sales” measure, which is how it looks at sales in stores open at least a year, dropped 0.8%. The company saw net income of $85 million during the quarter. For the rest of the year, Albertsons predicts those identical sales will continue to shrink by.5% to 1.5%.

The stock dropped by more than 20% Thursday morning.

“As we look ahead, we are moving decisively,” Morris said. “Given continued softness in industry unit trends and a more cautious consumer, we are accelerating investments and operational changes designed to strengthen our customer value proposition and improve our competitive position.”

Albertsons said it would “invest in pricing” to help lower costs on some items for customers in some instances in some markets.

![Zamzows 116 - July - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/46bf9fe4-7aa0-4f20-a644-e8911393869f.jpg)

“We’re investing very surgically and selectively,” Morris told an analyst during the company’s earnings call. “This is not about broad-based discounting. It’s about very targeted market-based investments in the areas where we believe customers are making purchase decisions.”

Morris said it has been testing price discounting in three markets across the country, and will work to take what it has learned to other areas of the company. Albertsons says while it competes on price, it also works to compete on other factors like product freshness, selection and service.

At the store level, Morris said they are working to reconfigure stores to better allocate square footage to the ways customers shop today.
