Recently, perennial Democratic candidate David Roth, who was running against U.S. Sen. Jim Risch in the general election, [stepped down to clear a path](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/politics-government/state-politics/article316693055.html) for independent candidate Todd Achilles.

It came after some back-and-forth infighting about who should lead the fight against Risch.

Now that the die is cast, does Achilles have a shot? Maybe not.

Statewide general elections in Idaho have gone only to Republicans every single year since U.S. Rep. Walt Minnick was defeated in 2010. Those are tough, likely insurmountable obstacles for Achilles or anyone else. To call Achilles an underdog understates the case considerably.

But a separate question is whether the 83-year-old Risch, who first ran for elected office in 1970, has done a single thing in office that merits reelection. Stop and ask yourself right now: Can you name one?

I didn’t think so.

There’s no excuse for Risch’s lack of accomplishment, particularly in the most recent term.

It’s not like Risch is one random senator among 100. He holds the coveted chairmanship of the Foreign Relations Committee, which traditionally would make him one of a handful of the most important foreign policy figures in the country.

What has he done with that role? Embraced lazy irrelevance with gusto.

Under Risch’s leadership, the Foreign Relations Committee has been the last place you’d look for information about the most important foreign policy questions of the day.

While the Trump administration has blundered and flailed in Iran, leaving the regime with more regional power than before the war began and with what appear to be only minor setbacks to its nuclear weapons program, Risch has failed to look after his constituents’ interest — or the national interest — in any appreciable way.

Every time you put $4 gas in your car or truck, you should see Risch’s face staring back at you.

What have we gained? Risch hasn’t bothered to explore that question either.

I’m hardly the first to note Risch’s dereliction of duty. [Many commentators](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2026/04/02/by-failing-to-hold-iran-war-hearings-idahos-u-s-sen-jim-risch-abdicates-his-constitutional-duty/) and [observers](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/23/risch-senate-venezuela-boat-strikes-trump) have been struck by how the Foreign Relations Committee has [declined into irrelevance](https://www.lmtribune.com/opinion/jim-risch-has-just-lost-the-certainty-of-reelection-51b1d87c) under his chairmanship.

Under any prior Foreign Relations chair, you might expect hearings to address the question: What is the current status of Iran’s nuclear program? Despite repeated assertions that it had been “obliterated,” [many experts believe](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/fact-checking-trumps-claims-about-how-the-iran-war-is-going) there have been only minor setbacks as a result of the war — the sort of thing that pushed back nuclear breakthrough by a few months.

What’s true? Don’t ask Risch’s committee.

Would you like to guess how many hearings the venerable Senate Foreign Relations committee has held focused on the Iran War under Risch’s leadership? The answer is zero. Not one.

The only hearing at which there was substantial testimony about the Iran War was during the committee’s hearing on the State Department budget, at which none of the big questions were raised by Risch. In fact, Risch’s one statement on the subject in [the hearing](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/watch-live-rubio-testifies-on-capitol-hill-for-the-1st-time-since-start-of-iran-war) contained only effusive praise for the administration.

“The administration has taken decisive action to protect American citizens from the threat posed by Iranian nuclear capabilities and ambitions,” he said.

Meanwhile, it appears defense officials are leaking news that the U.S. has [exhausted its supply](https://thehill.com/policy/defense/6009275-us-missile-interceptor-stocks-dwindling-dangerous) of long-range precision missiles on the feckless war. Is that true?

Predictably, it seems Risch does not care to know the answer. There’s [no scheduled hearing](https://www.foreign.senate.gov/hearings) on the topic, or anything else to do with the Iran War.

Why does Risch seem so uninterested in learning the truth?

Because Risch’s fundamental loyalty is neither to his state nor his county, not to the people who elected him or to the people he was elected to serve. Risch’s fundamental loyalty has always been to his party, and today the Republican Party is entirely servile to Trump. So [Risch remains his water boy](https://www.idahostatesman.com/opinion/opn-columns-blogs/article306904521.html).

[Most Americans](https://www.natesilver.net/p/iran-war-polls-popularity-approval) have long believed the Iran War was never worth fighting.

But that’s the kind of information that only matters to people who serve the American people.

*Bryan Clark is an opinion writer for the Idaho Statesman.*
