The Boise music scene recently lost a long-time staple of the area. Jon Faulkner died at the end of June but leaves behind a decades long legacy as a guitarist, father and friend.

Music in Boise has grown exponentially in that time and Jon was there through all of it. Whether in original bands like Passage, cover bands like Priest or his long-time musical duo Tauge and Faulkner.

"He sought out players, you know, because he loved to play," said Jeff Tauge in an interview. "The stage was his second home. Sometimes it was his first home."

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXWDC_2TFU8)

People across the state felt a connection with Faulkner whether they directly played with him or not. Todd Dunnigan, a producer at Audio Lab in Garden City called him "generally the coolest guy around."

"That's what stands out to me about him. So encouraging, so cool. And that's beyond the guitar playing," Dunnigan said choked up. "Just to be a good human."

The legacy and impact Faulkner had on the area was so wide, his loss was felt even across state lines. And when it came to his celebration of life, it was hundreds of his closest friends, family, bandmates and people who would watch him play packing Treefort Music Hall in downtown Boise.

An evening of live music. Some of Faulkner's favorite songs played by his favorite people.

"I'm gonna miss him for the rest of my life, you know. He was, you know, we were best friends for 52 years and then bandmates \[for\] 40. And that's a lot of history," said Tauge filling with emotion. "I was just very proud to be his friend and his bandmate."

Faulkner is survived by his two daughters, Bailey and Ivy; three grandchildren; his siblings Mark, Paul, Jeff and Christina.
