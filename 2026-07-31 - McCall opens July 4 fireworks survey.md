Should the City of McCall fund July 4 fireworks and other activities associated with the annual holiday celebration in the city?

That is the chief question asked in an online survey launched by the city at the request of the McCall City Council. [The survey is available here](https://www.surveymonkey.com/r/KGWVVLZ?fbclid=IwY2xjawTZrJZleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFjSkZWOUZxV3ZPUWtJUDh3c3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHvSwdqltdtk2MYx7OOQ5Csqd43IiyMKdp2fs5BsPOKgPXqJlJpnqXT6-9Wu__aem_CR2IXEZ0svW4iOfqw23N_g) and will be open until Aug. 7, according to city officials.

Earlier this month, the council floated the idea of cutting the fireworks display and other city-sponsored events amid rising costs to manage large crowds in Legacy Park and around downtown McCall, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2026/07/23/do-people-even-want-this-mccall-to-rethink-july-4-celebration-fireworks/).

![Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/115c0cfa-e547-4a5c-8f4e-7ea7e16f8bde.png)

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

A decision on the 2027 fireworks display must be made within the next month or two to ensure contractor availability, council members were told.

The city-hosted Lakeside Liberty Fest in Legacy Park started in 2016 as an effort by the city to revamp the culture of July 4 celebrations to be more family-friendly. It coincided with the adoption of an alcohol ban in lakefront city parks during the holiday.
