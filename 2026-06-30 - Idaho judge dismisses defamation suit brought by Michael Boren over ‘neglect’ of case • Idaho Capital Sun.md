## Idaho judge dismisses defamation suit brought by Michael Boren over ‘neglect’ of case

USDA under secretary and businessman had challenged critics of his Sawtooth National Recreation Area air strip permit

![Michael Boren of Idaho testifies during his confirmation hearing](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/GettyImages-2218492726.jpg)

Michael Boren of Idaho testifies during his confirmation hearing

Michael Boren of Idaho testifies during his confirmation hearing at the Russell Senate Office Building on Capitol Hill on June 3, 2025, in Washington, D.C. Boren is now the under secretary of Agriculture for Natural Resources and Environment. (Photo by Alex Wong/Getty Images)

A Custer County judge on Friday dismissed a defamation lawsuit brought by U.S. Department of Agriculture Under Secretary and Idaho businessman Michael Boren against critics of Boren’s private airstrip near Stanley.

Seventh Judicial District Judge Darren Simpson dismissed the case due to inactivity, but he said it could be brought back. Per Idaho [civil court rules](https://isc.idaho.gov/rules-procedure/ircp#rule-41), a case may be dismissed if there hasn’t been activity in it for 90 days. Simpson wrote in his opinion that Boren failed to show a good reason to keep litigating the case.

“The justifications asserted by Boren or Boren’s attorney show nothing more than pure neglect of the case,” Simpson wrote.

Boren, founder of Boise-based financial software business Clearwater Analytics, has been involved in [multiple](https://www.idahostatesman.com/outdoors/hiking/article258923758.html) lawsuits tied to a ranch he owns, some of which is located within the Sawtooth National Recreation Area.

In 2022, Boren sued former Blaine County Commissioner and Olympian Dick Fosbury, who died in 2023, as well as former Blaine County Commissioner Sarah Michael, former Sawtooth Search and Rescue Commander Gary Gadwa and Boise-area filmmaker Jon Conti. Boren alleged defamation related to their vocal opposition to his requested permit to use a strip of irrigated pasture within protected land along [the Salmon River to land Boren’s helicopter](https://www.mtexpress.com/news/cops_courts/michael-boren-appeals-defamation-case-to-idaho-supreme-court/article_a150a7c0-e53f-11ed-ad46-b7abf09e1c6f.html), the Idaho Mountain Express reported.

The defendants in the case have argued the lawsuit was meant to chill their free speech.

“I’m grateful to see the court dismiss this attack on my constitutional right to express my opinion about matters affecting my community,” Gadwa said in a press release. “I look forward to continuing to spread the word about protecting the natural beauty of the Sawtooth and Salmon River country.”

Attorneys for Boren could not immediately be reached for comment.

Another judge previously dismissed the case, and Boren appealed in 2023. Judge Simpson took over the case in September and heard arguments for why the case should be retained in early June, according to court documents.

## Boren argues federal nomination delayed case

Boren’s attorney said in court filings that his client’s federal nomination was an extenuating circumstance for the delay in the case.

Then-President-elect Donald Trump [appointed Boren](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/trump-names-idaho-businessman-founder-of-clearwater-analytics-as-usda-under-secretary/) to serve as under secretary for natural resources and environment at the USDA on Jan. 17, 2025, the Idaho Capital Sun reported. The U.S. Senate confirmed him in October of that year.

Boren’s attorney argued that, during that time, Boren was “evaluating his lawsuit and its potential impact on his job,” according to court documents.

The judge was not persuaded by this argument.

“This argument does not amount to good cause,” Simpson wrote. “Boren filed a complaint and prosecuted it through an appeal to the Idaho Supreme Court. He obtained a new job. Nothing in the affidavit asserts why he or his counsel could not have taken any action to move his case along such as asking the court for a scheduling conference, requesting a trial date, or following through by setting a hearing on his motion to amend his complaint.”

Boren’s nomination drew criticism from Democrats and some conservation groups who expressed concern with his past disputes with the U.S. Forest Service, the agency he now helps oversee.

Idaho Republican U.S. Sen. Jim Risch supported Boren’s appointment. In the senator’s introduction of Boren at the Senate confirmation hearing, Risch said that in the West, there are parcels called “inholdings” of private lands within public land boundaries — like parts of Boren’s Ranch near Stanley.

“I have never run into anybody that has an inholding property that has not had some disagreement with their neighbor, the Federal Government,” Risch said, according to the hearing [transcript](https://www.congress.gov/119/chrg/CHRG-119shrg60657/CHRG-119shrg60657.pdf). “… these are difficult things, and he has a considerable amount of land that is not an inholding.”

## Gadwa’s attorneys say Boren’s lawsuit was ‘clear example of a SLAPP suit’

Attorneys from the Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression, or FIRE, have argued that Boren’s lawsuit represented “a clear example” of what are known as “strategic lawsuits against public participation,” or SLAPP.

In general, SLAPP lawsuits are defined as legal actions meant to use prolonged and expensive litigation to dissuade people from speaking about certain issues.

The judge who dismissed Boren’s case in 2023 [wrote in his opinion](https://www.mtexpress.com/news/cops_courts/michael-boren-appeals-defamation-case-to-idaho-supreme-court/article_a150a7c0-e53f-11ed-ad46-b7abf09e1c6f.html) that it “appears to be a SLAPP suit brought to violate the constitutional rights,” of the defendants, the Idaho Mountain Express reported.

When Boren filed the lawsuit, Idaho had no laws addressing SLAPP suits. Last year, the Legislature approved [a new law](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/idaho-joins-states-with-anti-slapp-laws-aimed-at-combatting-frivolous-lawsuits/) to create an expedited process to dismiss SLAPP lawsuits.

“We’re thrilled that Gary prevailed in court, but not everyone has the resources to engage in years of litigation for protected speech,” said FIRE attorney Gabe Walters. “Other states should follow Idaho’s example and pass robust anti-SLAPP measures that defend Americans’ ability to criticize the powerful without fear of retaliation.”
