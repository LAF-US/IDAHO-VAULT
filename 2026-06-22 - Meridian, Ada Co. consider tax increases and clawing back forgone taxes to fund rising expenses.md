As local governments in Idaho grapple with the effects of past legislation, increasing costs, and some decreasing revenues, leaders are contemplating how to keep governments functional.

BoiseDev took a look at the factors shaping the City of Meridian and Ada County’s proposed budgets for fiscal year 2027, which begins on October 1, 2026.

During budget hearings, leaders of Meridian and Ada Co. expressed a desire to continue to provide residents with the services they are required to provide and to keep employee pay in line with the increasing cost of living, all while operating within the constraints of legislation such as House Bill 389. The passage of the legislation in 2021 caps the amount of property tax revenue, new construction, and forgone taxes local governments are allowed to collect each year.

This year, City of Boise staff initially proposed levying the full 3% property increase allowed under Idaho law, 90% of new construction value, and about $2 million in forgone property taxes, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/05/20/boise-2027-budget/). The city then received word that its new construction value was almost double its projections; costs associated with the Public Employees Retirement System of Idaho (PERSI) also wouldn’t rise as much as anticipated, as BoiseDev reported. Those factors equated to a savings of $2.9 million, resulting in a revised proposal of a 2.7% property tax increase, [as BoiseDev reported.](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/06/09/with-good-break-of-news-mclean-to-lower-proposal-for-tax-increase/)

## What is Meridian proposing?

Following budget workshops, the City of Meridian is considering an increase to its property tax roll of 3%, as well as 1% of the city’s forgone balance, or taxes it forwent taking in previous tax years. The 1% of forgone is the maximum allowed for ongoing expenses and operating needs; Idaho law also allows local governments to take up to 3% in forgone taxes for one-time capital expenditures.

Meridian Chief Financial Officer Todd Lavoie said that the change would raise the city’s property tax levy rate from 0.00197 to 0.00198.

Lavoie said that one major factor affecting the change is increasing healthcare costs. The city is anticipating a 13.5% increase in healthcare costs for employees in the coming year. While previous year-to-year healthcare cost increases used to be in the 8% range, Lavoie said he has been told by those familiar with industry trends to expect increases in the 13% range for a few years.

The city is also proposing a cost-of-living adjustment for employees of 2.76%. Lavoie said that the adjustment keeps pace with annual inflation, which tends to be in the 2.5% range, on average.

While sales tax revenues were 9.26% higher than initially projected, liquor sales revenues were about 8.62% lower, Lavoie said. Revenues from new construction have also slowed compared to several years ago, and are unlikely to reach similar levels again, Lavoie said.

“We’re still very healthy on a growth standpoint, but we just got used to some really big numbers five, six, seven years ago,” Lavoie said. At that point, the city was generating $4 million in new revenues, he said. “I think those days are behind us,” Lavoie said, though he added that the city would plan accordingly if that happened again.

City revenues overall have decreased by about 3.8%, or about $6.3 million, according to the [city’s draft budget book](https://mccmeetingspublic.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/meridianid-meet-b078df0104c04f479f63d02f95192e7e/ITEM-Attachment-001-b527d09895dc467ba13f56445ce93dac.pdf). The book was published in May and does not yet reflect changes made since.

One item that the council deliberated on during Monday’s hearing was whether to keep in $7 million as a contribution from the city for the construction of a downtown parking garage. The 300-stall garage would be built via a public-private partnership intended to provide employee parking for the city and expand downtown parking options.

Council members ultimately agreed that putting that figure in the budget was a good plan to indicate the city’s intent to explore a parking project without obligating the council to sign any kind of contract. Mayor Robert Simison said that the city is in talks with a developer on a potential garage and could have more information to share on the potential project closer to August.

Simison appeared to be referring to Ahlquist and Pacific Companies, the purchasers of the failed Union 93 project. [As BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/10/09/ahlquist-pacific-companies-union-93-meridian/), when Ahlquist and Pacific Companies acquired the project, Ahlquist said he was pondering expanding parking opportunities for the city’s downtown.

The city is also in the process of transitioning from contracting with the City of Boise for prosecution services to developing its own department in-house. Another adjustment made during Monday’s budget hearing was to plan for the hiring of two new personnel in that regard, a decrease from the initial ten proposed. Leaders said planning for two would allow for ample time to build out the staff roster and let a new lead prosecutor have a say in designing a new space for the department’s needs.

The council is scheduled to have a public hearing on its tentative budget on August 11.

## What’s in the works in Ada County?

The Ada County Board of County Commissioners concluded its budget deliberations in mid-June. Though the county has not yet published a proposed budget book online, Ada County Clerk Trent Tripple used a spreadsheet during deliberations to reflect the proposed direction of the budget and to show how potential changes would affect the budget overall.

After discussing proposed budgets for each department, Tripple’s spreadsheet of proposed budget changes indicated taking an additional 3% from base property taxes, or about $5.12 million. It also proposed taking about $4.5 million in new construction.

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

For forgone, the spreadsheet showed the county taking 1.11%, or $2 million, for capital expenditures, and 1%, or $1.8 million, for ongoing expenditures. On the employee side, Tripple’s spreadsheet showed a 1.5% personnel merit increase, which would equate to about $3 million, but no cost-of-living allowance. It also proposes a flat annual increase per employee of $1,248, or about $3.38 million.

One project in the works for the county includes purchasing land for an 18-bay vehicle shop. The shop would allow the county to service both sheriff’s department vehicles as well as others in the county, and to bring work, like oil changes, in-house.

A slide presented at a June 10 budget workshop estimated a cost of about $6.2 million to expand the shop, and about $220,000 to purchase the land.
