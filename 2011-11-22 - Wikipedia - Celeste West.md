**Celeste (Celestia) West** (November 24, 1942 – January 3, 2008) was an [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americans "Americans") [librarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Librarian "Librarian") and [lesbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian "Lesbian") author, known for her alternative viewpoints in librarianship and her authorship of books about [lesbian sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian_sex "Lesbian sex") and [polyfidelity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyfidelity "Polyfidelity"). She herself was [polyamorous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyamorous "Polyamorous").[^1]

## Biography

West was born in [Pocatello, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocatello,_Idaho "Pocatello, Idaho").[^2] [^3] [^4] She earned her [BA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in [journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalism "Journalism") from [Portland State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_State_University "Portland State University"),[^5] and her [Master's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") in Library Service from [Rutgers University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_University "Rutgers University") in 1968.[^6] She then moved to [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), where she worked at the headquarters of the [Bay Area Reference Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Area_Reference_Center "Bay Area Reference Center") at the [San Francisco Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Public_Library "San Francisco Public Library").[^7] She was the second editor of its magazine, *Synergy*, which won two [ALA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Library_Association "American Library Association") awards but lost its funding in 1973 after West published an unflattering photograph of [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon "Richard Nixon").[^8] [^9]

In 1972, West co-founded Booklegger Press, the first [woman-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman-owned_business "Woman-owned business") American [library publisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_publishing "Library publishing"),[^10] [^11] [^12] with Sue Critchfield (her partner at the time) and Valerie Wheat. The press' first publication was an [anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthology "Anthology") edited by West and Elizabeth Katz entitled *Revolting Librarians*. The anthology, which described biases in contemporary library practices and proposed alternative library models, sold 15,000 copies in three years.[^4] She also published the [feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminist_theory "Feminist theory") library journal *Booklegger Magazine* from 1973 through 1976.[^13] Between 1989 and 2006, West worked as the library director at the [San Francisco Zen Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Zen_Center "San Francisco Zen Center").[^14]

## Selected works

- *Revolting Librarians* (editor, 1972)
- *Women's Films in Print* (1975)
- *Positive Images: Non-Sexist Films for Young People* (1976)
- *The Passionate Perils of Publishing* (1978)
- *The Public Library Mission Statement and Its Imperatives for Service* (1979)
- *Where Have All the Publishers Gone?* (1980)
- *Book of Lesbian Etiquette* (1985)
- *Words in Our Pockets* (1985)
- *Elsa: I Come With My Songs* (editor, 1986)
- *A Lesbian Love Advisor* (illustrated by [Nicole Ferentz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Ferentz "Nicole Ferentz"), 1989) [^15]
- *Lesbian Polyfidelity* (illustrated by Nicole Ferentz, 1996) [^16]
