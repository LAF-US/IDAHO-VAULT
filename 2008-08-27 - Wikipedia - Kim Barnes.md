**Kim Barnes** (born 1958 in [Lewiston, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho "Lewiston, Idaho")) is a contemporary American author of fiction, memoir, and personal essays.[^1] She served as Poet Laureate of Idaho.

## Life

She returned with her mother to their [logging camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging_camp "Logging camp") on [Orofino Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orofino,_Idaho "Orofino, Idaho") in the [Clearwater National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_National_Forest "Clearwater National Forest"), where her father worked as a [lumberjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumberjack "Lumberjack"). For the next twelve years, she and her family lived in small communities and cedar camps in northern [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho") — [Pierce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierce,_Idaho "Pierce, Idaho"), [Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headquarters,_Idaho "Headquarters, Idaho"), and a number of places along the North Fork of the [Clearwater River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearwater_River_\(Idaho\) "Clearwater River (Idaho)"). In 1970, her family moved to [Lewiston, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho "Lewiston, Idaho"), where Barnes graduated from [Lewiston High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston_High_School_\(Idaho\) "Lewiston High School (Idaho)") in 1976.[^2]

Barnes received her [B.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") in English from [Lewis-Clark State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis-Clark_State_College "Lewis-Clark State College") in 1983, her [M.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Arts "Master of Arts") in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_studies "English studies") from [Washington State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University "Washington State University") in 1985, and her [M.F.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts "Master of Fine Arts") in [Creative Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Writing "Creative Writing") from the [University of Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Montana "University of Montana") in 1995. Barnes teaches [creative writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_writing "Creative writing") at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho"),[^3] and lives with her husband, [Robert Wrigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Wrigley "Robert Wrigley"), a poet, in [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"). They have three children.[^4]

Barnes's creative works addresses subjects including the American West, religious fundamentalism, women's issues, logging, and the environment, and reflects her interest in feminist interpretations of mythology and Jungian archetypes.[^5] In *A Country Called Home,* one of her main characters has the condition known as synesthesia and sees color when she hears music.

Her work has appeared widely in anthologies and journals, including *[The Georgia Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Georgia_Review "The Georgia Review")*, *[Shenandoah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenandoah_\(magazine\) "Shenandoah (magazine)")*, *MORE Magazine*, and the [Pushcart Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushcart_Prize "Pushcart Prize") anthology.

Barnes appeared on the [Lit Hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lit_Hub "Lit Hub") /Podglomerate podcast [Storybound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storybound "Storybound").[^6] [^7]

## Awards

She is the recipient of two grants from the Idaho Commission on the Arts. In 1995, she was chosen to receive the [PEN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_PEN "International PEN") /Jerard fellowship given to an emerging woman writer of nonfiction.[^8] In 1997, she was honored with a [Pacific Northwest Booksellers Association Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest_Booksellers_Association_Award "Pacific Northwest Booksellers Association Award") for *In the Wilderness*, which was also a finalist for the [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize "Pulitzer Prize"), and the [Quality Paperback Book Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_Paperback_Book_Club "Quality Paperback Book Club") 's New Visions Award.[^9] From 2004 to 2007, she served as Idaho Writer-in-Residence.[^10]
