# Abhorsen — Old Kingdom Lore Research Report

> A consolidated reference on the Abhorsen, the world of the Old Kingdom, and the three Free Magic beings (the Well, Mogget, the Disreputable Dog) — drawn from Garth Nix's *Old Kingdom* / *Abhorsen* series.

---

## 1. The Abhorsen & the Bloodline

### The office
The Abhorsen is the one who walks in Death so the rest need not. Where a necromancer pulls the Dead *back* across the border, the Abhorsen drives them down — past the Nine Gates and their precincts — to the final death from which there is no return. The office uniquely weds two opposed powers:

- **Charter Magic** — ordered, lawful; drawn from the Charter Stones, the Wall, and the bloodlines.
- **Free Magic** — wild, corrosive; the power necromancers wield.

The Abhorsen's bells are themselves a blend of both. The name echoes "Abhorson," the executioner in Shakespeare's *Measure for Measure* — the Abhorsen is, in the end, an executioner of the Dead who will not stay dead.

### The bloodline
One of the great Charter bloodlines (alongside the **Clayr**, the **Wallmakers**, and the **Royal** line), all descended from the Seven Bright Shiners who gave themselves into the Charter. The Abhorsen line is the only one whose originating Bright Shiner is *named*: **Astarael**, the Weeper.

**Inheritance is not strictly child-to-child.** The title passes within the blood, but the heir may be a niece, nephew, cousin, or sibling — whoever is fit. Hence an understudy is always named in advance.

### The Abhorsen-in-Waiting
The designated successor, who spends years — sometimes decades — at the side of the sitting Abhorsen learning to bind the Dead. Because the true bells are lethal to the half-trained, the Waiting one trains with **panpipes**: weaker echoes of the seven bells — less able, far less likely to send the apprentice walking into Death by accident.

## 2. The Seven Bells

Smallest to largest, each named for a Bright Shiner:

| # | Bell | Name | Effect |
|---|------|------|--------|
| 1 | **Ranna** | the Sleeper | Lulls the hearer to sleep |
| 2 | **Mosrael** | the Waker | Wakes the Dead into Life — but throws the *ringer* deeper into Death (balance always paid) |
| 3 | **Kibeth** | the Walker | Grants or compels movement; can force the Dead to walk — or walk *themselves* into Death |
| 4 | **Dyrim** | the Speaker | Restores or strips the power of speech |
| 5 | **Belgaer** | the Thinker | Restores or erases memory and independent thought |
| 6 | **Saraneth** | the Binder | Dominates and compels the Dead to the ringer's will — the Abhorsen's favored bell |
| 7 | **Astarael** | the Weeper (Sorrowful) | Casts *everyone* who hears it — including the ringer — far into Death. Last resort. |

## 3. Genealogy of the Line

- **Terciel** — the 52nd Abhorsen; father of both heirs.
- **Sabriel** — Terciel's daughter (mother Elinor, of Clayr blood). Becomes Abhorsen, later Queen of the Old Kingdom alongside **Touchstone**.
- **Lirael Goldenhand** — Terciel's daughter by **Arielle**, a Clayr who *Saw* she must bear his child, ~18 years after Elinor's death. Thus **Lirael is Sabriel's half-sister** — raised among the Clayr, ignorant of her father, until she comes into her inheritance as **Abhorsen-in-Waiting**.
- **Sameth** — son of Sabriel and Touchstone; first mistaken as the next Abhorsen-in-Waiting, but his true calling is **Wallmaker** — illustrating how the blood does not always pass the office where expected.

At the saga's height: **Terciel sits, Sabriel inherits, Lirael waits** — the elder sister Abhorsen, the younger sister Abhorsen-in-Waiting.

## 4. Geography of the World

### The fundamental division: the Wall
An ancient edifice (analogue to Hadrian's Wall) running east–west, the seam between two incompatible realities:

- **South of the Wall** — magic fails, technology works.
- **North of the Wall** — the Charter holds, magic lives, machines die.

Weather respects the boundary; the southern face is garrisoned by the Ancelstierran Army's **Northern Perimeter** (Crossing Point Scouts), who carry both rifles and swords because gunpowder is unreliable and the Dead don't fall to bullets.

### Ancelstierre — the South
A ~1920s nation: trains, motorcars, telephones, parliamentary government, standing army; most citizens disbelieve in magic (harder the closer to the Wall). Sites: **Wyverley College** (girls' school in the far north, the only Ancelstierran school teaching Charter Magic; Sabriel's school); **Corvere** (capital, far south); **Bain**.

### The Old Kingdom — the heart
- **River Ratterlin** — the great central river; plunges over the **Long Cliffs** in a vast waterfall, then bends east.
- **Abhorsen's House** — on an **island in the Ratterlin just above the falls**, ringed by fast water (the Dead and Free Magic cannot cross running water — a natural fortress).
- **Belisaere** — capital and largest city, on the **northeastern coast**; abandoned during the Interregnum, reclaimed under Touchstone and Sabriel.
- **Red Lake** and **Mount Abed** — in the west.
- **Great Charter Stones** — six massive stones in an underground reservoir/lake beneath the Royal Palace at Belisaere; the chief source of the Charter. Kerrigor broke two with the blood of his mother and sisters, triggering the Interregnum; Touchstone later repaired them via a life-draining blood ritual.

### The Clayr's Glacier — far north of the Kingdom
An ice-sheathed mountain, home to the **Clayr** (oracular sisterhood who See the futures) and the **Great Library of the Clayr** — a spiraling labyrinth of books, relics, and bound dangers. Lirael grew up here.

### The North & beyond
- **Greenwash River** — the Kingdom's northern boundary; rises in the **Athask Mountains**, runs west-to-east, bisecting the continent; spanned by the fortified **Greenwash Bridge**.
- **The North / Far North** — lower and higher steppes plus mountain ranges; nomadic clans; treated as another world impinging on the Old Kingdom (as Ancelstierre does from the south).
- **The Great Rift** — a jagged canyon at the extreme northeast; beyond it, **The Empty Lands**.

**The spectrum:** technological, magic-dead Ancelstierre → the Wall → Charter-bound Old Kingdom → the Greenwash → the wild steppe-North → the Great Rift → the Empty Lands.

### The geography of Death
Death is its own country — a grey, cold **river** flowing through **Nine Gates**, each a precinct with its own peril, pulling toward the final ninth gate. The living walk it in spirit while their body stands frozen in Life. It overlaps the living world everywhere at once — which is why running water in Life echoes the river of Death, and why the border must be kept.

## 5. The Nine Bright Shiners (cosmological frame)

Before the Beginning there were **nine** primordial Free Magic powers. When **Orannis, the Destroyer** (the Ninth) set out to annihilate all life — the Old Kingdom's world being the seventh planet it meant to burn — the other eight confronted it:

- **Seven** bound Orannis and then *made the Charter*, pouring themselves into it. Their power lives in the **seven bells**; they reincarnated into anchors — two into the **Great Charter Stones and the Wall**, five into the **bloodlines** (Abhorsen, Clayr, Wallmaker, Royal).
- The **Eighth, Yrael**, refused to take a side and hid — caught and bound into servitude. He is **Mogget**.
- The **Ninth, Orannis**, was split into two hemispheres and buried, awaiting reassembly.

The three entities below are pieces of this: the Well holds the Seventh, Mogget is the Eighth, the Dog is the Third.

### The Well
In the grounds of **Abhorsen's House** is a deep, seemingly bottomless **well** — not a water source but a *passage* and prison-shrine. At its bottom dwells **Astarael, the Weeper** — the seventh Bright Shiner, the power that originated the Abhorsen's authority, whose voice is the most terrible bell. When Lirael, Sameth, the Dog, and Mogget descend it in *Abhorsen*, the Charter itself **vanishes** at the bottom and Mogget's binding slips loose — a measure of the raw power below. A doorway pressed against the origin of the whole office.

### Mogget — Yrael, the Eighth
Outwardly a small, irritable, hungry **white cat** in a red collar bearing a miniature **Saraneth** — the shackle compelling him to serve the current Abhorsen. His true form is **Yrael**, a Free Magic being of immense, dangerous power; the one Shiner who would not choose a side. Unbound he is no friend (in *Sabriel* he tries to kill her, then is rebound with **Ranna** after Saraneth is lost). Bound, he is a sardonic, treacherous-but-occasionally-loyal helper. At the final binding of Orannis he is offered a true choice for the first time in millennia — and *chooses*.

### The Disreputable Dog — Kibeth, the Third
A scruffy, joyful hound who appears to Lirael in the Great Library when Lirael casts a **Charter sending** based on a **soapstone statue of a dog** found in the hidden Moon Room (off the Sun Room). The spell ran out of control because the statue held the preserved mind of **Kibeth, the Walker** — the third Bright Shiner. So the Dog is woven of **both Charter and Free Magic**; she works small wonders (baptizes a Charter mark onto Lirael with a lick) and hides her identity almost to the end. Only at the **final binding of Orannis** does she confess and retake her place among the reconstituted Seven.

**The shape of it:** Astarael sleeps in the Well at the root of the Abhorsen's power; Yrael walks bound as Mogget, the holdout who chose neither side; Kibeth walks free as the Dog, a fragment of the Charter's makers made companion. Three of the Nine — one imprisoned, one enslaved, one loose and loyal — all reckoned with at the binding of the Destroyer.
