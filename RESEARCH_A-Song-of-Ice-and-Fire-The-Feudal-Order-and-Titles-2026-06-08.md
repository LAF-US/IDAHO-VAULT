# The Feudal Order of Westeros — Lords, Bannermen, and Titles

> The in-world counterpart to the real-world `[[RESEARCH_Noble-Titles-and-Feudal-Hierarchy-2026-06-08]]`: how the Seven Kingdoms are held — King → Lords Paramount → bannermen → landed knights → smallfolk — and the deliberately **flat** Westerosi set of noble titles. ASOIAF-canon research.

> **Provenance.** Web-verified 2026-06-08 (A Wiki of Ice and Fire, Wiki of Westeros). Tiers: **[canon]**, ⚠️ **[show]** where book and screen differ. Paraphrased; no source text reproduced.

## The pyramid

**[canon]** Westerosi feudalism runs on **personal oaths of allegiance**, not an abstract nation-state, dividing a hereditary **nobility** above the commoner **smallfolk**:

- **The King** on the Iron Throne — sovereign of the Seven Kingdoms.
- **Lords Paramount** — the **Great Houses**, rulers of the (former) kingdoms/regions: the descendants or successors of the old kings Aegon conquered (Stark in the North, Lannister in the West, Tyrell in the Reach, Arryn in the Vale, Baratheon in the Stormlands, Tully in the Riverlands, Greyjoy in the Iron Islands, Martell in Dorne).
- **Vassal lords / bannermen** — the lesser Houses sworn to a Great House. The structure is layered: **Principal Houses** lead each kingdom; some pledge fealty **directly** to the Lord Paramount, others are **vassals of vassals** (e.g., the Webbers and Osgreys are bannermen of the Rowans, who are bannermen of the Tyrells).
- **Landed & household knights** — hold a holdfast or serve in a lord's hall.
- **Smallfolk** — the commoners at the base, who work the land.

**[canon]** Inheritance is by **primogeniture** — the designated heir takes all, to the exclusion of younger siblings.

## The titles — deliberately flat

**[canon]** GRRM **chose not to use** the graded European peerage (no **duke, marquess, earl/count, viscount, baron**). The whole set is:

| Title | Who holds it |
|---|---|
| **King / Queen** | the sovereign (addressed **"Your Grace"**) |
| **Prince / Princess** | the royal house (and the Prince of Dorne) |
| **Lord / Lady** | the **head of a lordly House** (great, major, or minor) — a grant of lordship normally comes with **lands or a castle** |
| **Ser** | a **knight** (anointed) — an honour, *separate* from lordship |
| **Master / Lady** | lesser landholders |

**[canon]** "**Lord**" is also given as a **courtesy** to small-council members and others in power (an honorific, distinct from a true grant of lordship); and "**my lord / my lady**" is used for almost anyone of recognized noble birth, ruling or not.

## The King's style

**[canon]** The full royal style: *"[Name] of House [Name], the [Nth] of His Name, **King of the Andals, the Rhoynar, and the First Men, Lord of the Seven Kingdoms, and Protector of the Realm**."* ⚠️ **[show]** the series often drops "**the Rhoynar**."

## Lords Paramount & Wardens

**[canon]** A **Lord Paramount** is the crown's chief lord over a whole region (the title formalized as Aegon's Conquest folded the seven kingdoms into one realm → `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-Aegons-Conquest-2026-06-02]]`). Distinct from it is a **military** command title — **Warden of the North / South / East / West** — usually held by the great lords. *(⚠️ Exact Warden-to-House assignments not re-verified in this pass.)*

## Fealty & bannermen

**[canon]** The bond is the **oath**: a vassal swears **fealty** to his **liege lord**, owing **counsel, arms, and men**; the liege owes **protection and justice**. When a lord "**calls his banners**," his **bannermen** muster their sworn swords to his host. Loyalty is personal and chained — a man may owe a vassal-lord who in turn owes a Great House who owes the king.

## Contrast with real-world feudalism

**[reading]** Against the European ladder (→ `[[RESEARCH_Noble-Titles-and-Feudal-Hierarchy-2026-06-08]]`), Westeros is **flatter and more personal**: no graded ranks between king and lord — just **King / Lord Paramount / Lord** — so precedence runs on the **prestige and antiquity of the House** rather than a duke-over-count-over-baron scale. And **knighthood (Ser)**, anointed by the **Faith of the Seven**, is an **honour orthogonal to land-rank** — a hedge knight may be a "ser" with no lands, while a great Northern lord (who keeps the old gods) may be no knight at all. The single word "**lord**" carries what real-world Europe split across five titles plus the manorial lordship.
