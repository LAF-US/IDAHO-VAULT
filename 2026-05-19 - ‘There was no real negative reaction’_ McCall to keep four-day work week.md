Thursday is the new Friday — at least in the City of McCall.

Last week, the McCall City Council signed off on a request by City Manager Forest Atkinson to permanently change the city’s work week to Monday through Thursday.

The move follows a six-month trial that began last December in a bid to reduce burnout and increase job satisfaction among the city’s 90 full-time employees. A survey of city employees rated satisfaction with the four-day schedule at 4.66 out of 5.

“What we’ve found overall is the employees who are able to benefit from this really find it of great value,” Atkinson said. “It’s allowed them to increase their work-life balance, and I’ve heard from a lot of staff that they feel even more productive.”

The four-day schedule applies to all city workers, but varies depending on the staffing needs of some city departments. Department heads plan staffing to ensure continued coverage of critical city functions, such as police and public works.

Adoption of the four-day schedule coincided with changing the city’s hours to 7:30 a.m. – 5:30 p.m., adding 30 minutes to the beginning and end of the city’s previous hours. Lunch breaks were also reduced to 30 minutes instead of an hour.

Total hours worked by city employees will remain similar under the new four-day model despite losing Friday as a workday. For 2026, the four-day schedule will result in 200 workdays totaling 2,000 hours, compared to 249 workdays totaling 1,992 hours under a five-day schedule.

During the trial, the schedule change coincided with a 9% drop in the number of sick days taken by employees when compared to the same period last year, according to data collected by the city.

The city also saw a 58% drop in overtime hours worked, though much of that Atkinson attributed to limited snowplowing this year amid historically low snowfall in McCall.

Atkinson credited the city’s public outreach efforts during the trial for a generally positive response from local residents.

“My observation is that there was no real negative reaction from the community,” Atkinson said.

Council Member Lyle Nelson agreed.

“I heard from the community about it, but I didn’t hear anything negative,” Nelson said.

Driggs, Moscow, Victor, and Bellevue are among cities in Idaho that have tested four-day workweeks. The four-day schedule was retained in each of the cities except Moscow. The City of Donnelly also operates on a four-day schedule.
