A proposal to trade thousands of acres of state land around Payette Lake could help maintain public access to the lands, according to the Idaho Department of Lands.

On Monday, IDL staffers briefed Valley County officials on the framework of a proposal that would trade nearly 16,000 acres of land around Payette Lake to the Payette National Forest.

In return, the state would receive up to 150,000 acres of timberlands from four national forests stretching between Boise and Sandpoint — though the exact details are still being determined.

The trade would return productive timberlands to IDL while ridding it of high-value lands around Payette Lake that, [in recent years, have been in the crosshairs of developers](https://boisedev.com/news/2021/08/10/trident-mccall-denied/).

“If it becomes Forest Service land, it’ll be there in perpetuity,” said Jake Strohmeyer, IDL’s operations division administrator. “The threat of needing to sell parts of it to meet our mandate and things like that are gone.”

State endowment lands are managed primarily to benefit public schools and state hospitals. The Idaho Constitution mandates IDL to maximize long-term profits from the lands, which are mostly managed for timber, with some leases for grazing, recreation, and mining.

The state’s endowment lands around Payette Lake currently produce an annual return of about $500,000, or less than 1% of the land’s total estimated valuation of $230 million, according to IDL Director Dustin Miller.

“We love that ground too, but we’ve got an obligation to produce revenue on there,” Miller said. “Because of the value of that ground, we’ve got a major income gap where it’s very difficult for us to meet that mandate.”

Valley County does not have the authority to reject the proposal, but broad political support could ease the federal legislative process needed to approve the Forest Service’s involvement in the trade. In April, the State Land Board directed IDL to complete more detailed work on the proposal and seek public comments, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2026/05/06/a-good-deal-for-idaho-state-likes-payette-lake-land-swap-proposal-but-questions-remain/).

Next week, IDL will host three public meetings on the land exchange proposal in Cascade, McCall, and New Meadows. More information on those meetings is [available here](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1J84qYhEjo/).

The land exchange must first be approved by the State Land Board, at which point the proposal would be vetted by the Forest Service and federal lawmakers.

“We know it’s going to take time,” Miller said, referencing other land exchanges that have taken as long as 10 years to complete. “We’re trying to jump out there now to lay the groundwork.”

Miller would not specify how soon the trade could be agreed upon, but said IDL staff are already working with federal legislators on a bill that would authorize the land exchange. U.S. Forest Service Chief Tom Schultz, a former logging executive in Idaho, has also been briefed on the deal.

“He’s asked his folks to be involved with this and work with us and come up with the strategy,” Miller said of Schultz.

## Forest management and Ponderosa State Park expansion

The county commissioners did not take a position on the proposal, but said their top priorities are protecting recreation and forest management to reduce wildfire risk.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“IDL is much more active and proactive when it comes to timber management and so I have concerns about turning it over,” Commissioner Katlin Caldwell said.

Besides wildfire danger, water quality in Payette Lake is also at stake, Caldwell said, referencing erosion into the lake that would follow a fire on lands around the lake.

Sherry Maupin, who chairs the board, echoed Caldwell’s comments and suggested timber management standards could be built into federal legislation authorizing the trade.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

Maupin also proposed that some of the endowment land around Payette Lake could become part of Ponderosa State Park — a suggestion to which Miller was receptive.

“They do want to expand, and if we can play a role in that, we would love to — as long as the endowments are made whole,” he said. “I think there are some things that we can certainly work on.”

## Wholesale trade favored

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/05/valleylookout.com-a-good-deal-for-idaho-state-likes-payette-lake-land-exchange-proposal-screenshot-2026-05-06-at-4.33.02-pm.jpeg)

Currently, IDL is weighing four variations of a land exchange involving the Payette Lake endowment lands. The agency expects to identify its preferred option this summer.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The state would trade all 15,700 acres of endowment land around the lake to the Forest Service, under Alternative 1.

Alternatives 2, 3, and 4 would see the state retain up to 2,500 acres of the endowment land closest to McCall — specifically, large tracts off the southern end of Warren Wagon Road and Eastside Drive.

“Those are the most sensitive areas, so if we leave these out of the land exchange, those will get sold in the future,” Maupin said. “That’s the whole purpose of doing this is to protect those lands, protect Payette Lake and the quality of the lake into the future.”

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

IDL has not officially selected a preferred alternative, but Strohmeyer told Maupin the agency also prefers a wholesale trade.

“We think it makes sense for IDL to exchange out all of it rather than retain some of it, but folks had asked for some options,” he said.

The prospect of a land exchange with the Forest Service gained steam after [a 2020 proposal that sought to privatize the Payette Lake endowment lands](https://boisedev.com/news/2021/08/10/trident-mccall-denied/) in exchange for timberlands in northern Idaho.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

That proposal, submitted by Trident Holdings of Boise, was ultimately rejected, but put a renewed focus on IDL’s constitutional mandate to maximize long-term returns from endowment lands

[United Payette](https://www.unitedpayette.org/), an advocacy group that organized in the wake of Trident’s proposal, remains among the biggest supporters of the proposed land exchange.

“This exchange may be our only real hope for maintaining public access and protecting the North Fork of the Payette River watershed,” said Brian Harris, a retired Forest Service staffer who serves on the group’s steering committee.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

Craig Utter, a steering committee member who is also the executive director of the Payette Land Trust, framed the proposal as a “win, win, win situation.”

“It’s a win for the Forest Service because of continuity of management, it’s a win for Idaho because Payette Lake is a gem at the top of the watershed, it’s a win for the endowment, and it’s a win for Valley County and people who enjoy natural places,” Utter said.
