The question of where to build a much-needed city maintenance and fire department training facility is heading back to the drawing board.

On Monday, the Eagle Planning and Zoning Commission considered whether to authorize a conditional use permit and a number of waivers for the construction of a building and maintenance yard, as well as a 41-ft. burn tower for use in training exercises by the Eagle Fire Department. Staff proposed the project for the northeast corner of Floating Feather Rd. and N. Horseshoe Bend Rd. The proposed location, owned by the city, encompasses about seven acres of the Ada/Eagle Bike Park.

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/eagle-rejects-building-project-that-would-let-citys-firefighters-train-on-citys-own-land-Screenshot-2026-08-0-1.jpeg)

Images show the location of a proposed new maintenance and firefighter training facility at the Ada/Eagle Bike Park, and a proposed site map. The large square in the middle would be the location of the burn tower. Images: Via City of Eagle

The planning and zoning commission hears land use cases and votes whether to recommend approval of the projects to the Eagle City Council. The commission voted to send the proposal back to city staff to rework in a way that would allow the consideration of maintenance facilities at the site, but not a burn tower and other fire training facilities.

Vice Chairman Steve Guerber, who made the motion, said, “I know it leaves the fire department hanging out there, but I think that seems to be where I’m hearing the greatest concern — a 41-ft. facility and the fact that this for years has been considered for maintenance for the City of Eagle.”

Testimony from dozens of residents, including several Eagle High mountain bike team coaches, also featured concerns about the loss of trails in the Ada/Eagle Bike park because of the project and the impact of smoke during fire training exercises on park users.

## Maintenance, fire training needs

Eagle Fire Chief Tyler Lewis told the commission that currently, the department’s staff must train at facilities owned by the City of Boise and City of Star. Lewis said the department has sought its own space for 26 years, constrained by a lack of viable city-owned land and high land prices.

The city, represented by Amanda Bidwell of EV Studio, said the plan called for a building that would provide classrooms, offices, and maintenance bays. It also proposed outdoor training areas and a burn tower, a rearrangeable structure made up of cargo containers that could reach a maximum height of 41 feet. City code caps building heights at 35 feet, so the department sought a waiver to be able to have a taller structure.

Lewis, the fire chief, said exercises with the burn tower would only take place under the right conditions, including having enough “lift” to carry smoke up into the air and away from the park and nearby homes. To create smoke, such exercises typically use treated lumber or straw, but not materials that could send embers outward, he said.

Bidwell said that the plans make an effort to relocate existing recreational trails in the Ada/Eagle Bike Park. She noted that the site in question would take about 2% of the park’s 700 acres out of service for recreation.

Eagle Public Works Director Eric Ziegenfuss estimated the cost of the project at $3.2 million. The city has worked to save that money over the past eight years, he said.

The proposal includes a number of waiver requests, including for the type of fencing surrounding the facility, landscaping tree placement, and to allow shipping containers on a site for more than 30 days.

## ‘This spot is wrong’

Many who testified publicly said that while they understood the need for such facilities, they wanted to see them in a different area.

![Corwin Ford - One Tank Trips - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/9934db1b-47e1-4fe3-ab77-78261066f89f.jpg)

Corwin Ford - One Tank Trips - 2:1

Kathy Ellison, a nearby resident, said that the project would be most appropriate for industrially zoned land instead of in areas near residences and a park.

“I know that this is tempting because the city owns this land,” Ellison said, “and they want this maintenance yard, and I agree, but putting it in this spot is wrong.”

Ziegenfuss said staff considered other city-owned properties to construct the building, including the city’s regional athletic park, Guerber Park, and Merrill Park. Those options, as well as the Ada/Eagle Bike Park property, were previously brought before the Eagle City Council. He said that the sports park, once fully built out, would not have room for the facility unless they were to nix plans for a baseball field or soccer field. Merrill Park’s parking issues wouldn’t be conducive to a facility, he said. The council opted for the bike park site as the most viable option.

![Western States - August 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/20757b72-0dba-404b-9b0c-9237c6d52f4f.jpg)

Ziegenfuss estimated that it would cost the city at least $2 million to purchase seven to eight acres for such a facility. The city at one time worked with a realtor to try to find a property for purchase, Ziegenfuss said. The city attempted to partner with the Eagle Sewer District to acquire land, but the district did not have land it could give up, he said. Staff also looked at the concrete plant at the intersection of Highway 55 and Highway 44. Because that land is slated for riverfront development when the plant’s use is over, the value of the land is high, he said. Leaving no other options available to the city without raising taxes or putting a bond or levy before voters.

Chairman Trent Wright said that the only way he would remotely support approving the project at the Ada/Eagle Bike Park would be to place even more conditions on it, such as heavy vegetation screening to make it less visible.

Addressing Lewis, Wright said, “You’ve got a tough, tough job and a huge responsibility. But you’ve been working on this for six years, and I think you’re going to find a location. I just don’t think that this is the right location for it.”
