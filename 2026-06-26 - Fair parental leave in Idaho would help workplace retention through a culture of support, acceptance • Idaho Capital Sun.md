## Fair parental leave in Idaho would help workplace retention through a culture of support, acceptance

Employers that don’t create space for the transition to parenthood will lose people, and most of them will be women, write guest columnists Tara Jalali Malek and Cora Whitney.

Starting July 1, employees at the Idaho Attorney General’s office [will see their paid parental leave cut](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/idaho-attorney-generals-office-to-cut-parental-leave-to-two-weeks-ending-extended-leave/) from eight weeks to two. The eight-week benefit, established by [Gov. Brad Little’s 2020 executive order](https://gov.idaho.gov/pressrelease/governor-little-signs-families-first-act-guarantees-state-employees-eight-weeks-of-paid-parental-leave/), remains a baseline for many of Idaho’s executive and state offices.

As a women-majority firm with offices in Idaho and Washington, we’ve spent years asking what it takes to keep talented people in the workforce, especially as they face significant life transitions. When it comes to welcoming a new adopted or birthed child, our solution is providing 16 weeks of paid parental leave, a collaborative team structure, and a workplace culture built around supporting the whole person.

Cora Whitney, who leads our business, real estate, and healthcare teams, has lived both sides of that question.

## Cora’s first child

Like so many mothers, deciding to go back to work after the birth of her daughter was hard for Cora. According to the U.S. Census, abou [t 17% of first-time mothers quit either before or after the birth of their child](https://www.census.gov/library/stories/2025/05/parental-leave.html), a significant exit point from the workforce tied to the transition to parenthood.

Armed with a pack-n-play on one side of her desk and bouncer on the other, Cora chose to stay in the private law firm market and came to work at Malek + Malek with her infant in tow. Far from being a big deal, it was joyful to have a baby around. Colleagues took turns holding Cora’s daughter in between meetings. Clients never flinched (in fact, some cooed).

More than schedule flexibility, what made it possible for Cora to return to work full time was finding a firm that let her be a mom and a lawyer both, without asking her to choose between those identities.

Among all the challenges new parents might expect, Cora also battled postpartum depression for years following the birth of her daughter. As she approached hard conversations with Tara, she was met with the same question every time, “What do you need?”

## “What do you need?” As Workplace Culture

Malek + Malek’s mission is to change the world for the better, and that includes our staff and attorney’s worlds, too. We worked together to create a workplace that supported Cora as a whole human, not just as a billable hour attorney.

Knowing the emotional burden Cora was shouldering, Tara met with her everyday to open channels of consistent communication and ensure that Cora and her clients were receiving the care they needed. Cora credits this level of support as what kept her in the workforce.

We’ve learned over the years and through speaking with Malek + Malek team members that two weeks is not enough time for a body to recover from childbirth, establish feeding routines, stabilize sleep, or begin to surface from the fog of postpartum depression, a condition that affects roughly [1 in 5 new mothers](https://policycentermmh.org/mmh-disorders/) and rarely resolves in a matter of weeks.

Framing that reality as a line item to cut misreads both the human cost and the business case. The “silver tsunami” of Boomer retirements is underway, and the generations coming up behind are not entering the workforce in the same numbers. In our current society, benefits that reflect what matters to employees are, by extension, part of a larger retention strategy.

## Cora’s second child

When Cora went on leave for her second child five years later, she was eligible to use Malek + Malek’s 16-week paid parental leave policy, which covers births and adoptions. While away, our collaborative team model, unique in law, ensured that her clients’ matters were advanced by other attorneys.

After four months, Cora returned to Malek + Malek as a focused, motivated, mother of two who was ready to show baby pictures to anyone who asked.

## Creating space for new parents

Employers that don’t create space for the transition to parenthood will lose people, and most of them will be women. That is a talent pipeline with skillsets, institutional knowledge, client relationships, and in some cases, years of taxpayer-funded training with them. That pipeline is already narrow in law, where [women represent roughly 37% of the legal workforce.](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/show/the-barriers-women-still-face-in-the-legal-profession-decades-after-oconnors-appointment)

The Legislature should make eight weeks of paid parental leave a floor, not a suggestion. Meanwhile, employers have to decide what type of culture they want to build for and with their people. That means asking employees what they need, working together to find creative solutions, and structuring a workplace so that no one has to suppress who they are in order to survive in it.
