New Meadows-area voters will decide this fall whether to create a new ambulance district that would no longer provide EMS service to — or collect property taxes from — Brundage Mountain Resort.

On Monday, the Adams County Board of County Commissioners approved a petition that seeks to dissolve an existing ambulance district and replace it with a new district.

The new district would be governed by appointed commissioners who live within the service area rather than the county commissioners, only one of whom lives within the district.

The new ambulance district’s boundaries would be similar to the existing district, but would exclude Brundage to avoid overwhelming limited staffing with calls at the resort — particularly during the winter and as [residential development ramps up in its base area](https://valleylookout.com/2026/04/17/this-season-attacked-us-brundage-caps-season-on-schedule-despite-lowest-snowfall-on-record/).

Supporters said the proposal would provide local control over long-term EMS planning and growth, while opponents said excluding Brundage would raise property taxes for the rest of the district.

If approved by a simple majority of voters in the Nov. 3 election, the new district would be formed on Dec. 31, 2027.

The county commissioners split 2-1 in favor of approving the petition, with Chairman Joe Iveson switching his vote from [last spring when the Meadows Valley Rural Fire Protection District sought to put the measure on the May ballot](https://valleylookout.com/2026/03/12/adams-co-delays-new-meadows-area-ems-decision-amid-uncertain-tax-implications/).

“I think it’s time for the people to decide,” Iveson said, adding that he personally thinks it’s too soon to break from the existing EMS model.

Iveson was joined in voting to approve the petition by Commissioner Daren Ward, who supported putting it to a vote last May.

Commissioner Viki Purdy, the only county commissioner who lives in Meadows Valley, voted against the proposal amid concerns that excluding Brundage will prove costly to taxpayers.

“The future of Brundage is going to be quite lucrative and de-annexing them will cost all of us here in this valley,” Purdy said. “I think it’s a huge mistake.”

## EMS contract

Currently, Adams County provides EMS service in the northern part of the county through a contract with the fire district, which filed the petition to create a new ambulance district.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The current ambulance district levies about $221,000 in property taxes that the county reroutes to the fire district through the EMS contract.

The EMS levy collects about $28 per 100,000 in assessed value from property owners in the district, according to Adams County Clerk Sherry Ward.

The fire district is not proposing a levy increase for the new district, but a final decision would be made by the commissioners appointed to govern the district.

## Brundage exclusion

The proposed district would mirror the existing ambulance district except Brundage Mountain Resort would be excluded.

That would shift the resort’s share of the property tax burden onto about 1,250 other property owners in the district. Brundage paid about $9,000 in EMS property taxes in 2025, and that figure is expected to exceed $30,000 in 2026 because of new residential development at the resort.

MV Fire has responded to one EMS call at the resort in the last two years due to the creation of Brundage Mountain Emergency Medical Services, which in 2024 became a state licensed EMS agency certified to transport patients by ambulance.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

Prior to the creation of that service, MV Fire was legally responsible for responding to EMS calls requiring transport at the resort. However, most calls were handled by McCall Fire and EMS under a mutual aid agreement that could not be renewed after 2023 due to a change in state law.

MV Fire officials worry long response times to the resort could leave the agency vulnerable to not being able to quickly respond to other calls in the district. Fire district officials said that vulnerability could become a problem again if Brundage EMS ever dissolved — unless Brundage is no longer included in the ambulance district.

The resort’s ski patrol responds to about 150 calls per winter, about 20 to 25 of which require transport by ambulance, according to Brundage General Manager Ken Rider.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

Brundage believes the “best long-term outcome” is a district that includes the resort, but Rider said the resort is prepared for either outcome following this November’s vote.

“We are confident in our ability to continue providing a high level of emergency medical service to Brundage Mountain guests and residents while supporting our regional response partners,” he said.

The creation of a private fire protection association and the addition of ambulance transport to Brundage’s emergency capabilities follows a 2022 approval by Adams County for the resort to build 88 homes at the base area.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

The homes mark the first phase of development for Brundage Mountain Village as envisioned by a 2009 master plan that calls for up to 1,200 homes, townhomes and hotel rooms to be built at the resort.

*CORRECTION: A previous version of this story incorrectly portrayed the Brundage Mountain Fire Protection Association and Brundage Mountain Emergency Medical Services as the same entity when in fact they are separate organizations*.
