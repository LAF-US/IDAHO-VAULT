**Thomas Lee Wright** is an American author, screenwriter and [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") -nominated filmmaker. A Minnesota native, Wright attended [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University"), writing and directing plays and earning a degree in English Literature with honors. He studied Irish Theater at [Trinity College, Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College_Dublin "Trinity College Dublin"), and played point guard for its national championship [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball "Basketball") team.

Moving to Los Angeles, Wright became a story editor at [The Walt Disney Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Walt_Disney_Company "The Walt Disney Company") and [Columbia Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Pictures "Columbia Pictures"), then worked as a creative executive at [Paramount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") where he helped develop *[48 Hrs.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48_Hrs. "48 Hrs.")*, *[Trading Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_Places "Trading Places")*, and *[Flashdance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashdance "Flashdance").* His first screenwriting assignment was writing a draft of *[The Godfather Part III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Godfather_Part_III "The Godfather Part III")*. This led to authoring projects for [Peter Guber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Guber "Peter Guber"), [Dino De Laurentiis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_De_Laurentiis "Dino De Laurentiis"), [Mike Medavoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Medavoy "Mike Medavoy"), and [Daniel Melnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Melnick "Daniel Melnick"), among others. Wright wrote the original screenplay for the [Warner Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Brothers "Warner Brothers") film *[New Jack City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jack_City "New Jack City")*. which the [New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times "New York Times") called "an urban classic" on the 25th anniversary of its premiere. He was nominated for [Best Documentary Short Subject](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Documentary_Short_Subject "Best Documentary Short Subject") at the [90th Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/90th_Academy_Awards "90th Academy Awards") for *[Edith+Eddie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith+Eddie "Edith+Eddie")*.

Wright is well known for his films about social justice issues, including:

- *[Edith+Eddie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith+Eddie "Edith+Eddie")*; (producer) which won the IDA ([International Documentary Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Documentary_Association "International Documentary Association")) Best Short award in 2017, along with Emmy and Oscar nominations.
- *Eight-Tray Gangster* (director) for the [Discovery Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Channel "Discovery Channel") about the [1992 Los Angeles riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Los_Angeles_riots "1992 Los Angeles riots") from a gang member's perspective.
- Trade Off (producer) about the [1999 Seattle WTO protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Seattle_WTO_protests "1999 Seattle WTO protests") for [Human Rights Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Rights_Watch "Human Rights Watch").

Three of his films deal with war veterans:

- *The Long Ride Home* (director)
- *To Them That's Gone* (executive producer)
- *Last Flag Flying* (executive producer) Wright developed this as a feature film for director [Richard Linklater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Linklater "Richard Linklater").

As an author, Wright co-wrote two books about filmmaking:

- *Working in Hollywood* traces the making of a motion picture through tasks performed by 64 different workers behind the scenes
- *American Screenwriters* is a collection of interviews with top writers discussing the craft and business of screenwriting.
