A 480-acre development is taking steps forward in one of Meridian’s “last areas of growth.”

The Fields, a development represented by Mark Bottles Real Estate, is looking to get approval on its annexation into city limits, a future land use map amendment, and a preliminary plot of the first portion of the project. [As BoiseDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/03/18/homes-retail-industrial-space-planned-for-466-acres-in-north-meridian/) retail and commercial space, light industrial space, and several different kinds of housing are all planned for the project. It is located between Can Ada Rd. and Owyhee Storm Ave., and McMillian Rd. and Ustick Rd., near the border of Nampa and Meridian.

The developers went before Meridian’s Planning and Zoning Commission in May, where they got a recommendation of approval, and are set to go before the city council in July.

## What’s planned

The 480-acre master community is planned to be built out in multiple sections. The first portion to come forward is a 200-acre development called Westveil.

Westveil is located on the east side of the property. Just under 900 homes are planned for Westveil — 602 single-family homes and 297 townhomes.

The average lot size for the single-family homes is 5,741 square feet, and the average lot size for the townhomes is 2,400 square feet. The developer plans to build out Westveil in 7 phases between 2027 and 2033 — with the first home sale happening in 2028.

According to Emily Mueller, general counsel for Mark Bottles, Westveil is estimated to bring in $16.4 million in impact fees to the surrounding taxing districts.

According to Meridian Associate City Planner Linda Ritter, each time the developer wants to build out a new portion of the property, they will have to submit a development agreement modification in addition to a preliminary plat. The developer cannot submit for a final plat for city approval until sewer is brought down to the area.

In addition to the single-family homes and townhomes planned for Westveil, three different areas for apartments could come in future phases to The Fields.

The Fields also has two different commercial centers planned: McMillian Marketplace and Owyhee Center.

According to documents filed with the city, McMillian Marketplace is approximately 13.8 acres planned to be a “pedestrian-first” area with restaurants, retail, banking, “neighborhood services”, and spaces for office or daycares. Renderings shown at the planning and zoning meeting showed a photo of a Whole Foods Market.

Owyhee Center is 45 acres planned for fast casual restaurants, office space, retail, and a grocery store serving as an anchor.

The Fields also has 51.64 acres of industrial land and a 40-acre park included in its concept plans.

One change made to The Fields’ concept map since BoiseDev first reported on the development shows that 45 acres that were previously planned as medium-density residential use are now planned as “neighborhood business.”

## The May meeting

The commission voted on a 4-1 vote to recommend approval of the application to Meridian’s City Council. Commissioner Jessica Perreault was the lone no vote. Perreault said she was opposed to the project because of her experience with what played out with the Ten Mile Area Specific Plan when she was on city council.

![Morrison Center - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/37e7ef09-855c-4668-ba54-b9eb50afe557.png)

Morrison Center - July 2026 - 2:1

“What was originally envisioned did not come to be,” Perreault said. “What we also learned in Ten Mile is that things significantly change over 15 years time, or 10 years time. And so for that reason, I’m not in favor of approving a preliminary plat that is going to take 10 years to build out.”

Perreault also raised concerns about the city’s ability to build out the donated park land, especially given that the city has another regional park planned for Cherry Ln. and McDermott Rd., and said she was hesitant to convert it to open space from industrial because “we already are hearing so much that we’re short on industrial in the City of Meridian.”

The next public hearing for The Fields is currently scheduled for July 14, according to the city’s active hearing website.
