**Other online information:**

[Artists from Idaho](http://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Artists_from_Idaho) in Wikipedia. Accessed August, 2015.

[*Bart Walker: Tour the West*](https://bonecreek.org/exhibitions/past-exhibitions/bart-walker-tour-the-west/) is a 2023 exhibit at the [Bone Creek Museum of Agrarian Art](https://bonecreek.org/) which says: "Walker backpacks throughout his hometown region of Teton Valley, Idaho documenting oil sketches on site and uses those in the studio to recapture the liveliness of the moment on a larger canvas." Accessed 6/23

Courthouse Murals, by Dianna Cammarota, from *Boise City Revue*. Accessed August, 2015.

[*Idaho Art History: 1950-1945*](https://www.tfaoi.org/aa/14aa/14aa357.htm) is a 2026 Gemini 3 Deep Research Report which says: "The development of artistic expression in Idaho between 1850 and 1945 is a profound case study in the intersection of extreme geography, forced cultural transitions, and institutional isolation. Unlike the established cultural capitals on the eastern seaboard or the rapidly developing urban centers of California, Idaho during this ninety-five-year span was characterized by its rugged terrain, vast distances, and a sparse, highly distributed population. These environmental and social factors did not stifle artistic creativity; instead, they forged a distinct regional aesthetic heavily reliant on self-sufficiency, localized materials, and personal or tribal narratives rather than academic consensus. By examining the paintings, sculptures, pottery, and textiles produced during this era, a clearer picture emerges of how Idaho's unique environment shaped the evolution of its visual culture. Accessed 4/26

Laura McPhee: The River of No Return is a 2013 exhibit at the [Kemper Museum of Contemporary Art](https://www.kemperart.org/) which says: "A journey across geographic place, art historical precedents, and the very history of photographic image making find a dynamic relationship in The River of No Return, a series created by Laura McPhee over a multiyear residency in the Sawtooth Valley in central Idaho, supported by the Alturas Foundation." Also see artist's [website](https://lauramcphee.com/river-of-no-return/) Accessed 2/19

[*This is Bliss: Jon Horvath*](https://griffinmuseum.org/show/jon-horvath-bliss/) is a 2020 exhibit at the [Griffin Museum of Photography](https://griffinmuseum.org/) for which Jon Horvath says: " *This Is Bliss* is a transmedia narrative project investigating the vanishing roadside geography and culture of a rural Idaho town named Bliss. The project is philosophically rooted in a broad consideration of how entrenched mythologies of place and traditional mythologies of happiness collide, and are frequently confounded, in a location that bears a complex narrative of booms and busts and reflects the complicated history of American Idealism and Manifest Destiny." Also see [website](https://www.jonhorvath.net/) of artist. Accessed 10/20
