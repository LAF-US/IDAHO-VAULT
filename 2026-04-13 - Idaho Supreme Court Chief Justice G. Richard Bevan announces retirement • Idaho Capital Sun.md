## Idaho Supreme Court Chief Justice G. Richard Bevan announces retirement

Idaho Gov. Brad Little to appoint successor to Idaho’s highest court

![Idaho Supreme Court G. Richard Bevan State of the Judiciary](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/G.-Richard-Bevan-2025-judiciary.jpg)

Idaho Supreme Court G. Richard Bevan State of the Judiciary

Idaho Supreme Court Chief Justice G. Richard Bevan, center, delivers the 2025 State of the Judiciary address in the Idaho Senate on Jan. 15, 2025. (Courtesy of the Idaho Supreme Court)

Idaho Supreme Court Chief Justice G. Richard Bevan will retire from the state’s highest court in October, the Idaho Judicial Branch announced in a press release issued Monday afternoon.

Bevan, 66, has served on the Idaho Supreme Court since former Gov. Butch Otter appointed him to become the 56th justice of the Idaho Supreme Court in 2017.

Bevan was first appointed as a district judge in 2003. Prior to that, he spent 16 years practicing law in Twin Falls, including a term as Twin Falls County prosecutor.

“Serving the people of Idaho in the Judicial Branch for nearly 23 years has been one of the great honors of my professional life,” Bevan wrote in a letter announcing his retirement to Gov. Brad Little.

In Monday’s press release, Bevan said “Our place is to resolve disputes,” and when the public needs help “they can trust our courts.”

Under state law, Little will appoint a successor to succeed Bevan from a list of applicants provided by the Idaho Judicial Council.

The new justice would serve out the remainder of Bevan’s term, which does not end until January 2031. A nonpartisan election for the next six-year term would be held in 2030.

Bevan said the most meaningful time as a judge came as a district judge, when he presided over treatment courts that help individuals struggling with their mental health or substance abuse turn their lives around. In the press release, Bevan said he watched people turn their lives around right in front of him in treatment courts.

“Those days were the best time of the week as a trial court judge,” Bevan said.

During a speech to Idaho legislators earlier this year, [Bevan said judges, court personnel and elected officials are facing an increasing number of violent threats](https://idahocapitalsun.com/2026/01/21/idaho-supreme-court-chief-justice-says-rise-in-violent-threats-places-pressure-on-court-security/). In the speech, Bevan challenged legislators to prioritize safety and access to the court system that provides rights guaranteed under both the U.S. Constitution and Idaho Constitution.

“The state of Idaho has a legitimate interest in the security of courthouses where state employed judges conduct the public’s business,” Bevan said during the speech.

In conjunction with Monday’s retirement announcement, other justices praised Bevan’s long service to the state’s court system.

“Chief Justice Bevan will leave a legacy defined by his deep, abiding commitment to the rule of law and his innate sense of fairness,” Idaho Supreme Court Vice Chief Justice Robyn Brody said in a written statement Monday. “He has dedicated his career to serving our community with integrity, ensuring that every person is treated with respect and dignity in the courtroom.”

Idaho Judicial Branch officials said Bevan will remain in his role as chief justice of the Idaho Supreme Court until his retirement in October.

Idaho Supreme Court justices will then select a new chief justice from among themselves.
