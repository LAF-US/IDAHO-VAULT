# Tarot

**Tarot** is a deck of 78 picture-cards read as a language — each card a glyph
of fate, character, or transformation, and a spread a sentence assembled from
them. It is the **keystone bridge** of the vault's symbolic-language cluster: it
carries the **seven classical planets** of [[Alchemy]] and [[Hermeticism]] in
its trumps, its modern correspondence system was built by the *Hermetic* Order
of the Golden Dawn, and it is the deck [[Jung and alchemy|Carl Jung]] read as
"archetypes of transformation." Where the other nodes touch one or two threads
of the cluster, Tarot touches them all.

This is an original synthesis grounded in the sources listed at the end, not a
verbatim copy.

## The deck

The 78 cards divide in two:

- **The Major Arcana** ("greater secrets") — 22 trumps, numbered from **0 (The
  Fool)** through **XXI (The World)**: the Magician, High Priestess, Empress,
  Emperor, Hierophant, Lovers, Chariot, Strength, Hermit, Wheel of Fortune,
  Justice, Hanged Man, Death, Temperance, Devil, Tower, Star, Moon, Sun,
  Judgement, World. These are the archetypal cards — the "great forces."
- **The Minor Arcana** ("lesser secrets") — 56 pip cards in four suits (Wands,
  Cups, Swords, Pentacles/Coins), each running Ace–Ten plus four court cards
  (Page, Knight, Queen, King). The suits map to the **four elements** — the same
  fire/water/air/earth quartet written as triangles in [[Alchemical symbols]].

## The planetary trumps

When the Golden Dawn systematized tarot's occult correspondences, it assigned
the 22 trumps across **12 zodiac signs, 7 classical planets, and 3 elements**.
The seven *planetary* trumps reproduce the cluster's recurring lexicon exactly:

| Trump | Planet |
| --- | --- |
| I — The Magician | Mercury ☿ |
| II — The High Priestess | Moon ☽ |
| III — The Empress | Venus ♀ |
| X — Wheel of Fortune | Jupiter ♃ |
| XVI — The Tower | Mars ♂ |
| XIX — The Sun | Sun ☉ |
| XXI — The World | Saturn ♄ |

The remaining trumps take the twelve zodiac signs (Emperor/Aries,
Hierophant/Taurus, Lovers/Gemini, and so on), and three — the Fool, the Hanged
Man, and Judgement — take the three elements air, water, and fire. So the same
seven governors that rule the metals in [[Alchemy]] and the mounts of the hand
in [[Palmistry]] here rule the trumps of the deck. One correspondence table,
three coded surfaces.

## From card game to oracle — a history

Tarot's history is the cluster's clearest case of an ordinary object being
**retrofitted** with esoteric meaning:

- **15th-century Italy — a game.** Tarot began around **1440** in the courts of
  Milan and Ferrara as *tarocchi*, a trick-taking card game for the nobility.
  The trumps were literally "triumphs" (winning cards), not mysteries. The
  oldest surviving deck, the hand-painted **Visconti-Sforza** (c. 1440), was a
  luxury status object, not a divinatory tool.
- **France — the standardized deck.** From the 16th century the **Tarot de
  Marseille** fixed a standard pattern that spread across Europe — still, for
  playing.
- **1781 — the occult turn.** The Swiss Freemason **Antoine Court de Gébelin**
  declared in *Le Monde Primitif* that the tarot was a survival of ancient
  **Egyptian** priestly wisdom (the "Book of Thoth"), encoding kabbalistic and
  divine secrets. This was **invented history** — but it stuck.
- **Etteilla and Lévi.** **Jean-Baptiste Alliette ("Etteilla")** published the
  first practical guide to cartomancy. The French occultist **Éliphas Lévi**
  (mid-19th c.) bound tarot to the **Hebrew alphabet and the Kabbalah**. The
  terms **"Major" and "Minor Arcana"** came slightly later, with **Jean-Baptiste
  Pitois ("Paul Christian")**, not with Etteilla or Lévi.
- **The Golden Dawn (founded 1888).** The London **Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn**
  produced the full correspondence system — each card tied to a Hebrew letter, a
  path on the Tree of Life, and an astrological assignment — fusing tarot to
  [[Hermeticism|Hermetic Qabalah]].
- **1909 — Rider-Waite-Smith.** Golden Dawn members **Arthur Edward Waite** and
  the artist **Pamela Colman Smith** produced the deck that became the modern
  standard. Its innovation was Colman Smith's **fully illustrated Minor Arcana** —
  every pip a little scene that can be *read* narratively, rather than a bare
  repeat of suit-marks. It is the deck most people now picture.

## Jung and the Fool's Journey

The cluster's [[Jung and alchemy|Jungian thread]] runs straight through tarot.
Jung wrote little directly about the cards, but his concepts — the **collective
unconscious**, the **archetypes**, **synchronicity**, **projection**, and above
all **individuation** — became the dominant modern frame for what a reading
*does*. He called the tarot images "descended from the archetypes of
transformation."

The popular **"Fool's Journey"** makes this explicit: the 22 trumps are read as
a single narrative of psychological maturation. **The Fool (0)** is pure,
unformed potential — the psyche before individuation begins. He passes through
the figures of the world (Magician, High Priestess, the parental Empress and
Emperor), confronts trial and dissolution (Death, the Tower, the Devil — the
shadow), and arrives at **The World (XXI)**, integration and wholeness. This is
the identical arc the [[Jung and alchemy|Jung node]] maps onto the *Magnum Opus*:
the Fool's Journey is the alchemical *nigredo → rubedo* in a second costume,
the *opus* told as a deck instead of a furnace.

## The skeptical correction

As with every node in this cluster — Beverly Seaton on the
[[Language of flowers]], Isaac Casaubon on [[Hermeticism]], Principe and Newman
on [[Jung and alchemy|Jung's alchemy]] — the honest caution: tarot's "ancient
Egyptian" or primordial pedigree is a **late projection**, not history. The deck
was a Renaissance card game; its esoteric meaning was assigned, in stages,
between the 1780s and 1900s. The Golden Dawn correspondences are a coherent
**designed system**, internally rich and genuinely influential — but they are
roughly a century old, not millennia. The cards are a living symbolic language
with real depth; they are not a recovered cipher from Thoth.

## Relationship to the cluster

Tarot is where the cluster's threads knot together. It is a **sibling** of the
[[Language of flowers]] and [[Palmistry]] (an ordinary object — a flower, a
hand, a card — read as a message); a **cousin** of [[Alchemy]] and
[[Alchemical symbols]] (it inherits their seven planets and four elements); a
**child** of [[Hermeticism]] (its modern form is Hermetic Qabalah dealt onto
pasteboard); and the **clearest illustration** of [[Jung and alchemy|Jung's]]
reading, with the Fool's Journey standing in for individuation. If the cluster
has a center of gravity, it is here.
