Gustav Sohon (1825–1903) was an artist, interpreter, and topographical assistant. Sohon executed some of the earliest landscape paintings of the Pacific Northwest. One of his first assignments was with [Lieutenant John Mullan](https://magazine.wsu.edu/2014/04/29/lost-highway/), who was surveying the country between the Rocky and Bitterroot Mountains for the Pacific Railroad Surveys led by Isaac Stevens.

![Walla Walla, 1862](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon04.jpg)

July 27, 1853 Sohon reached Hudson’s Bay Company’s Fort Wall Walla, as now a member of the supply train to establish a depot in Montana’s Bitterroot Valley for Isaac Steven’s railroad survey. Here is Sohon’s sketch of Walla Walla in 1862. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections)

![Cantonment Stevens](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon05.jpg)

In fall of 1853 Sohon made his way to Cantonment Stevens, where he served under Lt. John Mullan. Here is the published lithograph of Sohon’s watercolor and pencil rendering of Cantonment Stevens for the Pacific Railroad Surveys 1853-55. (Courtesy Archives and Special Collections, Eastern Washington University Libraries)

![Hellgate River crossing, 1850s](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon06.jpg)

Here Sohon sketched for the Pacific Railroad Surveys (1853-55) a dramatic image of the event showing Mullan on his back in fast water, desperately clinging to one of the tree’s branches when crossing the Hellgate River (today’s Clark Fork) near present-day Missoula. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections)

![Lolo Pass with Mullan](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon07.jpg)

A Sohon illustration of Mullan’s party in 1854 as they prepared to follow the course of Lewis and Clark over Lolo Pass, the last of Mullan’s expeditions seeking a railroad route. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections)

![Coeur dAlene Mission, 1859](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon08.jpg)

After completing his duty with Isaac Stevens’ Treaty Expedition\* of 1855, and honorably discharged from the army in 1857, Sohon was enlisted by Mullan for his military road building crew for 1859-62. Here is Sohon’s depiction of the Coeur d’Alene Mission when the crew was there. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections) \* See “Sketches by Gustavus Sohon of the Walla Walla Treaty Council,” WSM Gallery Fall 2009

![Cataldo Mission in 1842](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon09.jpg)

The Coeur d’Alene (Cataldo) Mission as drawn by Gustavus Sohon. Rendered while serving during Col. George Wright’s campaign. (Courtesy Library of Congress)

![Spokane River, 1858](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon10.jpg)

“Camp on the Spokane River, Sept. 6, 1858,” ink on paper by Gustavus Sohon. Rendered while serving during Col. George Wright’s campaign. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections)

![Great Falls of Spokane](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon11.jpg)

“Great Falls of the Spokane River, 1858,” pencil drawing by Gustavus Sohon. Rendered while serving during Col. George Wright’s campaign. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections)

![Snake River, 1850s](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon12.jpg)

“View of the Snake River at Mouth of the Tukanon, 1858,” pencil drawing by Gustavus Sohon. Rendered while serving during Col. George Wright’s campaign. (Courtesy WSU Manuscripts, Archives, and Special Collections)

![Cantonment Jordan, 1860](https://wpcdn.web.wsu.edu/wp-magazine/uploads/sites/902/2016/07/2014summer-sohon13.png)

In 1863, Sohon married and moved to San Francisco where he ran a photography studio. Two years later he moved to Washington, D.C., where he opened a shoe business that he operated for the rest of his life. Here is Sohon’s pencil on paper of “Cartographer’s at Work” at Cantonment Jordan (early 1860), rendered during John Mullan’s military road construction project 1859-62. (Courtesy Smithsonian Institution)
