Additional taxpayer subsidies for a St. Luke’s McCall employee housing project are on hold while the state’s top legal official determines if the subsidies are constitutional.

That revelation came last week as the [McCall Memorial Hospital District](https://mmhd.info/) Board of Trustees discussed how it might spend about $1.6 million in property taxes collected this year from property owners in the McCall-Donnelly area.

Since 2024, most of the tax funding collected by the district, or about $3.8 million, has been spent on [a 38-unit workforce housing project known as Trillium Ridge](https://valleylookout.com/2025/03/19/st-lukes-mccall-employee-housing-okd-by-mccall/). But despite high demand for the first four units, which will be ready for occupancy this spring, committing additional funds to the project in 2027 is not currently under consideration.

Valley Lookout house - Drew

“I’m understanding that that might be something you might want to consider at another time, which I think is fine,” St. Luke’s McCall Chief Operations and Nursing Officer Amber Green told the board before presenting other funding requests by the hospital.

The board did not discuss the topic any further, but Chairman Andy Laidlaw told Valley Lookout after the meeting that the district is awaiting the completion of Idaho Attorney General Raúl Labrador’s investigation into the public subsidies.

“It seems a good time to assume a lower profile until questions raised by Give Us a Vote! and the attorney general are resolved,” Laidlaw said.

The attorney general’s investigation followed a complaint made by [Give us a Vote!](https://tomigrote.substack.com/p/give-us-a-vote-home-page?r=5s7ai), a citizens’ group that includes Idaho House Rep. Faye Thompson, R-McCall. The complaint argues the district’s participation in the housing project, as well as other allocations to St. Luke’s projects in recent years, are an illegal use of taxpayer funding to subsidize a private medical conglomerate, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2025/08/05/attorney-general-investigating-taxpayer-subsidies-to-st-lukes-mccall/).

The attorney general’s office announced its investigation last July, but in August the hospital district board still moved forward with allocating another $1.4 million to the housing project.

## AG cites constitutional doubts

Since then, the hospital district has received two letters from the attorney general’s office, both of which cast doubt on the legality of the tax funding allocations.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“We question whether constructing and managing affordable housing projects for private healthcare workers falls within a hospital district’s duty to provide facilities for people’s necessary medical care and treatment,” Deputy Attorney General Stephanie Guyon said in a Feb. 2 letter, which followed [a similar letter on Jan. 14.](https://valleylookout.com/2026/01/26/labrador-concerned-taxpayer-subsidy-for-st-lukes-mccall-housing-may-be-unconstitutional/)

Both letters specifically probe whether the housing allocations violate [Article VIII, Section 3C, of the Idaho Constitution](https://law.justia.com/constitution/idaho/article-viii/section-3c/), which states that hospital districts may not use property taxes to fund “certain activities that are ancillary to their operations.”

Instead, ancillary projects, such as employee housing, must be funded using “charges, rents, or payments” from the hospital district’s existing facilities, Guyon said.

## Hospital district: 3C not applicable to housing project

Attorneys for the hospital district, however, argue that 3C applies only to ancillary projects that are funded by long-term debt, of which there is none associated with the employee housing project, since new units are being built only as funding is available.

“The district respectfully asserts that when placed in the context of other accompanying constitutional and statutory provisions, 3C does not state that all projects that are ancillary to a hospital district’s operations cannot be funded by property taxes,” McCall attorneys Amy Holm and Hannah Drabinski wrote in a Feb. 27 letter responding to Guyon.

“Instead, it provides that ancillary activities may be funded with long term indebtedness, so long as the debt is not secured with property tax revenues,” Holm and Drabinski said.

Damon Sidur, a spokesperson for the attorney general’s office, did not respond to Valley Lookout’s request for comment on the current status of the investigation.

## April move-in expected for first townhomes

While legal questions linger, St. Luke’s expects to move four employees into the first four housing units next month. Leases have not yet been signed, but the tenants will be existing employees at St. Luke’s McCall, according to Laura Crawford, a spokesperson for the hospital.

“Offering this round of units to current St. Luke’s McCall employees helps the hospital retain hospital and clinic staff to serve the community,” Crawford said.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

Green, the hospital’s top executive, told the district board last week that “there is quite a demand” among employees for spots in Trillium Ridge, noting that 17 applications were submitted for the first four units.

The hospital currently has 16 open positions and an average hiring time of 83 days, Green said.

## Four more townhomes coming this year

Construction of the next four townhomes in Trillium Ridge, which is off Mission Street south of McCall-Donnelly High School, is slated to begin this spring.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

“The modules are presently under construction and Jordan-Wilcomb Construction is ready to proceed with the foundations as soon as weather permits,” Laidlaw said. “So, by late summer we will have eight units inhabited.”

At buildout, Trillium Ridge would total 38 townhomes consisting of about 1,500 square feet with two or three bedrooms and a two-car garage. St. Luke’s workers receive priority for use of the townhomes, followed by local healthcare workers in the McCall area.

The total estimated cost of the project is about $25 million.

## Foundation partnership

The hospital district is funding the project in partnership with the St. Luke’s McCall Foundation. So far, the foundation has contributed $2.4 million to the project, Executive Director Jenny Ruemmele said.

The foundation’s funding for Trillium Ridge stems exclusively from private donations, but Ruemmele said the foundation plans to continue pursuing the project regardless of the hospital district’s participation.

“Our intent is to construct units based on our financial ability,” she said. “To date we are grateful that we are able to provide eight townhouses.”

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

Ownership of the townhomes is divided between the foundation and the district, based on each entity’s financial contributions to the project. If the hospital district were to be dissolved ownership of the district’s units would transfer to the foundation.

Guyon’s Feb. 2 letter said that “may violate” [state law](https://law.justia.com/codes/idaho/title-63/chapter-41/section-63-4105/) requiring the district’s property to be transferred to the county if it were to be dissolved. The district’s Feb. 27 response did not address that concern.

The district board was previously asked by Give us a Vote!, the citizens’ group that filed the complaint with the attorney general, to hold a non-binding advisory vote on the potential dissolution of the taxing district. Last year the board agreed to study the implications of dissolving the district, but declined the group’s request to put an advisory vote on the May 2025 ballot, [as Valley Lookout reported.](https://valleylookout.com/2025/02/19/mccall-hospital-district-to-study-dissolution-puts-advisory-vote-on-hold/)

## District weighing alternative uses for tax funding

While future hospital district funding for Trillium Ridge remains in flux, the board of trustees is weighing multiple alternative proposals from St. Luke’s McCall. A final decision must be made before the district adopts its 2027 budget in August.

Options for the 2027 funding include paying for studies of existing St. Luke’s buildings that could be remodeled to create more space for primary care, visiting specialists, and clinical patients.

Funding could also be allocated for St. Luke’s to buy new medical equipment to replace outdated or failing items.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The board asked St. Luke’s to bring more information on each of those options to its April 21 meeting.

The district could also choose not to allocate any tax funding in 2027 and instead absorb the $1.6 million into a reserve fund that could be allocated by the board in the future.

The McCall Memorial Hospital District was created by voters in 1984 to take control of the hospital from Valley County. St. Luke’s Health System began operating the hospital on a contract with the district in 1999.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

In 2010, the district agreed to a merger with St. Luke’s that saw the company take control of the hospital’s administration. The district now retains ownership of its assets, including land under St. Luke’s McCall, while continuing to allocate tax funding to healthcare improvements that the board believes St. Luke’s may not fund otherwise.
