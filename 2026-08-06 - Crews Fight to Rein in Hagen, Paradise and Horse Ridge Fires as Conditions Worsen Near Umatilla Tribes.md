#### Note: This story is based on the latest available information as of 10:30am. PST, Aug. 6. Please refer to your local government, fire agency websites and social media for the most timely information regarding evacuations and road closures. For information on where to donate, check with the tribes being impacted by fires on their websites and Facebook pages.

Now into its fourth week, the lightning-caused Hagen Fire has consumed nearly 41,000 acres and is 11% contained.

The Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation (CTUIR) in northeastern Oregon announced [a late opening of all government facilities](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1VJH2KrZXQ/) around Mission on Thursday due to the wildfires in their area. A [four-day excursion](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/197SAiA2TT/) to the Mount Adams area for huckleberry gathering has also been canceled due to the wildfires and poor air quality.

The Hagen Fire is burning away in the Umatilla National Forest. The [latest map](https://www.facebook.com/share/1gJEPXJexP/) shows its proximity to several populated cities: La Grande, Pendleton, and Elgin in Oregon, and Walla Walla, Washington. Officials advise locals to check the [evacuation map for Umatilla County](https://protect.genasys.com/en?lat=45.6881&lng=-118.4046&z=10.7) to see the latest levels and locations, while motorists are recommended to visit the Oregon Department of Transportation’s [TripCheck.com](http://tripcheck.com/) site for road conditions and closures if they need to travel near the vicinity of the fire.

In an update issued Wednesday afternoon, the CTUIR reported that helicopters dumped 100,000 gallons of water on the Hagen Fire to help contain it “south of the North Fork of the Umatilla River and aid firefighters on the ground on the south, southeast and eastern flanks.”

The Paradise Fire is burning in the Umatilla National Forest approximately 10 miles southeast of Walla Walla, Washington, on July 31, 2026. (Via InciWeb)

At last check, the Paradise Fire was at 1,664 acres. Being nearly 10 miles southeast of Walla Walla, fire crews have focused on protecting the watershed for the city. An update on Inciweb said steep terrain allowed burning material to roll downhill and ignite more flames. There are 375 firefighters assigned to the Paradise Fire who plan to “reinforce structure protection to the north and northwest of the fire and improve FS Roads 64 and 65 as indirect fire lines.”

The Horse Ridge Fire, about 5 miles northeast of Meacham, stayed within established lines. It remains at 860 acres and is 98% contained.

The fire incident teams’ biggest challenge is a warming and drying trend that is forecast to intensify through the rest of the week. Expected highs will be in the low 90s, with low relative humidity of 10% to 15%.

“These factors, coupled with instability and high mixing heights present a combination of fire weather elements with high potential for activity across the fires. Breezy winds are possible over the weekend as the upper level ridge flattens slightly,” according to a brief posted toInciweb.

Nearly 1,100 personnel are tackling all three fires, with the Northern Rockies Team 6 replacing Northwest 7 Incident Management Team on Thursday.

[Air quality levels](https://fire.airnow.gov/#13.13/45.99904/-117.98723) in the area range from “unhealthy for sensitive groups” (elders, infants, people with health issues) to “unhealthy.” People with delicate health conditions are advised to stay indoors and use air filters and purifiers.
