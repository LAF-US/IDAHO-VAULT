SPOKANE, Wash. — City officials responded on Tuesday after questions grew over an inflammatory sign displayed outside "2nd Base Espresso" in the Hillyard neighborhood.

The sign drew criticism from several community members on social media, with some people claiming that the coffee stand has displayed problematic signage in the past.

NonStop Local reached out to Spokane City Council member Sarah Dixit as well as the mayor’s office with questions about the issue. Dixit stated that her staff referred the sign to law enforcement to check whether it violated any public decency laws.

The mayor’s office referred to a statement from Erin Hut, the city’s director of communications and marketing.

“While we do not condone this type of messaging, it is our understanding that it's protected under the First Amendment,” Hut said. “Anyone who believes they’ve been subjected to a hate crime or bias incident is encouraged to call the Washington Hate Crimes and Bias Incidents Hot Line.”

In addition to city leaders, NonStop Local also contacted legal professionals at Gonzaga to ask about the First Amendment and how hate speech is determined under the Constitution.
