The Democratic candidate who hoped to unseat U.S. Sen. Jim Risch in Idaho announced Tuesday that he is dropping out of the race, leaving Independent Todd Achilles as the challenger to the longtime Republican.

David Roth, who [won the](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/politics-government/state-politics/article315799127.html) Democratic primary in May, said he was proud of his campaign and believes in what Democrats are fighting for across Idaho, but decided to shift his efforts to other areas.

“I have concluded that I can make the greatest difference by focusing my time and energy on helping Democrats win up and down the ballot and in communities across Idaho,” he wrote in a statement.

In the statement, Roth went after Republican leadership, accusing them of driving up healthcare costs and prioritizing the wealthy over working families in the state.

“Idaho needs leaders at every level who will stand up for ordinary people and defend our democracy,” he wrote.

He encouraged those who supported his campaign to stay involved, and said the work will continue despite the end of his campaign.

“I am also proud of the history we made,” he added. “Being the first openly gay man to ever receive a federal nomination in Idaho is something I will always carry with me. I hope this campaign showed LGBTQ+ Idahoans, especially young people, that they belong in our politics, their voices matter, and no office should ever feel beyond their reach.”

Roth said he will work with his team in the next few weeks to finish the formal withdrawal process.

The announcement came less than two weeks after The [Spokesman-Review reported](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/jul/18/idaho-democrats-think-jim-risch-could-be-beaten-bu/) that Ada County Democrats sent a letter to Roth earlier this month warning him that it would consider endorsing Achilles if Roth’s campaign didn’t raise $50,000 and put together an election plan by Aug. 12.

“We have a real opportunity to defeat Risch, but we’re seriously concerned your campaign isn’t positioned to meet the moment,” the executive committee wrote in the letter, according to the Spokane media outlet. “We can’t afford to let this moment slip away in the best political environment Idaho has seen in decades.”

Risch, 83, is running for a fourth straight term in the Senate in a state that is dominated by Republicans. Achilles is a U.S. Army veteran and former Democratic member of the Idaho House of Representatives.
