**Dan Gookin** (born 1960) is an American politician and author who is the mayor of [Coeur d'Alene, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coeur_d'Alene,_Idaho "Coeur d'Alene, Idaho").[^2] He has served as a member on the Coeur d’Alene City Council. He is best known for writing the first *[For Dummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Dummies "For Dummies")* books including *DOS For Dummies* and *PCs For Dummies*, which established the humorous and accessible style of the long-running *For Dummies* series.[^3]

## Background

Gookin earned a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts "Bachelor of Arts") degree in communications and visual arts from the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego "University of California, San Diego").[^3] He has written over 150 computer books. His website provides computer help sections and a blog which is updated several times a week. From 1987 to 1989, he served as editor of *[ComputorEdge Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ComputorEdge_Magazine "ComputorEdge Magazine")*, a computer periodical in [San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego").

Gookin lives in Coeur d'Alene where he has been active in the local arts community, serving as the artistic director of the Lake City Playhouse.[^3] He first ran unsuccessfully for the [Idaho Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Senate "Idaho Senate") in 2004 as a [Libertarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Party_\(United_States\) "Libertarian Party (United States)").[^4] Later, in November 2011, he was elected to a seat on the Coeur d'Alene City Council, defeating former [Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(United_States\) "Democratic Party (United States)") state representative George Saylor.[^5] [^6]

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic"), Gookin was a vocal critic of [mask mandates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mask_mandates "Mask mandates") in [[Idaho]], voting against a Coeur d’Alene citywide mask requirement that the council approved in October 2020 by a 4–2 vote.[^7] [^8] He frequently spoke at public meetings and appeared at protests opposing face-covering requirements.[^9] Gookin later drew controversy after posting a photo of a blank [COVID-19 vaccination card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_vaccine_card "COVID-19 vaccine card") that he said he had purchased from a [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook "Facebook") friend.[^10] He said that he did not use the card and emphasized that his intent was to demonstrate how easily vaccination cards could be forged. Gookin said the requirement to present proof of vaccination is “another injustice in the ongoing COVID-19 saga.” [^10]

Gookin is an elected precinct committeeman for the [Kootenai County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kootenai_County,_Idaho "Kootenai County, Idaho") Republican Central Committee (KCRCC),[^11] an organization he openly criticizes.[^12] In 2023, during a forum hosted by the Kootenai County Republican Women Federated (KCRWF), a subsidiary of the KCRCC, Gookin was questioned by members of both organizations about remarks on his [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") channel *[Kootenai Rants](https://www.youtube.com/@kootenairants5923)* which they viewed as "derogatory" toward [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christians "Christians") and [Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_\(United_States\) "Republican Party (United States)"). The exchange grew tense after Gookin defended his comments and accused the committee of refusing to condemn white supremacy and David Reilly, a former KCRCC endorsee who has been accused of antisemitic and white supremacist affiliations,[^13] leading to a heated confrontation in which a moderator briefly ripped the microphone from his hand.[^12]

Since the 2023 forum and the subsequent defamation lawsuit and 2025 settlement, Gookin has remained publicly critical of the KCRCC and its chairman, Brent Regan, accusing the committee of internal bias and a lack of transparency, while its leadership has maintained that his comments exceed the bounds of "protected" political speech.[^14] [^15] Gookin appeared as himself on the television series *[To Tell the Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/To_Tell_the_Truth "To Tell the Truth")*, where the panel attempted to determine whether he or another contestant was the author of the first *[For Dummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Dummies "For Dummies")* book.[^16] In November 2025, Gookin was elected mayor of Coeur d'Alene with 34.19% of the vote, with his primary challengers Debbie Loffman and the incumbent mayor Woody McEvers receiving 28% and 26.46% of the vote, respectively.[^17]
