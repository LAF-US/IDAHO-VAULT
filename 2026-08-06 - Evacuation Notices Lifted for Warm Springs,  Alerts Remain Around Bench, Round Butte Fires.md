#### Note: This story is based on the latest available information as of 12:15pm. PST, Aug. 6. Please refer to your local government, fire agency websites and social media for the most timely information regarding evacuations and road closures. For information on where to donate, check with the tribes being impacted by fires on their websites and Facebook pages.

Now into its third week, the lightning-caused Bench Fire has consumed more than 67,000 acres and is 84% contained as of late Thursday morning. 572 firefighters are mopping up and removing hot spots and excess fuel sources around it and the smaller Round Butte Fire. That 1,163-acre fire has 11 personnel working on it and is 90% contained. Its cause remains undetermined since it started Aug. 1.

The goal for both teams is to reach 100% containment and respond to any new fires that may appear. Near-record highs with an expected maximum temperature of 94 degrees on Friday — and near record lows for humidity falling to 12% — could spark red flag warnings this weekend.

As of Thursday morning, all Warm Springs evacuation notices [have been lifted](https://www.facebook.com/share/1DVDEuE5Dy/), with some Jefferson County areas near the Bench and Round Butte fires at either Level 1 (“Be Ready”) or Level 2 (“Be Set”). Locals are advised to check an interactive [evacuation map for Jefferson County](https://fr911orgis.maps.arcgis.com/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=e1000ff48be84d6ba23431f303d2c247&fbclid=IwY2xjawTh1vRwZG9mBWV4dG4DYWVtAjEwAGJyaWQRMVRKNjFJZ1RTOEtaeVo1clpzcnRjBmFwcF9pZBAyMjIwMzkxNzg4MjAwODkyAAEel4lHDiXb0Wd86gbKNs4KpY9tSjJMfHWeGkPZlxikwWV_ZvhF8LNLDe0Mx3s_aem_XnSXQ4Rm9uDyqX0vyEWV_g) for the latest.

Part of the Ochoco National Forest and Crooked River National Grasslands are temporarily closed. A map and a formal closure order can be found [here](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/ochoco/alerts/emergency-fire-closure-area-crooked-river-national-grassland?fbclid=IwcGRvZgVleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFUSjYxSWdUUzhLWnlaNXJac3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHttS1lpYo_-iM73x9Mc_3OJ2NsylKcTS3_uMpr6IpONBYHekO0_ThFsQRDpJ_aem_V2VVzxZkKckk-9u4aap6eA). And motorists are advised to visit the Oregon Department of Transportation’s [TripCheck.com](http://tripcheck.com/?fbclid=IwcGRvZgVleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFUSjYxSWdUUzhLWnlaNXJac3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHpeJRw4l29FnfOoGyjbOCqWPbUoyTHx1nhpD2ZcYpMFlf2b4RfCzSw3tDMd7_aem_XnSXQ4Rm9uDyqX0vyEWV_g) site for conditions and closures before traveling to the Warm Springs or Madras area.

In a release from the Confederated Tribes of Warm Springs, [the air quality](https://www.facebook.com/share/1DSuogj7wh/) around the reservation is deemed “moderate” but people with sensitive health conditions are still warned to be careful. In another release, the CTWS said distribution of relief supplies at the Old School Cafeteria would stop after 2 p.m. Thursday.

“Beginning Monday, August 10, anyone needing assistance may visit Emergency Management at 1116 Wasco Street,” said the release. “Remaining supplies and future donations will be available through Emergency Management, and water distribution will continue there as well.”
