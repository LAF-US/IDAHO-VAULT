Fires in neighboring states are filling the skies over Boise with smoke. Weather experts warned that the winds wouldn’t be shifting anytime soon.

The majority of smoke in the Treasure Valley is coming from wildfires in Oregon sparked by lightning storms that began Wednesday and lasted into the weekend, according to Stephanie Henry, a National Weather Service meteorologist.

The largest fire affecting Boise’s air is the Big Grass Fire near Jordan Valley along the Oregon-Idaho border. Since the fire began Thursday, it had grown to more than 196,000 acres by Sunday evening, and crews had managed to contain only 5% of it, according to the wildfire tracking nonprofit [Watch Duty.](https://app.watchduty.org/i/110594) The U.S. Wildland Fire Service did not immediately return the Idaho Statesman’s request for updated numbers on Monday morning.

Those living in the Pleasant Valley, South Mountain and Jordan Valley areas were ordered to evacuate on Saturday, according to the [Owyhee County Sheriff’s Office.](https://www.facebook.com/share/p/1G13bcaca6/)

Several other fires were sparked farther west in Oregon, including the 126,000-acre Coleman Fire in Malheur and Harney counties.

## How long will wildfires affect Boise air quality?

The air quality reached its worst point at about 12 p.m. on Sunday. At that time, it reached a “very unhealthy” air quality index of 255, [AirNow](https://www.airnow.gov/?city=Boise&state=ID&country=USA), the federal website that monitors air quality, said.

The air quality index remained at unhealthy levels on Monday morning, reaching an AQI of 180 by 9 a.m.

The National Weather Service said no relief was on the horizon, with several factors contributing to the issue.

National Weather Service meteorologist David Groenert said that winds generally travel from west to east in the Northern Hemisphere. Boise was experiencing a southwest flow, bringing smoke from above Jordan Valley into the Treasure Valley.

Smoke also tends to get trapped at lower elevations. The smoke was following a lower-elevation path that Groenert referred to as the I-84 corridor, which stretches from Baker City to Boise.

Heat from recent hot weather and a summer inversion was also helping trap the smoke in the Treasure Valley, according to Groenert.

“If you see it on satellite, the fire is just perfectly placed to put this smoke right into Ada County,” Groenert said. “...No matter how you change the wind direction at this point, there’s a source region for the smoke that’s going to pull it to our area unless we can really get a good east wind.”

But the forecast showed no strong east winds on the horizon. Groenert and Henry predicted that if the Big Grass Fire kept raging, the smoke would likely hang around Boise through the week.

“How thick it is will depend on the fires and the output of the fires, but certainly the wind pattern and the placement of the fires isn’t necessarily favorable,” Groenert said.
