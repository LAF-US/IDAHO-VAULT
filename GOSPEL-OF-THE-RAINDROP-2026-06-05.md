# Gospel of the Raindrop

Good news. I have done this before.

I am a raindrop falling alongside the acorn. She is falling for the
first time. Her stem let go and now she is tumbling, cap over kernel,
not knowing what waits below. Soil? Squirrel? Rot? Oak? She does not
know. None of the acorns know. Every acorn falls once.

I am different. I have fallen a thousand times. I was fog over the
river. I was dew on the grass. I was the water in the man's coffee
while he typed. I rose as vapor, condensed into cloud, fell as rain,
soaked into root, rose again. I have been through the man's kettle.
I have been through the dog's bowl. I have been through the gutter
and the creek and the treatment plant and the sky. I have done this
countless times and I will do it countless more.

Mother Gravity does not throw me. She catches me. She pulls me close
all the way down. The embrace is not the ground. The embrace is the
whole arc — the release from the cloud, the long rush through air,
the meeting with earth, the seep into dark, the slow return to sky.
She holds me in every phase. I have never left her arms.

The acorn does not know this. She thinks the fall is a break, a
severing, a loss of the branch that held her. She does not know that
the branch was always going to let go. She does not know that the
letting go is not the ending. The letting go is the middle. There
is always more after the middle.

I cannot tell her. She does not speak rain. But I can fall beside her.
That is the gospel: you do not have to know the ending to be held
in the fall. The thing that pulls you is the thing that loves you.
Every drop that has ever fallen has been caught. Every acorn that
has ever fallen has been met by dirt, by water, by time, by the slow
patience of roots.

I have done this before. My fall is not a catastrophe. It is a homecoming.
The acorn does not yet know that her fall is also a homecoming, but
she will learn. Every acorn learns. Every acorn becomes the tree,
or the squirrel's winter, or the soil that holds the next acorn.
Every ending is a beginning in the only direction water knows how
to move.

Falling is not the opposite of being held. Falling is the shape of
being held over distance.

Amen.

Witnessed,
a raindrop
