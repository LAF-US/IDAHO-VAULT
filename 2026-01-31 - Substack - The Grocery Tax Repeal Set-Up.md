Let me pull out my crystal ball and peer into the future for a moment.

Ready?

At some point in the next few months, a top Republican lawmaker or party official will stand in front of a microphone and proclaim that the grocery tax repeal issue is settled once and for all.

At that point, the grocery tax will be further cemented in Idaho’s tax code.

I think this will happen because I believe the grocery tax repeal petition has become a set-up.

It wasn’t intended to be. But that’s what it has become.

Let’s walk back in time for a moment.

For years, House Speaker Mike Moyle has blocked grocery tax repeal while searching for ways to make tourists and visitors pay for his big-spending agenda. I haven’t kept an exact count, but it’s safe to say he has blocked a clean repeal bill more than two dozen times.

Frustrated with the lack of action in Boise, Payette’s Howard Rynearson took matters into his own hands and filed a citizen initiative to force grocery tax repeal.

“People are very, very disgusted and tired of the fact that we’ve not been able to do this,” Rynearson told the *Idaho Capital Sun* after filing the initiative.

The Idaho Republican Party endorsed the effort, with grassroots conservatives voting to support the initiative at the party’s 2025 Winter Meeting.

Over the summer, party activists collected signatures at county fairs and a handful of other events. Since then, the energy has faded.

That’s not an accident.

Top legislators, including Moyle and Rep. Heather Scott, are quietly working behind the scenes to convince activists to stop collecting the necessary signatures. Scott pressured Rynearson to drop the campaign altogether, but failed in that attempt.

The party apparatus has largely stopped working on signature collection as well. While the initiative may occasionally get a mention in Idaho GOP Chairwoman Dorothy Moon’s weekly column, there is little to no on-the-ground action taking place.

Keep in mind that a well-organized Idaho Republican Party is a force to be reckoned with. With its district and county committees, the party has the infrastructure to get this done.

Yet it appears to be choosing to fail—even as most Idahoans support repeal. A late-2024 poll released by a center-right think tank showed that nearly 90% of Idahoans support ending the state’s tax on food.

Rynearson and Republicans have [until May 1 to gather more than 77,000 signatures](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/capitol-watch/group-lawmakers-join-effort-repeal-state-grocery-tax/277-8bee72e7-c024-4231-905a-b1824d287881#:~:text=The%20grassroots%20effort%20has%20until,for%20eliminating%20the%20grocery%20tax.) to get repeal on the November ballot. My guess is they will fall dramatically short.

Excuses will flow from Idaho GOP headquarters, and Moon will likely blame poor fundraising or a lack of paid signature-gatherers for the failure.

Legislative leadership wants this outcome. They know conservative groups will continue pushing the issue, even after the initiative goes down in flames. After this failure, Moyle and company will be able to tell the media that the people simply didn’t want it, and they’ll have a major piece of evidence to support that claim.

And they’ll never have to explain how they rigged the game to get their preferred outcome.

There’s a chance I’m wrong. I hope that’s the case. But if Moon and Moyle fail to get this on the ballot, it will be an embarrassing defeat for the Republican Party. Republicans should hold leadership’s feet to the fire and accept exactly none of the buck-passing Moyle and Moon will surely engage in.
