Teton County, Idaho, commissioners are planning to fund a study and talk about cost and revenue sharing with their counterparts in Wyoming. The discussions will likely center around Grand Targhee Resort’s advancing expansion.

Teton County, Idaho, officials are spending money to see if they can make some money from Grand Targhee Resort’s expansion.

On Wednesday, the Teton County (Idaho) Board of County Commissioners voted to contribute roughly $5,000 toward a $30,000 study examining cross-border impacts and whether they and their counterparts in [Wyoming can](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/town_county/as-forest-service-action-on-grand-targhee-expansion-looms-the-two-teton-counties-plan-to/article_7bef96d6-7917-4dc0-a18b-805a9572cd1f.html) develop a revenue-sharing agreement. If they succeed, the agreement could allow Teton County, Wyoming, to share taxes collected in Alta and at Targhee with Teton County, Idaho.

“I look at it not as money paid out,” Brad Wolfe, the chair of the Idaho commission, said Wednesday. “I look at it as an investment, so that we can recoup it and much more.”

The three-member commission then voted unanimously in favor of funding the study.

With a nod from the U.S. Forest Service, [Grand Targhee is set to grow](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/environmental/local/caribou-targhee-national-forest-greenlighting-grand-targhee-resort-expansion/article_548fc3ea-03eb-4ebe-8cae-58c78ade65ec.html). But the western Wyoming ski hill is only accessible through Idaho, creating a unique set of jurisdictional challenges. The taxes skiers pay on lodging and food bolster Wyoming’s coffers, while Idaho houses many of the resort’s employees and maintains many of the roads visitors use to get to the ski hill.

Idaho has a 3% cap on property tax growth and Teton County, Idaho, has chosen not to levy any additional sales tax beyond the state’s 6% rate. That has left western Teton communities in [dire financial straits](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/town_county/in-dire-financial-straits-teton-county-idaho-asks-wyoming-to-tax-targhee-skiers-and-share/article_1dd90113-0735-4afb-8eea-055d718c4c6f.html), struggling to pay for policing, firefighting, veteran’s services and more.

Cost and revenue sharing would address what Idaho officials describe as an unequal distributions of [costs](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/this_just_in/teton-county-idaho-commissioners-cut-cash-for-veterans-agriculture-and-economy-in-trimmed-budget/article_eaa88469-1c6d-4c1c-85d1-71bfa7a36f0c.html) and benefits tied to [Grand Targhee](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/environmental/local/caribou-targhee-national-forest-greenlighting-grand-targhee-resort-expansion/article_548fc3ea-03eb-4ebe-8cae-58c78ade65ec.html).

**The study**

Last Tuesday, Teton County, Wyoming, commissioners approved a contract with the University of Wyoming to study the broader economic relationship between the Teton counties, asking researchers to examine Grand Targhee Resort’s expansion alongside other cross-border impacts affecting both communities.

The Idaho county’s $5,000 will help fund that study, which is set to include a [facilitated workshop](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/town_county/teton-county-wyoming-to-oppose-targhee-boundary-expansion-on-mountain-restaurants/article_539b3314-08f6-474c-b0e6-9bd7f2776169.html). The meeting is tentatively set for June 10 at the Teton County, Idaho, courthouse.

![Joint meeting](https://bloximages.newyork1.vip.townnews.com/jhnewsandguide.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/4/b1/4b1ad368-add9-506b-ab71-234780c93a6d/68c068b4984cb.image.jpg)

Commissioners from the two Teton counties listen during a joint meeting in 2025. The two counties are set to reconvene for a workshop around cost and revenue sharing at Grand Targhee Resort, which is in Wyoming but only accessible through Idaho.

The workshop would be led by Wyoming’s Ruckelshaus Institute, part of the Haub School of Environment and Natural Resources, and the University of Idaho’s McClure Center for Public Policy Research. The Ruckelshaus Institute hopes fresh perspectives could help sort out longstanding issues.

“Our proposal is really to explore what might be possible,” Melanie Armstrong, director of the Ruckelshaus Institute, told the Jackson Hole Daily last month.

The institute’s proposal states the workshop will include a guest speaker who can share insights from [Lake Tahoe](https://www.sfgate.com/renotahoe/article/battle-to-save-tahoe-ski-resort-20761278.php), which straddles the California-Nevada border. A 14-year legal dispute over development at Palisades Tahoe recently resulted in a settlement between the resort and two environmental organizations, scaling back the resort’s proposal.

**The decision**

The decision to participate did not come lightly for Idaho commissioners. Wolfe, along with Commissioners Dan Powers and Ron James, wrestled with how much they should pay.

James initially voted against funding the workshop, arguing that Idaho was being asked to help fund a study intended to address a problem created by the existing tax structure.

“We’re getting crumbs off of this, and they’re asking us to pay? I got a problem with that,” said James, who attended the Wednesday meeting via Zoom.

James [broke with](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/search/?l=25&s=start_time&sd=desc&f=html&t=article,video,youtube,collection&app[0]=editorial&nsa=eedition&q=Grand+Targhee+Resort+&o=100) Wolfe and Powers earlier this year to support Targhee expansion, including [mountaintop restaurants](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/environmental/21-years-later-grand-targhee-resort-seeking-alpine-restaurants-revives-land-swap-debate/article_0ee65e39-40ca-46dc-b016-9c398fe39048.html) and expansion into the South Bowl of Peaked Mountain. He has also criticized Idaho and Wyoming leaders for failing to develop a plan to reduce the financial strain the resort’s growth could place on Teton Valley.

Unable to secure a meeting with Idaho Gov. Brad Little to discuss Teton County’s budget concerns, James challenged the governor in last week’s Republican primary. The freshman commissioner lost, earning [less than 2%](https://results.voteidaho.gov/results/public/id/elections/may2026?_gl=119igc3m_gaNDI3MzU2NDE3LjE3ODAwNzUzMzI._ga_20546Y1CC5czE3ODAwNzUzMzEkbzEkZzAkdDE3ODAwNzUzMzEkajYwJGwwJGgw) of the vote, but told the Daily his goal was to get the governor’s attention.

Because the Idaho commission was funding the study with money from the county’s contingency fund, James’ vote against the expense initially stopped the county’s participation.

But Wolfe asked James to reconsider because the commission needed a unanimous vote to spend the money. James did so, and the second motion passed.

“Teton County, Wyoming, is making an effort to collaborate with us, talk to us, and we should be entering into this meeting in that spirit — in a spirit of collaboration,” Powers said. “Offering to pay our small share is a good start to getting that meeting off upright foot.”

**Marin involved**

On Wednesday, as plans for the workshop moved forward, Idaho commissioners volunteered Teton County GIS Director Rob Marin to participate. That was notable because roughly a year ago, one of James, Wolfe and Powers’ predecessors, Michael Whitfield, [sparred over Marin’s involvement](https://www.jhnewsandguide.com/news/environmental/local/you-ignore-this-trend-at-your-peril-idaho-officials-spar-over-talking-about-targhee-expansion/article_b24fdcea-53be-4518-9ad0-74b0b24f4284.html) in Targhee discussions. James felt he was biased against resort development.

Commissioners volunteered Marin based on his previous work mitigating the resort’s expansion pressures in Teton Valley.

Marin had also already reached out to the McClure Center and the Ruckelshaus Institute, along with representatives from the City of Driggs and Teton County Clerk Kim Keeley, hoping to pinpoint specific goals for the workshop.

“If they didn’t bring some fresh thinking to the process, this meeting would just rehash the same talking points we’ve gone around and around on,” Marin said of the universities. “They were receptive, and they agreed to begin doing some research.”

*This article has been updated to clarify that Teton County GIS Director Rob Marin had been speaking with representatives from the University of Idaho and University of Wyoming about the workshop. — Eds.*
