## Illegal clauses in Idaho leases pose serious risks for renters and nonprofits

Jesse Tree case managers are trained to understand renter protections and leases that don’t uphold Idaho law, writes guest columnist Matthias Aleksander Ryan.

![House keys sitting on an eviction notice received in the mail.](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Eviction-notice-2048x1368.jpg)

Although illegal clauses cannot be upheld in court, the threat of these clauses results in tangible harm. A tenant facing financial hardship isn’t in a position to afford an attorney, writes guest columnist Matthias Aleksander Ryan. (Photo by Getty Images)

If rent isn’t paid by the tenth day of the month, your landlord will come in, remove you and your belongings, and lock you out of the property. You’re not allowed to sue and must waive your right to a trial by jury. Your landlord can also press charges against you for trespassing, without an eviction notice. If your initial reaction is that those actions sound illegal, you’d be correct. Nonetheless, these clauses were discovered in the lease of a recent [Jesse Tree](https://www.jessetreeidaho.org/) client.

Jesse Tree case managers are trained to understand [renter protections](https://www.jessetreeidaho.org/trc) and leases that don’t uphold Idaho law. Thanks to careful review by a Jesse Tree case manager, the organization was able to contact the property manager to discuss their concerns. Upon being notified of the illegal clauses, the property management company sent an updated lease. Illegal lease clauses like this are not a new phenomenon for the area. Jesse Tree has notified several landlords over the years with concerns over clauses that do not conform to Idaho codes.

Although illegal clauses cannot be upheld in court, the threat of these clauses results in tangible harm. A tenant facing financial hardship isn’t in a position to afford an attorney. Instead, these tenants would refer to their lease, realize that there is threat of forcible removal, and leave; forfeiting the eviction protections for renters and landlords.

“Clarity in the law, even if it doesn’t necessarily favor tenants, is often a step in the right direction,” Drew Dickerson, the litigation director from [Idaho Legal Aid](https://idaholegalaid.org/) stated in a discussion about how illegal clauses impact renters. “Without legislative clarity, it makes what is effectively an illegal lease pragmatically effective for the landlord. If somebody is already behind on rent, that is the fire to be put out. They’re not in a position to legislatively fight for more clear regulations.”

The lease Jesse Tree reviewed was in direct contradiction with the following portions of Idaho law:

## A tenant has the right to quiet enjoyment of a unit.

Annually, the Idaho Office of the Attorney General publishes the [Landlord and Tenant Manual](https://www.ag.idaho.gov/content/uploads/2025/08/LandlordTenant.pdf). It is not a substitution for the law, but it is a framework to the tenant/landlord relationship. Claims to enter a unit without notice are used as a common example of an improper lease clause in the manual, however, Idaho law does not enforce a mandatory notice period for landlord entry. Generally, a landlord will give 24 hours notice.

## A landlord cannot confiscate personal property. (Idaho Code 6-302)

Landlords cannot engage in self-help evictions, meaning evictions that remove a tenant without court intervention. They must follow the legal eviction process.

## A landlord cannot have a tenant sign away the right to trial by jury. (Idaho Code 6-313)

In the circumstance that a tenant sues their landlord, the landlord cannot stipulate that the tenant must refuse a trial by jury.

## A landlord must engage in the formal eviction process. Once an eviction has been processed by the court, the tenant has 72 hours to move out. (Idaho Code 6-304, 6-316)

When a tenant does not pay rent, a landlord must provide a three-day written notice in person or by mail. If the tenant fails to pay by the third day, then the landlord can begin filing the formal eviction process. During this time, the tenant is still allowed to live on the premises.

Idaho law does not hold landlords accountable for encouraging tenants to sign leases containing illegal clauses and many clauses still lack clear language on regulations. Lacking crucial protections for Idaho renters puts our community at risk of exploitation.
