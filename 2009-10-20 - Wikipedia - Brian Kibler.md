**Brian McCormick Kibler** [^8] is an American [collectible card game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectible_card_game "Collectible card game") player, game designer, and streamer. Previously, he designed [Ascension: Chronicle of the Godslayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascension:_Chronicle_of_the_Godslayer "Ascension: Chronicle of the Godslayer") with [Justin Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Gary "Justin Gary"), [Rob Dougherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Dougherty "Rob Dougherty") and John Fiorillo,[^9] and worked on *[Chaotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaotic "Chaotic")* and *[SolForge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SolForge "SolForge")* and was the lead designer of the [World of Warcraft Trading Card Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Warcraft_Trading_Card_Game "World of Warcraft Trading Card Game").[^10] [^11]

Kibler is also a professional card player, and has had great success at [Magic: The Gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering "Magic: The Gathering") with five [Pro Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Tour_\(Magic:_The_Gathering\) "Pro Tour (Magic: The Gathering)") Top 8s, winning [Pro Tour Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2009#Pro_Tour_Austin_\(16%E2%80%9318_October_2009\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2009") in 2009 [^12] and [Pro Tour Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2012#Pro_Tour_Dark_Ascension_%E2%80%93_Honolulu_\(10%E2%80%9312_February_2012\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2012") in 2012.[^13] He also has 13 [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_\(Magic:_The_Gathering\) "Grand Prix (Magic: The Gathering)") Top 8s, winning three of them including the first one held in the 1997–98 season and most recently Grand Prix Sendai.[^14] In August 2004, he won the inaugural [VS System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VS_System "VS System") Pro Circuit event taking home $40,000 and a spot in history as the game's first champion.[^15] Owing to a pause in his career, he was finally inducted into the Hall of Fame in 2010.[^16]

## Career

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/eb/Brian_Kibler.jpg/250px-Brian_Kibler.jpg)

Kibler at the 2009 Magic Worlds tournament in Rome

Kibler began his [Magic: The Gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering "Magic: The Gathering") career at the age of fifteen, placing 30th in the Junior Division of the first-ever Pro Tour, [Pro Tour New York 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_1996#Pro_Tour_%E2%80%93_New_York_\(16%E2%80%9318_February_1996\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 1996").[^11] Kibler would not qualify for the senior Pro Tour until [Pro Tour Chicago 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_1997%E2%80%9398#Pro_Tour_%E2%80%93_Chicago_\(10%E2%80%9312_October_1997\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 1997–98"), which he qualified for by winning [Grand Prix Toronto 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_1997%E2%80%9398#Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Toronto,_Copenhagen "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 1997–98"). All his opponents in the Top 8 of Grand Prix Toronto went on to work at [Wizards of the Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast "Wizards of the Coast"), including [Mike Turian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Turian "Mike Turian"), Matt Place and Erik Lauer.[^3] Kibler would also attend [Pro Tour Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_1997%E2%80%9398#Pro_Tour_%E2%80%93_Los_Angeles_\(6%E2%80%938_March_1998\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 1997–98") in the 1997–1998 season, placing within the Top 64, however he would not return to the Pro Tour until the [1999–2000 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_1999%E2%80%932000 "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 1999–2000").[^3]

Kibler made his first Pro Tour Top 8 at [Pro Tour Chicago 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2000%E2%80%9301#Pro_Tour_%E2%80%93_Chicago_\(1%E2%80%933_December_2000\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2000–01"). This was also the tournament where Kibler would earn the nickname of *The Dragonmaster*, after beating [Jon Finkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Finkel "Jon Finkel") in the Swiss rounds and [Zvi Mowshowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvi_Mowshowitz "Zvi Mowshowitz") in the quarterfinals with a combination of [Rith, the Awakener](http://gatherer.wizards.com/pages/card/Details.aspx?name=Rith%2C+the+Awakener) and [Armadillo Cloak](http://gatherer.wizards.com/pages/card/Details.aspx?name=Armadillo+Cloak).[^3] He would go on to lose to [Kai Budde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Budde "Kai Budde") in the semi-finals, taking third place in the overall standings.[^17] This would be Kibler's best Pro Tour performance before he retired from the game at the end of the [2004 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2003%E2%80%9304 "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2003–04"). In 2005, Kibler was one of a small number of players commemorated by Wizards of the Coast with a "Pro Player" collectible reference card.[^18] [^19]

After taking several years away from Magic (while working at [Wizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizards_of_the_Coast "Wizards of the Coast") ' direct competitor [Upper Deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Deck_Company "Upper Deck Company")) he returned in 2009.[^11] Kibler claims that a major motivating factor for his return was his desire to enter the [Magic: The Gathering Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Hall_of_Fame "Magic: The Gathering Hall of Fame"), which was created the year following his retirement.[^3] Kibler would make consecutive Pro Tour Top 8s upon his return, making Top 8 at [Pro Tour Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2009#Pro_Tour_Honolulu_\(5%E2%80%937_June_2009\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2009") before going on to win his first Pro Tour at [Pro Tour Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2009#Pro_Tour_Austin_\(16%E2%80%9318_October_2009\) "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2009"), defeating [Tsuyoshi Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuyoshi_Ikeda "Tsuyoshi Ikeda") in the tournament finals.[^20] By the end of the year he placed in the top 10 of the year's best performers on the Magic Pro Tour, achieving enough points to guarantee him invites to major championships, free air travel, and a guaranteed appearance fee for attending all 2010 events.[^21] Additionally, he was tied for 6th among player committee votes of the possible entrants for the 2009 [Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Hall_of_Fame "Magic: The Gathering Hall of Fame"), carrying around 20% of their vote.[^22] In 2010, Kibler's high performance continued, granting him his third Grand Prix title, another Pro Tour Top 8, and finally induction into the Hall of Fame. Kibler was inducted in the Hall of Fame Class of 2010 alongside [Gabriel Nassif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Nassif "Gabriel Nassif") and [Bram Snepvangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bram_Snepvangers "Bram Snepvangers").[^16]

In the [2012 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_Pro_Tour_season_2012 "Magic: The Gathering Pro Tour season 2012"), Kibler made his fifth career Pro Tour Top 8 at Pro Tour Dark Ascension in [Honolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honolulu "Honolulu"). The Top 8 would also feature two other Hall of Fame members in [Jon Finkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Finkel "Jon Finkel") and [Jelger Wiegersma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelger_Wiegersma "Jelger Wiegersma"). Brian Kibler defeated Finkel in the semi-finals in a match considered by commentators to be among the best matches in Magic history.[^13] [^23] This led to Kibler playing against teammate [Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Vitor_Damo_da_Rosa "Paulo Vitor Damo da Rosa") in the finals. Kibler then defeated Damo da Rosa 3–2 to win his second Pro Tour title. By winning the Pro Tour, Kibler secured his place in the first [Magic Players Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_World_Championship#2012_World_championship "Magic: The Gathering World Championship"). Kibler also became the United States National Champion by being the American player with the highest number of Pro Points after Pro Tour Avacyn Restored, leading the United States National Team to 12th place at the inaugural [World Magic Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_The_Gathering_World_Championship#2012_World_championship "Magic: The Gathering World Championship").

As of 2017, Kibler had earned a total of $279,747 in tournament winnings over his career,[^24] placing him then at #11 of all time earnings; and as of 2018, at #18 on the same list.[^4]

### Hearthstone

Kibler also plays the [digital card game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_card_game "Digital card game") *[Hearthstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearthstone_\(video_game\) "Hearthstone (video game)")*, winning the ChallengeStone tournaments in May 2015 and November 2016, the latter taking place at BlizzCon 2016.[^25] He took second place in the GEICO Brawl #1 in July 2015. Kibler has also been a commentator on *Hearthstone* tournaments, including the [BlizzCon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlizzCon "BlizzCon") and Championship tournaments.[^26] [^27] Kibler has been regularly streaming his *Hearthstone* gameplay on [Twitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitch_\(service\) "Twitch (service)"), and has a reputation in the community of being diplomatic, level-headed and rarely becoming angry.[^24] [^28] He prefers Constructed format to Arena.[^29]

In October 2019, Kibler announced that he would no longer be working with Blizzard as a commentator on their events, as a result of [their controversial decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blizzard_Entertainment#Hearthstone_ban_and_Hong_Kong_protests "Blizzard Entertainment") to ban esport competitor [Ng Wai Chung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blitzchung_controversy "Blitzchung controversy") for saying " [liberate Hong Kong, the revolution of our times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberate_Hong_Kong,_the_revolution_of_our_times "Liberate Hong Kong, the revolution of our times") " in a post-game interview. Kibler stated that although he agreed that Blizzard should issue a penalty, the actual penalty was too harsh. As such, he could "realistically never work with Hearthstone again".[^30] [^31] He still frequently streams Hearthstone.

## Personal life

Kibler grew up in [Hampstead, New Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead,_New_Hampshire "Hampstead, New Hampshire") and attended boarding school at [Phillips Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillips_Academy "Phillips Academy") in [Andover, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andover,_Massachusetts "Andover, Massachusetts").[^11] Kibler later attended [Emory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emory_University "Emory University") in [Atlanta, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta,_Georgia "Atlanta, Georgia"), majoring in Philosophy and Religion. On October 12, 2014, Kibler married Natalie Warren.[^32] [^33] On December 21, 2019, Kibler announced that he and his wife were separating.[^34]

In September, 2025, Kibler proposed to Olivia Gobert-Hicks live on stage at MagicCon Las Vegas [^35]
