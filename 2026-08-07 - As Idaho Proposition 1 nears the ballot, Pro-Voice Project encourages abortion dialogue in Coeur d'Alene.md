## As Idaho Proposition 1 nears the ballot, Pro-Voice Project encourages ‘abortion’ dialogue in Coeur d’Alene

## Pro-Voice Project hosts Coeur d’Alene abortion dialogue as Idaho Proposition 1 heads to voters, using personal stories to bridge divides and spark empathy.

COEUR D’ALENE, Idaho — The [Pro-Voice Project](https://www.theprovoiceproject.com/) (PVP) continues its mission to bring personal stories to the abortion conversation in majority conservative Kootenai County with Speak-Easier: Bridging the Abortion Divide as [Idaho Proposition 1](https://ballotpedia.org/Idaho_Proposition_1,_Reproductive_Freedom_and_Privacy_Act_Initiative_\(2026\)) awaits the people of Idaho to decide its fate in November.

The free event tonight at [Shenanigan Station](https://shenaniganstation.com/) between 5 p.m. and 8 p.m. will include part-interview style storytelling and part-interactive conversation designed to bring diverse voices to the discussion.

## Storytelling across divides

Jen Jackson Quintano, the executive director and founder of PVP, says the idea that abortion is cleanly drawn by partisan lines is a misconception.

“There’s way more nuance to it. The part that I love is sort of breaking down those perceived silos and seeing how we can connect on this, even when it seems like we’re coming from opposite directions,” Quintano said.

Quintano remembers in 2022 when the [Dobbs v. Jackson Women’s Health Organization](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/dobbs-v-jackson-womens-health-organization/) decision was leaked; she was deep in reflection. Quintano was writing about an abusive relationship in her 20s, during which she had an abortion, when the notification popped up on her phone.

“If I’d had to remain pregnant and remain in that difficult relationship, my life would be vastly different. I have an amazing daughter, an amazing husband. The thought that option was now taken away from so many people in my state … It just didn’t sit well, to say the least.”

Quintano, with a passion for the power of storytelling, founded PVP and staged their first storytelling event on a cold January day in 2023. Almost 200 people attended the Sandpoint production. Quintano, who had only planned it as a one-off, was amazed by the community’s response.

“It was healing for people, and it just opened up this whole conversation that we hadn’t been having,” Quintano said.

## Proposition 1 stories

Suzanne Gallus, a Catholic mother of seven, might not be the type of person you’d imagine taking part in gathering signatures to get [Idaho Proposition 1, Reproductive Freedom and Privacy Act Initiative (2026)](https://ballotpedia.org/Idaho_Proposition_1,_Reproductive_Freedom_and_Privacy_Act_Initiative_\(2026\)) on the ballot in November.

“I think there were people who listened to me that normally would not have,” Gallus said.

On the last day of door knocking Gallus said one of her most memorable doors was the last. A middle-aged man answered who happened to be a paramedic at one of the hospitals in Spokane.

He told Gallus that so many Idaho women in Kootenai County were coming to Spokane in emergency situations because they were warned off Idaho hospitals. In many cases the women didn’t have compatible insurance which resulted in loss of money for the hospital.

“He said, ‘I always considered myself pro-life, but these laws are crazy, not sustainable and they’re very dangerous.’ I just remember him saying, ‘I love Idaho. Idaho has a lot of good things about it, but this ain’t one of them,’” Gallus said.

Gallus, who has been pregnant 12 times, said “when you have somebody who has a large family saying, hey, we need access to abortion, because the more pregnancies you have, the more chances you have of something happening,” they tend to listen.

Gallus, who emphasized the importance of getting ‘our doctors back,’ will share a story this evening along with Megan Dardis-Kunz.

## Courage in the face of fear

Dardis-Kunz, a clinical assistant professor at Boise State University in social work, will share her personal experience engaging in conversations around abortion despite never personally seeking one.

“My focus is going to be on being brave, taking risks and doing scary things. Because talking about abortion here in North Idaho and Idaho in general can be really scary for folks,” Dardis-Kunz said.

The fear of speaking up for abortion access was realized for many after Summer Bushnell, a Post Falls blogger [found liable for defamation in 2024](https://www.krem.com/article/news/local/post-falls-blogger-liable-for-defamation/293-f57ae62f-1fd6-48e2-a12f-afa04e66f364), posted the names and counties of those who signed the ballot initiative.

In one post she went on to say, “In my opinion the 2,160 Republicans are highly likely to be RINOS \[republicans in name only\].”

Dardis-Kunz sent Bushnell an email asking her why she would post people’s names. She said Bushnell’s response was “‘because it’s public record.’”

## In defiance of silence

“I was just so irritated. I’m not concerned that my name is gonna be out there. But there’s going to be a lot of other people that are going to be really concerned,” Dardis-Kunz said. “Maybe a woman signed it and her husband is against it and they’re in an abusive relationship. To know that there are people in our community that are going to weaponize that against you is scary.”

Dardis-Kunz added she understands that not everyone has the safety or privilege to speak out, but if there are enough people who stand up it wont be scary anymore.

Quintano said that the event tonight is a part of building a community that can see one another and empathize.

“These laws have ripple effects. Let’s talk about that. You may feel one way or another about abortion, but we can all agree that we want to be able to deliver babies safely,” Quintano said.
