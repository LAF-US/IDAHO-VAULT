A new development is planned for a busy Meridian intersection, but it will only be accessible through surrounding neighborhoods.

The property is located at the southeast intersection of two major roads — Highway 16 and Chinden Blvd. But the project, Chinden16, will not be accessed by either road. According to permit documents filed with the City of Meridian, the development’s only two access points are located at the southern edge of the property. The roads leading into the proposed development come out of neighborhoods.

A site plan for the property shows 61 single-family homes and five buildings for “mixed employment.” Renderings for the single-family homes show two-story houses with front-facing attached garages. The site plan shows one large building and four smaller ones. Renderings of the buildings show both office-style buildings and some structures with large garage bay doors.

The permit documents say one of the “key considerations” for the property was to put in uses that have “minimal daily traffic.”

Franklin Sensors, a Meridian-based company that specializes in stud finders and digital levels, is the applicant for the project. According to the permit documents, the company is expanding its product line and needs additional space to grow. Part of the development will house their new location.

Franklin Sensors currently has a location on the north side of Chinden, across the street from this proposed development.

![Build Boise Display Ad 2 (1).png](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/60f7b78a-3c79-430c-9177-18a962229de0.png)

The largest building on site is planned for Franklin Sensors. The new building will house the company’s management, engineering, marketing, manufacturing, and inventory. At present, Franklin Sensors has 90 employees. The permit documents state this number could double in time at the new building.

The homes and building for Franklin Sensors are planned for the first phase of the project, and the four remaining buildings are planned for the second phase.

The homes are positioned in the middle and back of the property while the businesses surround the roadways. According to the permit documents, this layout was chosen to dampen any noise from passing cars.

Franklin Sensors President David Dorrough said the company has not decided what it will do with its current building on the north side of Chinden after it moves into the new one.
