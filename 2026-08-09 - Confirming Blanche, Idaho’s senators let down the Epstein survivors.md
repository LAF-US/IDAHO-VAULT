## Sad about Island Park development

For 36 years, I have owned a home in Island Park and watched our community grow. I respect private property rights, but decisions affecting the Henrys Lake Flats should reflect current planning, credible science, and meaningful public participation.

Caldera Flats is a proposed 32-lot subdivision on 160 acres under Rural Base zoning. On July 28, the Fremont County Planning and Zoning Commission denied the application, finding conflicts with the district’s purpose and Comprehensive Plan policies. In July 2026, the County Commission reversed that denial by a 2–1 vote.

Fremont County is still relying on a Comprehensive Plan adopted Dec. 17, 2008. Since then, development pressure and traffic have increased, while concerns about emergency services, groundwater, wetlands, wildlife habitat and cumulative development have grown.

The Henrys Lake Flats are not merely vacant acreage. They are part of a landscape valued for open space, water resources, wildlife movement and the Henrys Fork watershed.

The county should explain how it resolved Planning and Zoning’s findings, update the Comprehensive Plan through a transparent community process, and require independent analysis before approving major projects on the Flats.

Growth will come. It should follow a community vision, not be determined one project at a time.

*Richard Hodge, Island Park*

## What family values?

Our Idaho senators frequently invoke faith, family, and biblical values, yet they voted for legislation cutting roughly a trillion dollars from Medicaid. These cuts threaten healthcare access for seniors, individuals with disabilities, low-income families, and rural hospitals.

This vote directly contradicts the teachings of Jesus. In Matthew 25:35-40, Christ commands us to care for the hungry, the sick and the stranger, declaring: “Whatever you did for one of the least of these brothers and sisters of mine, you did for me.” He did not condition compassion on political convenience or budget spreadsheets.

While policy details are open to debate, leaders who claim to govern by Christian principles must align their legislative actions with Christ’s explicit mandate to protect the vulnerable. True faith is measured not by campaign rhetoric or Sunday photo opportunities, but by how we treat those in need.

Idaho voters must ask themselves: Do our senators’ votes reflect the teachings of Jesus, or simply the politics of the moment?

*Pamela W Nordby, Coeur d’Alene*
