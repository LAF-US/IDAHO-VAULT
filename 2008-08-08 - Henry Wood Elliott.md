Henry Wood Elliott, as a member of the 1871 Hayden U.S. Geological Survey expedition to Yellowstone.

**Henry Wood Elliott** (November 13, 1846 – May 25, 1930) was an American [watercolor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolor_painting "Watercolor painting") painter, [author](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Author "Author"), and [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism "Environmentalism") whose work primarily focused on [Alaskan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska") subjects. He was the author of the [1911 Hay-Elliott Fur Seal Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_Fur_Seal_Convention_of_1911 "North Pacific Fur Seal Convention of 1911"), the first international treaty on wildlife conservation.[^1]

A number of his works have an [ethnographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnography "Ethnography") bent, displaying [aboriginal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas "Indigenous peoples of the Americas") Alaskans engaging in traditional practices; some of these works are stored in the [National Anthropological Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anthropological_Archives "National Anthropological Archives") at the [Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian "Smithsonian"). Elliott also focused on the Alaskan landscape and wildlife.[^2]

In 1886, Elliott published a book entitled *[Our Arctic Province: Alaska and the Seal Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Arctic_Province:_Alaska_and_the_Seal_Islands?action=edit&redlink=1 "Our Arctic Province: Alaska and the Seal Islands (page does not exist)")*, which contains an in-depth exploration of Alaska's history, geography, people, and wildlife.[^3]

He became involved in early conservation efforts of the [fur seal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_seal "Fur seal"), in 1905 co-authoring a document with [United States Secretary of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_State "United States Secretary of State") [John Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hay "John Hay") that would eventually become the [North Pacific Fur Seal Convention of 1911](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pacific_Fur_Seal_Convention_of_1911 "North Pacific Fur Seal Convention of 1911"), the first international treaty dedicated to the conservation of wildlife.[^4]
