# The Others and the Long Night

> The threat the saga is half-named for. While the realm fights over a chair, an ancient enemy of ice gathers in the far north — and the histories have filed the last apocalypse under legend. One of a trilogy with `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Wall-and-the-Watch-2026-06-02]]` and `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-Beyond-the-Wall-2026-06-02]]`.
>
> **Provenance.** Tiers: **[canon]**, **[legend]** (in-world myth, esp. the deep past), **[theory]**, **[show]**, **[reading]**. ⚠️ The Others are **deliberately mysterious in the books**, and this is the topic with the **largest book-vs-show divergence** — flagged heavily. Verify before publication.

## The Others

- **[canon]** The **Others** (the books also call them **white walkers** on occasion; the HBO show standardized that as the primary name) are not wildlings, not ordinary monsters, and **not themselves the walking dead** — they are **ancient, alien beings of ice and cold**, tall, gaunt, and terrible-beautiful, with eyes like **blue stars**, drifting out of the **Lands of Always Winter**. They bring an unnatural, killing cold; their armor and crystalline swords are so cold that ordinary steel **shatters** against them.
- **[canon]** They **raise the dead as wights** — reanimated corpses (human *and* animal) with burning blue eyes, building an army from their enemies' fallen. ⚠️ *[show]* The rule that killing an Other drops the wights it raised is an **HBO** mechanic; the **books** establish no such per-Other link (wights are put down by **fire** or dismemberment).
- **[canon]** They can be slain by **dragonglass / obsidian** (Valyrians' *"frozen fire"*) — the one substance *demonstrated* on the page, when Samwell Tarly kills one. **Valyrian steel** is widely believed to work too, but ⚠️ *[theory]* that rests on the inference that the old word *"dragonsteel"* means Valyrian steel; **no Valyrian-steel blade has been tested against an Other in the books** (the show made the weakness explicit). The **children of the forest** gave the first Watchmen dragonglass; both substances tie the ice-enemy back to **fire and old magic** (→ the dragons node).
- **[canon/reading]** Their **motive is left unknowable.** **Craster's** sacrificed infant sons (→ Beyond the Wall node) hint the Others **take and transform** the young — one of the only oblique glimpses of what they want.
- ⚠️ **[show]** The series invented the **"Night King"** as their leader and had the **children create them** to fight the First Men; **neither is confirmed book canon.** The books' Others are far more enigmatic, with no named leader in the published text. (Distinguish, too, from the books' **Night's King** — a fallen human Lord Commander of legend → the Wall & Watch node.)

## The Long Night

- **[legend]** **~8,000 years ago**, in the **Age of Heroes**, came the **Long Night** — a winter that lasted a **generation**, a darkness in which the Others first swept down out of the north and nearly **ended humanity**. Crops failed, children starved, and "the cold gods" walked among men.
- **[legend]** It ended in the **Battle for the Dawn**. The legend of the **Last Hero**: with his companions and a dragonsteel sword, he sought out the **children of the forest** for aid; men, children, and giants together threw the Others back into the far north. In the aftermath came the founding of the **Night's Watch** and the raising of the **Wall** (→ the Wall & Watch node).
- **[legend / cross-cultural]** The Long Night is remembered **across the whole world**, not just Westeros — echoed in the prophecies of **R'hllor** (the long night that is to come; **Azor Ahai** and Lightbringer defeating the darkness), in **Yi Ti's** "Blood Betrayal" and the wrath of the **Lion of Night**, and in the Rhoynar and Asshai'i traditions. That a single catastrophe appears in every faith's deep memory is the strongest in-world sign it **really happened** (→ the religions node).
- **[reading]** The dread the saga runs on: *winter is coming*, and the Long Night with it. The Others' return at the very moment the realm is most divided is the engine beneath the whole story.

## Ice and fire — the title's war

- **[reading]** The series' name states the real conflict: **ice vs. fire.** The **Others (ice)** are answered, in prophecy and in the weapons that kill them, by **fire** — dragonglass ("frozen fire"), Valyrian steel (spell-forged in dragonflame), **R'hllor's** flames, and the **dragons** themselves, reborn in the east at the same hour the dead stir in the north (→ the dragons node, the religions node). The petty wars for the Iron Throne are the *wrong* war; **the song that matters is ice against fire**, and almost everyone is looking the wrong way.

## Theme — the apocalypse the record erased

**[reading]** This is the capstone of the session's whole spine. The realm's **curated histories wrote the Long Night off as legend**; the maesters file the Others under **"grumkins and snarks"** (→ the Maesters node); the Wall is starved of men (→ the Wall & Watch node). The single most important fact in the world — *the dark is coming back* — is the one the establishment chose to **forget**, because it was old and inconvenient and could not be used in the Game of Thrones. And the ones who still **remember** are the memory-peoples on the margins: the **North** that remembers, the **free folk** who never stopped living it, the **Watch** sworn to a threat no one funds. The propaganda fossil forgets on purpose; **winter remembers anyway.** (→ the Empty Office and Stylings notes.)
