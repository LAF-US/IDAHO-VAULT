[[Idaho]] workers are seeing some of the fastest wage growth in the country, according to the Idaho Department of Labor’s latest annual occupational employment and wages report, released this week for 2025.

The annual survey assesses wages across 830 occupations in Idaho. The Department of Labor reported an overall wage increase of 4.7%, or $1.31 an hour, from the previous year. Median wages rose 3.2% over the year, placing Idaho in the top three nationwide for wage growth.

The report also highlights wide differences between what Idaho workers earn, depending on their jobs. The state’s most populated role, fast food and counter workers, earns a median of $13.91. By comparison, the second most populated position in the state, general and operations managers, has a median wage of $38.65. That category employs more than 24,000 Idahoans.

The wage figures also show how some jobs compare with what experts estimate is needed to cover basic costs. According to the Massachusetts Institute of Technology’s Living Wage Calculator, a single adult with no children should make at least $23.60 an hour to be considered earning a living wage in Idaho, with that amount rising dramatically for people with children.

Despite disparities across occupations, Idaho’s overall median income across more than 850,000 employees is close to that benchmark for a single adult with no children, at a median wage of $23.06.

The highest earners in Idaho remain medical professionals. Anesthesiologists, general physicians, and family medicine physicians were the top three highest-paid positions, making between $216 an hour and $166, respectively.

Gov. Brad Little, in a statement today on the report, said, “Rising wages mean more opportunities for families, more confidence for workers, and more prosperity in communities across our state. Idaho’s continued wage growth reflects the strength of our economy, and our commitment to creating an environment where businesses can thrive, and workers can earn more.”
