In a revelation that might come as a surprise to many, especially those who navigate them daily, Idaho's roads have been ranked the best in the United States. According to a study by the personal injury firm Munley Law, which analyzed data from the federal Bureau of Transportation Statistics, a staggering 94.78 percent of Idaho's roads meet the acceptable quality standard. This places the Gem State at the pinnacle of road quality across the nation, a distinction that may shock Idahoans accustomed to critiquing their own roads.

![Photo by Drew Lindsley on Unsplash](https://townsquare.media/site/657/files/2024/04/attachment-drew-lindsley-7UdSup394EI-unsplash.jpg?w=980&q=75)

Photo by Drew Lindsley on Unsplash

The study sheds light on an often-overlooked aspect of state infrastructure, highlighting not only the annoyance of potholes and deteriorating road surfaces but also their impact on vehicle wear and tear. Idaho's top ranking suggests that the state has effectively managed to maintain its roads, thereby minimizing these issues. This achievement is particularly noteworthy given the common grievances voiced in every state about road conditions, proving that Idaho's road maintenance efforts have set a benchmark for the rest of the country.

Georgia and Tennessee follow closely behind Idaho, with 94.5 percent and 94.17 percent of roads meeting the acceptable quality standard, respectively. These states, like Idaho, likely benefit from significant investment in highway maintenance and a collaborative funding effort that spans federal, state, and local governments. Tennessee, for instance, has managed to avoid borrowing money for road construction for 45 years, indicating a stable and robust funding system similar to the one that may contribute to Idaho's success.

![Canva](https://townsquare.media/site/657/files/2024/04/attachment-33.-Logan-UT-5.jpg?w=980&q=75)

On the other end of the spectrum, states like New Jersey, Rhode Island, and Connecticut face the most challenges, with less than 60 percent of their roads meeting the acceptable quality standard. The study attributes these poor conditions to heavy traffic and adverse weather conditions, which are less prevalent in Idaho, giving it an advantage.

## The 9 Criminals on Idaho's Death Row and Their Disgusting Crimes

Held in their 12'x7' cells 23 hours a day, these are the nine people currently on death row in Idaho. Idaho has performed three executions since 1977.

## Azad Abdullah

The Boise man was found guilty of first-degree murder of his wife. Abdullah suffocated his wife before setting their family home on fire. According to archived news reports, Abdullah’s wife was seeking a divorce before the crime. There were four children in the home when he set the fire. Two older kids went to get a neighbor’s house for help. The neighbor rescued an infant from the burning home. Another child under two was found safe in the backyard. The murder took place in October 2002.

## Timothy Dunlap

In 1991, Dunlap robbed Security State Bank in Soda Springs. Tonya Crane was the teller behind the counter that day. She handed him close to $32,000, but he still shot her from less than two feet away. Murderpedia explains that Crane was the second woman that he murder that year. 10 days before the bank robbery, Dunlap took his girlfriend on a picnic in Ohio, blindfolded her like he had a surprise for her and then shot her in the neck and head with a crossbow.

## James Hairston

At the age of 19, Hairston robbed and killed a couple in their 70s at their farmhouse in Downey. [According to the Lewiston Tribue](https://lmtribune.com/northwest/executor-sentenced-to-death-james-hairston-20-helped-kill-elderly-couple-for-30-credit-card/article_94e14eef-e607-5067-8bc6-047f232bca5f.html), the judge believed that Hairston got a rush out of shooting people and would kill again if he had the opportunity. According to court documents, Hairston shot the husband in the head as he was sitting at his kitchen table before shooting the wife. They got away with $30 in cash, credit cards and personal property which they pawned.

## Gerald Pizzuto

According to court documents, Gerald Pizzuto murdered Berta Herndon and her nephew, Del Herndon, at a cabin north of McCall. He initially approached them with a rifle, before tying the pair up so that he could rob them. The crime escalated when he beat them to death with a hammer and also shot Herndon between the eyes.

Pizzuto's legal team has made several appeals to have his death sentence thrown out. In both 2002 and 2008, the court decided to reject an appeal saying the punishment should be voided on the grounds that it is unconstitutional to execute inmates found to have an intellectual disability. Under Idaho law, that means he'd have to have an IQ of 70 or below. [According to Murderpedia](https://murderpedia.org/male.P/p/pizzuto-gerald.htm), he scored a 72 before he was sentenced.

KTVB reports that Pizzuto is suffering from terminal bladder cancer and that his lawyers tried to get his death sentence changed to life without parole since he was already dying of natural causes. That request was denied in October 2022.

His execution has been paused twice since then due to the lack of availability of the chemicals needed to carry out the execution.

## Thomas Creech

The crime Creech is on death row for right now is for beating a fellow inmate to death with a sock full of batteries. We say right now because the 1981 crime led to his second conviction to death row. He’d previously been sentenced to death row for killing two men in Valley County in the 1970s. That sentence was reduced to life in prison. While serving time for the double homicide, Creech told prison staff that he’d committed more than 40 other murders. [According to Murderpedia.org](https://murderpedia.org/male.C/c/creech-thomas-eugene.htm), Creech explained many of his killings were actually sacrifices made for a Satanic cult.

## Robin Row

What mother could kill her own children? The answer in Idaho is Robin Row. In 1992, she set her family home on fire, leading to the carbon monoxide poisoning deaths of her husband, her 10-year-old sons and her 8-year-old daughter while they were sleeping. She killed them to collect life insurance money from outrageous policies she took out on her family. Some believe that she may have killed two of her other children in the late 70s and early 80s.

## Chad Daybell

We're not even sure that we have to get too in depth with why Daybell is on death row. He and his wife, Lori's, crimes were the focus of national news for months. Daybell was found guilty of first degree murder for the deaths of his first wife, Tammy and Lori's kids, Tylee Ryan and JJ Vallow. A great deal of evidence showed that the couple had extreme religious beliefs and thought their family members were possessed by dark spirits.
