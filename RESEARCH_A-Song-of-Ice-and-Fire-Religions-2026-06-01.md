# Research: *A Song of Ice and Fire* — Competing Religions, a Layered Anthropology

**Source:** Online research, fanned out across five angles and cross-checked.
**Compiled by:** Claude Code, 2026-06-01, on branch `claude/song-ice-fire-sCaAD`.
**Companion to:** the current-state survey and the geography-&-politics note. The **Drowned God** is treated in depth in the geography-&-politics file's institutions section; here it sits in the comparative frame.

> **Provenance note (read first).** These are **fictional facts**: "verification" = consistency with **canon** — the novels, *The World of Ice & Fire* / *Fire & Blood* (in-world "histories," themselves unreliable), and **A Wiki of Ice and Fire** (awoiaf). awoiaf, Wikipedia, and Fandom all returned **HTTP 403** to automated fetching, so claims rest on search-engine extracts. Three tiers are flagged throughout: **[legend]** = in-world myth (esp. deep-past / *TWOIAF*); **[show]** = TV divergence from the books; **[GRRM]/[critic]** = real-world authorial or critical commentary, *not* in-world fact. Book canon is preferred over the adaptations.

## The anthropology first: four through-lines

Before the catalogue, the patterns that make this a *system* rather than a list:

1. **The asymmetry of demonstrable magic.** The faiths split into those whose power visibly *works* and those whose gods are silent. The **old gods** (greensight through weirwoods), **R'hllor** (resurrections, shadow-assassins, fire-visions, glamors), and the **Many-Faced God** (face-changing) all manifest on the page. The **Faith of the Seven** (dominant across the south) and the regional **Drowned God** of the ironborn show little or no verifiable magic. ⚠️ *[critic]* This is a sound reading of the text, **not** an explicit authorial statement; GRRM keeps the question open by design (below).
2. **Religion maps to peoples and economies.** Gods fit their societies: the pastoral **Lhazareen** keep the gentle **Great Shepherd**; nomad-horsemen **Dothraki** worship a **Horse God** and fear "the poison water"; mercantile slave-founded **Braavos** elevates the **Moonsingers** who guided its founders; **Qohor's** dark guild-economy feeds the **Black Goat**; **Lys's** pleasure-house culture weeps with its **Weeping Lady**.
3. **Religion is politics by other means.** The **High Sparrow** asserts "the law of the gods stands above kings"; **Cersei** rearms the Faith Militant and is destroyed by it; **Melisandre** reframes Stannis's civil war as a holy war for **Azor Ahai**; **Aeron Damphair** invokes the Drowned God to legitimize a kingsmoot. Faith is leverage, and it tends to recoil on those who wield it cynically.
4. **Conquest displaces gods.** The religious map is a record of invasions — the **Andals** cutting down the southern weirwoods and imposing the Seven; the **North** and **Iron Islands** as the holdouts; **Valyrian** religious dissidents founding the "dark" Free Cities; **Daenerys** colliding with Ghiscari slaver-religion. *[GRRM]* Martin — a self-described lapsed Catholic — built **many competing faiths on purpose**, with no "one true church," and leaves it unconfirmed whether any god is real.

## The Westerosi faiths

### The Faith of the Seven (the "New Gods")

- **One god, seven aspects** ("the Seven Who Are One"), not seven gods: the **Father** (justice), **Mother** (mercy/fertility), **Maiden** (innocence), **Crone** (wisdom; bears a lantern), **Warrior** (courage), **Smith** (craft) — and the **Stranger** (death/the unknown), who is genderless and shunned (no one sings of him). Symbol: the **seven-pointed star**; holy book: **The Seven-Pointed Star**; doctrine of seven heavens and seven hells. [awoiaf: Faith of the Seven]
- **Origins:** brought by the **Andals** from Andalos in Essos ~6,000 years before the series; Andal warriors carved seven-pointed stars into their flesh, and the conquest supplanted the old gods across **most of the south** — though a few southern houses (notably **House Blackwood**) still keep them — leaving the **North** the only region where the old gods stayed dominant. [awoiaf: Andals]
- **Structure:** the **High Septon** (head, resides at the Great Sept of Baelor; ⚠️ canonically sets aside his name on elevation), the **Most Devout** (governing council, elects him), **septons/septas**, the **Silent Sisters** (death-rite order sworn to the Stranger; vows of silence — the removed-tongue idea is an in-world myth), and **begging brothers**. [awoiaf: Silent sisters]
- **The Faith Militant:** two orders — the **Warrior's Sons** ("Swords," knights) and the **Poor Fellows** ("Stars," smallfolk). Outlawed by **Maegor I** (bounties: a gold dragon per Warrior's Son scalp, a silver stag per Poor Fellow), settled under **Jaehaerys I** via Septon Barth. ⚠️ *[legend / Fire & Blood]* **Revived** ~300 years later when the **High Sparrow** maneuvers Cersei into rearming it — leading to her **walk of atonement**.
- **Practice:** prayer to the apt aspect (Warrior before battle, Mother in childbirth…), the **cloak ceremony** at weddings, anointing with **seven oils**, sanctuary, and sworn oaths. ⚠️ *[show]* The verbatim wedding-vow script is an adaptation rendering; the books give the structure, not a fixed liturgy.
- ⚠️ *[GRRM/critic]* **Modeled on medieval Catholicism** — the seven-in-one as a transformed Trinity ("Holy Septinity"), aspects functioning like patron saints, the Faith Militant like crusading orders (Templars/Hospitallers), and the **Sparrows explicitly inspired by the Reformation** and the mendicant friars.

### The old gods of the forest

- **Nameless, numberless** gods of stream, stone, and tree — **no clergy, no scripture, no temples**; worship is private and silent. The **oldest** religion in Westeros. [awoiaf: Old gods]
- **Origins:** worshipped first by the **children of the forest**, who carved the faces into the **weirwoods**; adopted by the **First Men** after **the Pact** (sealed on the **Isle of Faces** in the Gods Eye, ending their war and sparing the weirwoods). [awoiaf: Pact; Children of the forest]
- **Weirwoods & godswoods:** white bark, red leaves, red sap; a carved **heart tree** sits at the center of a **godswood**. The children believe the trees *are* the gods. Most southern heart trees were cut down (First Men, then Andals); living weirwoods survive mainly in the **North and beyond the Wall**. [awoiaf: Weirwood; Godswood]
- **Greenseers & the weirnet:** rare children born with green/red eyes could **see through the weirwoods' faces** across space and time. The **last greenseer** is **Brynden Rivers ("Bloodraven")** — the **Three-Eyed Crow** — fused to the roots of a great weirwood beyond the Wall, training **Bran Stark** (feeding him weirwood paste to "wed him to the trees"). Tied to **warging/skinchanging**, more accepted among the free folk. [awoiaf: Brynden Rivers]
- ⚠️ *[critic]* Structurally the **indigenous, pre-Andal animism displaced by conquest** — and the faith with the clearest *empirical* basis (greenseer magic demonstrably works), though whether the old gods are *conscious deities* or just the weirwood/greenseer network is left open.

### The Drowned God (ironborn)

The dualistic sea-faith of the Iron Islands — **Drowned God vs. Storm God**, the creed *"What is dead may never die, but rises again, harder and stronger,"* the drowning/resuscitation baptism, the **Drowned Men** priesthood, and **Aeron Damphair**. **Treated in full** in `RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-Geography-and-Politics-2026-06-01.md` → *Cross-cutting institutions → The Drowned God*. In the comparative frame: a faith that sanctifies a raiding economy, holdout against the Andal Seven, and (like the Seven) shows little demonstrable magic. ⚠️ *[GRRM]* loosely **Norse/Viking**; the watery halls echo Valhalla and the rite inverts Christian baptism.

## The Essosi faiths

### R'hllor, the Lord of Light (the red faith)

- **Genuine dualism** — unusual in this world: **R'hllor** (Lord of Light, Heart of Fire, "God of Flame and Shadow") against **the Great Other** (god of ice, darkness, death). Litany: *"The night is dark and full of terrors."* ⚠️ *[critic]* compared to **Zoroastrianism** and **Catharism**. [awoiaf: R'hllor]
- **Messianic prophecy:** ⚠️ *[legend/prophecy]* **Azor Ahai**, who forged **Lightbringer** by tempering it in his wife **Nissa Nissa's** heart, prophesied to be **born again** "amidst salt and smoke" amid a bleeding star, to wake dragons from stone — equated with **"the prince that was promised"** (a gender-neutral term). Candidates the text dangles: Stannis (Melisandre's pick), Daenerys (Maester Aemon's), Rhaegar/Aegon. The **red comet** is read as the sign.
- **Priesthood & temples:** red-robed priests — **Melisandre** of Asshai (Stannis), **Thoros** of Myr, **Moqorro**, and **Benerro**, High Priest at the **Volantis** Temple of the Lord of Light (the **largest temple in the world**, ~3× the Great Sept; staffed by slaves bought as children, flame-tattooed). Strongest among **slaves and freedmen** — Benerro preaches **Daenerys as Azor Ahai reborn**, alarming the slave-owning triarchs. [awoiaf: Benerro]
- **Magic that plainly works:** fire-visions/prophecy (though often **misread** — Melisandre backs the wrong man), **glamors** (Mance as Rattleshirt), **shadow-assassins** (the shadow that kills **Renly**), and **resurrection** via the **last kiss** (**Thoros** raises **Beric Dondarrion** six times; Beric's final gift revives Catelyn as **Lady Stoneheart** — book canon). ⚠️ Fire-immunity is **contested** (ruby-mediated, not innate). ⚠️ *[show]* **Jon Snow's resurrection is show-only**; in the books his stabbing is an unresolved cliffhanger.
- ⚠️ *[critic]* The rare faith with *evidence* — and the series frames that as **dangerous**: real prophecy that is opaque and misread, zeal sliding into burnings and human sacrifice (the burning of the Seven at Dragonstone; king's-blood leeches).

### The Many-Faced God & the death cults

- **The Many-Faced God** (Faceless Men, **House of Black and White**, Braavos): a **syncretic** theology that *all* death-gods are **one** god of death by many faces — the **Stranger**, the **Black Goat**, the **Lion of Night**, the **Weeping Lady**, the Merling King, Bakkalon, etc. (statues of which line the temple). Death is **"the gift"**: worshippers drink from a poisoned pool for a painless end. Mottoes: ***valar morghulis*** / ***valar dohaeris*** ("all men must die / must serve"). Predate the Doom; trained from a multi-ethnic slave underclass that merged its faiths. [awoiaf: Faceless Men]

#### The Many-Faced God (the deity)

Distinct from the temple/order below — the *theology* itself, one of the setting's most pointed pieces of comparative religion.

- **A god of death known by many faces.** The central tenet is radically **syncretic**: every culture's death-god is the *same* god wearing a different mask — the **Stranger** (the Seven), the **Black Goat** (Qohor), the **Lion of Night** (Yi Ti), the **Weeping Lady** (Lys), the Hooded Wayfarer, Bakkalon, the Moon-Pale Maiden, the Merling King, a horse god. All worship of death is worship of *him*. [awoiaf: Faceless Men]
- **Born from a slave underclass.** The doctrine arose among slaves of a hundred lands and faiths in the **Valyrian mines** (before the Doom), facing death daily; the first Faceless Man concluded their many death-gods were one and made himself its instrument — so the theology is itself a product of forced multiculturalism. [awoiaf: Faceless Men]
- **Death as mercy — "the gift."** The god is served, not feared; killing — both the suffering who drink the poisoned pool and contracted targets — is a **sacrament** ("the gift"). A death is *owed* the god: a life for a life. [awoiaf: House of Black and White]
- **The creed as theology:** ***valar morghulis*** / ***valar dohaeris*** ("all men must die / all men must serve") doubles as password and doctrine — death is the one universal truth, so to serve death is to serve the only god none escape.
- **The annihilation of self.** To serve him, the acolyte becomes **"no one"** — identity dissolved, faces interchangeable. The god of all faces is served by people of *no* face.

#### The House of Black and White (in depth)

The cult's temple in Braavos — and the fullest expression of the Many-Faced God's theology.

- **The building:** a **windowless** temple of dark grey stone on a rocky knoll near Braavos's temple district, black-tiled. Its great door has two twelve-foot leaves — one **weirwood (white)**, one **ebony (black)** — each bearing a carved **moon-face** of the opposite wood. (⚠️ The carved moon-face is canonical; an "iron-ring knocker" is *not* corroborated — treat as unverified.) The interior is dark and candlelit. [awoiaf: House of Black and White]
- **The poisoned pool — "the gift":** at the sanctuary's center, a **black pool ~10 ft across** lit by dim red candles. The suffering come to **light a candle, pray, and drink the sweet dark water** from a stone cup — it is poisoned, granting a **peaceful death**. The temple is, functionally, a place people come to die; the priests deem giving that death holy. [awoiaf: House of Black and White]
- **The Hall of Faces:** beneath the temple, acolytes **strip and wash the corpses** ("serving the dead"); the deepest vault preserves the **faces of hundreds of the dead** in alcoves — the raw material of the Faceless Men's **face-changing** magic. A side passage is walled with bones and columns of skulls. [awoiaf: House of Black and White]
- **The priesthood:** the gentle **"kindly man,"** the **"waif"** (childlike but decades old), and acolytes, all in **black-and-white robes**. Initiates train via a **lying game** (detect others' lies; master your own face), plus poisons and languages, toward the goal of becoming **"no one."** [awoiaf: Kindly man; Faceless Men]
- **The order:** ⚠️ *[legend]* arose among **slaves in the Valyrian mines before the Doom**; the first Faceless Man concluded all the slaves' gods were one god of death and made himself its instrument. Prices scale to the target (killing Daenerys was said to cost "more than an army"), and a **death is owed** — payment may be non-monetary (a man who asks them to kill a child may be told the price is *his own* child). They refuse contracts against their faith. ⚠️ *[legend/speculation]* some in-world scholars tie the **Doom of Valyria** to Faceless Men assassinating the mages who held the Fourteen Flames in check. [awoiaf: Faceless Men] → see `RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Doom-of-Valyria-2026-06-01.md`
- **Arya Stark's arc** (book canon, *AFFC*/*ADWD*): she arrives with the **iron coin** and *"valar morghulis"*; washes corpses; lives as **"Cat of the Canals"** gathering secrets; commits an *unsanctioned* killing of the deserter **Dareon**; is **blinded** as discipline (becoming the beggar "Beth") and regains sight after passing; her first *sanctioned* kill is poisoning a **ship-insurance seller**. ⚠️ *[show]* HBO compressed this heavily — the "game of faces," the waif as a pursuing antagonist Arya kills and skins, and the Meryn Trant murder are show inventions; the Cat-of-the-Canals / insurance-seller and Dareon material are book-only.
- **The Black Goat of Qohor:** daily **blood sacrifice** — beasts ordinarily, condemned criminals on holy days, ⚠️ *[legend]* nobles' own children in crisis. Founded by Valyrian dissidents rejecting the Freehold's tolerance.
- **The Moonsingers** (Braavos): a lunar priestesshood (of the Jogos Nhai) who ⚠️ *[legend]* guided the escaped slaves to found Braavos; their temple is the city's largest.
- **The Weeping Lady of Lys** (a sorrowful goddess bearing a tear-filled bowl); **the Great Shepherd** of the pastoral **Lhazareen** (the "lamb men"; **Mirri Maz Duur** is his godswife); the **Bearded Priests of Norvos** (theocratic rulers, ritual flagellation, a god whose name is revealed only to initiates — ⚠️ "Noborosi" is **unverified**, do not assert); and the **Church of Starry Wisdom** (⚠️ *[legend]* an explicit **Lovecraft** homage tied to the Bloodstone Emperor and a fallen black stone).

### The Dothraki, the Ghiscari, and the Further East

- **Dothraki:** worship a **Horse God** (⚠️ *[show]* "the Great Stallion" is the TV name; the books say "Horse God") — **henotheistic**. Prophecy of **"the stallion who mounts the world,"** a khal of khals to unite and conquer; the **dosh khaleen** are seer-widows at Vaes Dothrak. The stars are a herd of fiery horses ridden by the dead in the **night lands**; the dead khal is burned on a pyre with his horse; the sea is **"the poison water."** [awoiaf: Dothraki]
- **Ghiscari (slaver cities):** polytheistic, centered on the **Harpy** (the highborn are "sons of the harpy"; hence the Meereenese insurgency). All-female clergy, the **Graces** — **Green Grace** (high priestess), **Blue Graces** (healers/surgeons), Pink Graces (⚠️ function "unrevealed" in canon). Blood spilled in the **fighting pits** is held a sacrifice — the crux of Daenerys's conflict over reopening them. [awoiaf: Ghiscari]
- **Yi Ti / the Further East:** ⚠️ *[legend, from TWOIAF]* a dualistic creation myth — the **Lion of Night** and the **Maiden-Made-of-Light**, whose son was the **God-on-Earth**; the **Long Night** recast as divine punishment after the Bloodstone Emperor usurped the Amethyst Empress. Yi Ti is ruled by worshipped **god-emperors**.

## Synthesis

Martin's world has **no orthodoxy and no confirmed god** — only a plurality of faiths that feel real because they do real social work: legitimizing kings, organizing economies, marking conquests, and consoling the dying. The deepest tension he builds is **epistemic**: the faiths with the most *visible* power (R'hllor, the old gods, the Many-Faced God) are not obviously the most *true* — R'hllor's prophecies are real but misread, the old gods may be a network rather than minds, and the two faiths that govern most of Westeros (the Seven, the Drowned God) work no visible miracles at all. ⚠️ *[GRRM]* Martin has said we will essentially **never "meet" a god** in the books, and that a force stops being "magic" once it can be reliably commanded — so the supernatural stays ambiguous on purpose. The throughline he names for the whole series applies here too: *"the human heart in conflict with itself."* (Faulkner, quoted by GRRM as a writing creed — not a statement specifically about religion.)
