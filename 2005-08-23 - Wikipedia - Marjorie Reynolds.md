**Marjorie Reynolds** ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") **Goodspeed**; August 12, 1917 – February 1, 1997) was an American film and television actress who appeared in more than 50 films, including the 1942 musical [*Holiday Inn*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holiday_Inn_\(film\) "Holiday Inn (film)"),[^1] in which she and [Bing Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Crosby "Bing Crosby") introduced the song " [White Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Christmas_\(song\) "White Christmas (song)") " in a duet, albeit with her singing dubbed by [Martha Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Mears "Martha Mears").

## Early life

Reynolds was born Marjorie Goodspeed in [Buhl, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhl,_Idaho "Buhl, Idaho"),[^2] the daughter of a doctor and his wife.[^3] She acted under the names Marjorie Goodspeed and **Marjorie Moore**.[^4] When she was three years old, her family moved to Los Angeles, California.[^3] She began to take dancing lessons at age 4. She attended [Los Angeles High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_High_School "Los Angeles High School").[^5]

## Career

Beginning at age 6,[^3] Reynolds was a featured child actress in such silent films as *[Scaramouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scaramouche_\(1923_film\) "Scaramouche (1923 film)")* (1923). At age 8 she stopped acting to concentrate on education until leaving school at 16 to play a ballerina in [Herbert Brenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_Brenon "Herbert Brenon") 's *[Wine, Women and Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine,_Women_and_Song_\(film\) "Wine, Women and Song (film)")* (1933). She went on to appear in bit parts in many films, including *[Gone with the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_\(film\) "Gone with the Wind (film)")* (1939) and as a [chorus girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorus_girl "Chorus girl") in [Paramount Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramount_Pictures "Paramount Pictures") musicals. Her first speaking role was in *[Murder in Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_in_Greenwich_Village "Murder in Greenwich Village")* (1937) and she then appeared in a number of [westerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerns "Westerns") for [Poverty Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Row "Poverty Row") studios opposite most of the cowboy stars of the time with the exception of [Gene Autry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_Autry "Gene Autry").[^6]

Reynolds played the loyal girlfriend opposite wrongly accused [Richard Cromwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Cromwell_\(actor\) "Richard Cromwell (actor)") in *[Enemy Agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemy_Agent "Enemy Agent")* (1940). That same year, in *[The Fatal Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fatal_Hour_\(1940_film\) "The Fatal Hour (1940 film)")*, Reynolds appeared for [Monogram Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monogram_Pictures "Monogram Pictures") as a reporter on the trail of [Boris Karloff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Karloff "Boris Karloff") 's detective James Lee Wong and opposite [Grant Withers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Withers "Grant Withers") as a cop.

Perhaps her best-known film was *Holiday Inn* (1942), which introduced the classic song " [White Christmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Christmas_\(song\) "White Christmas (song)") ". She performed the song both as a duet with [Bing Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bing_Crosby "Bing Crosby") and later in a solo performance, although her singing was dubbed by [Martha Mears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martha_Mears "Martha Mears").[^7] The movie also showcased her dancing ability.

She also had major roles in [Fritz Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Lang "Fritz Lang") 's *[Ministry of Fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Fear "Ministry of Fear")* (1944) and in the movie *[Up in Mabel's Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Up_in_Mabel's_Room_\(1944_film\) "Up in Mabel's Room (1944 film)")* (1944).[^8] Her career progression was hindered by the premature death of her mentor, [Mark Sandrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Sandrich "Mark Sandrich").

Reynolds starred with [Abbott and Costello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_and_Costello "Abbott and Costello") in the supernatural comedy *[The Time of Their Lives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Time_of_Their_Lives "The Time of Their Lives")* (1946), one of the few films with Abbott and Costello as leads but not together as a team. Instead, Costello spends most of his screen time with Reynolds; they play a pair of [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution "American Revolution") ghosts who need the help of Abbott and his friends to get to [heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heaven "Heaven"). [Leonard Maltin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Maltin "Leonard Maltin") 's review of the film describes it as "Most unusual film for A&C and one of their best... Imaginative, funny, and well done." [^9] She was cast in a supporting role in [Mario Lanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Lanza "Mario Lanza") 's film debut, *[That Midnight Kiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Midnight_Kiss "That Midnight Kiss")* (1949).

She later appeared in the NBC version of the television series *[The Life of Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Life_of_Riley "The Life of Riley")* [^10] (1953–1958) and appeared on three episodes of the television series *[Leave it to Beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_it_to_Beaver "Leave it to Beaver")* (1960–1963).

## Personal life

Reynolds was married to Jack Reynolds, a casting director.[^11] The couple had a daughter, Linda, and divorced in 1952.[^12] Her second husband was film editor Jon M. Haffen (who during his acting career had been billed as John Whitney). He died in 1985.[^2] [^13] [^7]

## Death

Reynolds died of [congestive heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestive_heart_disease "Congestive heart disease") in [Manhattan Beach, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Beach,_California "Manhattan Beach, California"), on February 1, 1997, having collapsed while walking her dog. She was 79 years old.

## Legacy

Reynolds has a star in the Television section of the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame "Hollywood Walk of Fame"), at 1525 Vine Street.[^14]
