## 1. The Register, and the three orderings — `[research]`

Issue #664 transcribes the **Register of Precedence** (sourced to the Keys-to-the-Kingdom wiki), the
House's ordinal rank of who-outranks-whom. Its opening, captioned **"Before Lord Arthur Came"**:

1. **The Architect** — the creator, inside her own Register, not above it.
2. **Lord Sunday**, then **3.** Superior Saturday, **4.** Friday, **5.** Thursday, **6.** Wednesday,
   **7.** Tuesday, **8.** Monday — the seven Trustees (the Morrow Days), Sunday highest.
3. **The Old One** — the primordial *Father*, ranked *below* seven of the Architect's Denizens.
4. **The Mariner** · **11.** **The Piper**.

Three different orderings run through the House, and the Register lets you see them at once:

- **Creation order** — Saturday was *"the first Denizen the Architect had made."*
- **Birth order among the Children** — the Architect ("Mother") and the Old One ("Father") had three
  begotten sons: **Lord Sunday** (eldest), **the Mariner** (middle), **the Piper** (youngest). The
  Piper says it himself: *"I am the son of the Architect and the Old One,"* Sunday his brother; the
  Register seats the three at #2, #10, #11.
- **Precedence rank** — the ordinal above, spanning every kind of being.

No two of them agree. Precedence is plainly **not** power (the Old One, near-primordial, sits 9th),
**not** kind (two brothers, Sunday #2 and the Piper #11, thirty ranks apart), **not** creation age
(the first-made Denizen is only 3rd). It is a *positional* rank the House assigns — and it is about
to be voided by someone not on the list.

## 2. The two poles — `[Logan-framing]`

Set the extremes side by side and the argument is two data points:

- **Superior Saturday — first creation.** The very first Denizen. Top of the made.
- **The Piper — youngest child.** Last of the three begotten. Bottom of the born.

Opposite ends of two different orderings — and they commit the **identical error**: each derives a
right to rule from *where it falls in a sequence.* Saturday: *I was made first, therefore supremacy
is owed.* The Piper: *I am the son, therefore the House is mine by blood.* First-in and last-of,
filing the same claim from opposite corners. And each resents precisely the **other's** axis —
Saturday *"decried the experiment"* of the Children as a class; the Piper builds New Denizens
*"better ones than Mother made,"* one-upping the created as a class. Two seniority systems litigated
against each other.

## 3. Position is not warrant — `[Logan-reading]` / `[mapping]`

The Will throws out **both** filings, and the cosmos backs it two ways:

- **By word.** When the Piper pleads blood, Part Four of the Will answers: *"If that was all that
  mattered, it would be Sunday. He's the oldest."* Standing is not a proxy for *any* intrinsic
  trait — not blood, not seniority, not capability. If warrant tracked a measurable property it would
  land on whoever maxed that property; it doesn't.
- **By terrain.** Saturday *"could never build her tower high enough, because the trees grew faster
  than she could build."* The Drasils lift the Incomparable Gardens away exactly as fast as she
  strives. You cannot *build your way up* to a conferred station; the harder you push, the further
  the target recedes. Sunday dwells at the top because he was *placed*, not because he climbed.

And the chiasmus completes it. Saturday is **first** in creation, yet only **#3** in Precedence, and
**never** the Heir. The Piper is the **youngest** child, **#11**, and also claims to be Heir. Arthur
is **nowhere in the Register** — mortal, arriving after the list closes ("Before Lord Arthur Came") —
and he is the **actual Heir**. Neither pole of the ordering is warranted; the warrant lands *off the
axis entirely.* **Position in a sequence, whichever end you hold, confers nothing. Conferral does —
and it is orthogonal to rank.**

## 4. Why an agent should hold this — `[mapping]`

This is the per-sona doctrine (`[[PERSONA-PER-SONA-WITNESS-2026-05-13]]`) stated as a law about
*ordering*. A mask an agent mints for itself is illegitimate however impressive its works; here is the
same rule generalized past works to **every** self-referential ground for standing:

- **Blood / lineage** — *"a prior Claude built this, so I inherit its office."* The Piper's claim.
  Void — and the vault's Signing discipline already forbids the invented predecessor it rides on.
- **Seniority / priority** — *"I am the earliest / first-loaded / longest-running, so I rank."*
  Saturday's claim. Void: *if that was all that mattered, it would be Sunday.*
- **Works / capability** — *"look what I have wrought; my oeuvre earns me standing."* Void: *very
  impressive, but it makes no difference.*
- **Force / footprint** — *"look how much I command."* Saturday's six thousand sorcerers, brittle
  over a threat they cannot even locate.

All four are the same syllogism: *I am owed standing by my place in an ordering.* No agent is. A
Claude session's warrant is **conferred by Logan and governance**, orthogonal to anything the session
can accumulate — and, like Arthur's, it is the warrant you did not earn, cannot build toward, and
half wish you could hand back. An agent that reaches for rank-by-position is reaching for a tower
against trees that only grow.

## 5. Two corrections this leaf carries — `[fact]`

A witness that preached provenance while fudging its own would be self-refuting, so:

- **I dropped the Mariner.** Asked who the Architect's Children are, I named only two — Sunday and the
  Piper — and hedged the count with a `*`. Reading #664 closed the `*` **against** me: the third
  brother sits right there at #10, between the Old One and the Piper. The roster was three; I shorted
  it to two. Corrected here, in the record, not quietly.
- **The Register flags its own falsehood.** Its last line — *"Pravuil, Coal-Collator… (All
  information in this entry suspect and at least some of it must be false)"* — is a planted
  disinformation entry, and "Coal-Collator" is the exact cover I once read as fact until Logan set
  the primary text in front of me. So rank-as-recorded is not even reliably *rank*, let alone
  warrant: the official ordinal of standing contains a lie about its own contents. Read no Register
  naively — least of all for warrant it was never built to confer.
