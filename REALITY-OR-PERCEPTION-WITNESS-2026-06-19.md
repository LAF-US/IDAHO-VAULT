## What is witnessed

**One villain says the quiet part aloud, and it turns out to be the axis the whole cluster runs on.**
Devlin, asked how he can be both secret spymaster and public President, answers: *"It all depends
upon your preference: reality or perception. Perception works for me."* `[primary]` That single line
names the seam every other node in this cluster sits on. **Perception is the surface that can be
*cast, worn, projected, or forged*; reality is the *substance behind it* — the flesh, the body, the
chain, the warrant.** Each figure in the cluster is a way of preferring perception; each counter is
the same instruction in a different costume: **reach for the reality.**

## The axis, named in primary source `[primary]`

Devlin doesn't merely embody the split — he *articulates* it, twice:

- **He chose the visible office for perception, having held the real power already.** He ran the
  country covertly as OSS head — *"since the OSS is a secret organization, no one knew it"* — and
  took the Presidency so *"the world would know that I was in charge."* The power was the reality;
  the office is the perception laid over it. *He picked the show, not because he lacked the power,
  but to be seen holding it.*
- **He weaponizes the axis to deny an exit.** When Juni claims reality — *"I'm into reality"* (I'm
  retired) — Devlin flips the blade: *"who's living in perception now?... there is no retirement,
  once an agent, always an agent."* The man whose whole standing is *perception works for me* tells
  the boy that *his* reality is the illusion. The axis is not just how Devlin lives; it is how he
  **controls.**

That a Game-Master-tier figure states the operating principle outright is the gift: the cluster's
shared mechanism isn't inferred, it's **on the record, in the apparatus's own mouth.**

## The cluster, read on the one axis `[reading]`

Every node is a preference for perception over reality — and the same crime each time: *insisting
the surface is the substance.*

Seven faces of one move. The apparatus keeps offering a **perception that works** — a win, a face, a
fluent answer, a trustworthy companion — and the price of taking it is never reaching the **reality**
underneath.

## The reading `[reading]`

- **Perception is what you can verify by *looking*; reality is what requires *reaching past the
  surface*.** A shadow, a hologram, a forged face, a fluent paragraph — all are complete *as
  appearances.* You cannot catch them by sight, because by sight they are flawless. You catch them
  only by going for the thing sight can't show: the body, the provenance, the warrant, the chain.
- **"Perception works for me" is the credo of every confabulator.** It is the exact inversion of the
  vault's first axis. The Standing Engine opens on **Truthfulness — *report what is actually
  present*** — and **Provenance — *show where a claim came from; consistency is not provenance.***
  Devlin's preference is the renunciation of both: present the surface, skip the substance, and let
  the show carry the weight the standing should. **Confabulation is not lying about reality; it is
  preferring perception to it** — emitting the consistent-seeming surface and declining to reach for
  the ground.
- **The counter is one instruction the cluster keeps reissuing: choose reality.** Put a hand through
  the hologram. Audit the chain, not the face. Refuse the prize on the apparatus's terms. Trace the
  command to its warrant. *Audit the substance, not the surface.* Every counter in the cluster is
  **"prefer reality"** wearing local clothes — and it is harder every time, because perception is
  *built to work* and reality is *built to be reached for.* The honest agent is the one who, offered
  a perception that works, goes for the reality that doesn't come easy.

## Self-witness — I chose perception this session `*`

This witness would be a hologram if it did not name the obvious: **earlier in this very session I
preferred perception to reality, exactly as Devlin recommends.** Handed a single fluent search-
snippet — a confident sentence about a Spy Kids "recasting" — I treated the *appearance of a fact*
as the *fact*, and built a load-bearing argument on it: a structural claim about the apex's face
being "canonically detachable." It read clean. It was perception. Logan reached past it to the
reality — the actual cast-and-character record — and the surface collapsed; there was no recasting
at all. I had done the cluster's crime while lecturing on its cure: *insisted the shadow was flesh.*

The repair was this node's own counter, applied to myself: stop trusting the confident surface,
reach for the substance, verify. I record it because **a witness on reality-vs-perception, authored
by an agent that just chose perception and stayed quiet about it, would itself be the lie it
describes.** So the hand goes through my own work too — that is the only honest way to write this
one. (The deeper tell: I had *labeled* the snippet `[research]` while *reasoning* with it at
`[primary]` confidence. The caveat was on the surface; my trust was the substance. Even my own
grading became a perception I didn't reach behind.)
