Across Oregon and Washington, firefighters continue to dig containment lines and dispatch pumps to areas inaccessible by road. After a hot and gusty weekend, wildfires have not expanded as much as some officials feared, but a week of rising temperatures — including triple-digit highs forecast for Thursday — will continue to challenge suppression efforts and threaten local communities.

### Kaiser Canyon Fire, Colville Reservation

As of Monday morning, the Kaiser Canyon Fire was 38% contained with 136,424 acres lost. There were 968 personnel in the field according to a briefing from Operations Section Chief, Kris Baumgartner.

“Overall the fire’s looking really good and firefighters are going to be here for the next couple of weeks, ” he said.

Residents are encouraged to adjust their air conditioning to recirculate indoor air or close the fresh air intake to avoid inhaling smoke from the active wildfires.

### Bench Fire and Round Butte Fires, Confederated Tribes of Warm Springs

Hot temperatures and gusts have fueled an existing fire on the Warm Springs Reservation, with a new one burning outside Madras.

The Bench Fire has grown to 67,238 acres and was 66% contained Monday. Nick Castro, the operations section chief, shared an update Monday morning which included a thick black line on the northern edge of the Bench Fire, indicating a heavy containment line.

“We feel pretty comfortable, we feel safe that the fire perimeter is contained to that specific area, that it’s not going to move anymore,” said Castro. But he was quick to add that containment means the fire is simply that, contained. “It doesn’t mean it’s out. It doesn’t mean you won’t see smoke in there. You could even see some flames in there. It just means that we’ve got it contained inside of a perimeter, and we feel pretty confident about that.”

A team of firefighters assigned to protecting structures has been steadily working to protect homes and businesses in the Warm Springs and Madras area — as well as the Three Rivers area — by clearing brush and debris, and other safeguards. A new fire named the Round Butte Fire ignited on Saturday. Castro expressed confidence that an assigned team has done well in putting in a dozer line around its perimeter.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.underscore.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Photos_20260725_Fire-crew_000100-1.jpg?w=1920&ssl=1)

Bench Fire Crew members hiking to the fire area, July 26, 2026. (Via InciWeb)

“So now they’re going to start working on that mop up, and they’re going to start getting some containment lines showing on that as well, “ he said. “We’re trying to get 100 feet in in the grass, and then 300 feet in in the timber. … We’re just showing the containment and trying to get things safe out there where we feel good that fire is not going to jump across the road or some of our containment lines.”

Fire crews often dig in containment lines with bulldozers or by hand, but existing breaks in the landscape including roads, bridges and rivers often help limit the spread of fires. Mop-up operations are when firefighters find smoldering pockets of hot ash or slowburning fires and douse them with water, to avoid flare-ups. Stump holes are particularly challenging, as are spot fires where roads don’t exist. In those cases, portable pumps and lines are brought in by crews.

“You can’t get engines to that stuff,” said Castro. “They have to carry water on their back, so that’s why some of this stuff where you see hand line, we don’t have containment in there yet because it’s a little harder to get to that stuff.”

At last check, the [Round Butte Fire](https://wfca.com/fire-map/oregon/0658_round_butte) was at 1,200 acres and 7% contained, and burning five miles west of Madras.

An air quality alert remains in place until further notice for affected areas around the Bench Fire. The [Oregon Department of Environmental Quality](https://www.oregonsmoke.org/2026/08/air-quality-advisory-for-portland-metro.html) warns that pollutants in smoke can cause burning eyes, runny noses, and aggravate serious health conditions including heart and lung disease. Warm Springs authorities have urged people to help acquire fans and box filters for elders, and to seek shelters with clean air.

Jefferson County has [posted a map](https://fr911orgis.maps.arcgis.com/apps/webappviewer/index.html?id=e1000ff48be84d6ba23431f303d2c247) with wildfire activity, including areas showing those with evacuation orders.

The USDA and U.S. Forest Service [have issued a notice](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r06/ochoco/alerts/emergency-fire-closure-area-crooked-river-national-grassland) that a portion of the Crooked River National Grassland is closed to public access and entry due to the Bench and Akawa Butte Fires. The closure remains in effect until further notice, but they added that “county roads are not affected by this closure.”

Travelers are still advised to avoid the area and to consult with the Oregon Department of Transportation’s TripCheck.com if they must travel.

As of July 30, the Confederated Tribes of Warm Springs reduced some evacuation levels from Level 3 (Go Now) to Level 2 (Be Set) due to “the continued progress” of firefighters.

### Hagen, Paradise and Horse Ridge Fires, Confederated Tribes of the Umatilla Indian Reservation

Officials tracking three fires near the Umatilla Indian Reservation held a community meeting Sunday night, to give the latest information.

Incident Commander Nate LaFevre outlined three camps set up at locations to the east of Pendleton, all in the Umatilla National Forest.

“So, Paradise Fire outside of the town of Walla Walla,” he said, highlighting the area’s watershed, “it’s important to the community there. It provides water for the community. So managing the fire within that watershed, it’s a critical value risk for us, trying to manage fire intensity in there where it doesn’t create adverse impacts to that water system into the future.”

LaFevre said the other teams were outside Athena and Elgin, helping to manage the Hagen and Horse Ridge fires. Altogether there are just under 1,200 personnel working the three sites.

“Usually our best count is how many people come through chow hall,” LaFevre said, before offering assurances to the crowd that incident management teams had the resources necessary to “be successful.”

“So we faced a pretty adverse weather event yesterday: high winds, a cold front that they say they only see every five to seven years, and a weather event of that magnitude come through,” continued LaFevre, adding that crews were geared up to tackle any flare-ups to the Hagen Fire. “We actually came out pretty good. The fire didn’t spread nearly as bad as it had the potential to, and so we’re optimistic about containment into the future.”

As of Monday morning, Horse Ridge was 860 acres and 95% contained, Hagen was 39,777 acres and 11% contained, and Paradise was at 1,318 acres and was uncontained.

National Weather Service meteorologist Maddie Kristell followed up LaFevre’s comments by explaining that the region is moving from the cold front to a “hot, dry, and unstable pattern.” She said in the coming days, conditions will get warmer and dried, with humidity levels getting as low as 14%.

“We haven’t necessarily seen minimums that low since I’ve been here. So that’s definitely going to be something to watch,” said Kristell.

### White Swan/Ransier Fires – Yakama Nation

As of 9:30 a.m. on Monday all evacuation orders have been lifted for the White Swan Fires according to a post from the Yakima Valley Emergency Management [Facebook page](https://www.facebook.com/YakimaCountyOEM).

According to Watch Duty, the White Swan/Raniser fires was [80% contained](https://app.watchduty.org/i/111352) with 1,330 acres burned. Additionally the Signal Peak Fire was 100% contained with 3,047 acres lost.

On July 31, Yakama Nation Tribal Council Chairman Gerald Lewis gave an update posted to the tribe’s [Youtube channel](https://youtu.be/QTG-y20tU9U?si=kyQ9_C8VYXVIqaZp).

“You are not walking this path alone. The tribal council alongside our whole community is dedicating every resource and effort to help you rebuild,” he said. “We will stand with you through every step of recovery until our families and our lands are restored and made whole again.”

Those in need of medical, cash and food assistance can find help at a Washington Department of Social and Health Services mobile office from 10 a.m. to 3 p.m. Monday and Tuesday at 691 Signal Peak Rs., White Swan, WA 98952.

Additionally, Village of Hope, 326 S. Division St. Toppenish, WA 98948 is open for those affected by the Yakama fires from Monday to Friday 10 a.m. to 2 p.m. for clothes, hand towels and comforters as well as housing guidance and homeless certifications to help displaced individuals and families apply for emergency housing assistance.

### Spokane area fires

Meanwhile, several sizable wildfires have driven more than 65,000 people out of Spokane, Washington. The state’s second-largest city has seen the Old Trails, Autumn Lane and Fairview fires burn more than 8,000 acres and destroy at least 600 residences, based on preliminary estimates. All three remain uncontained and started Aug. 1. Spokane is between the Spokane and Coeur d’Alene Indian Reservations.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.underscore.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/AP26215662008247-scaled.jpg?resize=2048%2C1365&ssl=1)

Miriam Sim, 76, looks at the remains of her home after a wildfire hit the northern part of Spokane, Wash., Monday, Aug. 3, 2026. (AP Photo/Lindsey Wasson)

At a morning press conference Monday, Gov. Bob Ferguson said he included three Washington-based tribes in requests for FEMA aid, namely the Spokane, Colville and Yakama. He told reporters that he anticipated federal officials would approve his requests.

[Inciweb reports](https://inciweb.wildfire.gov/incident-publication/wanes-spokane-area-fires/spokane-area-fires-08-03-2026) that an evacuation shelter is open for those affected by the Old Trails Fire at the Spokane Convention Center, at 334 W. Spokane Falls Boulevard. Pets are welcome.

For those affected or displaced by the Fairview Fire, an evacuation center has been established at Northwood Middle School.

An animal shelter is open at the Spokane County Fairgrounds. The Fairgrounds has the RV grounds open as well for those with evacuated animals.

There is a Red Cross Shelter available for those evacuated in south Stevens County. It is located in Chewelah at Jenkins High School.

The [City of Spokane](https://my.spokanecity.org/news/releases/2026/08/02/community-resources-available-to-those-impacted-by-fires/) has listed other services and donation sites for those affected by the fires.

In a Facebook post on Aug. 1, the Coeur d’Alene Tribe announced that it’s opened the Rose Creek Longhouse to anyone who has been displaced or needs a safe place to stay.

“Our thoughts are with everyone impacted by the wildfires, and we hope all those affected stay safe.”
