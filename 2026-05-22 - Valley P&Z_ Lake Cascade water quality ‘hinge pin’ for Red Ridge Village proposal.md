The environmental risk posed to Lake Cascade could be a deciding factor on a plan to build what local officials have described as a “new city” near McCall.

On Thursday, the Valley County Planning and Zoning Commission met with representatives from regulatory agencies and DF Development to discuss the company’s plan for Red Ridge Village, which would build a commercial village surrounded by 722 homes, apartments, and condos on 2,250 acres off West Mountain Road.

Much of Thursday’s discussion centered on information the P&Z wants to see to gauge the viability of central drinking water and wastewater systems being proposed to serve Red Ridge Village.

Christine Richman, a Salt Lake City planner hired by DF to lead the project, told the commissioners that central treatment plants would provide sewer service and drinking water to almost all of the development, though plans have not yet been finalized.

The systems would be permitted by the Idaho Department of Environmental Quality and the Idaho Department of Water Resources.

Currently, DF is planning to build a mechanical wastewater system that would be run by a third-party operator. The system would recycle treated wastewater by using it to water landscaping within the subdivision. During the winter, the treated wastewater would be discharged into groundwater aquifers.

P&Z Chairman Ken Roberts raised concerns about the wastewater disposal – specifically that phosphorus and nitrates contained within it could ultimately end up in Lake Cascade.

“If it is not absorbed by those plants, it will migrate. That’s the concern,” Roberts said. “We’re spending millions of dollars on Cascade Reservoir… and the last thing we want to do is build a development that continues to add to the problem.”

Richman said she would follow up at a future meeting with more information on the topic.

“If that question can’t be answered, that is a hinge pin decision on the entire development,” he said.

Lake Cascade, known as Cascade Reservoir until 1999, has been plagued by high phosphorus levels for decades. The nutrient-rich water, which stems from a variety of sources, fuels toxic cyanobacteria blooms that are harmful to people, animals, and water quality.

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency is currently working on a report that recommends solutions for lower phosphorous levels in Lake Cascade, [as Valley Lookout reported.](https://valleylookout.com/2026/03/23/this-is-a-huge-undertaking-lake-cascade-water-quality-at-center-of-plans-for-new-sewer-treatment-plant/)

## Groundwater capacity also key

Another key analysis will be the capacity of the groundwater aquifer DF is planning to tap into to serve Red Ridge Village. That study could begin soon, Richman said, adding that she currently has no reason to believe that there isn’t enough capacity in the aquifer.

“That’s a limiting factor on what we would be able to build,” Richman said. “If, for instance, there’s only water available adequate to support 500 units, then we would be limited to 500 units. We do understand that.”

Roberts told Reichman that the study should include an analysis of the long-term sustainability of the aquifer as the drinking water source for Red Ridge.

The P&Z is currently gathering comments on the Red Ridge proposal from a host of state and local agencies that could be affected by the project. Thursday’s meeting also included the Idaho Department of Fish and Game, the City of McCall, McCall Fire and EMS, and the Idaho Transportation Department.

DF is seeking county approval on conceptual plans for the planned community. The P&Z’s decision on the concept plan would be a recommendation to the Board of County Commissioners. The final design of each development phase would be subject to further county review.

In April, the county selected a planning firm that DF is paying to complete an analysis showing how the project is expected to affect various public agencies, including new property taxes, capital expenses, and increased service demand. The results of that study will be presented at a June 25 work session.

## Red Ridge Village proposal

As proposed, Red Ridge Village would include 302 lots for single-family homes, 170 townhomes, and 250 condos within the Village Center, which would include 50,000 square feet of commercial space.

The Village Center is also expected to include stations for the Valley County Sheriff’s Office and McCall Fire and EMS. The facilities and funding obligations would be determined in a development agreement binding DF to approval conditions stipulated by the county.

The village would be flanked by a 149-acre meadow, as well as a working farm that would provide produce to village restaurants and also buffer existing homes on West Mountain Road from residential development.

The [Red Ridge Village](https://dfdevelopmentllc.com/communities/red-ridge-village/) proposal is part of 30,920 contiguous acres of DF land that stretches about 12 miles from west of McCall along West Mountain Road to U.S. 95 west of New Meadows, near the Evergreen Forest Products sawmill. No development is currently proposed on the land in Adams County.

[A traffic study](https://www.co.valley.id.us/media/Departments/PlanningZoning/PUD%2026-001%20Red%20Ridge%20Village%20Application/Appendix%20J%20Traffic%20Impact%20Study.pdf) estimates Red Ridge would create about 8,100 daily vehicle trips. The study recommends several roadway improvements that would be paid for by DF, including straightening out a series of sharp curves on West Mountain Road.

Improvements are expected to include two new stoplights at the intersections of West Valley Road and Boydstun Street, as well as Boydstun Street and Idaho 55.

A southbound turn lane would be added on Third Street (Idaho 55) at its intersection with Deinhard Lane. The timing of the existing stoplight could also be adjusted to account for Red Ridge traffic.
