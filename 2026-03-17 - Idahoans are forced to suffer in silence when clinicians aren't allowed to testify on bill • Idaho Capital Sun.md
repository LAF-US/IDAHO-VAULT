## Idahoans are forced to suffer in silence when clinicians aren’t allowed to testify on bill

There is something especially insidious about invoking ‘child protection’ while silencing professionals trying to keep children safe and alive, writes guest columnist Jo Seiders.

![The exterior of the Idaho Statehouse](https://idahocapitalsun.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/011426legislaturePSutphin085-2048x1365.jpg)

The exterior of the Idaho Statehouse

The exterior of the Idaho Statehouse as seen on Jan. 14, 2026, in Boise. (Photo by Pat Sutphin for the Idaho Capital Sun)

I have never conducted more crisis sessions than I am right now with trans children, their parents, queer+ adults, and community providers. Even seasoned clinicians who once felt steady and grounded are unraveling under the strain of the inhumane policies being pushed by the Idaho Legislature. And yet, when lawmakers rushed [House Bill 822](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/H0822/) through the Statehouse, they did not allow a single Idaho clinician currently responsible for safeguarding children’s well-being to testify in person.

HB 822 is not a child-safety measure in practice. It is an overbroad state mandate that replaces individualized, ethical, evidence-based judgment with compelled disclosure and legal threat. At its core, this bill teaches children that it may not be safe to talk honestly to trusted adults. When a young person believes that asking for help or support may prompt family conflict, punishment, or danger, they become less likely to disclose distress, less likely to seek support, and more likely to suffer in silence.

When I showed up at the Statehouse to explain this, as a professional who has dedicated my life to protecting children and marginalized populations, I was turned away along with Dr. Noreen Womack, a board-certified pediatrician in Boise, Idaho, and one of the founders of Idaho Children Are Primary. Not one Idaho clinician currently responsible for safeguarding children’s well-being was allowed to testify about the devastation already caused simply by introducing this bill. If it becomes law, the harm will be irreparable.

I prepared 10 pages of testimony explaining this, along with a 108-page reference index of reliable, evidence-based documentation. I canceled important appointments to write it and to be there to deliver it in person, and I was not permitted to speak.

There is something especially insidious about invoking “child protection” while silencing professionals trying to keep children safe and alive. The hearing did, however, make space for ideology, spectacle, and scripture. It accepted testimony from out-of-state individuals who have never set foot here. But it did not allow those of us carrying the immediate fallout, panic, destabilization, and fear to testify.

Idaho State Police repeatedly removed distraught attendees because so few people were permitted to speak. That is what environments of erasure create: rage, grief, and rupture. Since this bill was introduced, I have already seen harm. An elementary school professional called me in distress, describing what legislation like this does to children. A scheduled therapy session escalated into crisis intervention for a young person and caregiver destabilized by fear of this bill. I also met outside business hours with an experienced provider to help stabilize them because the cumulative impact of legislative overreach is burning through ethical practice and human nervous systems alike.

Confidentiality is not a political preference. It is foundational to effective care. Ethical standards already govern disclosure in cases of serious, foreseeable, and imminent harm. This bill overrides individualized safety assessments with a blanket rule, one that creates direct conflict with licensure obligations, therapeutic integrity, privacy standards, and evidence-based practice. The consequences are moral injury, burnout, and workforce attrition in systems already strained beyond capacity.

This legislation also exposes providers to severe civil penalties for maintaining ethical standards. A provider could lose their license and livelihood for upholding the very ethical code that keeps children safe. That is not protection. It is coercion.

Supporters frame this as parental rights or transparency. Parents matter, and transparency matters, but disclosure must remain individualized and safety-based. Families are not identical, and responses are not predictable. Ethical practice already allows disclosure when serious harm is present. The question is not whether parents matter. The question is whether vulnerable children can safely reach for help. Children are safer when more trusted adults are available to them, not fewer.

I am tired of leaving the Capitol in tears. I am furious and heartbroken. And I feel like I am watching people with power knowingly choose harm, leaving the rest of us holding the panic, trauma, fallout, and grief that never ends. Still, I won’t let them silence me. I know children need more safe adults to speak to, not more reasons to stay silent. If the goal is child safety, HB 822 fails the test.
