# HOUSE CLOCK WITNESS — Chron Agenda Research and Development

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is a research-and-development witness, not an amendment to Garth Nix's
> published canon and not a declaration that fictional House Time equals Earth
> civil time. The novels remain the literary authority. Logan's mockup is the
> design source for the proposed chron agenda; the analysis and synthesis are
> attributed to ChatGPT Codex under Logan's authority.

## Finding

The House is best read administratively before it is read morally. It is a
cosmic institution built to observe and record the Secondary Realms, divided
into seven demesnes, governed through delegated Keys and Trustees, staffed by
function-bound Denizens, and ordered by precedence. The Will acts like a
founding directive; the Keys join jurisdiction to capability; and Dawn, Noon,
and Dusk are superior offices beneath each Morrow Day. The series' official
framing—seven days, seven Keys, and one mysterious House—supports this
sevenfold institutional reading.[^nix-series]

Logan's mockup superimposes two administrative cycles on one civil day:

1. three equal eight-hour watches called **Dawn**, **Noon**, and **Dusk**; and
2. seven equal 204-minute jurisdictions called **Yan**, **Tan**, **Tethera**,
   **Methera**, **Pits**, **Sethera**, and **Azer**, with six visible two-minute
   handoffs.

That construction fills a linear midnight-to-midnight chart exactly. It does
not close as a clock cycle because the seventh handoff—from Azer back to
Yan—is absent. No arrangement can simultaneously make all seven jurisdictions
equal, all seven buffers equal, every boundary a whole minute, and the total
exactly 24 hours. This is a divisibility limit, not a rounding failure.

10:16–10:18, 13:42–13:44, 17:08–17:10, and 20:34–20:36. The linear ledger is

$$

$$

The cyclic seam at 24:00/00:00 is the unresolved seventh boundary.

## I. The House as an administrative cosmos

The official author and publisher pages establish the sevenfold structure but
leave much of its machinery to the novels. Garth Nix's series page presents the
seven books as seven days and seven Keys.[^nix-series] Scholastic identifies
Monday as controller of the Lower House, names Dawn and Noon as loyal officers
and Dusk as a dissenter, and explicitly states that Tuesday transformed his
Dawn, Noon, and Dusk into seven Grotesques.[^scholastic-characters]
*Publishers Weekly* recognized the first novel's House-and-Key explanation as
cosmological: an account of the universe's origin and purpose expressed through
a clock-shaped Key, a Will, inspectors, sentries, and a vast House.[^pw]

The administrative reading can be stated as a chain of delegation:

| House element | Administrative function | Chron-agenda implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Architect | Constituting authority and maker of the institutional order | Source of the clock's authority, not a scheduled shift |
| The Will | Founding directive and succession instrument | Rule that outlasts officeholders |
| The House | Central record, access, and coordination institution for the Secondary Realms | The agenda is an operating surface for the institution |
| Seven Keys | Jurisdictional instruments that join office to power | Each scheduled jurisdiction needs a named scope |
| Seven Trustees / Morrow Days | Delegated governors of demesnes and namesake weekdays | Natural names for a sevenfold rota |
| Dawn, Noon, Dusk | Superior deputies or watch offices | Natural names for three broad supervisory watches |
| House Precedence | Rank ledger governing command inside the House | Rank order must not be confused with chronological order |
| Denizens | Function-bound civil service | Roles may be precise even when officeholders change |
| Front Door, Seven Dials, Border Sea | Access and routing infrastructure | Handoffs are operational boundaries, not empty time |
| Dame Primus / steward | Continuity and regency during succession | The cycle requires an explicit keeper at the seam |

Community reference material, used here as a map back to the novels rather
than as independent canon, describes the House as a record-keeping institution
for the Secondary Realms under an Original Law of non-interference. It also
distinguishes constant House Time from malleable time in the Secondary
Realms.[^house] That makes an Earth-style agenda a **translation layer**: useful
for governing work, but not a claim about the metaphysics of House Time.

## II. Seven demesnes as departments

Deprioritizing the Trustees' associated sins reveals a surprisingly complete
institutional anatomy. Each demesne is not only scenery; it performs a kind of
work for the House.

| Trustee | Demesne | Administrative portfolio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mister Monday | Lower House | Intake, inspection, access control, low-level records, service corps, and the Front Door. Its disorder demonstrates what happens when delegated administration is abandoned. |
| Grim Tuesday | Far Reaches | Industrial production, raw-material extraction, fabrication, logistics, credit, debt, and House-wide supply. Its failure produces shortages elsewhere. |
| Drowned Wednesday | Border Sea | Maritime transit, ports, navigation, lost-property circulation, and routes into waters throughout the Secondary Realms. |
| Sir Thursday | Great Maze | Military administration, recruitment, training, defense, gates, arsenals, and a moving territorial grid. |
| Lady Friday | Middle House | Restoration, binding, gilding, improvement, and movement of records through a text-sensitive canal system. |
| Superior Saturday | Upper House | Senior office hierarchy, promotion and demotion, sorcery, and high-level records administration. |
| Lord Sunday | Incomparable Gardens | Apex headquarters, curation of exhibits from the Secondary Realms, and custody of the cosmological center. |

The strongest evidence for this departmental reading is Tuesday's supply chain:
the Far Reaches produces everything from writing implements and elevator parts
to telephone cable and Commissionaires for other demesnes, and production
stoppage creates House-wide scarcity.[^far-reaches] The Middle House is
explicitly organized around restoring and improving records, with specialized
guilds and transport infrastructure.[^middle-house] The Great Maze is the base
of the Architect's Army and contains the Citadel, arsenals, boundary defenses,
and time-offset facilities.[^great-maze] Saturday's towers convert precedence
into architecture: office blocks physically rise or fall with promotion and
demotion.[^saturday]

This is administration made cosmological. Records, rank, logistics, transit,
defense, production, and succession are not background details; together they
are the mechanism by which the House mediates the rest of creation.

## III. Dawn, Noon, and Dusk are offices before they are hours

The canonical term is **Dawn**, not “Morning.” “Morning” may be used as an
informal gloss, but the titled office is Dawn.

[Issue #664](https://github.com/LAF-US/IDAHO-VAULT/issues/664) preserves the
pre-Arthur Register of Precedence. It lists the Trustees from Sunday through
Monday, then the surviving superior officers, and then Tuesday's Grotesques.
Within a complete retinue the precedence order is **Noon, Dawn, Dusk**—not the
chronological order Dawn, Noon, Dusk.[^precedence] The distinction matters:

- **Precedence order** answers who outranks whom.
- **Chronological order** answers who holds the successive watch.
- **Agenda order** is Logan's operational design and may use either only if it
  says which one it means.

The ordinary pattern is three superior deputies for one Trustee. Monday's
officers actually manage Lower House affairs in their master's absence;
officeholders can exchange Noon and Dusk, showing that these are transferable
offices rather than immutable personal essences.[^monday] Sunday's offices are
functionally titled the Sower, Grower, and Reaper—Dawn, Noon, and Dusk
respectively—making the threefold cycle one of initiation, maintenance, and
closure.[^sunday]

Wednesday and Tuesday demonstrate two different administrative failures.
Wednesday's original Noon and Dusk are gone, leaving an incomplete retinue that
must later be reappointed. Tuesday instead destroys the three-office structure
by combining and recasting it. One failure creates vacancies; the other creates
an overextended substitute hierarchy.

## IV. Grim Tuesday: three made seven, and the split-minute limit

Tuesday's transformation is the literary hinge of the clock design. He fused
his Dawn, Noon, and Dusk into one being and recast that being as seven
Grotesques: **Yan, Tan, Tethera, Methera, Pits, Sethera, and Azer**, in order of
precedence. The seven were less capable than the original superior officers and
remained coupled strongly enough that the death of one destroyed them all.[^grotesques]
The Far Reaches account gives Tuesday an administrative motive: his industrial
operation had become too large for three chief officers to manage.[^far-reaches]

The mathematical application below is Logan's chron-agenda R&D, not a claim
made by Nix.

Let:

- $S$ be the duration in whole minutes of each of seven equal Grotesque
  jurisdictions; and
- $B$ be the duration in whole minutes of each of seven equal cyclic
  handoffs.

An exact 24-hour cycle would require:

$$
7S + 7B = 1{,}440,
$$

or:

$$
7(S+B)=1{,}440.
$$

But $1{,}440 \bmod 7 = 5$. Therefore $S+B$ cannot be a whole number of
minutes. **Equal whole-minute jurisdictions plus equal whole-minute buffers
cannot close an exact 24-hour cycle.**

The mockup selects $S=204$ and $B=2$, but displays only six buffers:

$$
7(204)+6(2)=1{,}440.
$$

Adding the cyclic seventh buffer produces:

$$
7(204)+7(2)=1{,}442,
$$

which is a 24-hour-and-2-minute rotation. The two excess minutes are the exact
cost of closing the mockup without changing any displayed duration.

### Whole-minute compromise set

| Preserved property | Exact 24-hour construction | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Equal 204-minute jurisdictions | Five 2-minute buffers and two 1-minute buffers | Buffers differ by one minute |
| Seven equal 2-minute buffers | Five 204-minute jurisdictions and two 203-minute jurisdictions | Jurisdictions differ by one minute |
| Seven equal 1-minute buffers | Five 205-minute jurisdictions and two 204-minute jurisdictions | Jurisdictions differ by one minute |
| Equal 204-minute jurisdictions and equal 2-minute buffers | 1,442-minute cycle | Clock drifts two minutes per rotation |
| Equal jurisdictions and buffers with seconds permitted | For 204-minute jurisdictions, each buffer is $12/7$ minutes, or about 1:42.857 | Violates the whole-minute-mark requirement |

There is no hidden rounding convention that removes the conflict. One of four
requirements must yield: daily exactness, equal jurisdictions, equal buffers,
or whole-minute boundaries.

From an administrative perspective, the least disruptive daily compromises
are the first two rows. If equality of office is paramount, keep all seven
Grotesque jurisdictions at 204 minutes and let two handoffs be one minute. If
uniform handoff procedure is paramount, keep all seven buffers at two minutes
and shorten two jurisdictions by one minute. The witness does not choose which
requirement Logan should subordinate.

## V. Cosmological and calendrical comparison

### The civil day is a convention made operational

NIST states the ordinary civil conversion directly: one day is 24 hours,
1,440 minutes, or 86,400 seconds. It also cautions that Earth's astronomical
rotation is not perfectly constant; modern time is maintained atomically while
rotation varies.[^nist-day] The minute grid in the mockup is therefore a civil
administrative standard, not a perfect mirror of the planet's motion.

The base-60 subdivision is unusually factor-rich: 60 divides evenly by 2, 3,
4, 5, 6, and 10. NIST traces modern minutes and seconds to sexagesimal
astronomical calculation and notes that this divisibility made time division
convenient.[^nist-time] Seven is conspicuously absent from those factors. The
House Clock is encountering the seam between a sexagesimal civil clock and a
sevenfold institutional order.

### Dawn, Noon, and Dusk are not naturally equal watches

The U.S. Naval Observatory defines solar noon as the Sun's local meridian
transit and defines dawn/dusk through twilight and the Sun's angular position
below the horizon. These events vary with date, latitude, atmosphere, terrain,
and observation conditions.[^usno] Logan's fixed 00:00–08:00,
08:00–16:00, and 16:00–24:00 bands should therefore be understood as named
**watches**, not literal astronomical dawn, noon, and dusk. That is not a flaw;
it is the difference between environmental observation and administrative
timekeeping.

### The seven-day week offers a stronger analogue than seven equal daily blocks

Historical scholarship from University College London treats the seven-day
week as a standardizing system formed from parallel biblical and planetary
traditions. In the Roman planetary model, the seven visible “planets”—the Sun,
Moon, Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn—rotate as rulers of successive
hours. Because 24 is not divisible by seven, the remainder advances the ruler
of the first hour and generates the weekday order.[^ucl-week] The old system
does not eliminate non-divisibility; it turns the remainder into succession.

That suggests a second House Clock model for later R&D:

- A day contains 24 equal one-hour offices assigned cyclically among seven
  Trustees.
- Since $24=3(7)+3$, three Trustees receive four hours and four receive three
  hours on any one day.
- Across a complete seven-day week there are 168 hours, and
  $168/7=24$. Every Trustee receives exactly 24 hours per week.
- The daily imbalance becomes the mechanism that rotates precedence; weekly
  equality replaces impossible daily equality.

This “planetary-hour” option is more cosmologically faithful to the weekday
names and mathematically exact at the weekly horizon. Logan's present mockup is
more legible as a one-day agenda. The two models solve different design
problems and should not be silently merged.

## VI. R&D determinations

1. **Use two named layers.** Dawn/Noon/Dusk are eight-hour supervisory watches;
   Yan through Azer are seven operational jurisdictions.
2. **State that the watches are administrative.** They are not claims about
   local sunrise, solar noon, or twilight.
3. **Keep precedence separate from sequence.** Noon outranks Dawn and Dusk in
   the Register, while Dawn precedes Noon and Dusk chronologically.
4. **Treat every boundary as governed time.** A buffer is a handoff office, not
   an absence. The midnight seam needs the same explicit treatment as the six
   internal seams.
5. **Record the divisibility exception.** A whole-minute daily cycle cannot
   preserve both kinds of equality. Any working agenda must identify which
   invariant it relaxes.
6. **Consider weekly equality.** The planetary-hour precedent shows how a
   sevenfold order can become exact across 168 hours even though it is unequal
   inside each 24-hour day.
7. **Preserve the mockup as Logan's exhibit.** It is a design artifact and
   should remain visibly attributed even if later schedules revise its seam.
