BOISE, Idaho —

On July 9, KTVB reported an immigration enforcement operation near Fairview Avenue and Curtis Road that ended with the arrest of a former DACA recipient.

But video obtained by KTVB shows another encounter unfolding during that same operation, this time involving a 19-year-old Boise resident and U.S. citizen who says he was detained by ICE after stopping to record.

Eric Ornelas said he was driving to work when he saw ICE agents [pull over another man](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/idaho/ice-detains-former-daca-recipient-boise-sparking-debate-over-immigration-enforcement/277-2c185002-96c3-45e6-9c01-95a913fb8f61) near the intersection.

“I was on my way to work. I got off the freeway on the Curtis exit, and I saw these ICE agents pulled this guy earlier, and I went and stopped by. Then I took a video,” Ornelas said.

Ornelas recorded the encounter on his phone. He said that minutes later, agents asked him for identification and ran the license plate of the truck he was driving.

According to Ornelas, agents told him the person associated with the truck had a deportation order. The truck Ornelas was driving was registered to his father.

“I was used as bait,” Ornelas said.

He said agents then took his phone and told him he needed to contact the person who owned the vehicle.

Ornelas said ICE agents handcuffed him and told him he could not leave until the registered owner arrived to pick up the truck.

He said the encounter lasted about 30 minutes.

“After that, they said, ‘Oh, well, you're abusing your right. You're abusing the privilege that we gave you,’” Ornelas said

### Boise Police officers called to the scene

Boise Police Department body camera footage obtained by KTVB shows officers arriving during the final six minutes of the encounter.

The footage does not show Ornelas in handcuffs when Boise officers arrive.

In the body camera video, ICE agents tell Boise officers that Ornelas had been confrontational. Agents also told officers they contacted police because Ornelas would not identify himself and because they learned his driver's license was suspended.

The body camera footage shows Boise officers discussing the situation with ICE agents before determining they had no reason to keep Ornelas there.

About five minutes after Boise police arrived, an officer told Ornelas, “I have no reason to keep you here.”

Ornelas was then free to leave.

Ornelas said ICE contacted Boise police because his license was suspended.

### Attorney says recording ICE is protected

Ornelas said he recorded the operation because he wanted to document what was happening and keep the detained person's family informed.

“If you see something like that that makes you upset, and you have the right to go record it, why not?” Ornelas said.

Civil rights attorney Richie Eppink said incidents involving U.S. citizens during immigration enforcement operations have been reported across the country.

Eppink said people generally have a constitutional right to observe and record law enforcement officers performing their duties in public, although circumstances can affect what officers may legally do during an encounter.

“Where someone's being detained or retaliated against just for observing and videotaping ICE, that unfortunately now is becoming common around the country,” Eppink said. “But it's a constitutional violation.”

Eppink said even a relatively brief detainment can have a significant impact.

“Thirty minutes is far from nothing,” Eppink said. “To be restrained by law enforcement, armed law enforcement, is trauma that may last a lifetime for a lot of people.”

Eppink advises people who encounter law enforcement to understand their rights, including the right to remain silent and the right to refuse consent to searches.

“If officers ask you to answer questions, you can say, "I'm going to remain silent. If officers ask to search your car or your bag or your pockets, you can say, "I don't consent to any searches. And if they do hold you and they keep trying to ask you questions, you can say that you want a lawyer, and you should,” Eppink said.

### Ornelas' father later detained

For Ornelas, the incident has become more than the 30 minutes he spent in handcuffs. Days after the encounter, Ornelas said, ICE detained his father.

Ornelas said his father was the family's primary breadwinner, and the family is now adjusting to life without him.

“My dad was like the breadwinner of the family,” Ornelas said. “So we're all pitching in to help. So it's just one of those things that we have to keep on going.”

KTVB reached out to U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to ask about the encounter and whether information gathered during an interaction like this can be used to locate other people suspected of being in the country unlawfully.

ICE has responded to questions about other immigration enforcement operations previously reported by KTVB. But as of Aug. 9, ICE has not responded to KTVB's questions about Ornelas' specific encounter.
