**Melville Ruick** (July 8, 1898 – December 24, 1972) was an American actor.

## Early years

Ruick was born in [Boise, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho"), in 1898 to Amanda (née Hughes) and Norman Ruick.[^1] His father was a U.S. [district attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_attorney "District attorney") in Idaho,[^2] and Ruick studied law at the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California "University of California").

## Military service

[World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I") changed Ruick from a student lawyer to a student pilot. Ruick won his wings in the Air Service, Signal Corps, two weeks before the end of the war. He was the United States' youngest flier in the war at age 17 and became a lieutenant at age 18.[^2]

During World War II, Ruick returned to uniform as a captain in the [USAFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAFF?action=edit&redlink=1 "USAFF (page does not exist)"), attached to the Radio Production Unit as a producer-director.

## Career

Ruick worked five years as master of ceremonies for the [Fanchon and Marco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanchon_and_Marco "Fanchon and Marco") shows.[^2]

During the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression_in_the_United_States "Great Depression in the United States"), Ruick doubled as a bandleader and as an actor in theatrical stock. An offer came from [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS "CBS") to work as a local [radio announcer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_announcer "Radio announcer") while Ruick was leading a dance band in Los Angeles. He later won an audition for the spot of announcer on the CBS radio show *[Lux Radio Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lux_Radio_Theater "Lux Radio Theater")*,[^3] staying with the show for six years.

After World War II, Ruick reentered show business, touring with [Leo Carrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Carrillo "Leo Carrillo") in a revival of *The Bad Man*. While trying his luck on [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_theatre "Broadway theatre"), Ruick received an offer to direct [Paul Whiteman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Whiteman "Paul Whiteman") 's radio show for the [U.S. National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_of_the_United_States "National Guard of the United States").

Ruick starred as Chief John Randolph on the NBC adventure series *[The Door with No Name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doorway_to_Danger#1951 "Doorway to Danger")* (1951) [^4]<sup><span title="Page: 278">: 278</span> </sup> and as Dr. Barton Crane on the dramatic series *[City Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Hospital_\(US_series\) "City Hospital (US series)")* (1951) on ABC-TV [^4] and CBS radio.[^5] He also guest-starred in a number of popular 1960s shows, including *[The Fugitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fugitive_\(1963_TV_series\) "The Fugitive (1963 TV series)")*, *[The Wild Wild West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wild_Wild_West "The Wild Wild West")* and *[The Invaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Invaders "The Invaders")*.

## Personal life

He was married to actresses [Lurene Tuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lurene_Tuttle "Lurene Tuttle") and Claire Niesen (the former of whom he had a daughter with, actress [Barbara Ruick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Ruick "Barbara Ruick")).[^6] He was also the father-in-law of composer-conductor [John Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Williams "John Williams") and the maternal grandfather of [Joseph Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Williams_\(musician\) "Joseph Williams (musician)").

### Death

Ruick died in Los Angeles on December 24, 1972, aged 74.[^7]
