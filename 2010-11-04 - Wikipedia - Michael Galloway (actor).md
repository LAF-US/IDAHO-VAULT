**Michael F. Galloway** (May 8, 1925 – October 12, 2010) was an American actor with more than three hundred and fifty [film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film "Film"), [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television "Television") and [theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater "Theater") credits throughout his career.[^1] He was perhaps best known for his role in the early 1960s [television series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_series "Television series"), *[The Blue Angels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blue_Angels_\(TV_series\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "The Blue Angels (TV series) (page does not exist)")*, starring along with [Dennis Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Cross "Dennis Cross"), [Morgan Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Jones_\(actor,_born_1928\) "Morgan Jones (actor, born 1928)"), [Warner Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Jones "Warner Jones") and [Don Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Gordon_\(actor\) "Don Gordon (actor)").[^2] [^3]

Galloway was born in [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise "Boise"), [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"), on May 8, 1925.[^1] His father was a [lawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawyer "Lawyer") and he was the youngest of four brothers.[^1] Galloway enlisted in the [United States Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force "United States Air Force") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").[^1] He enrolled at both the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") and the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California "University of Southern California") on basketball [scholarships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholarships "Scholarships") following the end of the war.[^1] Galloway began to pursue [acting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acting "Acting") following a [car accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car_accident "Car accident") which ended his athletic career.[^1]

Galloway died on October 12, 2010, in [Los Angeles, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California "Los Angeles, California"), at the age of 85.[^1]
