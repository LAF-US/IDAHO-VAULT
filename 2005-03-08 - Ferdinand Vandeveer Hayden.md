**Ferdinand Vandeveer Hayden** (September 7, 1829 – December 22, 1887) was an American [geologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geologist "Geologist") noted for his pioneering surveying expeditions of the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains "Rocky Mountains") in the late 19th century. He was also a physician who served with the [Union Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army "Union Army") during the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War").

## Early life

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d9/Fvhayden.jpg/250px-Fvhayden.jpg)

Hayden during the Civil War

Ferdinand Hayden was born in [Westfield, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield,_Massachusetts "Westfield, Massachusetts").[^2] As a young boy he was fascinated with all nature and wildlife, which led him into the field of medicine. He worked in Cleveland under [Jared Potter Kirtland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jared_Potter_Kirtland "Jared Potter Kirtland") and thereafter in Albany, NY, where he worked under [James Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hall_\(paleontologist\) "James Hall (paleontologist)"), of the *Geological Survey of New York*.[^3]

He graduated from [Oberlin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberlin_College "Oberlin College") in 1850 and from the [Albany Medical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albany_Medical_College "Albany Medical College") in 1853, where he attracted the notice of Professor James Hall, state geologist of New York, through whose influence he was induced to join in an exploration of [Nebraska Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebraska_Territory "Nebraska Territory"),[^2] with [Fielding B. Meek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielding_B._Meek "Fielding B. Meek") to study geology and collect fossils.[^4]

Hall sent him on his first geological venture in the summer of 1853. Being of independent mind Hayden ended his commission with Hall, and with the encouragement of S. F. Baird, and a partial sponsorship from the Smithsonian Institution, he spent the remainder of the 1850s on various exploring and collecting expeditions in the northern Missouri River areas.[^3]

In 1856 and 1857, Hayden accompanied exploration expeditions led by Lieutenant [Gouverneur K. Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gouverneur_K._Warren "Gouverneur K. Warren") and in 1859, the [Raynolds Expedition of 1860](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raynolds_Expedition "Raynolds Expedition") led by Captain [William F. Raynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_F._Raynolds "William F. Raynolds"), both of the [Topographical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers#Corps_of_Topographical_Engineers "United States Army Corps of Engineers"). One result of the expedition was his *Geological Report of the Exploration of the Yellowstone and Missouri Rivers in 1859–1860* (1869).[^5]

During the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") he served as an army surgeon.[^2] He rose to be chief medical officer of the [Army of the Shenandoah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Army_of_the_Shenandoah "Union Army of the Shenandoah") and received a [brevet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brevet_\(military\) "Brevet (military)") to lieutenant colonel.

## Geological surveys

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/7/7b/Hayden_Wyoming_1870_jwh00282.jpg/250px-Hayden_Wyoming_1870_jwh00282.jpg)

A noon meal in Ferdinand V. Hayden's camp of the U.S. Geological and Geophysical Survey. Red Buttes, Wyoming Territory, August 24, 1870. Hayden sits at far end of table in dark jacket. (Photo by William Henry Jackson, standing at far right)

After the American Civil War Hayden led geographic and geologic surveys of the Nebraska and Western Territories for the United States Government. In 1867 he was appointed geologist-in-charge of the [United States Geological and Geographical Survey of the Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Geological_and_Geographical_Survey_of_the_Territories "United States Geological and Geographical Survey of the Territories").[^6]

In 1869, Hayden led an expedition along the Front Range to Denver and Santa Fe. In 1870 he received a $25,000 governmental grant to lead a 20-man expedition to South Pass, Fort Bridger, Henry's Fork, and back to Cheyenne.

About this time, he also became identified with the [Megatherium Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megatherium_Club "Megatherium Club") at the [Smithsonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian "Smithsonian") Institution in Washington, D.C. To measure distances during their journeys into the western frontier Hayden employed the use of an [odometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odometer "Odometer"), a device used by mappers to estimate distances traveled. The device was mounted on a mule-drawn cart that measured distances as the cart wheels rolled along. Because of rough terrain the device was accurate to within about 3%.[^7]

### Yellowstone

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/61/Yellowstone_1871b.jpg/250px-Yellowstone_1871b.jpg)

F.V. Hayden's map of Yellowstone National Park, 1871

In 1871, Hayden led America's first federally funded [geological survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden_Geological_Survey_of_1871 "Hayden Geological Survey of 1871") into the [Yellowstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellowstone "Yellowstone") region of northwestern [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming "Wyoming"), given directions by President [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant "Ulysses S. Grant") 's Secretary of Interior [Columbus Delano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus_Delano "Columbus Delano").[^8] The survey consisted of some 50 men which included notables such as painter [Thomas Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Moran "Thomas Moran") and famous frontier/Civil War photographer [William Henry Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Jackson "William Henry Jackson"). The following year, Hayden and his work, *Preliminary Report of the United States Geological Survey of Montana and Portions of Adjacent Territories; Being a Fifth Annual Report of Progress* [^9] was instrumental in convincing Congress to establish Yellowstone as the first U.S. [National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Park "National Park"), aided by Jackson's stunning large-format photographs and Moran's dramatic paintings. These publications also encouraged the westward expansion of the United States.

## Later life

Hayden was elected to the [American Philosophical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Philosophical_Society "American Philosophical Society") in 1860.[^10] He was made professor of geology at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") in 1865 with the help of Leidy.[^5] [^11] Hayden was elected a member of the [American Antiquarian Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Antiquarian_Society "American Antiquarian Society") in 1873.[^12] He was elected a Foreign Member of the [Geological Society of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geological_Society_of_London "Geological Society of London") in 1879.[^13] Upon the reorganization and establishment of the [United States Geological Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Geological_Survey "United States Geological Survey") in 1879 he acted for seven years as one of the geologists. He died in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia") on December 22, 1887, and is interred at [The Woodlands Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Woodlands_Cemetery "The Woodlands Cemetery").

## Legacy

From his twelve years of labor and annual survey journeys there resulted a most valuable series of volumes in all branches of natural history and economic science; and he issued in 1877 his *Geological and Geographical Atlas of [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado "Colorado")*.[^2] The last of the annual survey journeys was in 1878. As a result of Hayden's extensive geological work, he uncovered numerous dinosaur fossils which he brought back east with him for further scientific study. Much of what he brought back is still housed in the collection of the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution "Smithsonian Institution"). American paleontologist [Joseph Leidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Leidy "Joseph Leidy") obtained most of his fossil specimens from Hayden.[^5]

[Hayden Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden_Valley "Hayden Valley") in Yellowstone is named after him.[^14] In 2018, Native American leaders have called for it to be renamed, because he "advocated for the extermination of tribal people who refused to comply with federal dictates".[^15] [^16] The town of [Hayden, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayden,_Colorado "Hayden, Colorado"), located in the [Yampa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yampa_River "Yampa River") valley, is named for him. Many mountain peaks bear his name as well.[^7] The sedge *Carex haydeniana* was named for him by Stephen Thayer Olney, in 1871. A subspecies of garter snake, *[Thamnophis radix haydenii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thamnophis_radix "Thamnophis radix")*, was named for him by [Robert Kennicott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Kennicott "Robert Kennicott") in 1860.[^17] [^18] A land snail, *Oreohelix haydeni*, was named for him by [William Gabb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gabb "William Gabb") in 1869.[^19] Hayden Hall at the University of Pennsylvania which formerly housed the dental school now houses the bioengineering and earth sciences departments.[^20] Hayden was eulogized by United States Geological Survey Director [John Wesley Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wesley_Powell "John Wesley Powell") in an annual report to the United States Secretary of the Interior in 1889.[^21]

## Other publications

With [FB Meek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fielding_Bradford_Meek "Fielding Bradford Meek"), he wrote (*Smithsonian Institution Contributions*, v. 14. Art. 4) "Palaeontology of the Upper Missouri, Pt. 1, Invertebrate." His valuable notes on Native American dialects are in *The Transactions of the American Philosophical Society* (1862) in *The American Journal of Science* (1862) and in The Proceedings of the American Philosophical Society (1869). With ARC Selwyn he wrote North America (1883) for Stanford's *Compendium*.[^2]
