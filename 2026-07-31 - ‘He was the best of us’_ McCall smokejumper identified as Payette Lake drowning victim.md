The body of the man recovered from Payette Lake following a cliff jumping incident earlier this week was identified Friday as a smokejumper based in McCall.

Luke Befumo, 29, of Missoula, Montana, drowned on July 27 after jumping from The Cliffs, a popular recreation area on Eastside Drive about 8 miles north of McCall, according to Valley County Coroner Scott Carver.

An autopsy completed on Friday morning by Carver found Befumo’s cause of death to be asphyxia due to drowning.

![Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/115c0cfa-e547-4a5c-8f4e-7ea7e16f8bde.png)

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

Befumo did not resurface on Monday night after making the jump into the lake shortly before 8:45 p.m., prompting a search and rescue effort by the Valley County Sheriff’s Office. It is unclear why Befumo was unable to resurface following the jump, Carver said.

“We interviewed all witnesses and there is no evidence pointing to any certain action or reaction to explain why he drowned,” he said.

The search was suspended overnight amid limited visibility, but Befumo’s body was recovered by a diver on Tuesday afternoon in about 60 feet of water beneath the cliffs, Valley County Sheriff Kevin Copperi said.

“Our efficiency and safety factors deteriorate quickly in the dark,” Copperi said. “Once the mission turns from rescue to recovery, we will wait until we have the correct resources, equipment, and lighting to accomplish the mission with the least risk possible for our manpower and equipment.”

## Fellow smokejumper remembers Befumo

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/07/valleylookout.com-he-was-the-best-of-us-mccall-smokejumper-identified-as-payette-lake-drowning-victim-screenshot-2026-07-31-at-1.41.01-pm.png)

McCall Smokejumper Luke Befumo drowned on July 27 after cliff jumping into Payette Lake. Photo: Courtesy Jamie Carter via GoFundMe.

Befumo had worked the last eight years as a wildland firefighter, including three seasons as a smokejumper, the last two of which came as a member of the McCall Smokejumper Base. He also spent eight years as a wild land firefighter on the Willamette National Forest, [according to a Payette National Forest news release](https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/payette/newsroom/releases/forest-service-mourns-tragic-loss-mccall-smokejumper).

“Luke was a dedicated member of our smokejumper community, and his loss is deeply felt across the wildland fire community and the Payette National Forest,” Payette Supervisor Matthew Davis said. “With reflection and fond memory, we extend our humble gratitude to loved ones as we are so honored to have been touched by such a kindred spirit.”

As of Friday afternoon, [a GoFundMe page](https://www.gofundme.com/f/honoring-luke-befumo?utm_id=97758_v0_s00_e0_tv2_a1demo04cb7mdt&fbclid=IwY2xjawTZsU5leHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETFMMkhyYTBIVWc2eTNhZmkwc3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHuLOPzhEwxiFPipupI7lZuHD_xRn7yTxRBQ6fcFR4WDACDQoDS1Hmh4sjG3I_aem_tN99yNuP3njDE5OJfXBy0Q) had raised nearly $49,000 to help Befumo’s family with funeral arrangements, travel, and other assistance.

“Luke’s passing leaves an irreplaceable absence amongst his family, friends, the McCall Smokejumper community, and the wildland firefighting community as a whole,” the GoFundMe page said.

The page was launched by fellow McCall smokejumper Jamie Carter, who described Befumo as “the best of us” and someone who excelled in “every aspect of the smokejumper profession.”

“Personally, I knew Luke as the best friend and bro you could think of,” Carter said. “He was a ripping snowboarder, mountain biker, snowmobiler. He always had a smile on his face and was never too busy to listen what you had to say, or to help you with a project.”

## Sheriff: deaths rare, but Payette Lake cliffs ‘extremely dangerous’

Befumo’s death marks the first death at The Cliffs in years, though emergency personnel are called to the recreation spot often, Copperi said.

“The Cliffs have historically generated several calls a year for injuries, noise, fires, and general drinking issues,” he said.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

The Cliffs features a range of rocks to jump from into Payette Lake, with some rocks towering nearly 50 feet above the surface of the water. The natural features have attracted thrill seekers for more than 50 years, but have become increasingly popular with the rise of tourism in McCall.

Copperi said The Cliffs are “extremely dangerous” and urged anyone thinking of making the jump into the lake to use caution.

“If you hit the water in a bad way, it can knock the wind out of you, break a neck or back, cause trauma to heart or arteries, or other medical emergencies that quickly become deadly when in deep water,” he said.
