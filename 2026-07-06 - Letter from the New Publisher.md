As America celebrates the 250th anniversary of our nation’s independence, I’ve found myself thinking about Benjamin Franklin.

Benjamin Franklin understood something most people have forgotten. The press wasn’t just a business. It was the mechanism by which a free people stayed free. His Pennsylvania Gazette wasn’t neutral. It was unapologetically political, opinionated, and designed to inform citizens well enough to hold their government accountable. Franklin believed a free press was as essential to liberty as any militia or courthouse.

He didn’t wait for someone else to build it.

Neither will I.

If you followed the Idaho Dispatch, you probably saw Greg Pruett’s post last December. He built something Idaho genuinely needed: independent, conservative news coverage that the mainstream media wouldn’t provide. He did it largely alone.

What he proved was that Idahoans are hungry for honest, conservative reporting focused on the issues affecting their communities and their state.

When I read Greg’s post, I didn’t see the end of the Idaho Dispatch. I saw the beginning of what it could become.

I reached out to Greg and bought the Dispatch because I believed, and still believe, that Idaho needs this publication.

The Idaho Constitution itself recognizes a truth our founders understood well:

“The stability of a republican form of government depending mainly upon the intelligence of the people…”

An informed citizenry is not optional in a Republic. It is essential.

The Idaho Dispatch cannot succeed as my publication alone. It must become Idaho’s publication.

A free press in a constitutional Republic only survives when citizens take ownership of it. By supporting it, contributing to it, sharing it, defending it, and helping uncover the truth in their own communities.

That is the kind of publication I intend to build.

My plan was to bring it back to life after winning reelection. I worked as hard as I’ve ever worked on that campaign. I lost.

But losing that race freed me to do something I couldn’t have done otherwise. I now have the time and the ability to dedicate my full attention to making the Dispatch what it was always meant to be.

My name is David Leavitt. I was born and raised in the Magic Valley. I served in the U.S. Army as an infantry soldier during the initial invasion of Iraq in 2003, and later as a Civil Affairs Specialist in Afghanistan in 2006 and 2009.

Like many veterans, coming home was not the end of the story. It took years to rebuild my life and find a new sense of purpose.

Eventually, I answered Idaho’s call and served as your State Representative for District 25B, where I was ranked among the most conservative legislators in the state during my tenure. Now I’m answering a different call.

A press that doesn’t challenge power isn’t a free press. It’s a managed one. Franklin saw that in 1729. Idahoans are living it today.

The Idaho Dispatch will focus on local and state politics. These are the stories that actually affect your life and your community. I’m actively looking for guest contributors willing to cover what is happening in their own backyards: city council meetings, county commissioner decisions, school board votes. The things that matter and the things that too often go uncovered.

Idahoans are hungry for honest, conservative news coverage. I intend to provide it.

Franklin didn’t wait for permission to speak truth to power. He picked up his pen and got to work.

One more thing: keep an eye on the Dispatch in the coming weeks. A new look is on the way, along with new shows, new contributors, and new ways to get involved with the publication directly. The mission stays the same. Everything else is about to get better.

Welcome back to the Idaho Dispatch.
