BOISE, Idaho — The Idaho Transportation Department (ITD) announced Thursday it will reopen Highway 95 between New Meadows and Council on Monday after completing [emergency repairs](https://www.ktvb.com/article/travel/idaho-gathers-data-businesses-impacted-highway-95-closure/277-35af60c8-1bb0-471c-b632-fd077857e4fe).

The stretch of the damaged highway is about 10 miles north of Council. Officials with the ITD believe the ground beneath the road slid away due to heavy rainfall in the area.

"US-95 is one of Idaho's most essential transportation routes, and from day one we knew we had to act swiftly and decisively to reopen in April," said Dan McElhinney, ITD chief deputy and chief operations officer.

ITD stated Monday will mark one month since the closure, and officials say construction crews have been working extended hours and two shifts a day to accelerate repairs. Drivers are currently directed to use State Highway 55 as an alternate route.

Crews may complete roadside and guardrail work using temporary lane closures after the highway reopens.

Travelers should check 511.idaho.gov before hitting the road on Idaho highways this summer, as construction projects on SH-55 are scheduled to begin once US-95 reopens.

BOISE, Idaho — Over [70 million Americans are expected to travel](https://www.ktvb.com/video/news/local/aaa-estimating-over-72-million-will-travel-for-america250-celebrations/277-e5d65f8c-a103-4a3a-8f40-12d6d6be1936) this holiday weekend, with over five million expected to fly in the coming days.

Holiday travel at Boise airport is no different and is already taking off. Airport Officials are preparing for a busy Fourth of July weekend, with arrivals on Thursday jumping nearly 25%. Departing passenger traffic on Sunday is forecast to jump as well compared to a typical travel day.

Despite the crowds, airport officials say travelers can expect a smooth experience.

"The Fourth of July weekend is a busy week for travel and it's a favorite holiday of so many people. It's the 250th anniversary of the country, and so there is a ton of excitement around travel and America 250 and all of it happening here," said Boise Airport spokesperson Jennifer Kronberg.

"People can expect to see a lot of people in the Boise Airport all weekend long," said Kronberg. "We think it's going to be a very fun atmosphere."

Earlier in the day the airport also held a passenger event, welcoming arriving flyers with an America250 balloon sign, as well as red, white and blue cookies.

Franklin McManus, a traveler from Michigan described the Boise airport as "really nice" and said that even for his group of 20, it didn't feel too busy.

"I can see the people, but you can't see the congestion... so it's really fantastic. You can't feel the busyness, but you can feel the liveliness," McManus said.

Airport officials continue to encourage travelers to arrive early, especially during peak departure times, to allow extra time for TSA screening and check-in.
