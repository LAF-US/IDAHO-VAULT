American flags on headstones catch the breeze last Sunday, where crews stopped flames from the Old Trails Fire at Fairmount Memorial Park in Spokane. (Tyler Tjomsland/The Spokesman-Review)

Many of the more than 900 structures estimated to have been destroyed or damaged in the Spokane Complex fires were single-family homes. But a rifle club, church and other facilities are among the other properties wiped out by the wind-driven Old Trails fire.

Flames reached the property lines of countless other businesses, assisted-living facilities and other structures. The official tally won’t be known for weeks or perhaps longer because the fire still is burning and Level 3 “go now” evacuations are in place.

Lance Beck, Greater Spokane Inc. chief strategy officer, said he suspects home-based businesses also were lost in the fire.

Beck said GSI is working to learn the scope of the fire’s impact on businesses by identifying commercial spaces in the fire zone that may have been lost or damaged. He said he hopes to have a more clear data picture in the coming weeks.

Then, the business development organization can establish what kind of help the businesses need.

The following list includes some of the businesses, assisted-living facilities, schools and other non-single-family homes destroyed, damaged or threatened by the Old Trails fire.

### Nine Mile Road area

**Riverside Real Life**

*7111 N. Nine Mile Road*

Riverside Real Life on Nine Mile Road burned to the ground. (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)

The church and the neighborhood behind it were reduced to rubble. Brick pillars, metal poles and the foundation were all that remained standing in the church footprint.

Pastor Kelly Armstrong has been providing daily updates on social media to his congregation since the fire started.

“When I broke the news to the elders, their immediate response was that the church is not a building,” Armstrong said in one of his video updates. “It’s people and it belongs to Jesus, and it’s never going to stop moving forward no matter what.”

He said elders and staff are meeting to discuss next steps.

Several churches in the area are forming a coalition, and Real Life is consulting with an international relief agency to help the church chart a plan for what will be a “long, slow recovery,” Armstrong said. The assessment team from that organization was expected to land last Monday in Spokane.

He said he is “genuinely encouraged and hopeful” that the church belongs to God and that he has good plans to come from the fire’s destruction.

“While it might take a little bit of time to see that happen, he’s still on the move,” Armstrong said.

**Spokane Rifle Club**

*6411 N. Aubrey L. White Parkway*

![Rick St. Marie, vice president of the Spokane Falls Muzzle Loaders club, holds a charred target as he sifts through the rubble of the Spokane Rifle Club on Monday, Aug 3, 2026, after the Old Trails fire burned it on Aug. 1, 2026, in Spokane.  (Tyler Tjomsland/The Spokesman-Review)](https://thumb.spokesman.com/sPBw5wCyggtVBiGg6tGvR7XFc8g=/2500x0/smart/media.spokesman.com/photos/2026/08/07/6a7158d1f10e1.hires.jpg)

Some buildings survived, others burned on the property of the 101-year-old historic rifle club, located on the Spokane River, abutting Riverside State Park and accessible by Rifle Club Road.

The shotgun clubhouse is leveled. Structures that surrounded the outdoor rifle and pistol shooting ranges have burned up, reduced to mangled metal and blackened grass.

Some of the grounds of the outdoor, 200-yard shooting range are singed, but the targets stand.

The indoor pistol range, which sits away from the direct line of the fire, was unscathed. Six storage sheds also remain standing.

**Dwight Merkel Sports Complex**

*5701 N. Assembly St.*

![The Old Trails fire burned right up against the softball field fences at Dwight Merkel Sports Complex.   (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)](https://thumb.spokesman.com/Q0f-QyYiAFq3n8Uumj2ePJ4Hh5Y=/2500x0/smart/media.spokesman.com/photos/2026/08/07/6a767238e89a8.hires.jpg)

The Old Trails fire burned right up against the softball field fences at Dwight Merkel Sports Complex. (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)

The popular sports complex sustained minimal damage from the fire. Some trees burned on the west end of the property, and the forest abutting the west perimeter of the complex was torched.

Four soccer goals along the western chain-link fence line burned. The chain-link fence, while burned, was upright and appeared fine.

The green plastic vines in the outfield softball fences on the west end melted but were mostly intact.

Spokane parks spokeswoman Fianna Dickson said in an email Monday that city officials haven’t had a chance to assess the damages yet because the facility still was in a Level 3 “go now” evacuation zone.

Ty Bruner, who recently retired from the Spokane Fire Department after 27 years with the agency, was with his wife watching their daughter play in a Spike and Dig volleyball tournament Aug. 1 when he saw smoke from the fire rolling east toward the complex.

“When I started to be able to see the definition of the plumes, I was like, ‘OK, this is getting really close,’ and I didn’t know how far back that tree line went or what was on the other side of it,” Bruner said. “But I just knew I was starting to get concerned.”

Tournament officials evacuated people at the facility shortly after that. Bruner said thousands of people were at the facility that day.

He said if the fire did come through the tree line, the Dwight Merkel fields would slow the fire down. The fire did burn through the forest and stopped at the complex.

**Avista Senior Living Spokane**

*7310 N. Pine Rock St.*

![Pink fire retardant covers the roof of Avista Senior Living Spokane just off Nine Mile Road, but it was not damaged in the fire.   (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)](https://thumb.spokesman.com/KR4J6abHcLR788BT8lfX0v8ZQdk=/2500x0/smart/media.spokesman.com/photos/2026/08/07/6a76744a9bd8c.hires.jpg)

Pink fire retardant covers the roof of Avista Senior Living Spokane just off Nine Mile Road, but it was not damaged in the fire. (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)

A short walk from Real Life down Nine Mile Road, Avista Senior Living was spared in the fire. The neighborhood between the assisted living facility and Real Life was lost.

Kory Williams, Avista Senior Living plant operations director, said the fire got close to the facility.

As residents evacuated Saturday afternoon, a plane flew over and dropped red retardant on the roof of the building, which Williams said may have saved the facility. A layer of retardant still covered the roof Thursday.

“I don’t know how the facility got spared,” he said.

Williams, who lost his home in the Autumn Lane fire, said all assisted-living residents evacuated safely, and some were taken to other Avista Senior Living facilities in Yakima and Ellensburg.

“I think everybody’s just grateful we were able to get our people into safety,” Williams said.

**Brookdale Nine Mile**

*5329 W. Rifle Club Court*

![Brookdale Nine Mile just off Nine Mile Road narrowly avoided damage from the Old Trails fire.  (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)](https://thumb.spokesman.com/yKDXo91H3-DV32LmmHBb8P5xSkk=/2500x0/smart/media.spokesman.com/photos/2026/08/07/6a7673be9b508.hires.jpg)

Brookdale Nine Mile just off Nine Mile Road narrowly avoided damage from the Old Trails fire. (Garrett Cabeza / The Spokesman-Review)

The Alzheimer’s and dementia care facility was the only building on the corner of Rifle Club Court and Nine Mile Road that didn’t burn. Structures were wiped out on two corners, and a third corner of the intersection had a thicket of trees burned and the ground covered in ash.

The residents were evacuated and relocated to two Brookdale facilities in the Tri-Cities, “where they are getting settled and doing well,” according to a statement from Brookdale on Monday.

“We remain in close communication with local emergency officials and, at this time, it appears that our community has not sustained excessive damage,” the statement continued. “While we do not yet know when it will be safe to return to the community, the health and well-being of our residents remains our highest priority.”

**Fairmount Memorial Park**

*5200 W. Wellesley Ave.*

![The Sunset Chapel and Mausoleum at Fairmount Memorial Park still stands on Sunday after being saved from the Old Trails fire in Spokane.  (Tyler Tjomsland/The Spokesman-Review)](https://thumb.spokesman.com/N7WnFQdszVPnshAJaegJ0IbuiMo=/2500x0/smart/media.spokesman.com/photos/2026/08/07/6a6fd6b59942c.hires.jpg)

The Sunset Chapel and Mausoleum at Fairmount Memorial Park still stands on Sunday after being saved from the Old Trails fire in Spokane. (Tyler Tjomsland/The Spokesman-Review)

The park’s maintenance building sustained significant damage and a pump house down the hillside that irrigates the cemetery burned down, according to Charlie Peckham, director of cemetery operations.

The fire burned lawn and landscaping as well, a Fairmount Memorial Association Facebook post said Friday.

The post stated the Sunset Mausoleum sustained some smoke damage and broken windows. Services scheduled in the mausoleum will be rescheduled until further notice. Fairmount will work with affected families regarding alternative arrangements and new service dates.

“Fairmount Memorial Park will remain closed while the park is under a Level 3 evacuation,” the post said. “For everyone’s safety, we are asking the public to please stay out of the park at this time, including entering on foot. Conditions are not yet safe for visitors or for our team to fully access all areas of the property.”

The post stated there was no significant damage to headstones, niches, crypts or other family property.

“However, we will not be able to confirm the condition of every area until it is safe for our team to return and carefully inspect the park lawn by lawn and section by section,” the association said.

### Indian Trail Road area

**Excelsior Wellness Center**

*3754 W. Indian Trail Road*

The mental health services center appeared to escape the fire without damages, but flames got close as the hillside that abuts the campus to the northeast burned.

The center’s voicemail said Friday that in-person services are suspended until further notice and virtual and off-site services will continue.

**Bulk Water Fill Station**

*4225 W. Indian Trail Road*

The bulk water station on the Indian Trail Road side is inoperable, Davis said in a text. She was unsure of the extent of the station’s damage. Much of the property’s fields and trees were scorched from the fire.

**Lilac Learning Center**

*8621 N. Five Mile Road*

The private special education school housed in the historic Five Mile Prairie School and its grounds avoided damage thanks to neighbors who used their tractors and lawnmowers to keep the fire just beyond the school property line, according to Jeffrey Kalles, owner and CEO of the center.

“Without those folks who dug that fire break, the kids would be out of school for months,” Kalles said. “They saved that program.”

Those neighbors are couple Heather and Jesse Weston, their 12-year-old son Macguire Weston and another neighbor, Vitaly Matsyplyuk.

The four neighbors used tractors to dig fire line and mowers to cut tall grass to minimize fire damage.

Heather Weston said she believed their efforts saved the southwest corner of Five Mile and Strong roads, which contains their home, Lilac Learning Center and other homes to the south of them.

The Weston home is on the south side of the school.

She estimated they put out six spot fires the first night of the fire.

“We watched that fire play hopscotch across the field toward us,” she said.

She said she sustained a burn to her leg because her pants got stuck on chicken wire while she was letting animals out of a burning barn.

“We’re thankful, but we’re trying to keep it all together,” she said. “This is pretty scary. We’re thankful our home’s still standing.”

Kalles said Lilac has rented the building from Mead School District the last four years. The facility serves 18 students, most of whom have severe autism, he said.

Kalles expressed deep gratitude to the neighbors for saving the school.

“It was incredible,” he said.

**New Horizons Community Church**

*3122 W. Lincoln Road*

The church was unharmed, but fields near it “completely burned down,” Pastor Ryan McClelland said in a Facebook video update this week.

**Providence Adult Family Home**

*2719 W. Strong Road*

The senior-living facility and its grounds were untouched by the fire, according to a woman who answered the phone Friday. The woman, who declined to provide her name, said four residents live at the facility.
