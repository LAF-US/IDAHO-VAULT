State Sen. Tammy Nichols has flip-flopped on Idaho depending on money from the federal government to pay for programs.

Again.

In [a Substack post](https://nicholsforidaho.substack.com/p/article-10-state-sovereignty-and) released a few days ago, Nichols warned that taking federal dollars can have adverse effects on states.

“Federal funds can artificially inflate state budgets, making it difficult to scale back when those dollars disappear or conditions change,” she wrote. “Idaho has worked hard to remain fiscally responsible, and leaning on the principles of the Tenth Amendment helps ensure today’s spending decisions do not become tomorrow’s liabilities.”

It’s a quick flip-flop from Nichols, who spent the past two weeks defending her vote to accept federal funds for a childcare program ripe for Somali-style fraud. That vote on 2025’s Senate Bill 1206 authorized the transfer of millions from the federal government to cover the program.

“But a ‘no’ vote wouldn’t have stopped the program or lowered taxes,” [Nichols wrote in a Facebook comment](https://substack.com/home/post/p-183756459). “It would have just sent Idaho taxpayer money to other states and taken Idaho out of the loop. I’m not interested in symbolic votes that change nothing. I voted based on what actually happens in the real world.”

Activists blasted Nichols for her reasoning, pointing out that activists used that same logic to persuade voters to expand Medicaid services in 2018.

The conservative Idaho Freedom Foundation gave [Senate Bill 1206 a -2 rating on its Spending Index](https://idahofreedom.org/senate-bill-1206-health-and-welfare-developmental-disability-services-early-learning-and-development-appropriations-fy26/), noting that the measure continued Idaho’s reliance on federal dollars.

Nichols’ defense of her vote on Senate Bill 1206 also serves as a flip-flop from an earlier position on federal funding. In a recent candidate questionnaire for the Idaho Republican Party, Nichols expressed interest in reducing Idaho’s dependence on federal dollars.

“Reclaiming sovereignty is essential for preserving Idaho’s distinct identity and values, ensuring decisions reflect the will of its citizens,” Nichols wrote. “It also allows Idaho to distance itself from reliance on federal debt dollars, promoting fiscal responsibility and economic independence.”

It remains unclear when or why Nichols changed her position—twice—on bringing additional federal dollars into Idaho.

Data suggests Idaho residents receive $1.15 for every $1 they pay in federal taxes. In short, Idahoans already get back more than they pay.
