If you’ve spent any time on X lately, you’ve likely seen two self-appointed social media influencers claiming they provide indispensable research and “overwatch” to lawmakers and Idahoans trying to follow the political process.

Brandy Paradee and Lauren Walker have even gone so far as to suggest that without their tweets, nobody would know what’s happening inside the Idaho Statehouse.

It’s a bold claim. And a pretty flimsy one.

Let’s take a look.

Every legislative session, lawmakers are required to do one thing: pass a budget. It’s not optional. It’s the job.

Heading into this session, Paradee, Walker, and their allies in the Idaho Freedom Caucus loudly criticized Launch—a multi-million-dollar program that provides free college opportunities to Idaho high school students.

They didn’t just question it. They excoriated it.

And yet, here’s what actually happened. Here are the receipts:

Despite this X outrage, Launch is fully funded again.

Remember, [tweets don’t move votes](https://substack.com/home/post/p-189850077).

That funding sits inside [House Bill 867](https://legislature.idaho.gov/sessioninfo/2026/legislation/H0867/), which passed the House on March 13 by a 51–18 vote. Idaho Freedom Caucus Co-Chair Heather Scott voted for it. So did the rest of her caucus.

Yes—the same group aligned with the loudest critics of the program ultimately supported funding it.

Over in the Senate, a similar story unfolded. Sens. Tammy Nichols and Phil Hart, Idaho Freedom Caucus members, voted for the bill, while Sens. Glenneda Zuiderveld and Christy Zito, Gang of 8 members, did not.

So the reality is a little more complicated than the narrative being pushed online.

And here’s where it gets interesting.

A quick review of Paradee and Walker’s X feeds shows little to no recent discussion about Launch or its funding. For influencers who claim to be essential watchdogs of the process, that silence is, uh, notable. And telling.

It’s also worth noting that both have made efforts to align themselves with Rep. Josh Tanner, co-chair of the Joint Finance-Appropriations Committee, the very committee that shapes what gets funded. Tanner supported this bill both in committee and on the floor.

Maybe that’s a coincidence. Or maybe it explains the sudden lack of outrage.

The truth is, real oversight isn’t measured by how loud you are on social media. If you’re going to claim you’re the public’s eyes and ears, you don’t get to go quiet when your allies do the very thing you criticized.

Because at that point, you’re not informing the public. You’re shaping a narrative. And those are two very different jobs.
