# Moscow buried by records requests on Kohberger

## Most of the thousands of requests center around police probe

[

![Anthony Kuipers avatar](https://public-assets-prod.pubgen.ai/brand_b31a1432-2c12-4a67-8456-0baf383b7aac/asset_37b14eed-73b1-4bc7-89e5-32040363ce5c.jpg?w=3840)

Anthony KuipersNews reporter

](https://www.dnews.com/writers/anthony-kuipers)

![Bill Belknap](https://public-assets-prod.pubgen.ai/brand_b31a1432-2c12-4a67-8456-0baf383b7aac/asset_d95f7090-5f8a-5c12-8cb1-b83f78833130.jpg?w=3840)

Bill Belknap

The Moscow city government is receiving hundreds of public records requests each month related to the 2022 Moscow murders, according to a city official.

According to City Administrator Bill Belknap, Moscow is on pace to receive more than 2,100 public records requests this year.

For comparison, it received less than 1,000 requests per year from 2020 to 2022, according to data shared by Belknap.

In July alone, the city received 243 requests. It received 41 requests just on Monday, Belknap said.

Belknap said the workload of processing these records has strained his staff and caused them to fall behind on the timeline for delivering them to the requester.

“We can’t keep up,” he said.

That is why Belknap plans to ask the city council for permission to hire a temporary legal assistant to process public records. This comes at a time when the city has left other city positions vacant in recent years because of budget constraints.

Belknap estimated 85% to 90% of these requests are related to the Nov. 13, 2022, murders of University of Idaho students Ethan Chapin, Kaylee Goncalves, Xana Kernodle and Madison Mogen.

Bryan Kohberger pleaded guilty and was sentenced for those murders in July 2025.

Belknap was hoping the public records requests would begin to decline following Kohberger’s conviction, but they have not.

“We just think we’re going to be in it for a lot longer,” he said.

He believes the continued media attention surrounding the case, including new documentaries and books that have been released, continues to spur interest in the case’s details. Belknap referenced a new documentary series about the murders that premiered July 29 on Netflix and is this week’s most viewed show on the streaming service, according to Netflix.

Belknap said the requests are wide-ranging, but he noticed many of them focus on the interviews police conducted during the initial investigation.

Moscow is currently the target of a civil suit filed by EWU Media, an independent media company headquartered in Nevada. The company is trying to compel the city to provide access to certain records related to the murder case. The city claims some of those records are exempt because they are under the jurisdiction of federal agencies or Pennsylvania police.

Belknap discussed public records requests during a bi-monthly meeting Thursday with other leaders from the city, UI, Latah County and Moscow School District. Dan Ewart, the UI’s vice president of information technology and chief information officer, said that the UI has also seen an uptick in public records requests.
