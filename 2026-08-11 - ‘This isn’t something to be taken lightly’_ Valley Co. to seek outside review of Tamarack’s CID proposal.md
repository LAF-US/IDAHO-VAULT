A new taxing district proposed to fund public infrastructure for an expansion of Tamarack Resort will be vetted by third-party experts.

On Monday, the Board of County Commissioners agreed with Valley County Treasurer Johanna Defoort’s recommendation to hire a consultant to independently review Tamarack’s proposed Community Infrastructure District, or CID.

“This is something that will be established that is going to affect many, many future generations,” Defoort said. “We want to make sure we do it correctly.”

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

Hiring a consultant would not cost the county any money, as state law requires CID applicants to pay for analysis on behalf of the county, Defoort said.

A consultant cannot be hired until Tamarack formally submits its application to create the district. Resort officials previously said that could happen as soon as this month.

Tamarack is proposing the CID to finance an estimated $158 million in roads, water and sewer systems, stormwater drainage, and other infrastructure for Heritage, a planned 911-unit development on 1,111 acres south of existing residential development and Tamarack Village, [as Valley Lookout reported.](https://valleylookout.com/2026/07/21/this-is-how-growth-pays-for-itself-tamarack-eyes-new-taxing-district-to-fund-residential-development/)

The district would encompass the land slated for development and allow Tamarack to borrow money at lower interest rates. Future homeowners in Heritage would repay the debt through special assessments added to their property tax bills.

## Who will maintain the infrastructure?

One of the key questions county officials want answered is who would ultimately be responsible for operating and maintaining the infrastructure built with CID financing.

State law requires infrastructure financed through a CID to be owned by the district or another public agency, rather than by Tamarack itself.

![Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/115c0cfa-e547-4a5c-8f4e-7ea7e16f8bde.png)

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

That means the county or another public agency could ultimately be responsible for maintaining and operating the infrastructure — unless that responsibility is assigned to Tamarack through agreements made in advance.

“We need to have clear and direct examples of what public infrastructure will be delegated to what public entity and who will be responsible for the maintenance and ongoing operation of it,” Defoort said.

## Time commitment unclear

If the district is created, the county commissioners would serve as its governing board — a role Tamarack officials and consultants hired by the resort said would require very little time.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

That role, however, would come with a list of responsibilities, including maintaining district budgets, approving monthly claims, overseeing development agreements, managing debt, retaining public records, contracting, and other duties.

“This isn’t something to be taken lightly,” Defoort said. “There is work to be done, and when you convene as the CID board, you clock out for the county.”

Commissioner Neal Thompson said the county needs clarity on how much time the CID responsibilities would require, while Commissioner Katlin Caldwell noted the proposal is already consuming county resources.

“There is going to be a cost to the general taxpayer in Valley County,” Caldwell said. “Right now, we’re talking about it. We have four elected officials here spending time on it.”

## Heritage background

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/07/valleylookout.com-this-is-how-growth-pays-for-itself-tamarack-eyes-new-taxing-district-to-fund-residential-development-screenshot-2026-07-21-at-1.32.18-pm.jpeg)

A conceptual site plan for Tamarack Resort’s planned Heritage expansion. Map: Via Tamarack Resort

Preliminary plans for Heritage call for 314 village condominiums, 189 estate lots, 187 chalets, 120 village townhomes, 60 ranch lots, and 41 village cottages.

The development would also include an 80-room hotel, 20 acres of commercial and retail development, and about 200 acres of open space, including a small “pitch and putt” golf course.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

The land on which Tamarack is planning to build Heritage includes about 500 acres south of the Tamarack Village that was purchased by the resort in 2024. That acquisition linked the resort’s private land holdings with about 300 acres farther south than Tamarack acquired in 2019.

Overall, Tamarack is approved to build 2,043 residential units under a master plan approved by Valley County in 2002. So far, about 700 of those units have been platted.

Despite the land use approval, the resort still needs Valley County to approve the final design for each subsequent phase of development, including Heritage.

The Heritage project aligns with Tamarack’s long-term vision of expanding the ski resort south of its existing boundaries. Plans to add new chairlifts and ski terrain were initiated in 2022 but are currently inactive, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2026/06/24/tamarack-resort-expansion-on-hold-for-now-after-forest-service-application-expired/).
