The $1.1 billion expansion of St. Luke’s Regional Medical Center in Boise has reached its highest point, with a topping-out ceremony on a rainy summer Friday.

Officials with general contractor Layton Construction and St. Luke’s Health System gathered to watch a crane operator lift a beam topped with a pine tree and an American flag to the top of the expansion along Fort St.

“This is an old tradition that comes from eons ago, when they used to put an evergreen tree on top of a home after they built it,” Layton Construction Vice President Jeremy Hobbs said. “It’s to represent prosperity and good luck, and we hope to have the same thing here.”

Construction work on a pair of intertwined projects at the site started in 2024. The hospital building will add another 860,000 square feet of space across nine above-ground and two-below ground stories. It will connect to a new medical office plaza building that will jut out over First Street and add 180,000 of medical office space.

“It is so critical to look at this, this topping out ceremony, to literally pause, literally look up and see what we’re accomplishing together as a team,” St. Luke’s Area Vice President Dennis Mesaros said. “Sometimes we don’t underscore enough the importance of collaboration and working together. Fifteen months ago, it was a hole in the ground. This is a watershed moment for all of St. Luke’s and the community.”

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Screenshot-2026-06-2-Screenshot-2026-06-2.png)

The 2014 St. Luke’s Master Plan map outlines a number of projects being worked on across the campus. Map: Via City of Boise

[As BoiseDev has reported](https://boisedev.com/project/st-lukes-downtown-expansion/), the new hospital tower will add 80 new hospital beds to the health system’s regional medical center campus, bringing the capacity over the 500 mark. Surgical services will relocate to the new building and add seven new operating rooms, bringing the total at the Boise campus to 27. Crews will also add a second helipad atop the expansion. The building extends over the former Jefferson St., which St. Luke’s won the right to close after a multi-year battle with the City of Boise late in the last decade, [over objections from neighbors in Boise’s east end.](https://boisedev.com/news/2023/03/27/st-lukes-jefferson/)

![Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/6f251bf1-00e6-48d7-8365-570820fe299b.gif)

Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow

The office plaza building will house a number of clinics and doctors, with direct access to the main hospital. St. Luke’s said it will bring in facilities currently housed in the Jefferson Medical Office Plaza building, currently located at Jefferson St. and Fort St.

The [multi-year expansion project](https://stlukesonline.org/en/healthier-communities/commitments-and-impact/st-lukes-boise-development-plan/master-plan-overview/) will continue until late 2029, St. Luke’s said, and it hopes to start welcoming patients in early 2030. St Luke’s is also planning to “modernize and renovate” the current main hospital tower. It says it will update its critical care units, labor and delivery department, the emergency room area, and revamp about 60 additional patient rooms.
