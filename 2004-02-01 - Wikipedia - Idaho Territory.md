The **Territory of Idaho** was an [organized incorporated territory of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organized_incorporated_territory_of_the_United_States "Organized incorporated territory of the United States") that existed for over 27 years, from March 3, 1863,[^1] until July 3, 1890, when the final extent of the territory was admitted to the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") as the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") of [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho").

### 1860s

The territory was officially organized on March 3, 1863, by Act of [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37th_United_States_Congress "37th United States Congress"),[^1] and signed into law by President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln "Abraham Lincoln"). It is a successor region that was created by areas from existing territories undergoing parallel political transitions beginning with disputes over which country owned the region (See [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country "Oregon Country")). By 1863, the area west of the [Continental Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Divide_of_the_Americas "Continental Divide of the Americas") that was formerly part of the huge [Oregon Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Territory "Oregon Territory") had been sundered from the coastal [Washington Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Territory "Washington Territory") north of the young state of [Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon "Oregon") (1859) to the far west and the remnant of the Oregon Territory was officially "unorganized". Most of the area east of the Continental Divide had been part of the loosely defined [Dakota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory "Dakota Territory") ending along the 49th parallel—now the border with [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada "Canada"), then a [colonial possession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonialism "Colonialism") of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain "Great Britain").

The original newly organized territory covered all of the present-day states of [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho") and [Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana "Montana") and almost all of [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming "Wyoming"), omitting only a corner in the state's extreme southwest portion. It was wholly spanned east-to-west by the bustling [Oregon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Trail "Oregon Trail") and partly by the other [emigrant trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigrant_trail "Emigrant trail"), the [California Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Trail "California Trail") and [Mormon Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_Trail "Mormon Trail") which since hitting stride in 1847, had been conveying settler wagon trains to the west, and incidentally, across the continental divide into the [Snake River Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_River_Basin "Snake River Basin"), a key gateway into the Idaho and [Oregon Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Country "Oregon Country") interiors.

The first territorial capital was at [Lewiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewiston,_Idaho "Lewiston, Idaho"), from 1863 to 1866;[^3] [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho") was made the territorial capital in 1866 by a one-vote margin of the [territorial supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_justices_of_the_Idaho_Supreme_Court "List of justices of the Idaho Supreme Court"). Congress failed to provide official criminal or civic codes for the Idaho territory upon its inception, so it was not until January 4, 1864, that the territory's original laws were adopted.[^4]

The upheaval caused by the Civil War and [Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era_of_the_United_States "Reconstruction era of the United States") was a distant concern to those in the comparatively stable Idaho Territory, a situation which encouraged settlement.

In 1864, the [Montana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Territory "Montana Territory") was organized from the northeastern section of the territory east of the [Bitterroot Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitterroot_Range "Bitterroot Range"). Most of the southeastern area of the territory was made part of the [Dakota Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Territory "Dakota Territory").

In the late 1860s, Idaho Territory became a destination for displaced Southern [Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(United_States\) "Democratic Party (United States)") who fought for the [Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America") during the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War"). These people were well represented in the early territorial legislatures, which often clashed with the appointed [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_\(United_States\) "Republican Party (United States)") territorial governors. The political infighting became particularly vicious in 1867 when [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Idaho "List of governors of Idaho") [David W. Ballard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_W._Ballard "David W. Ballard") asked for protection from federal troops stationed at [Fort Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Boise#New_Fort_Boise_\(1863%E2%80%931912\) "Fort Boise") against the territorial legislature. By 1870, however, the political infighting had decreased considerably.

In 1868, the areas east of the [111th meridian west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/111th_meridian_west "111th meridian west") became the newly created [Wyoming Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_Territory "Wyoming Territory"), and Idaho Territory assumed the boundaries of the modern state. The discovery of gold, silver, and other valuable natural resources throughout Idaho beginning in the 1860s, as well as the completion of the [transcontinental railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad "First transcontinental railroad") in 1869, brought many new people to the territory, including Chinese laborers who came to work the mines. As Idaho approached statehood, mining and other extractive industries became increasingly important to its economy. By the 1890s, for example, Idaho exported more lead than any other state, primarily extracted from the [Silver Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Valley_\(Idaho\) "Silver Valley (Idaho)").

### 1870s

Construction began on the [Idaho Territorial Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Idaho_State_Penitentiary "Old Idaho State Penitentiary") in 1870; completed by 1872, it was in use for over a century as the state penitentiary, until [late 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_State_Correctional_Institution "Idaho State Correctional Institution"). Placed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places "National Register of Historic Places") in 1974 for its significance as a territorial prison; the site currently contains museums and an [arboretum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arboretum "Arboretum").[^5]

Almost immediately after Idaho Territory was created, a public school system was created and [stage coach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_coach "Stage coach") lines were established. Regular newspapers were active in Lewiston, [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho") and [Silver City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_City,_Idaho "Silver City, Idaho") by 1865. The first telegraph line reached [Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin,_Idaho "Franklin, Idaho") in 1866, with Lewiston being the first town linked in northern Idaho in 1874. The first telephone call in the Pacific Northwest was made on May 10, 1878, in Lewiston.

Although forming a sizable minority, members of the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints") in Idaho were held in suspicion by others in Idaho. By 1882, notable and powerful Idahoans successfully disenfranchised members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints voters in Idaho Territory, citing their illegal [practice of polygamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism_and_polygamy "Mormonism and polygamy"). Idaho was able to achieve statehood some six years before Utah, a territory which had a larger population and had been settled longer, but was majority members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints with voting polygamists.

There were four thousand Chinese living in the Idaho Territory from 1869 to 1875. Like many [Chinese immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American "Chinese American"), they came to "Gold Mountain" to work as miners, or found work as laundrymen and cooks.[^6] The 1870 census reported there were 1,751 Chinese in [Idaho City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_City "Idaho City") who were nearly half of city residents.[^7]

The [Nez Perce War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nez_Perce_War "Nez Perce War") began in the [north-central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Central_Idaho "North Central Idaho") portion of the territory at [White Bird Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_White_Bird_Canyon "Battle of White Bird Canyon") in June 1877, and concluded four months later in eastern [Montana Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana_Territory "Montana Territory") at [Bear Paw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bear_Paw "Battle of Bear Paw").

### 1880s

After the capital relocation controversy, proposals to split the two regions became widespread. For the rest of the decade, Idaho Territory was nearly legislated out of existence. In 1885, the Idaho legislature published a bipartisan memorial to Congress asking for northern Idaho to be returned to Washington Territory. There were two attempts during the [49th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_United_States_Congress "49th United States Congress") to accomplish this. On March 1, 1887 Congress sent a bill to President [Grover Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grover_Cleveland "Grover Cleveland") which would have returned northern Idaho to Washington Territory. Cleveland, likely as a favor to Governor [Edward A. Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_A._Stevenson "Edward A. Stevenson"), refused to sign the bill and it was [pocket vetoed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_veto#United_States "Pocket veto") when the session of Congress ended two days later.[^8] Nevada and Utah showed interest in acquiring the remainders of southern Idaho.[^9] [^10] [^11]

In 1889, the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho") was awarded to the northern town of [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow,_Idaho "Moscow, Idaho") instead of its original planned location at Eagle Rock (now [Idaho Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Falls,_Idaho "Idaho Falls, Idaho")) in the south. This served to alleviate some of the hard feelings felt by [North Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Panhandle "Idaho Panhandle") residents over losing the capital.

In a bid for statehood, Governor Stevenson called for a [constitutional convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Constitutional_Convention "Idaho Constitutional Convention") in 1889. The convention approved a constitution on August 6, 1889, and voters approved the constitution on November 5, 1889.[^12] [^13] [^14] President [Benjamin Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Harrison "Benjamin Harrison") signed the law admitting Idaho as a [U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") on July 3, 1890.
