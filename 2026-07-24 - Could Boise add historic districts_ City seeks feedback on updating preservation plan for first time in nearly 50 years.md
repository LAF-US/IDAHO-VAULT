A nearly fifty-year-old plan is getting an update.

The City of Boise is working on updating its Historic Preservation Plan, an effort last undertaken in 1979. The plan analyzes the state of the city’s current historic preservation program and outlines goals and recommendations for preserving historic resources, including residential and commercial buildings and landmarks.

According to the draft, the city’s historic preservation program includes its historic preservation ordinance and activities related to the city’s Historic Preservation Commission, such as design review.

In June, the City of Boise [published a draft](https://issuu.com/cityofboise/docs/boise_historic_preservation_plan_draft_june_2026) of an updated plan and is seeking public feedback [via online comments](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/planning-and-development-services/planning/planning-a-project/planning-process-overview/historic-preservation/historic-preservation-plan/) and two open houses:

- July 28, [at the Library! at Cole and Ustick](https://www.cityofboise.org/events/pds/2026/july/historic-preservation-plan-update-open-house/) from 6-7:30 p.m.
- July 29, [at the Boise Depot](https://www.cityofboise.org/events/pds/2026/july/historic-preservation-plan-update-open-house-1/) from 6-7:30 p.m.

The city hired the consulting firm Northwest Vernacular in the fall of 2025 to develop the plan, the draft said. Initial work included coordinating with city staff and the Idaho State Historic Preservation Office to review planning efforts, listed resources, development patterns, city planning documents, and more. A survey about people’s perception of historic preservation in the city was open to the public from January 2026 through June 10, 2026. The survey feedback is included in the draft.

## A chance for clarity

At a July 15 city council workshop, Boise City Council members expressed hope that an updated plan would alleviate ambiguity and frustration during land use and property discussions.

![Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/6f251bf1-00e6-48d7-8365-570820fe299b.gif)

Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow

Council Member Luci Willits recounted a meeting in which a homeowner in a historic district had replaced windows in their garage, which were not historic, and faced having to replace them again to comply with requirements of being in the district.

“As I recall, the historic district was created as a catch-all, whether your property was historic or not,” Willits said. “Is there an opportunity to ensure that folks can invest in their property, and (have the city) maintain the historic character which we all love?”

Council Member Jimmy Hallyburton said that he’d like to see more attention given to balancing maintaining the historic integrity of neighborhoods while also allowing for the use of more sustainable materials and energy efficiency upgrades. He also expressed interest in clarifying city policy on historic trees in historic neighborhoods.

Mayor Lauren McLean said that next steps for the plan will include gathering additional feedback from the community about what is working or not with the city’s preservation plan and ordinance. Ultimately, decision-making by the council will be required to change specific parts of city policy, she said.

## Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats

In order to guide recommendations for historic preservation, part of the draft plan includes a strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats (SWOT) analysis for the city’s historic preservation efforts.

Some strengths include having twelve historic districts and 35 designated historic landmarks, and neighborhood plans “that recognize historic preservation as an asset.” Other strengths include having qualified staff who manage the historic preservation program and the city’s investment in history and heritage.

Some threats, however, are that the city faces development pressure in established neighborhoods that aren’t designated historic districts, and that there are few designated historic properties south of the river. Another threat is called “demolition by neglect,” in which buildings in historic areas are not adequately maintained and begin to deteriorate. The plan also categorizes the perception of historic preservation as a barrier to housing affordability as a threat.

The analysis also lists the formatting of the city’s historic preservation ordinance as a weakness. At the moment, language about the ordinance is “distributed throughout the zoning code rather than in one location, making connections to the historic preservation program difficult.”

## Potential vision, goals, strategies

Based on its efforts so far, the draft states the vision of the plan as, “Creating and sustaining community through the preservation of historic places that have shaped Boise’s identity.”

![Valley Lookout house - Drew](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/12c5b6ba-e344-4f74-a83b-fe4af9ea9df5.gif)

Valley Lookout house - Drew

To achieve that vision, the plan outlines strategies and goals grouped into four categories.

One category is city planning. One strategy is to incorporate historic preservation language, including references to the city’s historic preservation commission and ordinance, into city and neighborhood planning documents. Another is clarifying the historic preservation ordinance.

Another category is stewardship. Those strategies include planning for future landmark and historic district designations, encouraging adaptive reuse and compatible infill, and establishing a regular survey program to update the city’s inventory of historic properties.

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

Watkins Carter - 2:1

An “incentives” category proposes several strategies to provide historic property owners with the resources to maintain and rehabilitate their properties, such as identifying and making owners aware of incentives and establishing incentives that the city manages to encourage upkeep and adaptive reuse. Adaptive reuse tends to refer to the ability to alter the use of buildings, for example, by converting a historically commercial building into housing.

The plan also suggests strategies around public outreach and education, including creating a public outreach and education program about the benefits of historic preservation.

A section of the plan outlines short-term, mid-term, and long-term plans to achieve those goals. One mid-term action item listed is establishing incentives such as streamlined permitting, fee waivers, zoning and building code allowances, and tax credits to support private investment in historic building reuse.
