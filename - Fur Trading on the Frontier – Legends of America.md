One of the earliest and most important industries in North America, the American fur trade played a significant role in the development of the United States and Canada for more than 300 years. Involving half a dozen European nations and numerous [American Indian](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/native-americans/) [tribes](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/na-tribelist/), the fur trade began in the 1500s. Native Americans traded furs for supplies such as tools, weapons, and horses. The furs, in turn, were utilized to make hats, coats, and blankets, which were very popular in Europe.

Some of the earliest fur traders were French explorers and fishermen who arrived in what is now Eastern Canada during the early 1500s. By the early 1600s, demand for beaver fur increased dramatically as fashionable European men began wearing felt hats made from beaver fur.

Further exploration of North America made legends of dozens of men, incl [uding](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/fur-trade-companies/) [John Jacob Astor](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/we-johnjacobastor/) [and the](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/we-johnjacobastor/) [American Fur Company](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/american-fur-company/), [the Hudson’s Bay Company](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/hudsons-bay-company/) (the oldest company in North America), [Manuel Lisa and the](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/we-manuellisa/) [Missouri Fur Company](https://www.legendsofamerica.com/missouri-fur-company/), and many others. However, by the late 1700s, the fur trade began to decline as fur-bearing animals became increasingly scarce. In the 1830s, demand for beavers declined as European manufacturers began using silk instead of felt for hats. By 1870, most fur-trading activities had ended.
