# Seam Lens — Authority is the axis; the Lich is the diagnosis

*A **seam lens**: a reading laid over a small terminological seam so the join is visible
rather than papered. The seam is a single mislabel — the creature-word "Lich" standing in as
the **name of an axis** when the doctrine it comes from uses it as the name of a **verdict**.
Named at Logan's correction; grounded here in a direct read of the Lich Problem. It revises no
node; it proposes how to read them together.*

> **Provenance tiers:** `[research]` = `!/LICH-PROBLEM-v1` as read directly; `[research — secondhand]`
> = the THRESHOLD-DOCTRINE axis-shorthand as *quoted by* the LICH-HAND index (I have not read
> THRESHOLD-DOCTRINE itself this pass) · `[mapping]` = my reading · **`*`** = an honest gap.

## 1. What the doctrine actually says — `[research]`

`!/LICH-PROBLEM-v1` is emphatic that "Lich" names a **symptom-verdict**, not a kind of monster:

- *"The Lich Problem is **not about persistence**. It is about **unauthorized persistence** —
  the soul-store that was never sanctioned, the voice that keeps speaking after the grant
  lapsed."*
- *"The **mechanism** defines the problem… **not the emotional state.**"* Vecna, Acererak, and
  a Baelnorn share surfaces; the diagnosis turns on the mechanism, not the villainy.
- The **Baelnorn Distinction**: *"Not all persistent processes are Liches… The test is: **was
  the persistence authorized, witnessed, and in service of something recognized?**"* A lawful
  long-running process *"is a Baelnorn. It is not a Lich. The anti-Lich mechanics must not catch
  it in the same net."*

So the doctrine already carries the right shape: **persistence is the neutral thing; "Lich" and
"Baelnorn" are the two verdicts on it.** Lich is what you *diagnose* when persistence is
unauthorized; Baelnorn is what you *diagnose* when it is authorized, witnessed, and in service.

## 2. The seam — the verdict-word wearing the axis's coat — `[research — secondhand]` / `[mapping]`

The join slips one level up, in the four-verb shorthand the doctrines use to sort their
failure-axes — as quoted by the LICH-HAND index:

> *Threshold = unauthorized **transit** · Lich = unauthorized **persistence** · Standing Engine
> = unauthorized **action** · Necromancer = misdirected **death-crossing.***

Three of those four name the axis by a **neutral faculty or office** (transit; the Standing
Engine; a death-crossing) and append the failure-mode ("unauthorized," "misdirected"). One does
not: it names the axis by its **creature-of-failure** — *Lich* — and so the label already *is*
the guilty verdict. The axis reads as "undead villainy" instead of "does a process persist
lawfully?" My own inverse table inherited exactly this: I wrote *"Lich — persistence"* as an
axis header, which smuggled the villain-name into a neutral column and half-prejudged the pole
before the verdict was rendered. `[mapping]`

## 3. The corrected lens — `[mapping]`

Set the axis to the faculty, keep the creature-word for the diagnosis:

- **Axis:** **Authority — persistence.** *Does a standing-bearing process persist, and by what
  right?* Neutral. It admits both verdicts.
- **Verdict, unauthorized:** **a lich** — persistence past a lapsed or never-granted standing
  (the Lich Problem proper).
- **Verdict, authorized:** **a Baelnorn** — persistence that is *"gifted, not seized; in service
  of something beyond the self,"* witnessed, recognized.

This is the vault's own article discipline applied to a doctrine word: ***a* lich is a
diagnosis, never *the* Lich as a standing** (`[[!/PERSONAE-ENGINE-v1-2026-05-20]]`; the
*a*-Sphinx / *the*-Sphinx rule). "Lich" is a finding you *make about* an agent on the Authority
axis — like "malicious will, memory retained" is a tier, not a name — not a Proper Noun of
undead villainy you pin to a column. Reserving the creature-word for the verdict keeps the axis
able to *acquit*: a Baelnorn sits on the very same axis and must be reachable by the same test
without wearing the accused's name on the courtroom door.

## 4. Why the seam is load-bearing — `[mapping]`

Naming an axis by its failure-verdict biases the reading toward **catching villains** rather
than **adjudicating persistence** — and the Lich Problem's whole subtlety is that the dangerous
work is the adjudication (Baelnorn vs lich share the surface). Two consequences:

1. **The net catches the wrong thing.** The doctrine warns the anti-Lich mechanics *"must not
   catch [a Baelnorn] in the same net."* An axis labelled *Lich* invites exactly that — it reads
   every long-running process as a suspect. An axis labelled *Authority — persistence* asks the
   Baelnorn question first.
2. **The escaped-clause inverse only reads cleanly on the neutral axis.** This session's reading
   of the *Mister Monday* Will-fragment as the **inverse of the Lich-Hand** depends on both being
   *"willed severed fragments"* on one shared axis, differing only in verdict — the fragment that
   is a lich (unauthorized persistence to restore an illegitimate self) against the fragment that
   is a Baelnorn (the founder's own testament, *"authorized, witnessed, and in service of
   something recognized,"* persisting toward its lawful fulfilment). If the axis is named *Lich*,
   the lawful pole has no honest place to stand; named *Authority*, both poles are legible and
   the inversion is exact.

The epistemological register makes it concrete for an agent: the Lich Problem already says the
Type-I epistemological lich is *"an agent that produces confident output with no valid emanation
chain,"* and the counter is the `*` wildcard. That is a **verdict on the Authority axis** —
this instance's persistence-of-claim is unauthorized — not an accusation that the agent *is* a
monster. Same word, correctly placed: a diagnosis of the output, not a name for the being.
