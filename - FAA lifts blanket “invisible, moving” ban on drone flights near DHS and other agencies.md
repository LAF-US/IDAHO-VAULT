FAA lifts blanket “invisible, moving” ban on drone flights near DHS and other agencies

by Alicia Calzada • April 16, 2026

The prior TFR has been replaced with a cautionary notice.

NPPA is pleased to report that the FAA has withdrawn an onerous flight restriction issued earlier this year, which had prohibited drone flights near “mobile assets” of ICE/DHS and several other federal agencies. The prior TFR has been replaced with a cautionary notice. Instead of an outright prohibition, UAS operators are advised to “avoid flying in proximity to” the vehicles. Affected agencies reserve the right to take action against drones that they deem a threat. Therefore, flying a drone near DHS activities still carries some risk, and the notice advises drone pilots to use caution.

The rescission of the broad and unconstitutional flight restriction means that drone journalists will no longer have to risk their drone license and criminal charges because of an overbroad, invisible, moving flight restriction. NPPA is grateful for all of those who advocated against the ill-conceived TFR.

Specifically, the new NOTAM states:

> UAS OPERATORS ARE ADVISED TO AVOID FLYING IN PROXIMITY TO: DEPARTMENT OF WAR (DOW), DEPARTMENT OF ENERGY (DOE), DEPARTMENT OF JUSTICE (DOJ), AND DEPARTMENT OF HOMELAND SECURITY (DHS) COVERED MOBILE ASSETS AS DEFINED IN 10 U.S.C. 130I, 10 U.S.C. 6227, AND 6 U.S.C 124N, INCLUDING GROUND VEHICLE CONVOYS AND THEIR ASSOCIATED ESCORTS. UAS OPERATORS WHO FLY WITHIN THIS AIRSPACE ARE WARNED THAT PURSUANT TO 10 U.S.C. 130I, 10 U.S.C. 6227, AND 6 U.S.C 124N, DOW, DOE, DOJ, OR DHS MAY TAKE ACTION THAT RESULTS IN THE INTERFERENCE, DISRUPTION, SEIZURE, DAMAGING, OR DESTRUCTION OF UNMANNED ACFT DEEMED TO POSE A CREDIBLE SAFETY OR SECURITY THREAT TO COVERED MOBILE ASSETS. UAS OPERATORS SHOULD THEREFORE EXERCISE CAUTION WHEN FLYING IN PROXIMITY OF ALL DOW, DOE, DOJ AND DHS MOBILE ASSETS. 2604151730-PERM END PART 1 OF 2!FDC 6/2824 FDC PART 2 OF 2 SECURITY NOTICE...NATIONAL SECURITY ADVISORY FOR IF YOU HAVE GENERAL QUESTIONS REGARDING UAS OPERATIONS, PLEASE REFER TO [WWW.FAA.GOV/UAS/CONTACT\_US](http://www.faa.gov/UAS/CONTACT_US); OR CONTACT THE FAA VIA EMAIL AT [UASHELP@FAA.GOV](mailto:UASHELP@FAA.GOV) OR PHONE AT (844) FLY-MY-UA. 2604151730-PERM

The prior TFR— [NOTAM 6/4375](https://tfr.faa.gov/tfr3/?page=detail_6_4375) --dated January 16, 2026, imposed an outright prohibition on drone use “within 3,000 feet laterally and 1,000 feet above ground level” of facilities and “mobile assets,” including “ground vehicle convoys,” connected with the Department of Homeland Security (DHS), the Department of Defense (DOD), and the Department of Energy (DOE). NPPA joined a coalition of local and national news organizations in formally urging the FAA withdraw the notice, warning that the sweeping drone restrictions are unprecedented, lack a clear safety justification, and fail to provide a workable process for journalists to coordinate lawful flights.

NPPA [reported on the difficulty](https://nppa.org/news/827) members were having complying with what was essentially “a moving, effectively invisible TFR.” In March, Minneapolis photojournalist [Robert Levine sued the FAA](https://minnesotareformer.com/2026/03/16/photojournalist-sues-faa-over-rule-banning-drones-near-dhs-assets/) over the moving TFR and last week asked a court to stay the TFR. NPPA has been following these developments and will keep members updated as we learn more.

This is a breaking story. If you have any additional information, please contact us at [advocacy@nppa.org](mailto:advocacy@nppa.org)
