The nine-year-old Labrador Retriever is the second courthouse dog Ada County has had. A recent veterinary diagnosis showed he has chronic kidney disease.

ADA COUNTY, Idaho — For seven years, Yuko, a nine-year-old Labrador Retriever, has acted as a source of calm for individuals at the Ada County Courthouse going through what could be the most difficult day of their lives.

Due to a recent diagnosis of chronic kidney disease, it was decided retirement would provide Yuko with the best quality of life.

In a news release, the Ada County Courthouse described how Yuko would sit "quietly beside children during forensic interviews," provide "a steadying presence for those preparing to testify," and act as a form of reassurance for many.

"Yuko has been part of our family for seven years," said Ada County Prosecutor Jan Bennetts. "He showed up every day with patience and gentleness, and he made a real difference for the people we serve. We are so grateful for everything he has given this office and this community."

Joining the team in 2019, Yuko was certified through Canine Companions for Independence and followed the first courthouse dog, Sunday, who reportedly logged over 16,500 hours from 2010 to 2018.

Yuko and his handler, Kristin Friend, said farewell at the officer's annual employee picnic, where he received "endless pets, scratches, and belly rubs from his many colleagues," the release said.
