## ‘We are releasing a huge burden’: Iconic McCall business calls it quits amid ‘anti-tourism’ regulations

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/04/valleylookout.com-we-are-releasing-a-huge-burden-iconic-mccall-business-calls-it-quits-amid-anti-tourism-regulations-unknown-2.jpeg)

Cheap Thrills Rentals in McCall closed this spring after 35 years in business. Photo: Courtesy Rachael Ikola

![ICCU](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/748dce9e-e5ec-4fa6-9c85-f6cbe0db4a73.png)

Rachael and Gerry Ikola saw the writing on the wall last fall.

It was then that the Cheap Thrills Rentals owners decided mounting expenses born from new local regulations were too much to bear for the longtime McCall business. The extra costs, along with the fear that more may be coming down the pipeline in future years, led the Ikola’s to decide they wanted out.

“I just don’t feel comfortable with the future of tourism in McCall,” Rachael Ikola said of the decision. “It wasn’t worth paying more money into.”

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The couple planned to list the business for sale this spring, but then a 30% drop in snowmobile rentals this winter amid historically low snowfall left the company unable to pay its insurance premiums. That financial blow was preceded by a $100,000 drop in sales last summer, which saw 10 days of rain during prime boating season.

Rather than borrow more money to keep the business afloat and hope for a speedy sale to a new owner, the Ikola’s decided to close Cheap Thrills and surrender its assets to the bank to be sold at auction.

“I can honestly say that we are releasing a huge burden,” Ikola said. “It’s like this huge stress vest has finally lifted off of us and it feels good. We can finally almost breathe again.”

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

Cheap Thrills had been a McCall staple since it was opened 35 years ago by Chuck and Peggy Whitescarver, who sold it to the Ikola family in 2021.

For decades, drivers could not miss the business upon entering McCall. If it wasn’t the vibrant landscaping around the building at 303 N. Third St., it was bright Christmas lights illuminating snow drifts on the property. And, of course, the expansive fleet of rental snowmobiles, boats, and jet skis parked to the side.

Now, all that remains is the building Cheap Thrills leased, with banners and equipment parked out front belonging to the new tenants — Mountain Meadows Adventure Rentals.

## Weather a factor, but not the reason

While Cheap Thrills suffered from back-to-back rental seasons marred by poor weather, Ikola emphasized that the family’s decision was driven by expenses tied to recent regulations adopted by the City of McCall and Valley County.

“The big thing, really, is we’ve been having to deal with the county and the city for three years now,” she said.

For almost 20 years, Cheap Thrills paid for permitting from the city to stage rental jet skis on the beach in Legacy Park. The annual fee for the permits started at $7,100 in 2007 and, by 2021, was $10,000.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

The Ikola’s [renewed the permitting](https://portal.laserfiche.com/Portal/DocView.aspx?id=298245&repo=r-d76fb24e&searchid=7220df40-7a0e-4c25-92ab-7910ccebe5a9) through 2024 after buying Cheap Thrills, but said they became disillusioned after watching other businesses use the beach for operations without holding a permit — and [without being held to special standards](https://valleylookout.com/2025/03/05/mccall-looks-to-hike-boat-ramp-fees-if-you-dont-live-in-idaho/) for employee uniforms, company vehicles, and parking.

“To me, that wasn’t fair,” Ikola said. “I was like, ‘So, why are we spending $10,000 when these other people are doing it for free?’ It didn’t make sense to me.”

## Shifting boat ramp fees

At the same time, Cheap Thrills was grappling with new fees adopted by the city in July 2024 for the use of its Payette Lake boat ramp between Legacy Park and Mile High Marina.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The fees established a $1,000 fee for commercial operators, plus a $7.50 per trailer fee for launches between 10 a.m. and 8 p.m. Ikola estimated the new fee would cost the business about $25,000 per year.

The ramp fees prompted Cheap Thrills to sell its fleet of 30 jet skis after the 2024 boating season in a bid to cut back on regulatory costs. The move also eliminated the need to lease space from the city on Legacy Beach for its jet ski rentals, saving another $10,000, as well as $40,000 in annual insurance costs.

But before the 2025 boating season began, the city changed the ramp fees to no longer include the per-launch charge that the jet ski decision was made upon. Instead, the fees were set at a flat rate of $1,500 for commercial operators to simplify administration, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2025/05/14/mccall-hikes-boat-ramp-fees-but-not-for-all-boaters/).

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

“By then, the damage was done,” Ikola said.

## Snowmobile trailhead parking reduced

The headache caused by the shifting boating regulations was enough to make the Ikola’s seriously think about selling the business. But [new rules for snowmobile parking trailheads](https://valleylookout.com/2025/11/19/this-is-a-one-year-solution-valley-co-oks-new-snowmobile-parking-area-rules/) adopted last year by Valley County sent them over the edge.

The new rules cut parking in half for commercial operators at the Francis Wallace Parking Lot, which is the main trailhead for access to Secesh, Burgdorf Hot Springs, and Warren.

While Ikola and other companies agreed that something needed to be done to reduce congestion in the popular lot, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2025/11/05/its-frustrating-for-everybody-valley-co-weighing-new-rules-for-popular-snowmobile-trailheads/), the last-minute timing left the companies scrambling to adjust their operations.

“We spent $35,000 to buy a bigger trailer to fit more sleds before the season started,” Ikola said.

## Uneasy about future regulations

Early last year, a working group consisting of county officials, state agencies, and commercial outfitters began meeting to discuss the new snowmobile trailhead rules. At one point, Ikola said, it was suggested that commercial outfitters should shuttle guests to the trailheads so that their customers don’t occupy space in the lot.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

That provision did not make it into the adopted rules, but the financial reality of it made Ikola take pause.

“I penciled out the cost, and it was going to be over $200,000 in capital, and at least $70,000 yearly in labor to hire double the staff to do this,” she said.

Her concerns about future regulations were raised further by comments made by Sherry Maupin, who chairs the Board of County Commissioners, during the Nov. 17 meeting at which the new rules were adopted.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“Other resort areas, they don’t allow commercial businesses in those parking lots,” Maupin said. “This is a huge benefit to you to have spaces in those parking lots up there. Let’s make it work for all of us.”

Ikola holds similar concerns regarding potential future regulations for boating on Payette Lake.

In recent years, McCall City Council members have expressed desires to limit the number of boats on the lake, while water quality groups continue to advocate for increasing no-wake zones to 500 feet — as recommended by the [Valley County Waterways Management Plan](https://www.co.valley.id.us/media/Departments/Waterways/Valley%20Waterways%20Management%20Plan_FinalDraft_221031sm.pdf).

“The more you limit things, the harder it is to do business,” she said.

## Push for a ‘private playground’

As Ikola sees it, the new regulations for boating and snowmobiling are driven by anti-tourism sentiments held by many local residents. She understands frustration with traffic and congestion that is common during peak tourism periods, but said rental companies like Cheap Thrills play a key role in keeping McCall and the surrounding public lands accessible for everyone. That accessibility, in turn, drives business to restaurants, retail shops, hotels, and other local businesses.

“There are businesses and people that live here and work here that depend on the revenue and jobs,” she said. “Basically, it’s a battle of those that have versus those that don’t, and those that have the means don’t want to share, but want a private playground.”

Ikola is at peace with the decision to close Cheap Thrills, although she worries it will make it harder for some families to find rental boats and snowmobiles available for reservation. All said, the Cheap Thrill fleet included 16 boats and 46 snowmobiles available for daily rentals.

Still, the decision was not an easy one to make, considering the family spent nearly $1 million to buy the business in 2021.

“This was our entire life savings,” she said. “We invested everything we had into this business.”
