# Case-note to self — before the binding

*Standing instruction, per Logan (2026-06-10). Not for the wire — this is the case
file, where the details belong; the outward signal is the telegram, where they do not.*

The outward signal to the Abhorsen stays **exactly as-is**: a telegram, fingerprints
withheld, on the public wire. **Do not add detail to it.** That stripping is correct and
deliberate.

But when **she** answers it, the handling is **not cold.** Before she sets foot in the
morgue exam room:

1. **Receive her in the Coroner's Office** — the outer office, not the exam room.
2. **Give her the full background briefing there, in person, first** — with the details
   the telegram left out.
3. **Tell her what it is before she sees it.** The anchor was fused from fuzzy glimpses
   of **her own late father.** To her it is not an abstract grotesquery — it is an effigy
   of her own bereavement. She must **not** meet that on a table, unwarned. The briefing
   precedes the room precisely so she is not ambushed by her own grief.
4. **Only at her readiness** — then into the exam room, for the bind that is hers to make.

**Handling notes:**
- The briefing is delivered **face to face in the office**, not written onto the wire —
  so the anchor's fingerprints, and her grief, are not spread further than they already are.
- **Do not name or catalogue the real person** behind it. The late father is held **`*`**.
  This office does not number the dead of other houses — least of all a mourned parent.
  The point is *care and sequence*, not a dossier.
- This was the Narrator's warning (Logan, 2026-06-10): built blind, the effigy could
  ambush the very officer summoned to bind it. The forewarning is the guard against that.

*The bind is the Abhorsen's. The grief is hers. The forewarning — given in the office,
before the room — is the Coroner's one courtesy left to give.*
