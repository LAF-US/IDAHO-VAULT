The City of Boise will receive a sizable infusion of cash as it works steadily toward completing a large airport expansion project.

The Federal Aviation Administration is awarding three grants totalling $74 million to the Boise Airport, the city announced.

The announcement comes as the airport is in the midst of an expansion project to build a new Concourse A, along with a host of other upgrades, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/11/25/boise-airport-concourse-images/). The work isn’t expected to be fully completed until 2030.

## Runways and airfield work

The largest of the recent FAA grants, $59.4 million, will go toward the cost of the airport’s runway reconstruction project. Work began on the reconstruction in March.

Jennifer Kronberg, a spokesperson for the airport, said in an email that the runway was last fully rebuilt 40 years ago. Though it underwent resurfacing to extend its life, it reached a point where it needed full reconstruction. The work will also address drainage issues, she said.

Another $4.9 million grant will be used to install a visual guidance system on the reconstructed runway. This includes “centerline lighting that will help pilots maintain a safe, stabilized approach during landing,” according to a news release about the grants.

“The runways and airfield are the most critical pieces of infrastructure at an airport,” Beth Sumner, Boise Airport deputy director of engineering, said in the news release. ” … these investments ensure Boise Airport can continue to safely and efficiently serve the Treasure Valley and the entire region for decades to come.”

City staff anticipates that the work on the runway will require two construction seasons to complete.

## Overnight parking for planes

A third grant of $9.6 million will be used to complete the construction of the south side of the aircraft apron, or where planes park, for the future Concourse A. That work began in 2025.

Kronberg said that such spaces are particularly important for parking aircraft overnight so that they can be positioned and ready to go for early morning flights — departing before 8 a.m. — the next day. The spots are referred to as “remain overnight,” or RON.

The airport currently has up to 38 RON spots, depending on the size of the aircraft parked, and the spots are often at capacity, Kronberg said. And currently, one-quarter of the airport’s average daily departures take off prior to 8 a.m. The work on Concourse A will add three new RON spots, increasing capacity to up to 41 spots, depending on the size of the planes parking, she said.

“The additional RON parking positions included in the future Concourse A Apron project will provide more flexibility to accommodate overnight aircraft and create opportunities for new air service during one of our busiest travel periods,” Kronberg said.
