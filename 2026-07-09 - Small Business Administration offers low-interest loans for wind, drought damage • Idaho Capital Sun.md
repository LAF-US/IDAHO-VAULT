## Small Business Administration offers low-interest loans for wind, drought damage

Some eligible Idaho residents have until next year to apply for supportThousands could be eligible for loans from the Small Business Administration after straight-line winds and drought rocked Idaho. (Photo by Chip Somodevilla/Getty Images)

Homeowners, renters, businesses and nonprofits harmed by windstorms and earlier drought conditions can now apply for [loans from the Small Business Administration](https://lending.sba.gov/search-disaster/) to help replace and repair their property or other assets.

Last December, counties across the state experienced severe windstorms as gusts exceeding 80 miles per hour triggered widespread power outages. Now, residents affected by the storms in Idaho County and Shoshone County who need financial aid to restore or relocate homes and replace personal items are eligible for fixed-rate, low-interest loans from the SBA.

The SBA is also offering loans of up to $2 million for businesses and nonprofit organizations in Idaho and Shoshone counties to support routine financial obligations regardless of the level of physical damage they experienced.

Some counties in [south Idaho](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/06/01/sba-offers-relief-idaho-small-businesses-private-nonprofits-affected-drought) are also eligible for Economic Injury Disaster Loans through the SBA in recognition of Idaho Gov. Brad Little’s April drought declaration. The program is open to small businesses, private nonprofits, and small agricultural cooperatives that were impacted financially as a result of the disaster.

According to Natalie Butz, a spokesperson for SBA’s office of disaster recovery, these loans offer relief to a wide range of businesses, regardless of how they were physically affected by the weather disasters.

“Those are our most underutilized portion of our program, because it’s very easy for survivors to understand when they have sustained physical damages as a result of the disaster,” she said. “But economic injury is a little bit harder to pin down.”

Butz says that it takes many businesses six to nine months to experience the full economic consequences of a disaster and the impacts can arrive in unexpected ways.

Losses in revenue, cash flow interruptions, or supply chain issues are all common occurrences as a result of disasters, according to Butz.

[Additional disaster assistance](https://idahocapitalsun.com/briefs/disaster-declaration-approved-to-help-counties-in-north-idaho-after-storm-causes-5-9m-in-damages/) was announced last week, after U.S. President Donald Trump approved a major disaster declaration for counties in North Idaho affected by the windstorms. The damage amounted to $5.9 million, according to a press release announcing the approval.

## Counties that are eligible for loans and how to apply

**Physical disaster loans:**

Homeowners and renters in Idaho and Shoshone counties can receive up to $100,000 in loans to replace or repair personal property. Homeowners can borrow up to $500,000 for a damaged primary residence.

Business and nonprofit organizations in Idaho and Shoshone counties can receive up to $2 million for repairing and replacing damaged business assets.

The deadline to apply for physical disaster loans is Aug. 7.

**Economic injury disaster loans:**

Small businesses in the counties of Bannock, Bingham, Blaine, Butte, Camas, Cassia, Custer, Elmore, Franklin, Jerome, Lincoln, Minidoka, Oneida, Power, Idaho, and Shoshone can also apply for economic injury disaster loans. These loans which cap out at $2 million do not require physical damage as a result of disaster for eligibility.

Businesses in Bannock, Bingham, Blaine, Butte, Camas, Cassia, Custer, Elmore, Franklin, Jerome, Lincoln, Minidoka, Oneida, and Power counties have until Jan. 19, 2027, to apply. The deadline for Idaho and Shoshone counties is March 8, 2027.

To apply for a loan visit [sba.gov/disaster](http://sba.gov/disaster) or call 1-800- 659-2955
