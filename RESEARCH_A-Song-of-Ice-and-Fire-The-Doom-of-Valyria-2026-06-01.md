# Research: *A Song of Ice and Fire* — The Doom of Valyria

**Source:** Online research, cross-checked.
**Compiled by:** Claude Code, 2026-06-01, on branch `claude/song-ice-fire-sCaAD`.
**Companion to:** the geography-&-politics note (Essos geography; the Valyrian Freehold) and the religions note (the Faceless Men theory). This file investigates the single event that reshaped the world's history.

> **Provenance note (read first).** Fictional facts; "verification" = consistency with canon — the novels, *The World of Ice & Fire* (TWOIAF) and *Fire & Blood* (in-world "histories," deliberately unreliable). awoiaf/Wikipedia/Fandom returned **HTTP 403** to automated fetching, so claims rest on search-engine extracts. The single most important caveat: **the *cause* of the Doom is canonically unresolved — GRRM keeps it a mystery.** Competing in-world theories are flagged **[theory]**; deep-past myth is **[legend]**; book-vs-show divergence is **[show]**.

---

## What the Doom was

The **Doom of Valyria** was the volcanic supercataclysm that destroyed the **Valyrian Freehold** — the greatest civilization the world had known — in roughly a single day.

- The **Fourteen Flames** (the ring of fourteen volcanoes whose mines and dragons made Valyria's power) **all erupted at once.** GRRM's own analogy: "imagine Krakatoa, Vesuvius, Mount St. Helens, Etna, and Mauna Loa all erupting at the same time within a few dozen miles of one another." [awoiaf: Fourteen Flames]
- **Every hill for five hundred miles split open**, flinging molten rock a thousand feet into the air; **red clouds rained dragonglass (obsidian)**; earthquakes leveled palaces, temples, and towns; lakes boiled or turned to acid. **Dragons died in the air.** Every dragonlord family **still in Valyria** was destroyed. *(House Targaryen, a minor dragonlord house, had already removed to Dragonstone with its dragons before the Doom and so survived long-term; a few dragonlords outside Valyria outlasted the cataclysm only briefly — e.g. **Aurion**, who raised a host from Qohor to claim the empire and was never seen again.)* [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]
- The **Valyrian peninsula shattered into islands** and the sea rushed in, forming the **Smoking Sea.** [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria; Smoking Sea]

## When, and on what scale

- The Doom struck **~100 years before Aegon's Conquest** — and therefore **~400 years before the events of *A Game of Thrones***. Search extracts give the precise in-world date as **102 BC** (Before Conquest). [awoiaf: Valyrian Freehold]
- The **Freehold had dominated for ~5,000 years**, spanning most of Essos — undone in a day. ⚠️ *Dating reconciliation:* Aenar Targaryen's move to Dragonstone is dated **~114 BC** ("twelve years before the Doom"); 114 − 12 = 102, internally consistent. "100 years before the Conquest" is a round-number approximation.

## Aftermath — the land

- The **Smoking Sea** now covers the drowned peninsula — full of smoking stacks of rock, said to boil. ⚠️ *[legend]* The ruins are held **cursed and demon-haunted** — *"the Doom still rules Valyria"* — and sailors believe **none who sail there return.** Canonical reinforcements: **King Tommen II Lannister** sailed there with the Valyrian-steel sword **Brightroar** and never returned; **Gerion Lannister** (c. 292 AC) sailed to recover it and also vanished. [awoiaf: Smoking Sea]
- ⚠️ *[show]* The **TV series** sends **Tyrion and Jorah Mormont** through the **Smoking Sea / ruins of Valyria** by boat, where **Jorah** contracts greyscale. The **books** do *not* stage this at the Smoking Sea: in *A Dance with Dragons* Tyrion travels the **Rhoyne through the Sorrows** with Young Griff's party, and it is **Jon Connington** who catches greyscale saving Tyrion from the stone men. The "ghost grass at Valyria" idea could **not** be sourced — likely a conflation with other Essos lore; treat as unverified.

## Aftermath — the world: the Century of Blood

- The century following the Doom is the **Century of Blood** (the **Bleeding Years**) — ~100 years of anarchy as the decapitated Freehold collapsed: "not a single war but a period of general anarchy, a mad free-for-all in which alliances constantly shifted." [awoiaf: Century of Blood]
- It was the **formative period of the nine Free Cities** — the Valyrian colonies now cut loose. **Volantis** — the eldest colony — tried to **conquer the others and rebuild the Freehold under its rule**, and **failed when the other Free Cities united against it.** The **Ghiscari cities of Slaver's Bay** (Astapor, Yunkai, Meereen) were **not founded in this period**: heirs of Old Ghis, they long *predated* the Doom as Valyrian subject-cities, and now **reasserted their independence** and old Ghiscari ways. [awoiaf: Century of Blood]
- ⚠️ *Timing note:* the **Rhoynar** flight under **Nymeria** ("ten thousand ships," → Dorne) responded to Valyria's earlier **conquest of the Rhoyne**, *before* the Doom — Nymeria routed her fleet to avoid Volantis and the dragonlords. (Historian Beldecar argues "ten thousand" is inflated.) Related to Valyrian power, but not to the Doom itself. [awoiaf: Nymeria]

## Why House Targaryen survived

- The **Targaryens were a relatively *minor* dragonlord family** among the ~40 great houses. [awoiaf: Valyrian Freehold]
- **Twelve years before the Doom**, **Daenys the Dreamer** (daughter of Lord **Aenar Targaryen** of Dragonstone) had a **prophetic dream** of Valyria's fiery destruction. Heeding her, Aenar **sold his Freehold holdings (~114 BC) and moved the whole family — and their five dragons, including Balerion — to Dragonstone**, the Freehold's western outpost in the narrow sea. [awoiaf: Daenys Targaryen]
- So House Targaryen **alone of the dragonlords survived** — the hinge on which all later Westerosi history turns, and the root of the canonical theme of prophetic **"dragon dreams"** in the Targaryen line. [awoiaf: Dreams and prophecies]

## What caused it? — the competing theories

**The cause is canonically unresolved, by design.** *The World of Ice & Fire* is narrated by **Maester Yandel**, who works from fragmentary sources and lists rival explanations rather than settling on one — "natural disaster, sorcerous overreach, divine vengeance, political intrigue, the calamity of dragons, or facechanging assassins, in any combination." The unreliable-narrator framing is the point: no one in-world can know. [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]

**Theory A — Natural volcanic calamity** *(the "common" / orthodox view).*
TWOIAF records that it is *commonly believed* the Doom was simple geology: the Fourteen Flames, an unstable volcanic region Valyria was built among, erupted. This is the default popular explanation — no magic required. [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria; Fourteen Flames]

**Theory B — Faltering magic (the Septon Barth thread)** *(the load-bearing scholarly theory).*
TWOIAF reports a minority of maesters, drawing on fragments of Septon Barth, who held that Valyria had used sorcery to tame the Fourteen Flames for thousands of years — and that its endless appetite for slaves and wealth served as much to *sustain* those spells as to expand the Freehold. On this reading, when the magic at last faltered the cataclysm became inevitable: sorcery had held the volcanoes in check, and the slave-and-conquest economy existed partly to *fuel* it — so when it failed, the Doom followed. [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]

- **Why this theory survives only as fragments:** Barth's *Dragons, Wyrms, and Wyverns: Their Unnatural History* was condemned by the Citadel as *"provocative but unsound,"* and **King Baelor I "the Blessed" ordered it expunged and destroyed**. (⚠️ My earlier prompt's "Baelor Breakspear" was a conflation — the book-burning is **Baelor I Targaryen**.) Barth also speculated the Valyrians *bred dragons by bloodmagic* — reinforcing the picture of Valyrian power as fundamentally sorcerous. [awoiaf: Barth; Dragons, Wyrms, and Wyverns]

> **Who was Septon Barth?** *(the source the whole theory rests on)*
> A **septon** (priest of the Seven) and a **blacksmith's son** — low-born, self-taught, and one of the most brilliant minds the realm produced. He served as **Hand of the King to Jaehaerys I Targaryen** (the Conciliator) for **~four decades**, and the long Targaryen golden age of peace and good governance is credited largely to his administration — so his magical speculation comes from the most capable royal minister in Westerosi history, not a crank. His notorious treatise *Dragons, Wyrms, and Wyverns: Their Unnatural History* argued dragons were **bred by Valyrian blood magic** and explored the "higher mysteries" (sorcery) — edging into heresy for a septon. After his death the pious **Baelor I** had his works **burned**, so he survives only in fragments and later-maester quotation. He is GRRM's device for planting the *real* lore (magic is real; dragons were made; the Doom may have been magical) while keeping it deniable — the figure most often **right about what the establishment dismisses**, and a pointed embodiment of the series' unreliable-narrator theme: the man who likely understood Valyria is the one whose books the realm chose to erase. ⚠️ Book/*TWOIAF*/*Fire & Blood* canon; dates via search-extracted awoiaf (wikis blocked direct fetch). [awoiaf: Septon Barth; Jaehaerys I Targaryen]
>
> **Inverted epithets — reading past the names** *(⚠️ interpretation, on canon facts).*
> The Doom's lost testimony is guarded by two epithets the Faith handed out — and the facts turn both upside-down:
>
> - **Septon Barth → "the Heretic":** the wisest man of his age, condemned for studying the higher mysteries and writing that dragons were *made* — i.e., for being **right about what the establishment denied**.
> - **Baelor I → "the Blessed":** sainted king (the **Great Sept of Baelor** bears his name) whose piety in practice **burned Barth's books**, **imprisoned his own sisters** in the Maidenvault, installed absurd High Septons (a stonemason; reputedly a boy of eight), and ended in a **devotional fast that starved him to death** heirless at ~27.
>
> So the realm **damned the man who wrote the knowledge and sainted the man who burned it.** Epithets here are **propaganda fossils** — compare honestly-named *Maegor the Cruel* (named by enemies) with flattering *Baelor the Blessed* (named by beneficiaries): the title records who won the argument, not what happened. The casualty in the middle is the truth about Valyria — which is exactly why the Doom's cause is, and stays, unknowable. [awoiaf: Baelor I Targaryen; Septon Barth]

**Theory C — Political intrigue / assassinated fire-mages** *(in-world, recorded).*
TWOIAF notes the great dragonlord families were in constant conflict, raising the possibility that rival houses **assassinated the sorcerers whose spells held back the Fourteen Flames** — a political route to the same magical failure as Theory B. [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]

**Theory C′ — The Faceless Men** *(⚠️ fan extrapolation, NOT a recorded scholarly position).*
The popular fandom version pins Theory C specifically on the **Faceless Men** — slaves who originated in the Valyrian mines, taking vengeance / serving their death-god by killing the binding-mages. TWOIAF only gestures at "facechanging assassins" as *one of many possibilities* the narrator entertains; it is **semi-canon speculation at most**, never confirmed by text or author. → cross-ref the religions note's House of Black and White subsection. [ASOIAF University; awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]

**Theory D — Divine punishment** *(the Faith's view).*
TWOIAF records that *some septons, "less wise"* (the narrator's loaded gloss), hold the Valyrians were punished for worshipping "a hundred gods and more" and that "in their godlessness they delved too deep and unleashed the fires of the seven hells." This is the textual home of the **hubris / "delved too deep"** motif. [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]

**Theory E — Other recorded framings.**
TWOIAF also lists the **curse of Garin the Great** (the Rhoynar prince whose people Valyria destroyed) and the **fire of R'hllor** (a Lord-of-Light framing). [awoiaf: Doom of Valyria]

**Adjacent canon (a bridge, not a stated cause): magic wanes with dragons.**
In *A Clash of Kings*, the pyromancer Hallyne recalls that "magic had begun to go out of the world the day the last dragon died." Fans build a "lost dragons → magic waned → spells failed → Doom" chain from this — but ⚠️ the chronology runs the *other* way (dragons survived the Doom), so applying it to the Doom is **fan extrapolation**, even though the dragons↔magic linkage itself is canon. [awoiaf: Dragon]

> **Bottom line:** Theory A (natural) is the popular default; Theory B (Barth's faltering magic) is the most textually developed; C/C′/D/E are the recorded alternatives, with the Faceless Men version being fandom-favored but unconfirmed. TWOIAF deliberately refuses to adjudicate — and there is no indication GRRM intends to resolve it.

## Legacy

- **Valyrian steel:** the secret of **forging new** Valyrian steel was **lost in the Doom** — none can be made anew; existing blades can only be **reforged** (the master-smiths of **Qohor** are among the few who can rework it). [Fandom: Valyrian steel]
- **Lost knowledge:** dragon-binding/taming lore, sorcery, and much recorded learning perished; surviving dragon lore is why the Targaryens kept dragons.
- **Language:** **High Valyrian** became a dead "high" tongue, surviving as the **bastard Valyrian** of the Free Cities.
- **Relics:** the indestructible **Valyrian roads** still mark the Freehold's old reach.
- **Theme:** *"The Doom still rules Valyria"* — the fall is the series' great meditation on **the impermanence of even the mightiest empire**: 5,000 years undone in a day, its true cause lost to history.
