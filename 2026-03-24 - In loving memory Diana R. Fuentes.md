#### Sept. 27, 1958 — March 20, 2026

Diana R. Fuentes, a glass ceiling breaker who was the first person of color to lead Investigative Reporters and Editors, died unexpectedly on March 20, 2026, on a trip to Washington, D.C. Her passing is an incalculable loss for so many — Texas journalists, women in journalism, journalists of color, and all who were lucky enough to know and love her.

Diana, or Dee Dee to so many, leaves an indelible mark on the industry and her people: fellow journalists, friends, family and staff — many of whom were all four.

Her sudden passing received a groundswell of support not just for IRE and its staff, but also for efforts to honor Diana as only she deserved. In just a few hours, the IRE ![](https://www.ire.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2420/2026/03/crystallite.jpg.webp) community raised nearly three times the amount required to induct her into [IRE's prestigious Ring of Honor](https://www.ire.org/awards/ring-of-honor/ring-of-honor-recipients/diana-r-fuentes/); a scholarship has been created in her name at Texas State University; and sister journalism organizations around the globe continue to uplift Diana, her legacy and her impact on the industry.

![](https://www.ire.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/2420/2026/03/Image-from-iOS-2048x2048.jpg.webp) A powerhouse journalist and fierce advocate for a free, diverse and ethical press, Diana was more than anything a woman of tremendous strength and tremendous kindness and spirit. Her laugh was infectious, her determination was ever present, and her belief in the good in people was unwavering. We honor Diana every day as we work to be more kind, more empathetic, and more committed to the important work we do.

We will miss you, Dee Dee. ❤️🩹

## Notes from friends and colleagues

###### Kelley Shannon, Freedom of Information Foundation of Texas

Fuentes made a deep impact on journalism during her lengthy news career and on open government in her many years with the Freedom of Information Foundation of Texas as a board member and president. She was committed to a free and robust press and the people’s right to know. And, she was a kind, loving and fun person.

###### Laura Moscoso, IRE & NICAR

Named after the Roman goddess of the jungle, hunting, the guardian of the crossroads. We say goodbye to you during the vernal equinox: your physical transition mixed together with the turning of the season. Now you'll be with us every time something blossoms. I get comfort in that. Gracias, Diana. Ya nos veremos en el otro barrio, con un tequilita.

###### Jen Guadarrama

When she came to the Del Rio News-Herald as publisher, she was met with a managing editor who had 0 college. In fact, I had a GED. Expecting to be fired when she arrived, I'd basically packed up my desk. She didn't even flinch at my lack of experience. She embraced me, got me training and then encouraged me to go bigger. I absolutely would not be where I am without that guidance and encouragement.

###### Rhiannon Meyers Collette, Arnold Ventures

We admired DeeDee tremendously. Beyond her leadership and her ability to inspire really great investigative reporting through IRE, she was just such a phenomenal human being — so kind, so thoughtful. Always putting others before herself. I don’t think I ever heard her say an unkind word about anyone. She was the epitome of grace and — in an industry ripe with cynicism and grumpiness — DeeDee was a breath of fresh air. I will miss her friendship deeply.

###### Richmond Acheampong, PhD

The world lost a beacon of courage and integrity in Diana R. Fuentes. Her guidance was swift, her generosity profound, touching lives across continents. I will forever remember her kindness in offering a door where none seemed open. Her legacy in journalism and mentorship will inspire generations yet unborn. May her spirit rest in peace, and her impact live on in all she has touched.

Diana's passion for investigative journalism and equity was so evident, and we were lucky to have her as a champion for our causes.

She was supportive and optimistic in an industry that desperately needs both. I will miss her tremendously, and I will seek to bring her spirit with me to this work each and every day.

###### The Center for Collaborative Investigative Journalism

Diana was a staunch supporter of our work at CCIJ. She contributed to our annual NewsMatch fundraisers on multiple occasions, and spent meaningful time with our team members at the 2023 Global Investigative Journalism Conference in Gothenburg, Sweden. This included attending a networking event we held during which Nigerian journalist Damilola Ayeni spoke about his period of being held by Beninese authorities for doing environmental investigative work. CCIJ worked with other journalism organizations to secure his release. The session aligned neatly with Diana’s passion and unflagging advocacy for journalist freedom and safety.

###### Tom Koch

Diana brought me back to IRE and my commitment to investigative work even if my newspapering days were done. With her and at her suggestion I funded a program that would provide funds for reporters who, today, were interested in public health reportage. This year the first two stories done with those monies will be presented. Her support of me and of investigative work was an inspiration and one that created the Koch Continuum for public health reportage. And that's something I'll continue to support in her name and with recognition of her contribution to not simply investigative journalism and reportage but to me. I will miss her.

[Source ↗](https://www.muckrock.com/news/archives/2026/mar/23/remembering-diana-fuentes/)

###### Michael Morisy, Muckrock

She was an inspiring presence, representing not just IRE but the power of a free press and an informed public. She consistently championed the right to know, the right to report and the need for newsrooms to better reflect their communities. She brought her energy and optimism to bear on strengthening and expanding the community of journalists, as well as helping make sure that the next generation had access to the resources, training and support they need to do their work ethically and effectively.

###### Saurav Rahman

It has only been 19 months since I first met her, in my role as a graduate research assistant at IRE Data Services. Dee Dee was a true inspiration to me; always kind, encouraging, and generous with her time. Whether I met her in Columbia at the Missouri School of Journalism, at the IRE Conference in New Orleans, or at NICAR in Minneapolis, she always made me feel supported and valued. I still remember the first time we met, she took me out for breakfast and made me feel welcome from the very beginning. The last time I saw her was at the School, just before a faculty meeting. I was so overjoyed to see her at the RJI building that I instinctively went in for a hug, and she warmly hugged me back. Later, I wondered if I had crossed a professional boundary. I carried that small guilt with me. But now, after hearing the news, I choose to believe it was a moment of grace; a final, human connection I was lucky to receive. Her kindness, leadership, and warmth will stay with me always.

###### Cheryl Philips

Dee Dee’s welcoming way was everpresent. She was always kind and so supportive of diversity and the staff. We will all feel this loss.

She was a mighty advocate for journalists and the absence of her light will continue to be felt for years to come.
