## Lutheran charity golden retrievers comfort Spokane Complex Fire evacuees as wildfires rage

### Golden retrievers Isaac and Fisher comfort Spokane wildfire evacuees and first responders, offering emotional support as destructive Eastern Washington fires burn.

**By Caleb McGever | FāVS News Reporter**

SPOKANE, Wash. – Isaac and Fisher, two purebred golden retrievers, sat outside the evacuation center with their handlers, smiling and wagging their tails at people forced to evacuate during the destructive Spokane Complex Fires.

The dogs and their handlers, who work as a part of Lutheran Church Charities K-9 Comfort Dogs program, had already spent a week visiting people affected by wildfires in Grand Coulee, Omak and Nespelum.

They arrived at the Spokane Convention Center on Sunday morning, where the American Red Cross had set up an evacuation shelter the day before.

## Comfort in a time of crisis

The dogs wore “pet me” vests and the team moved to different areas outside the shelter — sometimes sitting outside the front doors, other times moving around inside the center.

“Many of them, especially the ones who have lost homes, kinda drop to their knees and sit down and ‘yes I want to pet a dog,’” Julie Blyckert, Isaac’s “Top Dog” lead handler, said. She said one man at the convention center told the team his blood pressure went down the moment he started petting Isaac.

One of the program’s aims is to share Golden Retrievers’ comforting and calming presence to create a “bridge” for conversation.

“We’re there to hear their stories and just to be a support to them and to offer prayer if they want it, but mostly we’re just there to listen to their stories,” Blyckert said.

Isaac, Fisher and their Comfort Dog Teams don’t rest much. The week before responding to the Spokane Complex Fires, they traveled through the Colville Reservation, visiting people affected by the ongoing Kaiser Canyon and Modrite wildfires.

They travelled at the request of Zion Lutheran Church, which organized events around the area — including evacuation centers, state resource groups and a fire command center.

“There’s a lot of hurting people up there,” Gary Hutchens, one of Isaac’s handlers, said.

## Supporting communities and first responders

Hutchens said people at evacuation shelters approached them with all different demeanors: some chatty, some quiet.

One older woman who came up to pet Isaac during the visit didn’t say anything and pet him slowly with just one finger. Another visitor, a little girl whose dog went missing during their evacuation, spent “a long time” with the dog and didn’t want to leave, Hutchens said.

Later, the team visited first responders working to protect the community. Hutchens said their arrival was a surprise, but they ended up staying a long time.

“They didn’t know we were coming, but they were pretty excited. It’s been a pretty tough several weeks for them. They invited us into the firehouse, and we spent quite a bit of time there,” Hutchens said.

## The dogs’ work ethic

The dogs are local to the area and have been working in the community for years. Isaac is 8 years old and lives at Redeemer Lutheran Church in Spokane Valley. Fisher is 2 years old and lives at Christ the King Lutheran Church in Coeur d’Alene.

The dogs are trained from birth and work with eight-to-10 trained handlers at their local church. Blyckert said the training focuses on helping people’s hearts and mental health.

Hutchens said Isaac is out visiting people six days a week, two or three times a day. The local Comfort Dog team tends to visit veterans, homeless shelters, hospitals and other groups that would benefit from the happy presence of a golden retriever.

“We’re not there to share the gospel or to share what we believe in as much as we are there to be the hands and feet of Jesus and to be present and show them there’s someone who cares,” Hutchens said.

The [Spokane Complex Fires](https://favs.news/spokane-congregations-open-doors-wildfires-evacuations/), [Kaiser Canyon Fires](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/jul/22/the-colville-tribe-is-grieving-thousands-of-acres-/), [Modrite Fires](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/jul/31/like-a-volcano-erupted-inchelium-residents-working/) and [other destructive wildfires](https://www.king5.com/article/news/local/wildfire/list-washington-state-wildfires/281-b86ae358-b842-472c-b289-67e3f162491b) are still burning around Eastern Washington.
