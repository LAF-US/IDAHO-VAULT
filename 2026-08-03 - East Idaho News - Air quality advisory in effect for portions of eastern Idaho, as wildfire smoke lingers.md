IDAHO FALLS — As wildfires continue to burn in eastern and western Idaho, smoke is in the air, with some parts of our region experiencing unhealthy levels.

The Idaho Department of Environmental Quality has issued an air quality advisory for Bannock, Bingham, Caribou, Oneida and Power counties, as smoke has lowered the air quality to unhealthy levels.

Under this advisory, those counties are prohibited from open burning.

People in sensitive groups may experience health effects, while the general public may be less affected. DEQ also recommends that residents limit their time outside.

EastIdahoNews.com spoke with Jason Hart, airshed coordinator for Idaho DEQ, who said the smoke is coming from various parts of the northwest U.S. The main source of the smoke is Oregon, Washington and eastern Idaho.

The current advisory is expected to last until Wednesday, and Hart said that he expects the smoke to linger in the region for the next few days.

DEQ is expected to update its air quality advisory on Tuesday morning.

Idaho Falls and Rexburg are in the moderate range for air quality. Both cities are expected to have patchy smoke until Tuesday evening, according to the National Weather Service in Pocatello.

As eastern Idaho enters a cold dry front, Hart said the weather system is changing how quickly the smoke moves through the air.

He said that, unlike last week, when the smoke dissipated as quickly as it arrived, this new weather pattern is keeping the smoke here.

NWS Meteorologist Jack Messick told EastIdahoNews.com last Friday that, with this dry cold front moving into the area, aside from intense winds and low humidity, it will also lower temperatures.

According to a Facebook post by the NWS, temperatures in eastern Idaho will remain in the low to high 80s Monday and Tuesday, with temperatures reaching back into the low 90s by Wednesday evening.

While this break in the hot temperatures seen over the weekend will be a reprieve for residents of eastern Idaho, the hot temperatures on Saturday broke records for Pocatello, Idaho Falls, Burley and Challis.

Pocatello saw the hottest day at 103 degrees; Idaho Falls reached 101 degrees; and Burley and Challis both reached 100 degrees.
