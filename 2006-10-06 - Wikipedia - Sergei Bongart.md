**Serhii Romanovich Bongart** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language "Ukrainian language"): Сергій Романович Бонгарт; 1918–1985) was an American painter. Bongart is admired for his richly colored and emotionally expressive [landscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_art "Landscape art"), [still lifes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Still_life "Still life") and [portraits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portrait "Portrait"). He was best known as a colorist, working in exaggerated color, using dynamic but carefully controlled color relationships and extolling the virtues of approaching painting as "color first, subject last".[^2]

## Early life and education

Bongart was born in [Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv "Kyiv") in [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine "Ukraine"). He studied art in Kyiv, [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague "Prague"), [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna") and [Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich "Munich"), before emigrating to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States") in 1948. Bongart lived for six years in [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee "Memphis, Tennessee"), the location of his sponsor.

## Career

In 1954 Bongart moved to [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles "Los Angeles") where he founded an art school. He taught a number of aspiring young painters who later became well-known, nationally collected American artists—among them: [Peter Liashkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Liashkov?action=edit&redlink=1 "Peter Liashkov (page does not exist)"), [Del Gish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Gish?action=edit&redlink=1 "Del Gish (page does not exist)"), [Sunny Apinchapong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunny_Apinchapong?action=edit&redlink=1 "Sunny Apinchapong (page does not exist)"), [Ron Lukus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Lukus?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ron Lukus (page does not exist)"), [Rulon Hacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rulon_Hacking?action=edit&redlink=1 "Rulon Hacking (page does not exist)"), [Melinda Miles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melinda_Miles?action=edit&redlink=1 "Melinda Miles (page does not exist)"), [Patricia LeGrande Bongart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_LeGrande_Bongart?action=edit&redlink=1 "Patricia LeGrande Bongart (page does not exist)"), [Susan Greaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Greaves?action=edit&redlink=1 "Susan Greaves (page does not exist)"), [James Dudley Slay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Dudley_Slay?action=edit&redlink=1 "James Dudley Slay (page does not exist)"), [Don Sahli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Sahli "Don Sahli"), [Ted Pressett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Pressett?action=edit&redlink=1 "Ted Pressett (page does not exist)"), [George Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Phillips_\(artist\)?action=edit&redlink=1 "George Phillips (artist) (page does not exist)"), Guido Frick, Ovanes Berberian, S.Burkett Kaiser and Nancy Seamons Crookston.

In 1969 he established an art school in Idaho. He lived half the year in [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California "Santa Monica, California") and the other half near [Rexburg, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rexburg,_Idaho "Rexburg, Idaho"). Much of his art reflects the rustic settings which reminded him of his homeland. A few weeks before his death Bongart got the last visit from one of his students. That happened in a Hospital near Geneva in Switzerland, where the hope was, to save his life with a cell therapy. The student was Guido Frick, a painter from Germany. He remembers his visit:” To see Sergei in this hospital bed was heartbreaking. He was half the man I met just a few months before at his last Idaho- workshop. He was so weak and fragile, he hardly could rise his arm to wave me to come in. Several pillows stabilized him, while halfway sitting in his bed. When I left, we both knew, this was the last time we have seen each other. On a stretcher he was brought back to Santa Monica and died there a few days later in a hospital”.

Bongart's work is featured in prominent museums, and has received many awards, including a 1982 Gold Medal from the [National Cowboy Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cowboy_Hall_of_Fame "National Cowboy Hall of Fame") for his oil painting entitled "Spring Evening." In 1968, he was elected into the [National Academy of Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Design "National Academy of Design") as an Associate member.[^3]
