POST FALLS, Idaho — Post Falls will not use Flock camera systems, Mayor Randy Westlund said in a statement Friday addressing recent public concern over surveillance technology.  
  
However, Westlund clarified that the city does use a more limited license plate reader system.

In the statement, Westlund said he is "firmly opposed to unconstitutional mass surveillance and the construction of large national databases which track the movement of citizens."

The mayor said the city instead uses Motorola cameras that read license plates and check them against the National Crime Information Center's wanted list. He said the system is used in cases involving stolen vehicles, abducted children and vehicles connected to active warrants. The city also collaborates with federal law enforcement, including U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, in those cases.

The city owns the cameras and the data collected, which is never shared commercially, according to Westlund. He said every use of the system is tied to an active case and that access is regularly audited "to prevent abuse." The system does not track the movement of law-abiding residents and is configured only for real-time notification against active wanted lists.

Westlund also raised concerns about state data retention requirements. Idaho law currently requires cities to keep license plate reader images for a minimum of two years, and he said some jurisdictions retain them indefinitely. The mayor called on state legislators to amend the law to require deletion of the data within 90 days, calling the current retention requirement "a glaring flaw in our state code."

"I believe this is wrong, and opens the door to myriad privacy concerns," Westlund said of the state's retention rules.

The mayor said the city plans to publish its full policies and procedures around its camera use in the coming days.

"We are committed to enforcing the law without ever spying on citizens," Westlund said. "Technology may be a tool, but never a crutch. We will never have a surveillance state in Post Falls."

Dozens gathered at Atlas Waterfront Park on Saturday for a vigil honoring two men killed during two separate ICE enforcement operations.  
  
The Coeur d'Alene, Idaho vigil for Lorenzo Salgado Araujo and Joan Sebastian Guerrero was held as part of the national "ICE Out Day of Action" demonstration.  
  
The event was organized by North Idaho Indivisible, who say Salgado Araujo and Guerrero's deaths should not be forgotten.  
  
"This event, in my understanding, is just to remember the people that have been killed because of the ICE operations around the country," Tom Husby, a vigil attendee, said. "It seems pretty over the top that anybody should have to die."  
  
A bell was rung at the vigil to honor every person who died in ICE custody during the first 500 days of President Donald Trump's second term.  
  
North Idaho Indivisible focused on Salgado Araujo and Guerrero as they were the most recent people to have been shot and killed in ICE enforcement operations. Salgado Araujo died in Houston, Texas on July 7, 2026. Guerrero died in Biddeford, Maine on July 13.  
  
Both shootings remain under investigation.  
  
"The vigils help ensure that Lorenzo's and Johann's killings do not fade from public attention," Juli Celebert, an organizer of North Idaho Indivisible, said. "They send a clear moral message."  
  
The message of the national "ICE Out Day of Action" demonstrations is to call for greater accountability following the shootings of Salgado Araujo and Guerrero.  
  
Some gathered at the vigil to honor the men, and others hoped the event, and those like it, would lead to a larger national conversation about immigration enforcement.  
  
"We need to be a kinder nation, a gentler nation," Mike, a volunteer for North Idaho Indivisible, said. Mike asked KREM 2 not to use his last name due to security concerns. "We just ask everyone who has an opinion about it either way to come together and talk about it with your neighbors and friends. There's got to be a consensus about how we handle the situation before us."

*KREM 2 reached out to U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement for comment regarding today's nationwide "Ice Out Day of Action," and as of publication, we have not received a response.*
