An Idaho artist who was unable to hear, speak, read, or write found his own way of communicating and documenting his life in Idaho.

James Castle was born in Garden Valley, Idaho in 1899 and went to the School for the Deaf and the Blind in Gooding.

In 1931, James moved to a house on the outskirts of Boise – located at what’s now the intersection of Eugene Street and Castle Drive in Northwest Boise.

“James Castle had six siblings. He would often go into the upstairs attic loft to draw and make art privately,” [The Boise Art Museum notes.](https://www.boiseartmuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/James-Castle-Pre-Visit-Packet-2016.pdf) “He created art based on his life, where he grew up, his family, experiences, and memories, so his art is largely autobiographical.”

Castle also didn’t use pencils or other art supplies – but instead made his own – using soot mixed with saliva and sticks.

“He developed his own system of expression and communication through the artwork he created on a daily basis,” the BAM notes. “He used found materials and fashioned his own art tools and media.”

In the early 1950s, Castle was recognized when his nephew showed his uncle’s drawings to instructors at the Museum Art School in Portland, Oregon. From then on, his work was displayed at art exhibitions throughout the US – including at the Boise Gallery of Art (now the Boise Art Museum) in 1976. Castle passed away in 1977.

“At that time, overwhelmed by the constant demand to display his works, his family cut off public access to Castle’s oeuvre for some 20 years,” an article in the [Smithsonian Magazine notes.](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smart-news/eleven-new-artworks-found-walls-jame-castles-house-180968834/#:~:text=Webb%20reports%20that%20after%20a,for%20squirreling%20away%20his%20artworks.)

However, in 1998, the Outsider Art Fair in New York City displayed his work – recognizing his unique methods for creating his art.
In 1996, 150 pieces of Castle’s art were found in the ceiling of his house where he lived for several decades. And then in 2018, when the City of Boise renovated Castle’s home, 11 of his pieces were found stuffed in the walls.

“Castle was extraordinarily skilled at this unusual process, masterfully teasing out each desired tone to create nuanced pictures of his homes in Boise, Star, and Garden Valley,” t [he James Castle House website notes.](https://www.jamescastlehouse.org/about-james-castle/biography/) “Castle’s attention to detail in these works is remarkable given how often they were based on memory rather than direct observation.”

Today, the City of Boise’s Department of Arts & History owns and manages the James Castle House.

“We would also welcome you for a guided tour of the house, including the gallery where a new exhibition *[James Castle: Foundations](https://www.jamescastlehouse.org/whats-on/exhibitions/)* is currently on view, the studio space where [Resident Jenna Inzetta](https://www.jamescastlehouse.org/residencies/the-participants/) is currently working, and the outbuildings, including [James Castle’s historic shed](https://www.jamescastlehouse.org/about-james-castle/the-shed/),” Boise City Department of Arts & History’s Jennifer Yribar said.

In addition to guided tours, the house is free to visit and open to the public Thursdays through Saturdays from 11 am – 5 pm.

“Public interest in his art has grown exponentially in the last twenty-five years, including with recent, major exhibitions in Philadelphia, Washington, D.C., and Madrid,” The James Castle House website notes. “As people around the world experience Castle’s art, they engage with his exceptional vision of life in this place.”

Along with visiting the James Castle House, the Boise Art Museum has a permanent collection of his work.
