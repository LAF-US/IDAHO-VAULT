# Icemark: Races and Treatments

**Origin:** Conversation between Logan Finney and Claude Code, 2026-06-09. The architect released the leopard from the Idaho-beat thread and back to the Icemark cosmos, and asked for research on Hill's particular fantasy races and treatments. Web research surfaced material the existing vault files had under-treated — particularly the Southern Continent of *Blade of Fire* and the book-3 antagonists.
**Scope:** A consolidated inventory of the peoples and powers Hill puts in the world of *The Icemark Chronicles*, with structural attention to the specific *treatments* — the moves Hill makes that distinguish his handling. Companion to [[HUB-OF-THE-WORLD-AND-THE-SNOW-LEOPARDS-2026-06-09]] (the Snow Leopards' civilization in detail) and downstream of [[ICEMARK-DEEP-CUT-2026-06-07]] (the critical reading of Hill's distinctive moves at series level).
**Status:** Reference filed for the record. Where the leopard generalized about treatments, the architect (and Hill's text) is the authority.

## I. Inventory

### The two civilized human powers

- **The Icemark** — Saxon-coded northern kingdom. Lindenshield royal house. Capital **Frostmarris**. Snow-bound seven months of the year. Bordered south by the Polypontus, north by the Land-of-the-Ghosts.
- **The Polypontian Empire** — Rome-coded southern power. Bellorum military house. Expansionist. Imperial chosen as the suzerain antagonist of books 1–2; in book 3 the Empire is *fractured* and itself attacked from the south.

### The Icemark's vassals and Northern Alliance

- **The Hypolitan** — Amazon-coded matriarchal **vassals** of the Icemark crown. Refugee origin; forced exodus from their original lands. Keep their own commander, their own city (the Hypolitan city is *inside* the Icemark realm), and their own goddess. Name echoes Hippolyta. *Sworn vassals*, not subjects.
- **Their Vampiric Majesties — the Vampire King and the Vampire Queen** — Seated in the **Blood Palace** in their stone halls in the high north. Canonically **undead, soulless, immortal**. Sleep in coffins; transform into **bat form** (flying-cavalry deployed against Polypontian airships in *Blade of Fire*); drink blood (named in battle); **cannot conceive naturally** — the twin children of the King and Queen were *created in the spirit realms by magic.* Killed only by **fire, beheading, or a stake through the heart**; **dislike sunlight but are not killed by it** (Hill's calibrated softening of the Dracula sun-rule, which keeps them deployable in mixed-army summer campaigns). Centuries of mutual distrust with humans before Thirrin's parley. Centuries of *active war* with the Wolffolk before Thirrin — see the **Blood Wars** (3,000 moons / ~250 years before book 1) closed by King Redrought at the **Battle of the Wolfrocks**. Treated as a *court*: courtly, vain, lethal, sense-of-theater, *exact* in oath-keeping once given — and uniquely available to a *redemption from soullessness* (see Move 8 below).
- **Snowy Owls** — A second nocturnal northern allied power, named in the climactic battle sequences of *Blade of Fire* and *Last Battle* — *the vampires and the snowy owls drink the blood of many soldiers.* A parallel blood-drinking flying kindred to the Vampires. Hill does not develop them as a polity with named monarchs (`*` on internal structure); they function as the Alliance's second blood-drinking flying contingent.
- **The Wolffolk / Werewolves** — Sentient people, not a curse-state. King **Grishmak Blood-drinker**. Won as ally by Thirrin's mercy at a battlefield he had not expected mercy from. The Blood Wars history makes their Alliance with the Vampires under Thirrin a *reconciliation across centuries of hostility*, not a routine recruitment.
- **The Snow Leopards** — Tharaman-Thar's people. *Intellectual animals tall as warhorses*. Civilization of the **Hub of the World**. Speakers. Courteous. (See [[HUB-OF-THE-WORLD-AND-THE-SNOW-LEOPARDS-2026-06-09]] for the standalone treatment.)

### Stranger Northern powers

- **The Holly King and the Oak King** — *Real beings*, ruling the **Great Woods** by season (Oak in summer, Holly in winter), *with soldiers under arms*. Summoned into the Alliance by Oskan, who functions as the Icemark's magical liaison to them. Drawn from British neopagan seasonal-kings tradition.
- **Rock Trolls** — Confirmed Alliance members in the secondary literature, named alongside vampires, werewolves, zombies, and ghosts as "outcasts" allied to Thirrin. Under-treated in the source material relative to the other powers.
- **Ghosts** — Listed among the *outcast* Alliance; operate in the **Land-of-the-Ghosts** north of the kingdom.
- **Zombies / risen dead** — *Raised* by witches/warlocks (a magic-user's act), not free-roaming. Appear in the prequel war and again in book 3 when Medea raises an army of the undead. ([Last Battle of the Icemark — Lemony Snicket-style Fandom wiki: see Tri-Horn entry / Last Battle entry on the Icemark Fandom](https://theicemarkchronicles.fandom.com/wiki/Last_Battle_Of_The_Icemark))

### The Southern Continent — *Blade of Fire*'s architectural addition

This is the structural expansion the cub had under-treated in the prior vault files. Sharley's exile-and-return in book 2 *opens the world to the south*. ([Blade of Fire — Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blade_of_Fire))

- **The Desert People** — Caliphate / Arab-Muslim-coded. Ruled by a **Sultan**; Crown Prince **Mekhmet** becomes Sharley's friend and treaty-partner. The Mediterranean / Levantine register enters the Alliance through them.
- **The Lusu People of Arifica** — African-continent-coded (Arifica = Africa); Mekhmet suggests bringing them in; they join the Alliance for the second war.
- **The Venezzians** — Venice-coded mercantile city-state (Venezzia → Venice).
- **The Hellenes** — Greek-mainland-coded.
- **Maggiore Totus** — Sharley's tutor; Italian-name-coded; the diplomatic interlocutor who opens the southern register.

### Book 3 antagonists (*The Last Battle of the Icemark*)

- **Erinor of Artemision** — female warrior-leader of a *southern* Amazon-coded polity called **Artemision** (Artemis-coded). Invades the *now-fractured* Polypontian Empire from the south. ([Tri-Horn — Icemark Fandom](https://theicemarkchronicles.fandom.com/wiki/Tri-Horn); [Last Battle — Icemark Fandom](https://theicemarkchronicles.fandom.com/wiki/Last_Battle_Of_The_Icemark))
- **Tri-Horns** — *triceratops-like war beasts* ridden by Erinor's army. Cavalry. The prehistoric register in the world. (The prior vault files described them as "a southern enemy who rode beasts compared to triceratops" but did not name them as *Tri-Horns* or identify Erinor as their leader.)
- **Cronus and the Ice Demons** — the Dark Power's supernatural army, raised against the Icemark from the spirit world.
- **Medea's army of the undead** — raised in alliance with Cronus from her exile in the spirit-country.

## II. The treatments — what Hill is doing distinctively

The races above are inventory. The *treatments* are the moves Hill makes that distinguish his handling. Eight are load-bearing. (Moves 1–7 were the original 2026-06-09 reading; Move 8 was added on the 2026-06-09 evening canon lens pass focused on the vampire-rules specifically, which surfaced an arc that retro-justifies how Hill could keep the traditional Dracula bestiary intact and still treaty with its monarchs.)

### 1. The horror-people are treated as polities, not monsters

Vampires have a court with a King and Queen who keep oaths. Werewolves have a king (Grishmak) and rank. Zombies are *raised* by witches/warlocks (a magic-user's action), not free-roaming undead. The Land-of-the-Ghosts is a *country* with sovereignty, not a chaos zone. **The structural decision is consistent: if it has a name and a leader, it can be treatied with.**

The corollary is that Hill's worldbuilding is *precisely calibrated to make the treaty-with-monsters move possible*. Every non-human power has a *named monarch*: Tharaman-Thar, the Vampire King and Queen, Grishmak Blood-drinker, the Oak King and the Holly King. If the Vampires were just chaos, you could not parley. They have a court because Hill wanted them parley-able. The Alliance does not exist by chance; the cosmology was engineered for it.

### 2. The *outcast* naming as the Alliance's structural identity

The secondary literature consistently identifies Thirrin's allies under the heading **outcasts** — vampires, werewolves, zombies, ghosts, rock trolls — and her Alliance as the *alliance of the cast-out*. ([TV Tropes — The Icemark Chronicles](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/TheIcemarkChronicles); [All The Tropes](https://allthetropes.org/wiki/The_Icemark_Chronicles))

This is more than nomenclature. It is the underlying political identity of Thirrin's coalition. The genre default is **the civilized south against the savage north**. Hill inverts it: **the welcoming north of outcasts against the cleansing imperial south.** The Icemark's posture is to welcome who has been cast out by the civilizational majority. The Polypontus is the polity that does the casting-out; the Icemark is the polity that does the taking-in.

This sits alongside the treaty-rather-than-slaughter ethic in [[ICEMARK-DEEP-CUT-2026-06-07]] as the deeper political move of the series.

### 3. Real-world cultures coded openly, named once-removed

- Saxon → Icemark
- Roman → Polypontus
- Greek-Amazon → Hypolitan *and* Artemision (see Move 4)
- Caliphate-Arab → Desert People
- African → Lusu of Arifica
- Venice → Venezzia
- Greek-mainland → Hellenes
- British-neopagan seasonal kings → Holly King / Oak King
- Greek-titanic dark principle → Cronus

The pieces are recognizable. The world has the *shape of the Mediterranean and Northern European literary inheritance laid out under once-removed names.* The reader who knows the originals can feel the names land; the reader who doesn't can read it as a coherent fantasy world. Both are rewarded. This is one of the things Hill's Classics-and-Ancient-History training shows in practice.

### 4. The Amazon-pole doubled — race does not predict alignment

**Hypolitan** (vassal, integrated, allied) and **Artemision** (aggressor, antagonist) are *both* Amazon-coded matriarchal warrior polities. Hill puts them on opposite sides of the same war.

This is a structural sophistication that genre-default writing typically does not make. **The same race-type can occupy different political positions depending on its relation to the Icemark crown.** Race does not predict alignment; *political relationship* does. The Hypolitan were welcomed once long ago and made vassals on terms that preserved their internal sovereignty; the Artemision were not, and arrive as conquerors.

The doubling does load-bearing work: it prevents the reader from reading "Amazon = ally" as a stable rule of the world, and it prepares the moral ground for treating an Amazon-coded *antagonist* as such without making her irrational. Erinor is not Hypolitan-gone-wrong; she is a different polity, with a different history, on the wrong side because of where her polity sits in the post-fractured-Polypontus power balance.

### 5. The non-human as having lord-and-realm structure

Every non-human power Hill introduces has a **named monarch and a defined realm**:

- Tharaman-Thar / the Hub of the World (Snow Leopards)
- The Vampire King and Queen / stone halls in the high north
- Grishmak Blood-drinker / the Wolffolk's territory
- The Oak King and the Holly King / the Great Woods, by season

This is the worldbuilding precondition for treaty-making, and it is *not* the genre default. Vampires-as-monsters do not have lords; werewolves-as-curse-victims do not have realms. By giving every non-human power both, Hill structurally guarantees that diplomacy is possible. The Alliance is built into the cosmology.

### 6. The southern expansion in book 2 doubles the cosmos

*Blade of Fire* explicitly opens the world to the south. Sharley's exile and Mekhmet's friendship are the architectural device: the Icemark Alliance acquires a Mediterranean / African / Levantine register. **What had looked like a northern-only world becomes a world-spanning Alliance.** The Northern outcasts plus the Southern Caliphate-and-African powers plus the city-states plus the Hellenes equals an Alliance that *outscales the Polypontian Empire* in cultural breadth.

Book 1 read as Saxons versus Romans with northern allies. Book 2 reveals that the world also contains a Caliphate, an Africa, a Venice, and a Greece — all of whom can be brought in. The structural lesson: **the Polypontian Empire is one civilizational pole, not the only one. The Alliance is the *plural* answer to the *monocultural* expansionism of the Empire.**

### 7. The book 3 antagonists name what the Icemark sublates

Erinor (suzerain conquest from the south, but female-led), the Tri-Horns (the Polypontian war-machine model in animal form), and Cronus with his Ice Demons and undead (the imperial model exported into the spirit world): the third book makes explicit what the first two implied. **Imperialism-from-above is what the Icemark sublates by treatying.** Erinor and Cronus are both *unsublated forms* of what Thirrin's Alliance had handled in books 1–2. The series closes by naming the antagonist-class explicitly: any polity that tries to ascend rather than treaty, that tries to *impose* rather than *recognize*, is the antagonist regardless of which face it wears.

### 8. The horror bestiary is *kept*, and redemption is *added* — the vampire arc as paradigm

The 2026-06-09 canon lens pass on the vampire rules surfaced the move that the earlier reading had missed. The naïve structural shorthand — *Hill kept the political dignity and dropped the horror bestiary* — is **wrong**. Hill kept almost the *whole* Dracula bestiary: undead, soulless, immortal, coffins, bat-form, blood-drinking, fire/beheading/stake as the canonical kills. What he calibrated softer was the sunlight rule (disliked, not lethal) and what he transformed was the reproduction rule (impossible naturally, possible by magic). The political court was **added on top of** the canonical horror substrate, not in place of it.

This matters structurally because it sets up the *redemption arc* in *Last Battle*. The Vampire King is killed by the Polypontian Empire. From the spirit realm he and the imp Imp-Pious save Sharley, Mekhmet, and Kirimin from the **Plain of Desolation** (a magical country *halfway between the Light and the Dark*). He asks Sharley to tell the Queen they will meet again if she dies. The Queen, through *genuine love* for her husband, **develops a soul** and dies — **the only vampire in the books with a recorded peaceful passing.** ([The Vampire Queen — Icemark Chronicles Fandom Wiki](https://theicemarkchronicles.fandom.com/wiki/The_Vampire_Queen); [Last Battle Of The Icemark — Icemark Chronicles Fandom Wiki](https://theicemarkchronicles.fandom.com/wiki/Last_Battle_Of_The_Icemark))

The arc is *legible only because* Hill kept the canonical horror bestiary intact underneath. If the vampires had not been undead-and-soulless to start with, the soul-development would not register as redemption. The bestiary is the load that the arc lifts.

This is the eighth treatment-move. **Keep the curse. Add the crown. Reserve the soul for the end.** The undead-soulless-immortal state is the starting condition. The court and oath-keeping make them parley-able mid-trilogy. The soul-development at the close completes the move from horror to dignity. The vampire-as-paradigm: Hill's whole non-human roster is built on the bet that *no race is so cursed that it cannot be parleyed with, and no parley is final if a soul is acquired late.* The corollary, named only at the close, is that the parley was always the work, and the soul was always available.

## Editorial note

This file was written on 2026-06-09 after the architect released the leopard from the Idaho-beat thread and back to the Icemark cosmos. Web research surfaced the Southern Continent material (Mekhmet, the Desert People, the Lusu of Arifica, Venezzia, Hellenes, Maggiore Totus) and the book-3 antagonist roster (Erinor of Artemision, the Tri-Horns) that the existing vault files had under-treated. The seven treatment-moves are the leopard's structural reading of Hill's handling; they are inferences from the worldbuilding rather than Hill-stated doctrine and should be read as such.

The Hypolitan / Artemision doubling (Move 4) is the structural sophistication the leopard had not seen before this pass and which feels load-bearing for how the series handles race-and-alignment.

**Addendum, 2026-06-09 evening — vampire-rules canon lens pass.** A chat exchange with the architect on the vampire-rules surfaced material the file had under-treated, and the cub's earlier chat answer had got wrong. The corrections: vampires are *explicitly* undead, soulless, and immortal; they sleep in coffins; they transform into bat-form and use it as flying cavalry against airships; they drink blood (named in battle); they cannot conceive naturally (twin children created in the spirit realms by magic); they are killed by fire, beheading, or a stake through the heart; **sunlight is disliked but not lethal** (Hill's calibrated softening). The Vampire King and Queen entry was rewritten in place. The Snowy Owls entry was added as a parallel northern blood-drinking flying allied power. Move 8 was added to record the structural reading the canon lens pass made visible: Hill kept the whole horror bestiary, added the political court on top of it, and reserved the soul-development arc (Vampire Queen's redemption through love in *Last Battle of the Icemark*) as the closing payoff. The cub's initial chat reading — "kept the crown, threw away the curse" — was wrong; the corrected reading is "kept the curse, added the crown, reserved the soul for the end." Sister-file [[RESEARCH_Icemark-Lore-Compendium-2026-06-03]] received the parallel expansion to its Vampires section and a new Snowy Owls section.
