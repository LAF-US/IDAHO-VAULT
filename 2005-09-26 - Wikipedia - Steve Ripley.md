**Paul Steven Ripley** (January 1, 1950 – January 3, 2019) [^1] [^2] was an American recording artist, record producer, [songwriter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songwriter "Songwriter"), [studio engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audio_engineering "Audio engineering"), [guitarist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitarist "Guitarist"), and [inventor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inventor "Inventor"). He was also the leader/producer of [country rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_rock "Country rock") band [The Tractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tractors "The Tractors").

## Early life and education

Ripley was born in [Boise, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho"), but grew up in Oklahoma: he attended Glencoe High School in [Glencoe, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencoe,_Oklahoma "Glencoe, Oklahoma"), and graduated from [Oklahoma State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_State_University "Oklahoma State University").[^3]

## Career

Ripley's band Moses chose the name Red Dirt Records for their 1972 self-published live album; the first usage of [Red Dirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Dirt_\(music\) "Red Dirt (music)").[^4] And was an early marker for the Red Dirt movement.

Ripley worked as a studio musician, producer, and recording engineer, working with [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan "Bob Dylan"), playing guitar (on *[Shot of Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shot_of_Love "Shot of Love")*) and on the "Shot of Love" tour,[^5] with [J. J. Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Cale "J. J. Cale") (on *[Shades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shades_\(J._J._Cale_album\) "Shades (J. J. Cale album)")*, *[8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/8_\(J._J._Cale_album\) "8 (J. J. Cale album)")* and *[Roll On](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roll_On_\(J._J._Cale_album\) "Roll On (J. J. Cale album)")*), and he produced [Clarence "Gatemouth" Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_%22Gatemouth%22_Brown "Clarence \"Gatemouth\" Brown") and [Roy Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Clark "Roy Clark") (on *Makin' Music*) and [Johnnie Lee Wills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnnie_Lee_Wills "Johnnie Lee Wills") (on *Reunion*) and 20/20 (on “Sex Trap”).[^6] Dylan listed Ripley as one of his favorite guitarists.[^7]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/SteveRipley_OHC_Commerce.jpg/250px-SteveRipley_OHC_Commerce.jpg)

Ripley poses with the Oklahoma History Center Leon Russell mixer exhibit

In 1982, Ripley founded Ripley Guitars in [Burbank, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California "Burbank, California"). He created guitars for [Steve Lukather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Lukather "Steve Lukather"), [J. J. Cale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Cale "J. J. Cale"), [John Hiatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hiatt "John Hiatt"), [Ry Cooder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry_Cooder "Ry Cooder"), [Jimmy Buffett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Buffett "Jimmy Buffett") and [Eddie Van Halen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Van_Halen "Eddie Van Halen"), before moving to [Tulsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulsa "Tulsa") in 1987 and buying [Leon Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Russell "Leon Russell") 's former [recording studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recording_studio "Recording studio"), [The Church Studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_Studio "The Church Studio").[^8]

In 1994 he formed the country band, [The Tractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tractors "The Tractors"). He is the co-writer of the country hit " [Baby Likes to Rock It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_Likes_to_Rock_It "Baby Likes to Rock It") ".[^9]

In 2002, he created his own [record label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Record_label "Record label") (Boy Rocking Records) to produce artists including The Tractors, Leon Russell and The Red Dirt Rangers.[^10] In 2009, he produced and hosted a 20-part radio series on the history of Oklahoma [rock and roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_and_roll "Rock and roll"), that aired on Oklahoma public radio stations. It was entitled "Oklahoma Rock and Roll with Steve Ripley." [^11] In 2013 Ripley produced the album *Lone Chimney* by the Red Dirt Rangers.[^12]

Ripley was inducted into the Oklahoma Music Awards Red Dirt Hall of Fame along with Bob Childers and Tom Skinner at the ceremony for the First Annual Red Dirt Music Awards held on Sunday, November 9, 2003, at Cain's Ballroom in Tulsa.[^13]

In 2015, Ripley worked alongside staff at the [Oklahoma Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_Historical_Society "Oklahoma Historical Society") to create a "Church Studio" exhibit space at the [Oklahoma History Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_History_Center "Oklahoma History Center"). He remastered several of [Leon Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Russell "Leon Russell") 's songs that were available in a touchscreen kiosk that allowed a visitor to adjust individual instruments and vocal tracks to comprehend how [multitrack recording](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multitrack_recording "Multitrack recording") works.[^14]

In 2016 Ripley produced and curated a concert at Cain's Ballroom to celebrate the music and legacy of Bob Dylan.[^15]

After his death in 2019 Ripley was inducted into the Oklahoma Historians Hall of Fame by the Oklahoma Historical Society [^16] and also received the Restless Spirit Award from the Red Dirt Relief Fund.[^17]

On August 1, 2025, Ripley was posthumously inducted into the Oklahoma Music Hall of Fame.[^18]

## Death

Ripley died from cancer on January 3, 2019, two days after his 69th birthday, at his home in [Pawnee, Oklahoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pawnee,_Oklahoma "Pawnee, Oklahoma").[^1] [^19]
