Pound photographed in 1913 by Alvin Langdon Coburn

**Ezra Weston Loomis Pound** (30 October 1885 – 1 November 1972) was an [American poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_poets_from_the_United_States "List of poets from the United States") and critic, a major figure in the early [modernist poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_poetry "Modernist poetry") movement, and a [collaborator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany_and_Fascist_Italy "Collaboration with Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy") in [Fascist Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy") and the [Salò Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic "Italian Social Republic") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). His works include *[Ripostes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripostes "Ripostes")* (1912), *[Hugh Selwyn Mauberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Selwyn_Mauberley "Hugh Selwyn Mauberley")* (1920), and *[The Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cantos "The Cantos")* (c. 1915–1962).[^44]

Pound's contribution to poetry began in the early 20th century with his role in developing [Imagism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagism "Imagism"), a movement stressing precision and economy of language. Working in London as foreign editor of several American literary magazines, he helped to discover and shape the work of contemporaries such as [H.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.D. "H.D."), [Robert Frost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Frost "Robert Frost"), [T. S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._S._Eliot "T. S. Eliot"), [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway"), and [James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce "James Joyce"). He was responsible for the 1914 serialization of Joyce's *[A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Portrait_of_the_Artist_as_a_Young_Man "A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man")*, the 1915 publication of Eliot's " [The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Love_Song_of_J._Alfred_Prufrock "The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock") ", and the serialization from 1918 of Joyce's *[Ulysses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_\(novel\) "Ulysses (novel)")*. Hemingway wrote in 1932 that, for poets born in the late 19th or early 20th century, not to be influenced by Pound would be "like passing through a great blizzard and not feeling its cold".[^1]

Angered by the carnage of [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Pound blamed the war on [finance capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_capitalism "Finance capitalism"), which he called " [usury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usury "Usury") ".[^46] He moved to [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy "Kingdom of Italy") in 1924 and through the 1930s and 1940s promoted an economic theory known as [social credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_credit "Social credit"), wrote for publications owned by the British fascist [Oswald Mosley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Mosley "Oswald Mosley"), embraced [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini") 's [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism "Italian fascism"), and expressed support for [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler"). During [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"), Pound recorded hundreds of paid [radio propaganda broadcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Pound's_radio_broadcasts,_1941%E2%80%931945 "Ezra Pound's radio broadcasts, 1941–1945") for the [fascist Italian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_Italy "Fascist Italy") and its later incarnation as a German [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic "Italian Social Republic"), in which he attacked the United States government, [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt"), Britain, [international finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_finance "International finance"), the arms industry, [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews "Jews"), and others as abettors and prolongers of the war. He also praised both [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics") and the [Holocaust in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_in_Italy "Holocaust in Italy"), while urging American [G.I.s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I. "G.I.") to throw down their rifles and surrender. In 1945, Pound was captured by the [Italian Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement "Italian resistance movement") and handed over to the [U.S. Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army "U.S. Army") 's [Counterintelligence Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterintelligence_Corps "Counterintelligence Corps"), who held him pending extradition and prosecution based on an indictment for [treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason_in_the_United_States "Treason in the United States"). He spent months in a U.S. military detention camp near [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa "Pisa"), including three weeks in an outdoor steel cage. Ruled [mentally unfit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non_compos_mentis "Non compos mentis") to stand trial, Pound was incarcerated for over 12 years at [St. Elizabeths Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Elizabeths_Hospital "St. Elizabeths Hospital") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.")

While in U.S. military custody in Italy, Pound continued work on *The Cantos.* This new work was published by [New Directions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Directions_Publishing "New Directions Publishing") in 1948 as *[The Pisan Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pisan_Cantos "The Pisan Cantos")*, for which Pound was awarded the [Bollingen Prize for Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollingen_Prize "Bollingen Prize") in 1949 by the American [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress"), leading to enormous controversy. After a campaign by his fellow writers, he was released from St. Elizabeths in 1958 and returned to Italy, where he posed for the press giving the [Fascist salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascist_salute "Fascist salute") and called the United States "an insane asylum". Pound remained in Italy until his death in 1972. His economic and political views have ensured that his life and literary legacy remain highly controversial.

## Early life and education (1885–1908)

### Family background

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c0/Thaddeus_C._Pound_-_Brady-Handy.jpg/250px-Thaddeus_C._Pound_-_Brady-Handy.jpg)

Thaddeus Coleman Pound, Pound's paternal grandfather, in the late 1880s

Pound was born in 1885 in [a two-story clapboard house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer_Pound_House "Homer Pound House") in [Hailey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailey,_Idaho "Hailey, Idaho") in the [Idaho Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Territory "Idaho Territory") as the only child of Homer Loomis Pound and Isabel Weston,[^47] who married in 1884.[^48] Homer had worked in Hailey since 1883 as registrar of the [United States General Land Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_General_Land_Office "United States General Land Office").[^47] [^6] Ezra Pound's paternal grandfather, [Thaddeus Coleman Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaddeus_C._Pound "Thaddeus C. Pound"), a [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_\(United_States\) "Republican Party (United States)") member of the [U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._House_of_Representatives "U.S. House of Representatives") and the 10th [lieutenant governor of Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_lieutenant_governors_of_Wisconsin "List of lieutenant governors of Wisconsin"), had secured Homer the job. Homer had previously worked for Thaddeus in the lumber business.[^7]

Both sides of Pound's family had emigrated from [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England") in the 17th century. On his father's side, the immigrant ancestor was John Pound, a [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker "Quaker") who had arrived from England around 1650.[^48] Ezra's paternal grandmother, [Susan Angevine Loomis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loomis_Homestead "Loomis Homestead"),[^8] married Thaddeus Coleman Pound.[^50] On his mother's side, Pound was descended from [William Wadsworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wadsworth_\(patriarch\) "William Wadsworth (patriarch)"), a [Puritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritanism "Puritanism") who had immigrated to [Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston "Boston") on the *[Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_ship_Lion_\(1557\) "English ship Lion (1557)")* in 1632 and whose son Joseph Wadsworth helped to write the [first Connecticut constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Orders_of_Connecticut "Fundamental Orders of Connecticut").[^9] The Wadsworths married into the Westons of New York; Harding Weston and Mary Parker were Pound's maternal grandparents.[^48] After serving in the military, Harding remained unemployed, so his brother Ezra Weston and Ezra's wife, Frances Amelia Wessells Freer (Aunt Frank), helped to look after Isabel, Pound's mother.[^10]

### Early education

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fe/Ezra_Pound_and_Isabel_Pound_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Ezra_Pound_and_Isabel_Pound_%28cropped%29.jpg)

In his Cheltenham Military Academy uniform with his mother, 1898

Isabel Pound was unhappy in Hailey and took Ezra with her to New York in 1887 when he was 18 months old.[^11] Her husband followed and found a job as an [assayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgical_assay "Metallurgical assay") at the [Philadelphia Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Mint "Philadelphia Mint"). After a move to 417 Walnut Street in [Jenkintown, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenkintown,_Pennsylvania "Jenkintown, Pennsylvania"), in 1893 the family bought a six-bedroom house at 166 Fernbrook Avenue in nearby [Wyncote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyncote,_Pennsylvania "Wyncote, Pennsylvania").[^48] Pound's education began in [dame schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dame_school "Dame school"): Miss Elliott's school in Jenkintown in 1892 and the Heathcock family's Chelten Hills School in Wyncote in 1893.[^48] Known as "Ra" (pronounced "Ray"), he attended Wyncote Public School from September 1894.[^55] His first publication was on 7 November 1896 in the *Jenkintown Times-Chronicle* ("by E. L. Pound, Wyncote, aged 11 years"), a [limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_\(poetry\) "Limerick (poetry)") about [William Jennings Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan "William Jennings Bryan"), who had just lost the [1896 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_United_States_presidential_election "1896 United States presidential election").[^2]

In 1897, aged 12, he transferred to Cheltenham Military Academy (CMA), where he wore an [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War "American Civil War") -style uniform and was taught drilling and how to shoot.[^57] The following year he made his first trip overseas, a three-month tour with his mother and Aunt Frank, who took him to England, Belgium, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire "German Empire"), Switzerland, Italy, Spain, and Morocco.[^58] He attended CMA until 1900, at times as a boarder, but it seems he did not graduate.[^59] [^3]

### University

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b2/Hilda_Doolittle%2C_1921_%28cropped%29.jpg/250px-Hilda_Doolittle%2C_1921_%28cropped%29.jpg)

Hilda Doolittle (H.D.), c. 1921

In 1901, at 15 years old, Pound was admitted to the College of Liberal Arts at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania "University of Pennsylvania") in [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia").[^61] Years later he said his aim was to avoid drill at the military academy.[^62] His one distinction in first year was in geometry,[^63] but otherwise his grades were mostly poor, including in Latin, his major; he achieved a B in English composition and a pass in English literature.[^64] In his second year he switched from the degree course to "non-degree special student status", he said "to avoid irrelevant subjects".[^65] [^4] He was not elected to a [fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternities_and_sororities "Fraternities and sororities") at Penn, but it seemed not to bother him.[^67]

His parents and Aunt Frank took him on another three-month European tour in 1902, and the following year he transferred to [Hamilton College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_College_\(New_York\) "Hamilton College (New York)") in [Clinton, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton,_Oneida_County,_New_York "Clinton, Oneida County, New York"), possibly because of his grades.[^68] Again he was not invited to join a fraternity, but this time he had hoped to do so, according to letters home, because he wanted to live in a [fraternity house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternity_house "Fraternity house"), and by April 1904 he regarded the move as a mistake.[^69] Signed up for the Latin–Scientific course, he appears to have avoided some classes; his transcript is short of credits.[^68] He studied the [Provençal dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven%C3%A7al_dialect "Provençal dialect") of the [Occitan Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occitan_Language "Occitan Language") and read [Dante Alighieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri "Dante Alighieri") and [Anglo-Saxon poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon_poetry "Anglo-Saxon poetry"), including *[Beowulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beowulf "Beowulf")* and the 8th-century [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English_literature "Old English literature") poem *[The Seafarer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seafarer_\(poem\) "The Seafarer (poem)")*.[^70]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c5/Ezra_Weston_Pound_1905.jpg/330px-Ezra_Weston_Pound_1905.jpg)

Pound in the Hamilton College yearbook, 1905

After graduating from Hamilton in 1905 with a [PhB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Philosophy "Bachelor of Philosophy"), he returned to Penn, where he fell in love with Hilda Doolittle (who later wrote under the name " [H.D.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H.D. "H.D.")"). She was then a student at [Bryn Mawr College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryn_Mawr_College "Bryn Mawr College"), and he hand-bound 25 of his poems for her, calling it *Hilda's Book*.[^71] After receiving his M.A. in [Romance languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages "Romance languages") in 1906, he registered to write a PhD thesis on the [jesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracioso "Gracioso") in [Lope de Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lope_de_Vega "Lope de Vega") 's plays; a two-year Harrison fellowship covered his tuition and a $500 grant, with which he sailed again to Europe.[^72] He spent three weeks in Madrid in various libraries, including in the [Royal Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Palace_of_Madrid#Royal_Library "Royal Palace of Madrid"). On 31 May 1906 he was standing outside the palace during [the attempted assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morral_affair "Morral affair") of [King Alfonso XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Alfonso_XIII "King Alfonso XIII") and left the city for fear of being mistaken for an anarchist.[^73] After Spain he visited Paris and London, returning to the United States in July 1906.[^74] His first essay, "Raphaelite Latin", was published in the *Book News Monthly* that September.[^75] He took courses in English in 1907, where he fell out with just about everyone, including the department head, [Felix Schelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Emanuel_Schelling "Felix Emanuel Schelling"), with silly remarks during lectures and by winding an enormous tin watch very slowly while Schelling spoke.[^76] In the spring of 1907 he learned that his fellowship would not be renewed.[^77] Schelling told him he was wasting everyone's time, and he left without finishing his doctorate.[^78]

### Teaching

In Durance

> I am homesick after mine own kind,  
> Oh I know that there are folk about me, friendly faces,  
> But I am homesick after mine own kind.

—  *Personae of Ezra Pound* (1909) [^79]  
written in Crawfordsville, Indiana, 1907 [^80]

From September 1907 Pound taught French and Spanish at [Wabash College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabash_College "Wabash College"),[^81] a [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian "Presbyterian") college with 345 students in [Crawfordsville, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crawfordsville,_Indiana "Crawfordsville, Indiana"),[^82] which he called "the [sixth circle of hell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inferno_\(Dante\)#Sixth_Circle_\(Heresy\) "Inferno (Dante)") ".[^83] One former student remembered him as a breath of fresh air; another said he was "exhibitionist, egotistic, self-centered and self-indulgent".[^84]

He was dismissed after a few months. Smoking was forbidden, but he would smoke [cigarillos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cigarillo "Cigarillo") in his room in the same corridor as the president's office.[^85] He was asked to leave the college in January 1908 when his landladies, Ida and Belle Hall, found a woman in his room.[^86] Shocked at having been expelled,[^87] he left for Europe soon after, sailing from New York in March on the [RMS *Slavonia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Slavonia "RMS Slavonia").[^88]

## London (1908–1914)

### A Lume Spento

Pound arrived in [Gibraltar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibraltar "Gibraltar") on 23 March 1908, where he earned US$15 a day working as a guide for an American family there and in Spain.[^89] After stops in [Seville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seville "Seville"), [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada "Granada"), and [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoa "Genoa"), by the end of April he was in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice "Venice"), living over a bakery near the San Vio bridge.[^90] In the summer he decided to self-publish his first collection of 44 poems in the 72-page *[A Lume Spento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Lume_Spento "A Lume Spento")* ("With Tapers Quenched"), 150 copies of which were printed in July 1908.[^91] The title is from the third canto of [Dante Alighieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante_Alighieri "Dante Alighieri") 's *[Purgatorio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purgatorio "Purgatorio")*, alluding to the death of [Manfred, King of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred,_King_of_Sicily "Manfred, King of Sicily"). Pound dedicated the book to the Philadelphia artist [William Brooke Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Brooke_Smith "William Brooke Smith"), a friend from university who had recently died of [tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuberculosis "Tuberculosis").[^92]

In "Canto LXXVI" of *[The Pisan Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pisan_Cantos "The Pisan Cantos")*, he records that he considered throwing the proofs into the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_\(Venice\) "Grand Canal (Venice)"), abandoning the book and poetry altogether: "by the soap-smooth stone posts where San Vio / meets with il Canal Grande / between Salviati and the house that was of Don Carlos / shd/I chuck the lot into the tide-water? / [le bozze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley_proof "Galley proof") "A Lume Spento"/ / and by the column of Todero / shd/I shift to the other side / or wait 24 hours".[^93]

### Move to London

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/9/93/48_Langham_Street%2C_London_W1.jpg/250px-48_Langham_Street%2C_London_W1.jpg)

48 Langham Street, Fitzrovia, London W1

In August 1908 Pound moved to London, carrying 60 copies of *A Lume Spento*.[^94] English poets such as [Maurice Hewlett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Hewlett "Maurice Hewlett"), [Rudyard Kipling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudyard_Kipling "Rudyard Kipling"), and [Alfred Tennyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred,_Lord_Tennyson "Alfred, Lord Tennyson") had made a particular kind of [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era "Victorian era") verse—stirring, pompous, and propagandistic—popular. According to modernist scholar James Knapp, Pound rejected the idea of poetry as "versified moral essay"; he wanted to focus on the individual experience, the concrete rather than the abstract.[^95]

Pound at first stayed in a boarding house at 8 [Duchess Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess_Street,_London "Duchess Street, London"), near the [British Museum Reading Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum_Reading_Room "British Museum Reading Room"); he had met the landlady during his travels in Europe in 1906.[^96] He soon moved to [Islington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islington "Islington") (cheaper at [12s 6d](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C2%A3sd "£sd") a week [board and lodging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_and_board "Room and board")), but his father sent him £4, and he was able to move back into [Central London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_London "Central London"), to 48 Langham Street, near [Great Titchfield Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Titchfield_Street "Great Titchfield Street").[^97] The house sat across an alley from the Yorkshire Grey pub, which made an appearance in "Canto LXXX" (*The Pisan Cantos*), "concerning the landlady's *doings* / with a lodger unnamed / az waz near Gt Tichfield St. next door to the pub".[^98]

Pound persuaded the bookseller [Elkin Mathews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Elkin_Mathews "Charles Elkin Mathews") on [Vigo Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigo_Street "Vigo Street") to display *A Lume Spento*, and in an unsigned article on 26 November 1908, Pound reviewed it himself in the *[Evening Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evening_Standard "Evening Standard")*: "The unseizable magic of poetry is in this queer paper book; and words are no good in describing it." [^99] The following month he self-published a second collection, *[A Quinzaine for this Yule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Quinzaine_for_this_Yule "A Quinzaine for this Yule")*.[^100] It was his first book to have commercial success, and Elkin Matthews had another 100 copies printed.[^101] In January and February 1909, after the death of [John Churton Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Churton_Collins "John Churton Collins") left a vacancy, Pound lectured for an hour a week in the evenings on "The Development of Literature in Southern Europe" at the [Regent Street Polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Westminster "University of Westminster").[^102] [^5] Mornings might be spent in the British Museum Reading Room, followed by lunch at the [Vienna Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Caf%C3%A9 "Vienna Café") on [Oxford Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford_Street "Oxford Street"), where Pound first met the painter and writer [Wyndham Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyndham_Lewis "Wyndham Lewis") in 1910.[^104] "There were mysterious figures / that emerged from recondite recesses / and ate at the WIENER CAFÉ".[^105] [Ford Madox Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Madox_Ford "Ford Madox Ford") described Pound as "approach\[ing\] with the step of a dancer, making passes with a cane at an imaginary opponent":[^106] "He would wear trousers made of green billiard cloth, a pink coat, a blue shirt, a tie hand-painted by a Japanese friend, an immense sombrero, a flaming beard cut to a point, and a single, large blue earring."

### Meeting Dorothy Shakespear, Personae

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ae/DorothyPound.jpg/250px-DorothyPound.jpg)

Pound married Dorothy Shakespear in 1914.

At a literary [salon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salon_\(gathering\) "Salon (gathering)") in 1909, Pound met the novelist [Olivia Shakespear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Shakespear "Olivia Shakespear") [^107] and later at the Shakespears' home at 12 Brunswick Gardens, Kensington, was introduced to her daughter, [Dorothy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Shakespear "Dorothy Shakespear"), who became Pound's wife in 1914.[^108] The critic [Iris Barry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_Barry "Iris Barry") described her as "carrying herself delicately with the air, always, of a young Victorian lady out skating, and a profile as clear and lovely as that of a porcelain [Kuan-yin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanyin "Guanyin") ".[^109] "Listen to it—Ezra! Ezra!—And a third time—Ezra!", Dorothy wrote in her diary on 16 February 1909.[^110]

Pound mixed with the cream of London's literary circle, including Hewlett, [Laurence Binyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurence_Binyon "Laurence Binyon"), [Frederic Manning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederic_Manning "Frederic Manning"), [Ernest Rhys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Rhys "Ernest Rhys"), [May Sinclair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Sinclair "May Sinclair"), [Ellen Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellen_Terry "Ellen Terry"), [George Bernard Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bernard_Shaw "George Bernard Shaw"), [Hilaire Belloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilaire_Belloc "Hilaire Belloc"), [T. E. Hulme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._E._Hulme "T. E. Hulme"), and [F. S. Flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._S._Flint "F. S. Flint").[^111] Through the Shakespears, he was introduced to the poet [W. B. Yeats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._B._Yeats "W. B. Yeats"), Olivia Shakespear's former lover. He had already sent Yeats a copy of *A Lume Spento*, and Yeats had apparently found it "charming".[^112] Pound wrote to [William Carlos Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Carlos_Williams "William Carlos Williams") on 3 February 1909: "Am by way of falling into the crowd that does things here. London, deah old Lundon, is the place for poesy." [^113] According to [Richard Aldington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Aldington "Richard Aldington"), London found Pound amusing. The newspapers interviewed him,[^114] and he was mentioned in *[Punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punch_\(magazine\) "Punch (magazine)")* magazine, which on 23 June 1909 described "Mr. Ezekiel Ton" as "the most remarkable thing in poetry since [Robert Browning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Browning "Robert Browning")... \[blending\] the imagery of the unfettered West, the vocabulary of [Wardour Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wardour_Street "Wardour Street"), and the sinister abandon of Borgiac Italy".[^115]

Erat Hora

> "Thank you, whatever comes." And then she turned  
> And, as the ray of sun on hanging flowers  
> Fades when the wind hath lifted them aside,  
> Went swiftly from me. Nay, whatever comes  
> One hour was sunlit and the most high gods  
> May not make boast of any better thing  
> Than to have watched that hour as it passed.

—  *Personae: The Collected Poems of Ezra Pound* (1926) [^116]

In April 1909 Elkin Mathews published *Personae of Ezra Pound* (half the poems were from *A Lume Spento*) [^101] [^12] and in October a further 27 poems (16 new) as *Exultations*.[^119] The British poet [Edward Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Thomas_\(poet\) "Edward Thomas (poet)") described *Personae* in *English Review* as "full of human passion and natural magic".[^120] [Rupert Brooke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupert_Brooke "Rupert Brooke") complained in the *Cambridge Review* that Pound had fallen under the influence of the American poet [Walt Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Whitman "Walt Whitman"), writing in "unmetrical sprawling lengths that, in his hands, have nothing to commend them". But he did acknowledge that Pound had "great talents".[^121]

In or around September, Pound moved into new rooms at Church Walk, off [Kensington High Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensington_High_Street "Kensington High Street"), where he lived most of the time until 1914.[^122] He visited a friend, [Walter Rummel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Morse_Rummel "Walter Morse Rummel"), in Paris in March 1910 and was introduced to the American heiress and pianist Margaret Lanier Cravens. Although they had only just met, she offered to become a patron at $1,000 a year, and from then until her death in 1912 she apparently sent him money regularly.[^123]

### The Spirit of Romance, Canzoni, the New Age

In June 1910 Pound returned for eight months to the United States; his arrival coincided with the publication in London of his first book of literary criticism, *[The Spirit of Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Spirit_of_Romance "The Spirit of Romance")*, based on his lecture notes from the polytechnic.[^124] *Patria Mia*, his essays on the United States, were written at this time.[^125] In August he moved to New York, renting rooms on [Waverly Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waverly_Place "Waverly Place") and [Park Avenue South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Avenue_South "Park Avenue South"), facing [Gramercy Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramercy_Park "Gramercy Park").[^126] Although he loved New York, he felt alienated by the commercialism and newcomers from Eastern and Southern Europe who were displacing the [white Anglo-Saxon Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Anglo-Saxon_Protestant "White Anglo-Saxon Protestant").[^127] The recently built [New York Public Library Main Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Public_Library_Main_Branch "New York Public Library Main Branch") he found especially offensive.[^128] During this period his antisemitism became apparent; he referred in *Patria Mia* to the "detestable qualities" of Jews.[^129] After persuading his parents to finance his passage back to Europe, he sailed from New York on the [RMS *Mauretania*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RMS_Mauretania_\(1906\) "RMS Mauretania (1906)") on 22 February 1911. It was nearly 30 years—April 1939—before he visited the U.S. again.[^130]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Vienna_Caf%C3%A9%2C_London%2C_1897.jpg/330px-Vienna_Caf%C3%A9%2C_London%2C_1897.jpg)

First floor of the Vienna Café with its mirrored ceiling, Oxford Street, in 1897. The room became a meeting place for Pound, Wyndham Lewis, and other writers.

After three days in London he went to Paris,[^131] where he worked on a new collection of poetry, *Canzoni* (1911),[^132] panned by *[The Westminster Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Westminster_Gazette "The Westminster Gazette")* as "affectation combined with pedantry".[^133] He wrote in Ford Madox Ford's obituary that Ford had rolled on the floor with laughter at its "stilted language".[^134] When he returned to London in August, he rented a room in [Marylebone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marylebone "Marylebone") at 2A Granville Place, then shared a house at 39 [Addison Road North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addison_Road,_London "Addison Road, London"), [W11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W_postcode_area "W postcode area").[^135] By November [A. R. Orage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Richard_Orage "Alfred Richard Orage"), editor of the socialist journal *[The New Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Age "The New Age")*, had hired him to write a weekly column.[^136] Orage appears in *The Cantos* ([Possum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Possum's_Book_of_Practical_Cats "Old Possum's Book of Practical Cats") is T. S. Eliot): "but the lot of 'em, Yeats, Possum and Wyndham / had no ground beneath 'em. / Orage had." [^137]

Pound contributed to the *New Age* from 30 November 1911 to 13 January 1921,[^138] attending editorial meetings in the basement of a grimy [ABC tearoom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerated_Bread_Company "Aerated Bread Company") in [Chancery Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancery_Lane "Chancery Lane").[^139] There and at other meetings he met [Arnold Bennett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Bennett "Arnold Bennett"), [Cecil Chesterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Chesterton "Cecil Chesterton"), [Beatrice Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Hastings "Beatrice Hastings"), [S. G. Hobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S._G._Hobson "S. G. Hobson"), Hulme, [Katherine Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Mansfield "Katherine Mansfield"), and [H. G. Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._G._Wells "H. G. Wells").[^138] In the *New Age* office in 1918, he also met [C. H. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._Douglas "C. H. Douglas"), a British engineer who was developing his economic theory of [social credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_credit "Social credit"), which Pound found attractive.[^140] Douglas reportedly believed that Jews were a problem and needed to abandon a [Messianic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messianism "Messianism") view of themselves as the "dominating race".[^141] According to [Colin Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Holmes_\(historian\) "Colin Holmes (historian)"), *The New Age* itself published antisemitic material.[^142] It was within this environment, not in Italy, according to [Tim Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Redman "Tim Redman"), that Pound first encountered antisemitic ideas about "usury".[^138] "In Douglas's program," [Christopher Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Hitchens "Christopher Hitchens") wrote in 2008, "Pound had found his true muse: a blend of folkloric Celtic twilight with a paranoid hatred of the money economy and a dire suspicion about an ancient faith." [^143]

### Poetry magazine, Ripostes, Imagism

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fb/EZRA_POUND_-_10_Kensington_Church_Walk_Holland_Park_London_W8_4NB.jpg/250px-EZRA_POUND_-_10_Kensington_Church_Walk_Holland_Park_London_W8_4NB.jpg)

10 Church Walk, Kensington, London W8. Pound lived on the first floor (far left) in 1909–1910 and 1911–1914. 13

In May 1911, H.D. left Philadelphia for London. She was accompanied by the poet Frances Gregg and Gregg's mother; when they returned in September, H.D. stayed on. Pound introduced her to his friends, including Aldington, who became her husband in 1913. Before that, the three of them lived in Church Walk, Kensington—Pound at No. 10, Aldington at No. 8, and Doolittle at No. 6—and worked daily in the [Reading Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum_Reading_Room "British Museum Reading Room") of the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum "British Museum").[^122]

At the British Museum, Laurence Binyon introduced Pound to the East Asian artistic and literary concepts Pound used in his later poetry, including Japanese [ukiyo-e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukiyo-e "Ukiyo-e") prints.[^146] The visitors' book first shows Pound in the Prints and Drawings Students' Room (known as the Print Room) [^147] on 9 February 1909, and later in 1912 and 1913, with Dorothy Shakespear, examining Chinese and Japanese art.[^148] Pound was working at the time on the poems that became *Ripostes* (1912), trying to move away from his earlier work.[^149] "I hadn't in 1910 made a language", he wrote years later. "I don't mean a language to use, but even a language to think in." [^14]

In August 1912 [Harriet Monroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Monroe "Harriet Monroe") hired Pound as foreign correspondent of *[Poetry: A Magazine of Verse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry_\(magazine\) "Poetry (magazine)")*, a new magazine in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago").[^151] The first edition, in October, featured two of his own poems—"To Whistler, American" and "Middle Aged". Also that month Stephen Swift and Co. in London published *[Ripostes of Ezra Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripostes "Ripostes")*, a collection of 25 poems, including a contentious translation of *The Seafarer*,[^152] that demonstrate his shift toward minimalist language.[^122] In addition to Pound's work, the collection contains five poems by Hulme.[^153]

![book cover](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/92/Poetry_cover1.jpg/250px-Poetry_cover1.jpg)

First edition of Poetry, October 1912

*Ripostes* includes the first mention of *Les Imagistes*: "As for the future, *Les Imagistes*, the descendants of the forgotten school of 1909, have that in their keeping." [^154] While in the British Museum tearoom one afternoon with Doolittle and Aldington, Pound edited one of Doolittle's poems and wrote "H.D. Imagiste" underneath;[^155] he described this later as the founding of a movement in poetry, *[Imagisme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imagism "Imagism")*.[^156] [^15] In the spring or early summer of 1912, they agreed, Pound wrote in 1918, on three principles:[^158]

> 1. Direct treatment of the "thing" whether subjective or objective.
> 2. To use absolutely no word that does not contribute to the presentation.
> 3. As regarding rhythm: to compose in the sequence of the musical phrase, not in sequence of a metronome.

*Poetry* published Pound's "A Few Don'ts by an Imagist" in March 1913. Superfluous words, particularly adjectives, should be avoided, as well as expressions like "dim lands of peace". He wrote: "It dulls the image. It mixes an abstraction with the concrete. It comes from the writer's not realizing that the natural object is always the *adequate* symbol." Poets should "go in fear of abstractions".[^159] He wanted *Imagisme* "to stand for hard light, clear edges", he wrote later to [Amy Lowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Lowell "Amy Lowell").[^160]

In a Station of the Metro

> The apparition of these faces in the crowd:  
> Petals on a wet, black bough.

—  *[Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry_\(magazine\) "Poetry (magazine)")* (April 1913) [^161]

An example of Imagist poetry is Pound's " [In a Station of the Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_a_Station_of_the_Metro "In a Station of the Metro") ", published in *Poetry* in April 1913 and inspired by an experience on the [Paris Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Metro "Paris Metro"). "I got out of a train at, I think, [La Concorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concorde_station "Concorde station") ", he wrote in "How I began" in *[T. P.'s Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._P.'s_Weekly "T. P.'s Weekly")* on 6 June 1913, "and in the jostle I saw a beautiful face, and then, turning suddenly, another and another, and then a beautiful child's face, and then another beautiful face. All that day I tried to find words for what this made me feel.... I could get nothing but spots of colour." A year later he reduced it to its essence in the style of a Japanese [haiku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiku "Haiku").[^162]

### James Joyce, Pound's unpopularity

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/1e/Revolutionary_Joyce_Better_Contrast.jpg/250px-Revolutionary_Joyce_Better_Contrast.jpg)

James Joyce, c. 1918

In the summer of 1913 Pound became literary editor of *[The Egoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Egoist_\(periodical\) "The Egoist (periodical)")*, a journal founded by [Dora Marsden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dora_Marsden "Dora Marsden"), a [suffragette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette "Suffragette").[^163] At the suggestion of [W. B. Yeats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._B._Yeats "W. B. Yeats"), Pound encouraged [James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce "James Joyce") in December of that year to submit his work.[^164] The previous month Yeats, whose eyesight was failing, had rented Stone Cottage in [Coleman's Hatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coleman's_Hatch "Coleman's Hatch"), Sussex, inviting Pound to accompany him as his secretary, and it was during this visit that Yeats introduced Pound to Joyce's *[Chamber Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_Music_\(poetry_collection\) "Chamber Music (poetry collection)")* and his "I hear an Army Charging Upon the Land".[^165] This was the first of three winters Pound and Yeats spent at Stone Cottage, including two with Dorothy after she and Ezra married in 1914.[^166] "Canto LXXXIII" records a visit: "so that I recalled the noise in the chimney / as it were the wind in the chimney / but was in reality [Uncle William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._B._Yeats "W. B. Yeats") / downstairs composing / that had made a great Peeeeacock / in the proide ov his oiye." [^167] [^16]

In his reply to Pound, Joyce gave permission to use "I hear an Army" and enclosed *[Dubliners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubliners "Dubliners")* and the first chapter of his novel *[A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Portrait_of_the_Artist_as_a_Young_Man "A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man")*.[^165] Pound wrote to Joyce that the novel was "damn fine stuff".[^168] [Harriet Shaw Weaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Shaw_Weaver "Harriet Shaw Weaver") accepted it for *The Egoist*, which serialized it from 2 February 1914, despite the printers objecting to words like "fart" and "ballocks", and fearing prosecution over [Stephen Dedalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Dedalus "Stephen Dedalus") 's thoughts about prostitutes. On the basis of the serialization, the publisher that had rejected *Dubliners* reconsidered. Joyce wrote to Yeats: "I can never thank you enough for having brought me into relation with your friend Ezra Pound who is indeed a miracle worker." [^169]

Around this time, Pound's articles in the *New Age* began to make him unpopular, to the alarm of Orage.[^170] [Samuel Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Putnam "Samuel Putnam") knew Pound in Paris in the 1920s and described him as stubborn, contrary, cantankerous, bossy, touchy, and "devoid of humor"; he was "an American small-towner", in Putnam's view. His attitude caused him trouble in both London and Paris.[^171] English women, with their "preponderantly derivative" minds, were inferior to American women who had minds of their own, he wrote in the *New Age*. The English sense of what was right was based on respect for property, not morality. "\[P\]erched on the rotten shell of a crumbling empire", London had lost its energy. England's best authors— [Joseph Conrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Conrad "Joseph Conrad"), [William Henry Hudson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Henry_Hudson "William Henry Hudson"), [Henry James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_James "Henry James"), and Yeats—were not English. English writers and critics were ignorant, he wrote in 1913.[^172]

### Marriage

Ezra and Dorothy were married on 20 April 1914 at [St Mary Abbots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary_Abbots "St Mary Abbots") in Kensington,[^173] the Shakespears' parish church, despite opposition from her parents, who worried about Ezra's income. His concession to marry in church had helped. Dorothy's annual income was £50, with another £150 from her family,[^174] and Ezra's was £200.[^175] Her father, Henry Hope Shakespear, had him prepare a financial statement in 1911, which showed that his main source of income was his father.[^176] After the wedding the couple moved into an apartment with no bathroom at 5 Holland Place Chambers, Kensington, next door to the newly wed H.D. and Aldington.[^174] This arrangement did not last. H.D. had been alarmed to find Ezra looking for a place to live outside the apartment building the day before his wedding. Once Dorothy and Ezra had moved into the building, Ezra would arrive unannounced at H.D.'s to discuss his writing, a habit that upset her, in part because his writing touched on private aspects of their relationship. She and Aldington decided to move several miles away to [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead "Hampstead").[^177]

### Des Imagistes, dispute with Amy Lowell

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/a/ae/Ezra_Pound_by_Wyndham_Lewis%2C_1919.jpeg/250px-Ezra_Pound_by_Wyndham_Lewis%2C_1919.jpeg)

Pound by Wyndham Lewis, 1919. The portrait is lost.

The appearance of *[Des Imagistes, An Anthology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Imagistes "Des Imagistes")* (1914), edited by Pound, "confirmed the importance" of *Imagisme*, according to [Ira Nadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Nadel "Ira Nadel").[^178] Published in the American magazine *[The Glebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Glebe_\(literary_magazine\) "The Glebe (literary magazine)")* in February 1914 and the following month as a book, it was the first of five Imagist anthologies and the only one to contain work by Pound.[^179] It included ten poems by [Richard Aldington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Aldington "Richard Aldington"), seven by H.D., followed by Flint, [Skipwith Cannell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skipwith_Cannell "Skipwith Cannell"), Lowell, Carlos Williams, [James Joyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joyce "James Joyce") ("I Hear an Army", not an example of Imagism), six by Pound, then Hueffer (as he was known as the time), [Allen Upward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Upward "Allen Upward") and [John Cournos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Cournos "John Cournos").[^180]

Shortly after its publication, an advertisement for Lewis's new magazine, *[Blast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blast_\(British_magazine\) "Blast (British magazine)")* promised it would cover " [Cubism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism "Cubism"), [Futurism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futurism "Futurism"), Imagisme and all Vital Forms of Modern Art." [^181] Described by Pound as "mostly a painter's magazine with me to do the poems," [^182] and bearing the heavy influence of Futurism,[^183] *Blast* was the magazine of a London art movement formed by Lewis with Pound's collaboration. Pound named the movement [Vorticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorticism "Vorticism").[^17] Vorticism included all the arts, and in *Blast* "the Imagist propaganda merged into the Vorticist." [^186] In the end, *Blast* was published only twice, in 1914 and 1915. In June 1914 *The Times* announced Lewis's new Rebel Arts Centre for Vorticist art at 38 [Great Ormond Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Ormond_Street "Great Ormond Street").[^187]

Lowell, who was to win the [Pulitzer Prize for Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Poetry "Pulitzer Prize for Poetry") in 1926, was unhappy that only one of her poems had appeared in *Des Imagistes*. She arrived in London in July 1914 to attend two dinners at the Dieudonné restaurant in Ryder Street, the first to celebrate the publication of *Blast* and the second, on 17 July, the publication of *Des Imagistes*. At the second, Ford Madox Hueffer announced that he had been an Imagiste long before Lowell and Pound, and that he doubted their qualifications; only Aldington and H.D. could lay claim to the title, in his view. During the subsequent row, Pound left the table and returned with a tin bathtub on his head, suggesting it as a symbol of what he called *Les Nagistes*, a school created by Lowell's poem "In a Garden", which ends with "Night, and the water, and you in your whiteness, bathing!" Apparently his behavior helped Lowell win people over to her point of view, as did her offer to fund future work.[^188]

H.D. and Aldington were moving away from Pound's understanding of *Imagisme* anyway, as he aligned himself with Lewis's ideas.[^189] Lowell agreed to finance an annual anthology of *Imagiste* poets, but she insisted on democracy; according to Aldington, she "proposed a [Boston Tea Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Tea_Party "Boston Tea Party") for Ezra" and an end to his despotic rule.[^190] Upset at Lowell, Pound began to call *Imagisme* "Amygism";[^191] he declared the movement dead and asked the group not to call themselves *Imagistes*. Not accepting that it was Pound's invention, they refused and Anglicized the term.[^192]

## World War I and leaving England (1914–1921)

### Meeting Eliot, Cathay, translation

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/8/87/T.S._Eliot%2C_1923.JPG/250px-T.S._Eliot%2C_1923.JPG)

T. S. Eliot, 1923

When [war was declared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_declaration_of_war_upon_Germany_\(1914\) "United Kingdom declaration of war upon Germany (1914)") in August 1914, opportunities for writers were immediately reduced; poems were now expected to be patriotic.[^193] Pound's income from October 1914 to October 1915 was £42.10.0,[^194] apparently five times less than the year before.[^195]

On 22 September 1914 T. S. Eliot traveled from [Merton College, Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merton_College,_Oxford "Merton College, Oxford"), with an introduction from [Conrad Aiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Aiken "Conrad Aiken"), to have Pound read Eliot's unpublished " [The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Love_Song_of_J._Alfred_Prufrock "The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock") ".[^196] Pound wrote to Harriet Monroe, editor of *Poetry*, on 30 September to say that Eliot—who was at Oxford on a fellowship from [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") —had "sent in the best poem I have yet had or seen from an American... He has actually trained himself *and* modernized himself *on his own*." [^197] Monroe did not like Prufrock's "very European world-weariness", according to [Humphrey Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter "Humphrey Carpenter"), but she published it anyway, in June 1915.[^198]

The River Merchant's Wife:  
A Letter

> At fourteen I married My Lord you.  
> I never laughed, being bashful.  
> Lowering my head, I looked at the wall.  
> Called to, a thousand times, I never looked back.  
>   
> At fifteen, I stopped scowling,  
> I desired my dust to be mingled with yours  
> Forever and forever and forever.  
> Why should I climb the look out?

—  " [The River Merchant's Wife: A Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_River_Merchant's_Wife:_A_Letter "The River Merchant's Wife: A Letter") " by [Li Bai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Bai "Li Bai"), translated in *[Cathay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathay_\(poetry_collection\) "Cathay (poetry collection)")* (1915) [^199]

The 1915 poem *[Cathay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathay_\(poetry_collection\) "Cathay (poetry collection)")* contains 25 examples of [Classical Chinese poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Chinese_poetry "Classical Chinese poetry") that Pound translated into English based on the notes of the [Orientalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_studies "Oriental studies") [Ernest Fenollosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Fenollosa "Ernest Fenollosa"). Fenollosa's widow, [Mary McNeill Fenollosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_McCall "Sidney McCall"), had given Pound her husband's notes in 1913,[^200] after Laurence Binyon introduced them.[^201] [Michael Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_J._Alexander "Michael J. Alexander") saw *Cathay* as the most attractive of Pound's work.[^202] There is a debate about whether the poems should be viewed primarily as translations or as contributions to Imagism and the modernization of English poetry.[^203] English professor Steven Yao argued that *Cathay* shows that translation does not need a thorough knowledge of the source language.[^18]

Pound's translations from Old English, Latin, Italian, French and Chinese were highly disputed. According to Alexander, they made him more unpopular in some circles than the treason charge.[^206] [Robert Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Graves "Robert Graves") wrote in 1955: "\[Pound\] knew little Latin, yet he translated [Propertius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propertius "Propertius"); and less Greek, but he translated [Alcaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcaeus "Alcaeus"); and still less Anglo-Saxon, yet he translated *[The Seafarer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seafarer_\(poem\) "The Seafarer (poem)")*. I once asked [Arthur Waley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Waley "Arthur Waley") how much Chinese Pound knew; Waley shook his head despondently." [^207]

Pound was devastated when [Henri Gaudier-Brzeska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Gaudier-Brzeska "Henri Gaudier-Brzeska"), from whom he had commissioned [a sculpture of himself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieratic_Head_of_Ezra_Pound "Hieratic Head of Ezra Pound") two years earlier, was killed in the trenches in June 1915. In response, he published *Gaudier-Brzeska: A Memoir* (1916), writing "A great spirit has been among us, and a great artist has gone." [^208] Two months before he died, Gaudier-Brzeska had written to Pound to say that he kept *Cathay* in his pocket "to put courage in my fellows".[^209]

### "Three Cantos", resignation from Poetry

After the publication of *Cathay*, Pound mentioned that he was working on a long poem. He described it in September 1915 as a "cryselephantine poem of immeasurable length which will occupy me for the next four decades unless it becomes a bore".[^210] In February 1916, when Pound was 30, the poet [Carl Sandburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sandburg "Carl Sandburg") paid tribute to him in *Poetry* magazine. Pound "stains darkly and touches softly", he wrote:

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/5b/EzraPound_Pavannes.JPG/330px-EzraPound_Pavannes.JPG)

Pound by E. O. Hoppé on the cover of Pavannes and Divisions (1918)

> All talk on modern poetry, by people who know, ends with dragging in Ezra Pound somewhere. He may be named only to be cursed as wanton and mocker, poseur, trifler and vagrant. Or he may be classed as filling a niche today like that of Keats in a preceding epoch. The point is, he will be mentioned....
> 
> In the cool and purple meantime, Pound goes ahead producing new poems having the slogan, "Guts and Efficiency," emblazoned above his daily program of work. His genius runs to various schools and styles. He acquires traits and then throws them away. One characteristic is that he has no characteristics. He is a new roamer of the beautiful, a new fetcher of wild shapes, in each new handful of writings offered us.[^211]

In June, July and August 1917 Pound had the first three cantos published, as "Three Cantos", in *Poetry*.[^212] [^213] He was now a regular contributor to three literary magazines. From 1917 he wrote music reviews for the *New Age* as William Atheling and art reviews as B. H. Dias.[^214] In May 1917 [Margaret Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_C._Anderson "Margaret C. Anderson") hired him as foreign editor of *[The Little Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Review "The Little Review")*.[^215] He also wrote weekly pieces for *The Egoist* and *The Little Review*; many of the latter complained about provincialism, which included the ringing of church bells.[^216] (When Pound lived near St Mary Abbots he had "engaged in a fierce, guerrilla warfare of letters" about the bells with the vicar, Reverend R. E. Pennefather, according to Richard Aldington.) [^217] The volume of writing exhausted him.[^218] In 1918, after a bout of illness which was presumably the [Spanish flu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_flu "Spanish flu"),[^219] he decided to stop writing for *The Little Review*. He had asked the publisher for a raise to hire a typist, the 23-year-old [Iseult Gonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iseult_Gonne "Iseult Gonne"), causing rumors that they were having an affair, but he was turned down.[^220]

And the days are not full enough

> And the days are not full enough  
> And the nights are not full enough  
> And life slips by like a field mouse  
> Not shaking the grass.

—  *Personae* (1926) [^221]

A suspicion arose in June 1918 that Pound himself had written an article in *The Egoist* praising his own work, and it was clear from the response that he had acquired enemies. The poet [F. S. Flint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._S._Flint "F. S. Flint") told *The Egoist* 's editor that "we are all tired of Mr. Pound". British literary circles were "tired of his antics" and of him "puffing and swelling himself and his friends", Flint wrote. "His work has deteriorated from book to book; his manners have become more and more offensive; and we wish he would go back to America." [^222]

The March 1919 issue of *Poetry* published Pound's *Poems from the Propertius Series*,[^223] which appeared to be a translation of the Latin poet [Sextus Propertius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextus_Propertius "Sextus Propertius").[^19] Harriet Monroe, editor of *Poetry*, published a letter in April 1919 from a professor of Latin, [W. G. Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Gardner_Hale "William Gardner Hale"), who found "about three-score errors" in the text; he said Pound was "incredibly ignorant of Latin", that "much of what he makes his author say is unintelligible", and that "If Mr. Pound were a professor of Latin, there would be nothing left for him but suicide" (adding "I do not counsel this").[^224] Pound replied to Monroe: "Cat-piss and porcupines!! The thing is no more a translation than my 'Altaforte' is a translation, or than [Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_FitzGerald_\(poet\) "Edward FitzGerald (poet)") 's [Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubaiyat_of_Omar_Khayyam "Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam") is a translation." His letter ended "In final commiseration". Monroe interpreted his silence after that as his resignation from *Poetry* magazine.[^225]

### Hugh Selwyn Mauberley

Hugh Selwyn Mauberley

> [Pound reading *Mauberley*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mZ3uBTCsG_4), Washington, D.C., June 1958
> 
> ---
> 
> ![F](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Hugh_Selwyn_Mauberley_initial_F.png/120px-Hugh_Selwyn_Mauberley_initial_F.png)
> 
> OR three years, out of key with his time,
> 
> He strove to resuscitate the dead art  
> Of poetry; to maintain "the sublime"  
> In the old sense. Wrong from the start—  
>   
> No hardly, but, seeing he had been born  
> In a half savage country, out of date;  
> Bent resolutely on wringing lilies from the acorn;  
> [Capaneus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capaneus "Capaneus"); trout for factitious bait;  
>   
> Ἴδμεν γάρ τοι πάνθ', ὅσ' ἐνι Τροίῃ [^20]  
> Caught in the unstopped ear;  
> Giving the rocks small lee-way  
> The chopped seas held him, therefore, that year.

—  *[Hugh Selwyn Mauberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Selwyn_Mauberley "Hugh Selwyn Mauberley")* (1920) [^227]

By 1919 Pound felt there was no reason to stay in England. He had become "violently hostile" to England, according to Aldington,[^228] feeling he was being "frozen out of everything" except the *New Age*,[^229] and concluding that the British were insensitive to "mental agility in any and every form".[^230] He had "muffed his chances of becoming literary director of London—to which he undoubtedly aspired," Aldington wrote in 1941, "by his own enormous conceit, folly, and bad manners." [^228]

Published by [John Rodker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rodker "John Rodker") 's The Ovid Press in June 1920,[^231] Pound's poem *Hugh Selwyn Mauberley* marked his farewell to London, and by December the Pounds were subletting their apartment and preparing to move to France.[^232] Consisting of 18 short parts, *Mauberley* describes a poet whose life has become sterile and meaningless. It begins with a satirical analysis of the London literary scene before turning to social criticism, economics, and the war. Here the word *[usury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usury "Usury")* first appears in his work. Just as Eliot denied he was Prufrock, Pound denied he was Mauberley.[^233] In 1932 the critic [F. R. Leavis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._R._Leavis "F. R. Leavis"), then director of studies in English at [Downing College, Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downing_College,_Cambridge "Downing College, Cambridge"), called *Mauberley* "great poetry, at once traditional and original. Mr. Pound's standing as a poet rests on it, and rests securely".[^234]

On 13 January 1921 Orage wrote in the *New Age*: "Mr. Pound has shaken the dust of London from his feet with not too emphatic a gesture of disgust, but, at least, without gratitude to this country.... \[He\] has been an exhilarating influence for culture in England; he has left his mark upon more than one of the arts, upon literature, music, poetry and sculpture; and quite a number of men and movements owe their initiation to his self-sacrificing stimulus...".[^235]

> With all this, however, Mr. Pound, like so many others who have striven for advancement of intelligence and culture in England, has made more enemies than friends, and far more powerful enemies than friends. Much of the Press has been deliberately closed by cabal to him; his books have for some time been ignored or written down; and he himself has been compelled to live on much less than would support a navvy. His fate, as I have said, is not unusual... Taken by and large, England hates men of culture until they are dead.[^235] [^21]

## Paris (1921–1924)

### Meeting Hemingway, editing The Waste Land

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/Ezra_Pound_passport_photograph_undated.jpg/250px-Ezra_Pound_passport_photograph_undated.jpg)

Pound's passport photograph, c. 1919 145

The Pounds settled in Paris around April 1921 and in December moved to an inexpensive ground-floor apartment at 70 *bis* [Rue Notre-Dame-des-Champs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_Notre-Dame-des-Champs,_Paris?action=edit&redlink=1 "Rue Notre-Dame-des-Champs, Paris (page does not exist)").[^237] Pound became friendly with [Marcel Duchamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Duchamp "Marcel Duchamp"), [Fernand Léger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernand_L%C3%A9ger "Fernand Léger"), [Tristan Tzara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tristan_Tzara "Tristan Tzara"), and others of the [Dada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dada "Dada") and [Surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism "Surrealism") movements, as well as [Basil Bunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basil_Bunting "Basil Bunting").[^238] He was introduced to the American writer [Gertrude Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Stein "Gertrude Stein"), who was living in Paris. She wrote years later that she liked him but did not find him amusing; he was "a village explainer, excellent if you were a village, but if you were not, not".[^239]

Pound's collection *Poems 1918–1921* was published in New York by [Boni and Liveright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boni_&_Liveright "Boni & Liveright") in 1921. In December that year [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway"), then aged 22, moved to Paris with his wife, [Hadley Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadley_Richardson "Hadley Richardson"), carrying letters of introduction from [Sherwood Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwood_Anderson "Sherwood Anderson").[^240] In February 1922 the Hemingways visited the Pounds for tea.[^241] Although Pound was 14 years older, the men became friends; Hemingway assumed the status of pupil and asked Pound to edit his short stories.[^242] Pound introduced him to his contacts, including Lewis, Ford, [John Peale Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peale_Bishop "John Peale Bishop"), [Malcolm Cowley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Cowley "Malcolm Cowley"), and [Derek Patmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Patmore "Derek Patmore"), while Hemingway tried to teach Pound to box.[^243] Unlike Hemingway, Pound was not a drinker and preferred to spend his time in salons [^244] or building furniture for his apartment and bookshelves for [Sylvia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvia_Beach "Sylvia Beach") 's [Shakespeare and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare_and_Company_\(1919%E2%80%931941\) "Shakespeare and Company (1919–1941)") bookstore.[^245]

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/c7/Olga_Rudge_advertisement.jpg/250px-Olga_Rudge_advertisement.jpg)

Olga Rudge, 1920

Eliot sent Pound the manuscript of *[The Waste Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waste_Land "The Waste Land")* in 1922. Pound edited it with comments like "make up yr. mind",[^245] and reduced it by about half. Eliot wrote in 1946: "I should like to think that the manuscript, with the suppressed passages, had disappeared irrecoverably; yet, on the other hand, I should wish the blue pencilling on it to be preserved as irrefutable evidence of Pound's critical genius." [^246] His dedication in *The Waste Land* was "For Ezra Pound / *il miglior fabbro* " (the "better craftsman"), from Canto 26 of Dante's *Purgatorio*.[^247]

### Meeting Olga Rudge

Pound was 36 when he met the 26-year-old American violinist [Olga Rudge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Rudge "Olga Rudge") in Paris in the summer of 1922.[^248] They were introduced at a salon hosted by the American heiress [Natalie Barney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalie_Barney "Natalie Barney") at her 300-year-old house at 20 Rue Jacob, near the [Boulevard Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulevard_Saint-Germain "Boulevard Saint-Germain").[^249] The two moved in different social circles: Rudge was the daughter of a wealthy [Youngstown, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngstown,_Ohio "Youngstown, Ohio"), steel family, living in her mother's Parisian apartment on the [Right Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rive_Droite "Rive Droite"), socializing with aristocrats, while Pound's friends were mostly impoverished writers of the [Left Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rive_Gauche "Rive Gauche").[^250]

### Restarting The Cantos

Twice the length of *[Paradise Lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradise_Lost "Paradise Lost")* and 50 times longer than *The Waste Land*, Pound's 800-page *The Cantos* ("Canto I" to "Canto CXVI", c. 1917–1962) became his life's work.[^22] His obituary in *The Times* described it as not a great poem, because of the lack of structure, but a great improvisation: "\[T\]he exasperating form permits the occasional, and in the early *Cantos* and in *The Pisan Cantos* not so occasional, irruption of passages of great poetry, hot and burning lava breaking through the cracks in piles of boring [scree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scree "Scree")." [^252]

Canto CXVI

> I have brought the great ball of crystal;  
> Who can lift it?  
> Can you enter the great acorn of light?  
> But the beauty is not the madness  
> Tho' my errors and wrecks lie about me.  
> And I am not a demigod,[^23]  
> I cannot make it cohere.

—  *[The Paris Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Paris_Review "The Paris Review")*, 1962 [^24]

The first three [cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canto "Canto") had been published in *Poetry* magazine in June, July, and August 1917,[^212] but in 1922 Pound abandoned most of his work and began again.[^254] The early cantos, the "Ur-Cantos", became "Canto I" of the new work.[^255] In letters to his father in 1924 and 1927, Pound said *The Cantos* was like the medley of voices you hear when you turn the radio dial,[^25] and "\[r\]ather like or unlike subject and response and counter subject in [fugue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugue "Fugue") ":

> A.A. Live man goes down into world of Dead.  
> C.B. The 'repeat in history'.  
> B.C. The 'magic moment' or moment of metamorphosis, bust thru from quotidien into 'divine or permanent world.' Gods., etc.[^257]

Alluding to American, European and Oriental art, history and literature, the work is also autobiographical.[^258] In the view of Pound scholar Carroll F. Terrell, it is a great religious poem, describing humanity's journey from hell to paradise, a "revelation of how divinity is manifested in the universe... the kind of intelligence that makes the cherrystone become a cherry tree." [^259] The poet [Allen Tate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Tate "Allen Tate") argued in 1949 that it is "about nothing at all... a voice but no subject".[^260] Responding to *A Draft of XXX Cantos* (1930), [F. R. Leavis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._R._Leavis "F. R. Leavis") criticized its "lack of form, grammar, principle and direction".[^261] The lack of form became a common criticism.[^262] [^26] Pound wrote in the final complete canto, "Canto CXVI" (116, first published in *[The Paris Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Paris_Review "The Paris Review")* in 1962), that he could not "make it cohere",[^264] although a few lines later, referring to the universe: "it coheres all right / even if my notes do not cohere." [^265] According to Pound scholar Walter Baumann, the [demigod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demigod "Demigod") of "Canto CXVI"—"And I am not a demigod"—is [Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles "Heracles") of [Sophocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophocles "Sophocles") 's *[Women of Trachis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_of_Trachis "Women of Trachis")* (450–425 BCE), who exclaims before he dies (based on Pound's translation): "SPLENDOUR, / IT ALL COHERES".[^23] "Canto CXVI" ends with the lines "a little light, like a [rushlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushlight "Rushlight") / to lead back to splendour." [^267]

## Italy (1924–1939)

### Birth of the children

The Pounds were unhappy in Paris. Dorothy complained about the winters and Ezra's health was poor.[^268] At one dinner in the [Place de l'Odéon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rue_de_l'Od%C3%A9on "Rue de l'Odéon"), a [Surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealist "Surrealist") guest high on drugs had tried to stab Pound in the back; [Robert McAlmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McAlmon "Robert McAlmon") had wrestled with the attacker, and the guests had managed to leave before the police arrived.[^269] For Pound the event underlined that their time in France was over.[^270] They decided to move to a quieter place, leaving in October 1924 for the seaside town of [Rapallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapallo "Rapallo") in northern Italy.[^271] Hemingway wrote in a letter that Pound had "indulged in a small nervous breakdown" during the packing, leading to two days at the [American Hospital of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hospital_of_Paris "American Hospital of Paris") in [Neuilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuilly-sur-Seine "Neuilly-sur-Seine").[^272] During this period the Pounds lived on Dorothy's income, supplemented by dividends from stock she had invested in.[^273]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/dc/Home_of_Olga_Rudge%2C_Venice.jpg/250px-Home_of_Olga_Rudge%2C_Venice.jpg)

Olga Rudge 's home in Venice, from 1928, at Calle Querini 252. The plaque can be translated as: Without ever stopping loving Venice, Ezra Pound, titan of poetry, lived in this house for half a century.

Pregnant by Pound, Olga Rudge followed the couple to Italy, and in July 1925 she gave birth to a daughter, [Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_de_Rachewiltz "Mary de Rachewiltz"), in a hospital in [Brixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixen "Brixen") (Italian: Bressanone). Rudge and Pound placed the baby with a German-speaking peasant woman in [Gais, South Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gais,_South_Tyrol "Gais, South Tyrol"), whose own child had died and who agreed to raise Maria for 200 lire a month.[^274] Pound reportedly believed that artists ought not to have children, because in his view motherhood ruined women. According to [Hadley Richardson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadley_Richardson "Hadley Richardson"), he took her aside before she and Hemingway left Paris for Toronto to have [their child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Hemingway "Jack Hemingway"), telling her: "Well, I might as well say goodbye to you here and now because \[the baby\] is going to change you completely." [^275]

At the end of December 1925 Dorothy went on holiday to Egypt, returning on 1 March,[^276] and in May the Pounds and Olga Rudge left Rapallo for Paris to attend a semi-private concert performance at the [Salle Pleyel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salle_Pleyel "Salle Pleyel") of *Le Testament de Villon*, a one-act opera Pound had composed ("nearly tuneless", according to Carpenter) with the musicians Agnes Bedford and [George Antheil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Antheil "George Antheil").[^277] [^27] Pound had hired two singers for the performance; Rudge was on violin, Pound played percussion, and Joyce, Eliot and Hemingway were in the audience.[^279]

The couple stayed on in Paris after the performance; Dorothy was pregnant and wanted the baby to be born at the American hospital. Hemingway accompanied her there in a taxi for the birth of a son, [Omar Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Pound "Omar Pound"), on 10 September 1926.[^280] (Ezra was an admirer of [Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_FitzGerald_\(poet\) "Edward FitzGerald (poet)") 's translation of [Omar Khayyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Khayyam "Omar Khayyam").) [^281] Ezra signed the birth certificate the following day at Neuilly town hall and wrote to his father, "next generation (male) arrived. Both D & it appear to be doing well." [^280] Ezra ended up in the American hospital himself for tests and, he told Olga, a "small operation".[^282] Dorothy took Omar to England, where she stayed for a year and thereafter visited him every summer. He was sent to live at first in [Felpham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felpham "Felpham"), Sussex, with a former superintendent of [Norland College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norland_College "Norland College"), which trains nannies,[^283] and later became a boarder at [Charterhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charterhouse_School "Charterhouse School").[^284] When Dorothy was in England with Omar during the summers, Ezra would spend the time with Olga.[^285] Olga's father helped her buy a house in Venice in 1928,[^286] and from 1930 she also rented the top floor of a house in Sant'Ambrogio, Caso 60, near the Pounds in Rapallo.[^287]

### The Exile, Dial poetry award

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/en/thumb/5/5e/Ezra_Pound_by_EO_Hoppe_1920.jpg/250px-Ezra_Pound_by_EO_Hoppe_1920.jpg)

Pound in 1920 by E. O. Hoppe

In 1925 a new literary magazine, *This Quarter*, dedicated its first issue to Pound, including tributes from Hemingway and Joyce.[^288] In Hemingway's contribution, "Homage to Ezra", he wrote that Pound "devotes perhaps one fifth of his working time to writing poetry and in this twenty per cent of effort writes a large and distinguished share of the really great poetry that has been written by any American living or dead—or any Englishman living or dead or any Irishman who ever wrote English." [^289]

> With the rest of his time he tries to advance the fortunes, both material and artistic, of his friends. He defends them when they are attacked, he gets them into magazines and out of jail. He loans them money. He sells their pictures. He arranges concerts for them. He writes articles about them. He introduces them to wealthy women. He gets publishers to take their books. He sits up all night with them when they claim to be dying and he witnesses their wills. He advances them hospital expenses and dissuades them from suicide. And in the end a few of them refrain from knifing him at the first opportunity.[^289]

Against Hemingway's positive view of Pound, Richard Aldington told Amy Lowell that year that Pound had been almost forgotten in England: "as the rest of us go up, he goes down", he wrote.[^290] In the U.S., Pound won the $2,000 *[Dial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dial "The Dial")* poetry award in 1927 [^291] for his translation of the [Confucian classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucian_classic "Confucian classic") *[Great Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Learning "Great Learning")*.[^292] Using the prize money, he launched his own literary magazine, *The Exile*, in March, but only four issues appeared. It did well in the first year, with contributions from Hemingway, [E. E. Cummings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._E._Cummings "E. E. Cummings"), Basil Bunting, Yeats, William Carlos Williams, and [Robert McAlmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McAlmon "Robert McAlmon").[^293] Some of the poorest work consisted of Pound's rambling editorials on [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism "Confucianism") or in praise of Lenin, according to biographer J. J. Wilhelm.[^294] His parents visited him in Rapallo that year, seeing him for the first time since 1914. His father had retired, so they moved to Rapallo themselves, taking a small house, Villa Raggio, on a hill above the town.[^295]

### Antisemitism, social credit

Pound's [antisemitism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antisemitism "Antisemitism") can be traced to at least 1910, when he wrote in *Patria Mia*, his essays for the *New Age*: "The Jew alone can retain his detestable qualities, despite climatic conditions." The sentence was removed from the 1950 edition.[^129] In 1922, he apparently disliked that so many Jews were contributing to *[The Dial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dial "The Dial")*,[^296] and, in 1939, when he read his poetry at [Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard "Harvard"), he was said to have included antisemitic poems in the program because he believed there were Jews in the audience.[^297] [^28]

A friend of Pound's, the writer Lina Caico, wrote to him in March 1937, asking him to use his musical contacts to help a German-Jewish pianist in Berlin who did not have enough money to live on because of the [Nuremberg Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_Laws "Nuremberg Laws"). Normally willing to help fellow artists, Pound replied (at length): "You hit a nice sore spot... Let her try Rothschild and some of the bastards who are murdering 10 million anglo saxons in England." [^300] He nevertheless denied being an antisemite; he said he liked [Baruch Spinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baruch_Spinoza "Baruch Spinoza"), [Michel de Montaigne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_de_Montaigne "Michel de Montaigne"), and [Alexander del Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_del_Mar "Alexander del Mar"). "What I am driving at", he wrote to [Jackson Mac Low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Mac_Low "Jackson Mac Low"), "is that some [kike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kike "Kike") might manage to pin an antisem lable on me IF he neglected the mass of my writing." [^301] [^29]

Pound came to believe that World War I had been caused by finance capitalism, which he called "usury",[^46] and that the Jews had been to blame. He believed the solution lay in [C. H. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._Douglas "C. H. Douglas") 's idea of [social credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_credit "Social credit").[^140] Pound several times used the term *Leihkapital* (loan capital), equating it with Jews.[^303] Hitler had used the same term in *[Mein Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Kampf "Mein Kampf")* (1926).[^304] [^30] "Your enemy is Das Leihkapital," Pound wrote, in a 1942 radio script aimed at the UK, "international, wandering Loan Capital. Your enemy is not Germany, your enemy is money on loan. And it would be better to be infected with typhus... than to be infected with this blindness which prevents you from understanding HOW you are undermined... The big Jew is so bound up with this Leihkapital that no one is able to unscramble that omelet." [^306] The argument ran that without "usury" and Jews, there would be no class conflict.[^307]

In addition to presenting his economic ideas in hundreds of articles and in *The Cantos*, Pound wrote more than 1,000 letters a year throughout the 1930s.[^308] From 1932, he wrote 180 articles for *The New English Weekly*, a social-credit journal founded by A. R. Orage, and 60 for *Il Mare*, a Rapallo newspaper.[^309] He wrote to [Bill Bird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Bird "Bill Bird") that the press in Paris was controlled by the [Comité des forges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comit%C3%A9_des_forges "Comité des forges"). He also came under the influence of [Charles Maurras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Maurras "Charles Maurras"), who led the far-right *[Action Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_Fran%C3%A7aise "Action Française")*.[^310]

In 1935, Pound wrote an essay, "What is Money For?" where he advocated for [Silvio Gesell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silvio_Gesell "Silvio Gesell") 's concept of [free money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demurrage_currency "Demurrage currency"). In the essay, he criticized usury and argued that "vegetable money" was necessary to put people whose livelihoods are dependent on producing food on an equal level playing field with people with already have plenty of money stored in banks.[^311]

### Meeting Mussolini

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/48/Benito_Mussolini_1922.jpeg/250px-Benito_Mussolini_1922.jpeg)

Benito Mussolini in 1922

In December 1932, Pound requested a meeting with [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini") after being hired to work on a film script about [Italian fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_fascism "Italian fascism"). Pound had asked to see Mussolini previously—Olga Rudge had played privately for Mussolini on 19 February 1927—but this time he was given an audience.[^312] They met on 30 January 1933 at the [Palazzo Venezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Venezia "Palazzo Venezia") in Rome, the day [Adolf Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolf_Hitler "Adolf Hitler") was appointed [Chancellor of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Germany "Chancellor of Germany").[^313]

When Pound handed Mussolini a copy of *A Draft of XXX Cantos*, Mussolini reportedly said of a passage Pound highlighted that it was not English. Pound said: "No, it's my idea of the way a continental Jew would speak English", to which Mussolini replied "How entertaining" (*divertente*).[^314] Pound tried to discuss an 18-point draft of his economic theories.[^314] (Daniel Swift writes that this story has been "told and retold, and in each version, the details shift".) [^315] Pound recorded the meeting in "Canto XLI".[^316]

Pound wrote to C. H. Douglas that he had "never met anyone who seemed to *get* my ideas so quickly as the boss".[^317] The meeting left him feeling that he had become a person of influence, Redman writes, someone who had been consulted by a head of state.[^318] When he returned to Rapallo, he was greeted at the station by the town band.[^314]

Canto XLI

> ![M](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/d/d1/Handfull_img_initial_m.png/60px-Handfull_img_initial_m.png)  
> said [the Boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini "Benito Mussolini"), "è divertente."  
> catching the point before the aesthetes had got  
> there;  
> Having [drained off the muck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontine_Marshes#Bonifica_integrale "Pontine Marshes") by [Vada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vada,_Rosignano_Marittimo "Vada, Rosignano Marittimo")  
> From the marshes, by [Circeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Circeo "Mount Circeo"), where no one else wd. have  
> drained it.  
> Waited 2000 years, ate grain from the marshes:  
> Water supply for ten million, another one million " *vani* "  
> that is rooms for people to live in.  
> XI of [our era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Era_Fascista "Era Fascista").

—  On meeting Mussolini [^319]

Immediately after the meeting, Pound began writing *The ABC of Economics* and *[Jefferson and/or Mussolini: L'Idea Statale Fascism as I Have Seen It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_and/or_Mussolini "Jefferson and/or Mussolini")* (1935). The latter was ready by the end of February,[^320] although he had trouble finding a publisher. In 1942, Pound told Italy's Royal Finance Office that he had written the book for propaganda purposes in Italy's interests.[^321] He wrote articles praising Mussolini and fascism for T. S. Eliot's *[The Criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Criterion "The Criterion")* in July 1933, the *[New York World Telegram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_World_Telegram "New York World Telegram")* in November 1933, the *[Chicago Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Tribune "Chicago Tribune")* on 9 April 1934,[^322] and in 65 articles for the *British-Italian Bulletin*, published by the Italian Embassy in London.[^323]

Pound's antisemitism deepened with the introduction of the [Italian racial laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_racial_laws "Italian racial laws") in 1938,[^31] preceded by the publication in July that year of the [Manifesto of Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto_of_Race "Manifesto of Race"). Mussolini instituted restrictions against Jews, who had to register. Foreign Jews lost their Italian citizenship, and on 18 September 1938 Mussolini declared Judaism "an irreconcilable enemy of fascism".[^325]

### Visit to the United States

When Olivia Shakespear died in October 1938 in London, Dorothy asked Ezra to organize the funeral, where he saw their 12-year-old son, Omar, for the first time in eight years. He visited Eliot and [Wyndham Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyndham_Lewis "Wyndham Lewis"), who produced [a famous portrait of Pound reclining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Pound_\(Lewis\) "Ezra Pound (Lewis)").[^326]

Believing he could stop America's involvement in World War II, Pound sailed for New York in April 1939 on the [SS *Rex*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Rex "SS Rex") in a first-class suite.[^327] [^32] Giving interviews on the deck in a tweed jacket, he told reporters that Mussolini wanted peace.[^327] In Washington, D.C., he attended a session of Congress, sitting in a section of the gallery reserved for relatives (because of [Thaddeus Coleman Pound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaddeus_Coleman_Pound "Thaddeus Coleman Pound")).[^328] He lobbied senators and congressmen,[^329] had lunch with the Polish ambassador, warning him not to trust the English or Winston Churchill,[^330] and asked to see the President but was told it could not be done.[^328]

He took part in a poetry reading at Harvard, where he agreed to be recorded by the Department of Speech,[^331] and in July he received an honorary doctorate from Hamilton College, along with the radio commentator [H. V. Kaltenborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._V._Kaltenborn "H. V. Kaltenborn"). Kaltenborn, whom Pound referred to at the time as Kaltenstein, gave an anti-fascist speech after lunch ("dictatorships shall die, but democracies shall live"), which Pound interrupted loudly to the point where, according to one account, the college president had to intervene.[^332] Pound described this years later to Wyndham Lewis: "That was a music hall day, with a stage set/ only at a Kawledg Komencement wd/ one git in mouth-shot at that sort of wind-bag/ that fahrt Kaltenbourne." [^333] [^33] Pound sailed back to Italy a few days later on the [SS *Conte di Savoia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Conte_di_Savoia "SS Conte di Savoia").[^335]

Between May and September 1939 Pound wrote 12 articles for the *[Japan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Japan_Times "The Japan Times")* (he became their "Italian correspondent"),[^336] which included the claim that "Democracy is now currently defined in Europe as a 'country run by Jews'".[^337] He discussed the "essential fairness of Hitler's war aims" and wrote that Churchill was a senile front for the Rothschilds.[^338]

## World War II and fascist radio broadcasts (1939–1945)

### Letter-writing campaign

When [war broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II#War_breaks_out_in_Europe_\(1939%E2%80%9340\) "World War II") in September 1939, Pound began a letter-writing campaign to the politicians he had petitioned months earlier.[^339] On 18 June 1940, after the [fall of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France "Battle of France"), he wrote to Senator [Burton K. Wheeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burton_K._Wheeler "Burton K. Wheeler"): "I have read a regulation that only those foreigners are to be admitted to the U.S. who are deemed to be useful etc/. The dirtiest jews from Paris, [Blum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9on_Blum "Léon Blum")??" He explained that they were all a pox.[^340] To his publisher, [James Laughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Laughlin "James Laughlin"), he wrote that "Roosevelt represents Jewry" and signed off with "Heil Hitler".[^330] He began calling Roosevelt "Jewsfeldt" or "Stinky Rooosenstein".[^337] In *[Meridiano di Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridiano_di_Roma "Meridiano di Roma")* he compared Hitler and Mussolini to [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius "Confucius").[^338] In [Oswald Mosley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Mosley "Oswald Mosley") 's newspaper, *[Action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_\(newspaper\) "Action (newspaper)")*, he wrote that the English were "a slave race governed by the [House of Rothschild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rothschild_family "Rothschild family") since Waterloo".[^337] By May 1940, according to the historian [Matthew Feldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Feldman_\(historian\) "Matthew Feldman (historian)"), the British government regarded Pound as "a principal supplier of information to the BUF \[[British Union of Fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Union_of_Fascists "British Union of Fascists")\] from abroad".[^341] His literary agent in New York, [John J. Slocum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Slocum "John J. Slocum"), urged him to return to writing poetry and literary criticism; instead, Pound sent Slocum political manifestos, which he declined to attempt to publish in the United States.[^342]

### Radio broadcasts

Radio broadcast

> You let in the Jew and the Jew rotted your Empire, and you yourselves are (doomed) by the Jew.

—  Ezra Pound, Radio Rome, 15 March 1942 [^343]

Between 23 January 1941 [^344] and 28 March 1945, including during the [Holocaust in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_in_Italy "Holocaust in Italy"), Pound recorded or composed hundreds of broadcasts for Italian radio, mostly for [EIAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ente_Italiano_per_le_Audizioni_Radiofoniche "Ente Italiano per le Audizioni Radiofoniche") (Radio Rome) and later for a radio station in the [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic "Italian Social Republic"), a [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state "Puppet state") of [Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Germany "Nazi Germany") in northern and central Italy.[^345] Broadcast in English, and sometimes in Italian, German, and French,[^346] the EIAR program was transmitted to England, central Europe, and the United States.[^347]

Styling himself "Dr Ezra Pound" (his only doctorate was the honorary one from Hamilton College),[^348] he attacked the United States, Roosevelt, Roosevelt's family, Churchill, and the Jews. He praised Hitler, recommended [eugenics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenics "Eugenics") to "conserve the best of the race",[^349] and referred to Jews as "filth".[^350] The broadcasts were monitored by the United States [Foreign Broadcast Monitoring Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Broadcast_Monitoring_Service "Foreign Broadcast Monitoring Service"), and on 26 July 1943 the [United States District Court for the District of Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_District_of_Columbia "United States District Court for the District of Columbia") indicted Pound *in absentia* for [treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treason_in_the_United_States "Treason in the United States").[^351] According to Feldman, the Pound archives at Yale contain receipts for 195 payments from the Italian [Ministry of Popular Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Popular_Culture "Ministry of Popular Culture") from 22 April 1941 to 26 January 1944. Over 33 months, Pound received 250,000 lire (then equivalent to $12,500; $185,000 as of 2013).[^352]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Italian-social-republic-and-civil-war.svg/250px-Italian-social-republic-and-civil-war.svg.png)

Italian Social Republic, September 1943 – May 1945

On 9–10 September 1943, the German [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht "Wehrmacht") occupied northern and central Italy. Hitler appointed Mussolini head of a fascist puppet state, the [Italian Social Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic "Italian Social Republic") or Salò Republic.[^353] Pound called it the "Republic of Utopia".[^354] [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel "Schutzstaffel") officers began concentrating Jews in transit camps before deporting them to [Auschwitz-Birkenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp "Auschwitz concentration camp").[^355] In Rome when the German occupation began, Pound headed north to [Gais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gais,_South_Tyrol "Gais, South Tyrol"), on foot and by train, to visit his daughter, a journey of about 450 miles (720 km).[^356] [^34] On or around 23 November 1943, he met [Fernando Mezzasoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Mezzasoma "Fernando Mezzasoma"), the new Minister of Popular Culture, in [Salò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal%C3%B2 "Salò"). Pound wrote to Dorothy from Salò asking if she could obtain a radio confiscated from the Jews to give to Rudge, so that Rudge could help with his work.[^358]

From 1 December 1943 Pound began writing scripts for the state's new radio station.[^359] The following day he suggested to [Alessandro Pavolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandro_Pavolini "Alessandro Pavolini"), secretary of the [Republican Fascist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Fascist_Party "Republican Fascist Party"), that book stores be legally obliged to showcase certain books, including *[The Protocols of the Elders of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Protocols_of_the_Elders_of_Zion "The Protocols of the Elders of Zion")* (1903), an [Okhrana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhrana "Okhrana") hoax document purporting to be a Jewish plan to dominate the world. "The arrest of Jews will create a wave of useless mercy," Pound wrote, "thus the need to disseminate the Protocols. The intellectuals are capable of a passion more durable than emotional, but they need to understand the reasons for a conflict." [^360] On 26 January 1945, in a script called "Corpses of Course" for the program *Jerry's Front Calling*, Pound wrote: "Why shouldn't there be one grand beano; wiping out Sieff and Kuhn and Loeb and Guggenheim and Stinkenfinger and the rest of the nazal bleaters?" [^361]

### Arrest for treason

In May 1944 the German military, trying to secure the coast against the Allies, forced the Pounds to evacuate their seafront apartment in Rapallo. From then until the end of the war, the couple lived with Rudge in her home above Rapallo at Sant' Ambrogio.[^362] There were food shortages, and no coffee, newspapers, telephones, or letters.[^363] According to Rudge, Ezra and Dorothy would spend their nights listening to the [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_World_Service "BBC World Service").[^364] In addition to the radio scripts, Pound was writing for the newspaper *Il Popolo di Alessandria*. He wanted to write for the more reputable *[Corriere della Sera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriere_della_Sera "Corriere della Sera")* in Milan, but the editor regarded his Italian as "incomprehensible".[^365]

## United States (1945–1958)

### St. Elizabeths Hospital

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Center_building_at_Saint_Elizabeths%2C_August_23%2C_2006.jpg/330px-Center_building_at_Saint_Elizabeths%2C_August_23%2C_2006.jpg)

St. Elizabeths Hospital Center Building, Anacostia, Washington, D.C., 2006

Pound arrived back in Washington, D.C., on 18 November 1945, two days before the start of the [Nuremberg trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg_trials "Nuremberg trials").[^382] Lt. Col. P. V. Holder, one of the escorting officers, wrote in an affidavit that Pound was "an intellectual 'crackpot'" who intended to conduct his own defense.[^383] Dorothy would not allow it; Pound wrote in a letter: "Tell [Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Pound "Omar Pound") I favour a defender who has written a life of [J. Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Adams "John Adams") and translated [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius "Confucius"). Otherwise how CAN he know what it is about?".[^384]

He was arraigned on 27 November on charges of treason,[^35] and on 4 December he was placed in a locked room in the psychiatric ward of [Gallinger Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallinger_Hospital "Gallinger Hospital").[^386] Three court-appointed psychiatrists, including [Winfred Overholser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winfred_Overholser "Winfred Overholser"), superintendent of [St. Elizabeths Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Elizabeths_Hospital "St. Elizabeths Hospital"), decided that he was mentally unfit to stand trial. They found him "abnormally [grandiose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandiosity "Grandiosity")... expansive and exuberant in manner, exhibiting [pressure of speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressure_of_speech "Pressure of speech"), [discursiveness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repetition_\(rhetorical_device\) "Repetition (rhetorical device)") and [distractibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distraction "Distraction")." [^387] A fourth psychiatrist appointed by Pound's lawyer initially thought he was a [psychopath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathy "Psychopathy"), which would have made him unfit to stand trial.[^388]

On 21 December 1945, as case no. 58,102, he was transferred to Howard Hall, St. Elizabeths' maximum security ward, where he was held in a single cell with peepholes.[^389] Visitors were admitted to the waiting room for 15 minutes at a time, while patients wandered around screaming.[^390] A hearing on 13 February 1946 concluded that he was of "unsound mind"; he shouted in court: "I never did believe in Fascism, God damn it; I am opposed to Fascism." [^391] Pound's lawyer, [Julien Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Cornell "Julien Cornell"), requested his release at a hearing in January 1947.[^392] As a compromise, Overholser moved him to the more comfortable Cedar Ward on the third floor of the east wing of St. Elizabeths' Center Building.[^393] In early 1948 he was moved again, this time to a larger room in Chestnut Ward.[^394]

[John Tytell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tytell "John Tytell") writes that Pound was in his element in Chestnut Ward.[^395] At last provided for, he was allowed to read, write, and receive visitors, including Dorothy for several hours a day.[^396] (In October 1946 Dorothy had been placed in charge of his "person and property".[^397]) His room had a typewriter, floor-to-ceiling book shelves, and bits of paper hanging on string from the ceiling with ideas for *The Cantos*.[^395] He had turned a small alcove on the ward into his living room, where he entertained friends and literary figures.[^396] [^36] It reached the point where he refused to discuss any attempt to have him released.[^400]

While in St. Elizabeths, Pound would often decline to talk to psychiatrists with names he deemed Jewish (he called psychiatrists "kikiatrists"),[^401] and he apparently told [Charles Olson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Olson "Charles Olson"): "I was a Zionist in Italy, but now I'm for pogroms, after what I've experienced in here (SLiz)." [^402] He advised visitors to read the *[Protocols of the Elders of Zion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protocols_of_the_Elders_of_Zion "Protocols of the Elders of Zion")*, and he referred to any visitor he happened not to like as Jewish.[^403] In November 1953 he wrote to [Olivia Rossetti Agresti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Rossetti_Agresti "Olivia Rossetti Agresti") that Hitler was "bit by dirty Jew mania for World Domination, as yu used to point out/ this WORST of German diseases was got from yr/ idiolized and filthy biblical bastards. Adolf clear on the baccilus of kikism/ that is on nearly all the other poisons.\[sic\] but failed to get a vaccine against that." [^404]

### The Pisan Cantos, Bollingen Prize

Canto LXXX

> and [the Serpentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Serpentine "The Serpentine") will look just the same  
> and the gulls be as neat on the pond  
> and the sunken garden unchanged  
> and God knows what else is left of our London

—  *[The Pisan Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pisan_Cantos "The Pisan Cantos")* (1948) [^405]

[James Laughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Laughlin "James Laughlin") of [New Directions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Directions_Publishing "New Directions Publishing") had Cantos LXXIV–LXXXIV, known as *The Pisan Cantos*, ready for publication in 1946 and gave Pound an advance copy,[^406] but Laughlin held back, waiting for the right time to publish. A group of Pound's friends—T. S. Eliot, E. E. Cummings, [W. H. Auden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._H._Auden "W. H. Auden"), Allen Tate, and [Joseph Cornell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cornell "Joseph Cornell") —met Laughlin in June 1948 to discuss how to get Pound released. They planned to have him awarded the first [Bollingen Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollingen_Prize "Bollingen Prize"), a new national poetry award with $1,000 prize money donated by the [Mellon family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_family "Mellon family").[^395]

The awards committee consisted of 15 fellows of the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress "Library of Congress"), including several of Pound's supporters, such as Eliot, Tate, Conrad Aiken, [Katherine Anne Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Anne_Porter "Katherine Anne Porter"), and [Theodore Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Spencer "Theodore Spencer").[^37] The idea was that the Justice Department would be in an untenable position if Pound won a major award and was not released.[^395] Laughlin published *The Pisan Cantos* on 20 July 1948,[^408] and the following February the prize went to Pound.[^409] [^38] There were two dissenting voices, [Katherine Garrison Chapin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Garrison_Chapin "Katherine Garrison Chapin") and [Karl Shapiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Shapiro "Karl Shapiro"); the latter said he could not vote for an antisemite because he was Jewish himself.[^411] Pound had apparently prepared a statement—"No comment from the Bug House"—but decided instead to stay silent.[^412]

There was uproar.[^413] The *Pittsburgh Post-Gazette* quoted critics who said that poetry cannot "convert words into maggots that eat at human dignity and still be good poetry".[^414] [Robert Hillyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hillyer "Robert Hillyer"), a Pulitzer Prize winner and president of the [Poetry Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry_Society_of_America "Poetry Society of America"), attacked the committee in *[The Saturday Review of Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Saturday_Review_of_Literature "The Saturday Review of Literature")*,[^415] telling journalists that he "never saw anything to admire, not one line, in Pound".[^416] Congressman [Jacob K. Javits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_K._Javits "Jacob K. Javits") demanded an investigation into the awards committee. It was the last time the Library of Congress administered the prize.[^411]

### Diagnosis

During a case conference at St. Elizabeths on 28 January 1946, six psychiatrists had concluded that Pound had [psychopathic personality disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathic_personality_disorder "Psychopathic personality disorder") but was not [psychotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis "Psychosis"). Present during the meeting, he decided to lie on the floor while the psychiatrists interviewed him.[^417] In 1952 the American Psychiatric Association published its first *[Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnostic_and_Statistical_Manual_of_Mental_Disorders "Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders")* (DSM-1), and St. Elizabeths began diagnosing patients according to its definitions. In July 1953 a psychiatrist added to Pound's notes that he probably had [narcissistic personality disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissistic_personality_disorder "Narcissistic personality disorder"). The main feature of Pound's personality, he wrote, was his "profound, incredible, over-weaning *(sic)* narcissism". A [personality disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_disorder "Personality disorder"), unlike conditions that give rise to psychosis, is not regarded as a [mental illness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_illness "Mental illness"), and the diagnosis would have made Pound fit to stand trial. On 31 May 1955, at the request of the hospital's superintendent Winfred Overholser, the diagnosis was changed to "psychotic disorder, undifferentiated", which is classified as mental illness.[^418] In 1966, after his release from St. Elizabeths, Pound was diagnosed with [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder").[^419]

### Political activities while hospitalized

Pound struck up a friendship with [Eustace Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eustace_Mullins "Eustace Mullins"), apparently associated with the Aryan League of America and author of the 1961 biography *This Difficult Individual, Ezra Pound*.[^420] He was also visited in the hospital by American Nazi leader [Matt Koehl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Koehl "Matt Koehl"),[^421] and befriended [John Kasper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kasper "John Kasper"), a [Ku Klux Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan "Ku Klux Klan") member who, after *[Brown v. Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_v._Board_of_Education "Brown v. Board of Education")* (a 1954 U.S. Supreme Court decision mandating racial desegregation in public schools), set up a [Citizens' Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_Council "Citizens' Council") chapter, the Seaboard White Citizens' Council in Washington.[^422] Members had to be white, supportive of racial segregation, and believers in the divinity of Jesus.[^423] Kasper wrote to Pound after admiring him at university, and the two became friends.[^424] In 1953 Kasper opened a far-right bookstore, "Make it New", at 169 [Bleecker Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleecker_Street "Bleecker Street"), [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village "Greenwich Village"),[^425] that displayed Pound's work in the window.[^426] With Pound's cooperation, he and another Pound admirer, T. David Horton, set up Square Dollar Series, a publishing imprint that reprinted Pound's books and others he approved of.[^427]

It became increasingly clear that Pound was schooling Kasper in the latter's pro-segregation activism.[^428] In January and February 1957 the *[New York Herald Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Herald_Tribune "New York Herald Tribune")* ran a series of articles on their relationship, after which the FBI began photographing Pound's visitors.[^429] One article alleged that some of Kasper's pamphlets had, as [John Tytell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tytell "John Tytell") put it, "a distinctly Poundian ring" to them.[^430] [^39] Kasper was jailed in 1956 over a speech he made in Clinton, Tennessee,[^431] and he was questioned about the [1957 bombing of the Hattie Cotton School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hattie_Cotton_Elementary_School_bombing "Hattie Cotton Elementary School bombing") in Nashville.[^432] After Pound left hospital in 1958, the men kept in touch; he wrote to Kasper on 17 April 1959: "Antisemitism is a card in the enemy program, don't play it.... They RELY ON YOUR PLAYING IT." [^433]

### New Times articles

Between late 1955 and early 1957,[^434] Pound wrote at least 80 unsigned or pseudonymous articles—"often ugly", Swift notes—for the *New Times* of Melbourne, a newspaper connected to the [social-credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Credit_Party "Douglas Credit Party") movement. Noel Stock, one of Pound's correspondents and early biographers, worked for the paper and published Pound's articles there.[^435] A 24-year-old radio reporter at the time, Stock first wrote to Pound in hospital after reading *The Pisan Cantos*.[^436]

In the *New Times* in April 1956, Pound wrote: "Our Victorian forebears would have been greatly scandalized at the idea that one might not be free to study inherited racial characteristics", and "Some races are retentive, mainly of the least desirable bits of their barbaric past." There was a "Jewish-Communist plot", which he compared to syphilis. Equality was dismissed as "anti-biological nonsense".[^437] "There were no gas ovens in Italy", he wrote in April 1956; a month later he referred to the "fuss about Hitler".[^438] On 10 August 1956: "It is perfectly well known that the fuss about 'de-segregation' in the United States has been started by Jews." Instead, America needed "race pride".[^437] Using pseudonyms, he sent his articles directly to Stock, so that the newspaper's editor may not have realized they had all been written by Pound. Stock sent Pound copies of the published articles, which he would distribute to his followers.[^439] He contributed similar material to other publications, including *Edge*,[^440] which Stock founded in October 1956.[^441] Stock called *Edge* the magazine of the "international Poundian underground".[^436]

### Release

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/0c/Ezra_Pound_1963b.jpg/250px-Ezra_Pound_1963b.jpg)

Pound photographed on a walk in Venice, 1963

Pound's friends continued to try to get him out of St. Elizabeths. In 1948, in an effort to present his radio broadcasts as harmless, Olga Rudge self-published six of them (on cultural topics only) as *[If This Be Treason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/If_This_Be_Treason "If This Be Treason")*.[^442] She visited him twice, in 1952 and 1955, but could not convince him to be more assertive about his release.[^443] In 1950 she had written to Hemingway to complain that Pound's friends had not done enough. Hemingway and Rudge did not like each other.[^444] He told Dorothy in 1951 that "the person who makes least sense... in all this is Olga Rudge".[^445] In what John Cohassey called a "controlled, teeth-gritting response", Hemingway replied to Rudge that he would pardon Pound if he could, but that Pound had "made the rather serious mistake of being a traitor to his country, and temporarily he must lie in the bed he made". He ended by saying "To be even more blunt, I have always loved Dorothy, and still do." [^444]

Four years later, shortly after he won the [Nobel Prize in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Literature "List of Nobel laureates in Literature") in 1954, Hemingway told [*Time* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_magazine "Time magazine") "I believe this would be a good year to release poets." [^446] The poet [Archibald MacLeish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_MacLeish "Archibald MacLeish") asked him in June 1957 to write a letter on Pound's behalf. Hemingway believed Pound would not stop making inappropriate statements and friendships, but he signed MacLeish's letter anyway and pledged $1,500 to be handed to Pound upon his release.[^447] In an interview for *The Paris Review* in early 1958, Hemingway said that Pound should be released and Kasper jailed.[^448]

Several publications began campaigning in 1957. *[Le Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Figaro "Le Figaro")* published an appeal titled "The Lunatic at St Elizabeths". *The New Republic*, *Esquire*, and *The Nation* followed suit. *The Nation* argued that Pound was a "sick and vicious old man", but that he had rights.[^449] In 1958 MacLeish hired [Thurman Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurman_Arnold "Thurman Arnold"), a prestigious lawyer who ended up charging no fee, to file a motion to dismiss the 1945 indictment. Overholser, the hospital's superintendent, supported the application with an affidavit stating Pound was permanently and incurably insane, and that confinement served no therapeutic purpose.[^450] The motion was heard on 18 April 1958 by Chief Judge [Bolitha Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolitha_Laws "Bolitha Laws"), who had committed Pound to St. Elizabeths in 1945. The Justice Department did not oppose the motion,[^451] and Pound was discharged on 7 May.[^452]

## Italy (1958–1972)

### Depression

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ea/Ezra_Pound_in_1958%2C_with_Usher_Burdick_3.jpg/330px-Ezra_Pound_in_1958%2C_with_Usher_Burdick_3.jpg)

Pound with Congressman Usher Burdick just after his release from St. Elizabeth's in 1958. Burdick had helped to secure the release. 453

Pound and Dorothy arrived in Naples on the [SS *Cristoforo Colombo*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_Cristoforo_Colombo "SS Cristoforo Colombo") on 9 July 1958, where Pound was photographed giving a [fascist salute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_salute#Italy "Roman salute") to the waiting press.[^454] When asked when he had been released from the mental hospital, he replied: "I never was. When I left the hospital I was still in America, and all America is an insane asylum." [^455] They were accompanied by a young teacher Pound had met in hospital, Marcella Spann, ostensibly acting as his secretary.[^456] Disembarking at Genoa, the group arrived three days later at [Schloss Brunnenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schloss_Brunnenburg "Schloss Brunnenburg"), near [Merano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merano "Merano") in [South Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrol "South Tyrol"), to live with his daughter Maria,[^457] where Pound met his grandchildren for the first time.[^458] [^40] Dorothy had usually ignored his affairs, but she used her legal power over his royalties to make sure Spann was seen off, sent back to the United States in October 1959.[^460]

By December 1959 Pound was mired in depression.[^461] According to the writer Michael Reck, who visited him several times at St. Elizabeths,[^462] Pound was a changed man; he said little and called his work "worthless".[^463] In a 1960 interview in Rome with [Donald Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Hall "Donald Hall") for *The Paris Review*, he said: "You—find me—in fragments." He paced up and down during the three days it took to complete the interview, never finishing a sentence, bursting with energy one minute, then sagging, and at one point seemed about to collapse. Hall said it was clear that he "doubted the value of everything he had done in his life".[^464]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/ba/Tirol_Brunnenburg.jpg/330px-Tirol_Brunnenburg.jpg)

In 1958 Ezra and Dorothy lived with Mary at Brunnenburg.

Those close to him thought he had dementia, and in mid-1960 he spent time in a clinic when his weight dropped. He picked up again, but by early 1961 he had a urinary tract infection. Dorothy felt unable to look after him, so he went to live with Olga Rudge, first in Rapallo then in Venice; Dorothy mostly stayed in London after that with Omar.[^465] In 1961 Pound attended a meeting in Rome in honor of [Oswald Mosley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_Mosley "Oswald Mosley"), who was visiting Italy.[^466] [^41]

In 1966 he was admitted to the [Genoa School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Genoa "University of Genoa") 's psychiatric hospital for an evaluation after prostate surgery. His notes said he had [psychomotor retardation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_retardation "Psychomotor retardation"), insomnia, depression, and he believed he had been "contaminated by microbes".[^469] According to a psychiatrist who treated him, Pound had previously been treated with [electroconvulsive therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electroconvulsive_therapy "Electroconvulsive therapy"). This time he was given [imipramine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imipramine "Imipramine") and responded well. The doctors diagnosed [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder").[^419] Two years later he attended the opening of an exhibition in New York featuring his blue-inked version of Eliot's *The Waste Land*.[^470] He went on to Hamilton College and received a standing ovation.[^471]

### Reck allegations

Pound's biographer, Michael Reck, claimed to have had an encounter with Pound at the restaurant of the Pensione Cici in Venice in 1967,[^462] during which Pound told [Allen Ginsberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Ginsberg "Allen Ginsberg") and [Peter Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Russell_\(poet\) "Peter Russell (poet)") that his own poems were "a lot of double talk" and made no sense, and that his writing was "a mess", "stupid and ignorant all the way through". Reck wrote about the meeting in *[Evergreen Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_Review "Evergreen Review")* the following year. "At seventy I realized that instead of being a lunatic, I was a moron", Pound reportedly said. He "looked very morose" and barely spoke: "There is nothing harder than conversing with Pound nowadays", Reck wrote.[^472]

Pound had offered a carefully worded rejection of his antisemitism, according to Reck. When Ginsberg reassured Pound that he had "shown us the way", he is said to have replied: "Any good I've done has been spoiled by bad intentions—the preoccupation with irrelevant and stupid things." Reck continued: "Then very slowly, with emphasis, surely conscious of Ginsberg's being Jewish: 'But the worst mistake I made was that stupid, suburban prejudice of anti-Semitism.'" [^473] [^42]

### Death

![photograph](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/6/68/Gravesite_of_Ezra_Pound_%26_Olga_Rudge.jpg/330px-Gravesite_of_Ezra_Pound_%26_Olga_Rudge.jpg)

The graves of Pound and Olga Rudge at San Michele cemetery on the Isola di San Michele

Shortly before his death in 1972, an [American Academy of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Arts_and_Sciences "American Academy of Arts and Sciences") committee, which included his publisher James Laughlin, proposed that Pound be awarded the [Emerson-Thoreau Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson-Thoreau_Medal "Emerson-Thoreau Medal"). After a storm of protest, the academy's council opposed it by 13 to 9.[^476] In the foreword of a [Faber & Faber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faber_&_Faber "Faber & Faber") volume of his prose, he wrote in July: "In sentences referring to groups or races 'they' should be used with great care. re USURY: / I was out of focus, taking a symptom for a cause. / The cause is AVARICE." [^477]

On his 87th birthday, on 30 October 1972, he was too weak to leave his bedroom. The next night he was admitted to the San Giovanni e Paolo Civil Hospital in Venice, where he died in his sleep on 1 November of "sudden [blockage of the intestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intestinal_blockage "Intestinal blockage") ".[^478] Alerted by telegram, Dorothy Pound, who was living in a care home near Cambridge, England, requested a Protestant funeral in Venice. Telegrams were sent via American embassies in Rome and London, and the consulate in Milan, but Rudge would not change the plans she had already made for the morning of 3 November. Omar Pound flew to Venice as soon as he could, with Peter du Sautoy of Faber & Faber, but he arrived too late.[^479] Four [gondoliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondolier "Gondolier") dressed in black rowed Pound's body to Venice's municipal cemetery, the [San Michele cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Michele_Cemetery,_Venice "San Michele Cemetery, Venice"), where, after a Protestant service, he was buried in the Protestant section of the cemetery, near [Diaghilev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Diaghilev "Sergei Diaghilev") and [Stravinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Stravinsky "Igor Stravinsky") who rest at the adjoining Orthodox section, with other non-Catholic Christians.[^480] According to [Hugh Kenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Kenner "Hugh Kenner"), Pound had wanted to be buried in Idaho with his bust by [Henri Gaudier-Brzeska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Gaudier-Brzeska "Henri Gaudier-Brzeska") on his grave.[^481] Dorothy Pound died in England the following year, aged 87. Olga Rudge died in 1996, aged 100, and was buried next to Pound.[^470]

## Critical reception

### Rehabilitation efforts, scholarship

After the Bollingen Prize in 1949, Pound's friends sought to rehabilitate him.[^482] James Laughlin's [New Directions Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Directions_Publishing "New Directions Publishing") published his *Selected Poems*, with an introduction by Eliot, and a censored selection of *The Cantos*. [Ralph Fletcher Seymour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fletcher_Seymour "Ralph Fletcher Seymour") published *Patria Mia* (written around 1912) to show that Pound was an American patriot.[^483] In advertisements, magazine articles, and critical introductions, Pound's friends and publishers attributed his antisemitism and fascism to mental illness.[^484]

Literary scholar [Betsy Erkkila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betsy_Erkkila?action=edit&redlink=1 "Betsy Erkkila (page does not exist)") writes that no one was more important to Pound's rehabilitation than [Hugh Kenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Kenner "Hugh Kenner"),[^485] who was introduced to Pound by [Marshall McLuhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_McLuhan "Marshall McLuhan") in St. Elizabeths in May 1948, when Kenner was 25.[^486] Kenner's *The Poetry of Ezra Pound* (1951) adopted a [New Critical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Criticism "New Criticism") approach, where all that mattered was the work itself.[^487]

New Directions and [Faber & Faber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faber_&_Faber "Faber & Faber") published *Ezra Pound: Translations* in 1953, introduced by Kenner, and the following year *Literary Essays of Ezra Pound*, introduced by Eliot.[^483] The first PhD dissertation on Pound was completed in 1948, and by 1970 there were around ten a year. Kenner's *[The Pound Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pound_Era "The Pound Era")* (1971), which overlooked the fascism, antisemitism, World War II, treason, and the Bollingen Award, effectively equated Pound with modernism.[^488] Pound scholar Leon Surette argued that Kenner's approach was [hagiographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagiography "Hagiography"). He included in this approach Caroll F. Terrell's *Paideuma: A Journal Devoted to Ezra Pound Scholarship*,[^489] founded in 1972 and edited by Kenner and Eva Hesse,[^490] [^485] [^43] and Terrell's two-volume *A Companion to the Cantos of Ezra Pound* (1980–1984).[^489] In 1971 Terrell founded the [National Poetry Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Poetry_Foundation "National Poetry Foundation") to focus on Pound, and organized conferences on Pound in 1975, 1980, 1985, and 1990.[^492]

Following [Eustace Mullins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eustace_Mullins "Eustace Mullins") 's biography, *This Difficult Individual, Ezra Pound* (1961), was *Life of Ezra Pound* (1970) by Noel Stock. A former reporter, Stock was one of the publishers of Pound's newspaper articles in the 1950s, including his antisemitism.[^493] Ronald Bush's *The Genesis of Ezra Pound's Cantos* (1976) became the first critical study of *The Cantos*.[^494] Several significant biographies appeared in the 1980s: J. J. Wilhelm's three-volume work (1985–1994), beginning with *The American Roots of Ezra Pound*; [John Tytell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tytell "John Tytell") 's *Ezra Pound: The Solitary Volcano* (1987); and [Humphrey Carpenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_Carpenter "Humphrey Carpenter") 's 1005-page *A Serious Character* (1988). A. David Moody's three-volume *Ezra Pound: Poet* (2007–2015) combines biography with literary criticism.[^495]

Studies that examine Pound's relationships with the far right include Robert Casillo's *The Genealogy of Demons* (1988); [Tim Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Redman "Tim Redman") 's *Ezra Pound and Italian Fascism* (1999); Leon Surette's *Pound in Purgatory* (1999);[^496] [Matthew Feldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Feldman_\(historian\) "Matthew Feldman (historian)") 's *Ezra Pound's Fascist Propaganda, 1935–45* (2013); and Alec Marsh's *John Kasper and Ezra Pound* (2015).

### Legacy

Canto CXVI

> A little light, like a [rushlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushlight "Rushlight")  
> To lead back to splendour.

— Closing lines of *[The Cantos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cantos "The Cantos")* [^267]

Much of Pound's legacy lies in his advancement of some of the best-known modernist writers of the early 20th century, particularly between 1910 and 1925.[^497] In addition to Eliot, Joyce, Lewis, Frost, Williams, Hemingway, H.D., Aldington, and Aiken, he befriended and helped Cummings, Bunting, Ford, [Marianne Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianne_Moore "Marianne Moore"), [Louis Zukofsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Zukofsky "Louis Zukofsky"), [Jacob Epstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Epstein "Jacob Epstein"), [Margaret Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_C._Anderson "Margaret C. Anderson"), [George Oppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Oppen "George Oppen"), and [Charles Olson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Olson "Charles Olson").[^498]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/Hieratic_Head_of_Ezra_Pound_01_%28brightened%29.jpg/250px-Hieratic_Head_of_Ezra_Pound_01_%28brightened%29.jpg)

Ezra Pound in marble by Henri Gaudier-Brzeska (1914)

Beyond this, his legacy is mixed. He was a strong lyricist with an "ear" for words;[^499] his *Times* obituary said he had a "faultless sense of [cadence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadence "Cadence") ".[^252] According to [Ira Nadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ira_Nadel "Ira Nadel"), he "overturned poetic meter, literary style, and the state of the long poem". Nadel cited the importance of Pound's editing of *The Waste Land*, the publication of *Ulysses*, and his role in developing of Imagism.[^500] Hugh Witemeyer argued that Imagism was "probably the most important single movement" in 20th-century English-language poetry, because it affected all the leading poets of Pound's generation and the two generations after him.[^501] According to [Hugh Kenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Kenner "Hugh Kenner") in 1951, although no great contemporary writer was less read than Pound, there was no one who could "over and over again appeal more surely, through sheer beauty of language" to people who would otherwise rather talk about poets than read them.[^502]

Against this, [Robert Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Conquest "Robert Conquest") argued in 1979 that critics were responsible for having promoted Pound despite his "minimal talent", which was "grossly exaggerated".[^503] "This is an accusation less against the fantastic arrogance of Pound", he wrote, "than against the narrow-minded obscurantism of the departments of English and the critical establishment who have set up a system of apologetics which the slyest Jesuit of the seventeenth century would have baulked at." [^504] According to [Samuel Putnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Putnam "Samuel Putnam"), those who respected Pound's poetry were less likely to respect his prose or work as a critic.[^505]

The outrage over his collaboration with the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers "Axis powers") was so deep that it dominated the discussion. "A greater calamity cannot befall the art", [Arthur Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Miller "Arthur Miller") wrote in December 1945, "than that Ezra Pound, the Mussolini mouthpiece, should be welcomed back as an arbiter of American letters".[^506] Over the decades, according to Redman, critics argued that Pound was not really a poet or not really a fascist, or that he was a fascist but his poetry is not fascistic, or that there was an evil Pound and a good Pound.[^507] The American poet [Elizabeth Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Bishop "Elizabeth Bishop"), 1956 [Pulitzer Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_in_Poetry "Pulitzer Prize in Poetry") winner and one of his hospital visitors—Pound called her "Liz Bish"—reflected the ambivalence in her poem " [Visits to St. Elizabeths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visits_to_St._Elizabeths "Visits to St. Elizabeths") " (1957).[^508] "This is the time / of the tragic man / that lies in the house of Bedlam." As the poem progresses, the tragic man, never named, becomes the talkative man; the honored man; the old, brave man; the cranky man; the cruel man; the busy man; the tedious man; the poet, the man; and, finally, the wretched man.[^509]
