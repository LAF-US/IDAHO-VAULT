## Canon Notes

### The Three Devices

Penguin Random House's reader's guide names the three special devices across
the trilogy as the alethiometer, the subtle knife, and the amber spyglass. Each
is connected with the pursuit of Dust, but each performs a different grammar:
truth-reading, threshold-cutting, and perception.

### Dust

Dust is not ordinary grime or inert residue. Pullman's author-side explanation
sets up the central dispute: the Authority/Magisterium reads Dust as evidence
of original sin and corruption; Lyra's mature reading treats Dust as valuable,
humanizing, and worth preserving. Dust gathers around consciousness, adulthood,
knowledge, love, kindness, curiosity, and creative life.

Vault reading: **Dust is meaningful residue.** It is the fine-grained evidence
of attention, experience, relation, and moral consequence. Calling something
"dust" is not enough to discard it.

### The Amber Spyglass

The amber spyglass is Mary Malone's perception instrument. It does not create
Dust. It lets a human see what the mulefa can perceive directly. Its lesson is
epistemic before it is mechanical: the right instrument can reveal a real field
that ordinary unaided perception misses.

Vault reading: **a spyglass is a disciplined detection surface.** A source
ledger, branch census, manifest, provenance check, or witness file can function
as a spyglass when it makes meaningful residue visible without pretending to be
the source of meaning.

### Mulefa

The mulefa are not just background creatures. Publisher material foregrounds
them as Dust-seeing beings, and the spyglass is built in relation to their
world and perception. They demonstrate that perception is species-, world-, and
instrument-dependent.

Vault reading: **different agents and environments perceive different Dust.**
Local OS state, GitHub PR state, Obsidian links, Jupyter execution, and human
memory each reveal different parts of the same underlying system.

### Windows

The late-trilogy repair confirms the prior Subtle Knife source note: open
windows are not neutral. They leak Dust. Keeping every crossing open for
convenience would preserve travel while draining the condition that makes
conscious life meaningful.

Vault reading: **every open integration must have a closure plan.** Worktrees,
PRs, credentials, sync routes, plugins, cloud pipes, and branch-to-main cuts
are windows. A useful window without a closure protocol becomes a leak.

### Spectres

Spectres are the predatory consequence of bad threshold practice. They consume
adult consciousness and destroy the capacity to generate Dust. In Vault terms,
they are second-order attention predators: loops, stale automation, duplicated
branches, false signers, and unmanaged state that drain human and agent focus
without returning coherent record.

## Working Notes

- Dust is the opposite of meaningless debris.
- The spyglass is not the knife. The knife opens and cuts; the spyglass sees.
- The Vault needs both instruments: precise crossing and precise perception.
- The Arborscape tailings problem should not be reduced to cleanup. Some
  tailings contain Dust.
- The correct pass is: perceive, classify, salvage, close seams.
- A detector that merely shouts "dust" is not enough. The useful detector
  distinguishes waste dust from Dust-bearing residue.
