As [wildfires blaze across Native nations throughout the Pacific Northwest](https://www.underscore.news/land/wildfire/wildfires-blaze-through-pacific-northwest-reservations/), community organizers have quickly rallied together to support relief efforts for those affected by the devastation left in the wake of the fires.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.underscore.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Donations.jpg?w=2048&ssl=1)

Bags of supplies including first aid, non-perishable food items, hygiene supplies and water sit in bags at a drop-off site at Bison Coffeehouse in Northeast Portland on July 29, 2026, ready to be taken to Warm Springs later in the day. (Photo by Mackenzie Olivo, Underscore Native News)

In Washington, people on the Colville Reservation in the Nespelem area have had to evacuate their homes due to the Kaiser Canyon Fire, currently the [largest active wildfire](https://gacc.nifc.gov/nwcc/index.aspx) in Washington state. As of Wednesday, July 29, more than two dozen homes have been taken by the fire, according to community organizers.

“The whole entire rez is on fire,” said Faith Zacherle Tonasket, a citizen of the Colville Tribes, founder of River Warriors and xa?xa? Indigenous Birth Justice, a nonprofit organization working to support fire victims.

“We’re all doing what we can to take care of our communities,” she added.

### Items needed

For those impacted by wildfires across Oregon and Washington, there is a huge need for supplies including:

- Water
- Personal hygiene supplies — like soap, tampons, pads, toothbrushes
- Childcare supplies — including diapers, wipes, baby formula and bottles
- Non-perishable and no-cook foods
- Gift cards and hotel donations
- Pet supplies
- Blankets and pillows
- Towels
- New socks
- Small fans and box fans
- Filters
- N95 masks and respirators
- Monetary donations

Most of these efforts are being led by community members and non-profit organizations rallying to gather support from across Oregon and Washington during this time of crisis.

“Those systems are already built in, because this is not the first tragedy we’ve experienced,” said Robin-Michel Lenihan, a Two-Spirit citizen of the Cow Creek Band of Umpqua Tribe of Indians, and a wildfire survivor themself. “Fortunately or unfortunately, we’re trained to do this our whole lives to respond to the needs of our communities.”

Lenihan is the advancement manager at the Native Arts and Culture Foundation, which is currently accepting donations for the Warm Springs community affected by the wildfires.

### Organizing for relief

In Oregon, much of the Confederated Tribes of the Warm Springs Reservation has had to evacuate homes as well due to the Bench Fire. At least five homes and two buildings have been lost already.

“I’ll tell you, it’s heavy,” said Gilbert Brown, sitting outside Bison Coffeehouse in Northeast Portland on Wednesday morning, collecting supplies to bring out to Warm Springs later in the day.

“Little kids can’t go outside because \[they\] can’t breathe,” Brown said.

Brown, a citizen of the Klamath Tribes who lives on the North end of the Warm Springs Reservation, has been organizing since Monday to bring supplies from Portland to families in Warm Springs. He has not had to evacuate his home yet, and is hosting two other families who had to evacuate.

“All these places got fires. And we only have so much help for us, so I’m trying to help us,” Brown said.

His nephew lost his home earlier this week due to the fire. As the fire ate through the foundation of his home it also burned regalia, cultural artifacts and heirlooms that had been in his family for generations.

“Just like that, one fire took it away,” Brown said.
