Idaho has some real strengths: low concentrated poverty and strong two-parent household rates. But strengths in family structure don’t make up for underfunded schools, stratospheric incarceration rates for mothers and children and severely limited access to healthcare.

Our economic well-being is falling, with more kids in poverty, less secure parental employment and rising housing cost burdens. W

here we do choose to spend is telling. Idaho spends approximately six times as much per year to keep an adult incarcerated (over $60,000) as to educate a child ($11,127 — the lowest in the country).

Sixty-eight percent of Idaho’s fourth-graders can’t read at grade level, and 69% of our eighth graders aren’t proficient at math, but they are twice as likely to be incarcerated as the average kid in the US. The state budget for incarceration is around $120,000 a year per child.

Canada’s violent crime rate is three percent higher than Idaho, yet Idaho’s incarceration rate is 660% that of Canada. Idaho leads the nation in women’s incarceration at nearly 300% the national rate despite some of the lowest crime rates in America.

Most women are locked up for drug offenses, not violence. Sixty-two percent are somebody’s mother.

Not only is the state of Idaho failing to invest in our children’s freedom and educational futures, Idaho’s kids are losing health insurance at an alarming rate. Considering that it costs Idaho only $2,973 a year to keep a child on Medicaid, it is unconscionable that Idaho kids are 33% more likely to be uninsured than the US average and twice as likely to be uninsured as kids next door in Washington.

Even if Idaho kids could afford healthcare, Idaho has the lowest number of doctors per person of any state and our rural hospitals are shutting down at an accelerating pace. We have 22% less OB/GYN doctors than before the abortion ban and since the ban residency applications for doctors to train in Idaho have taken an extreme downturn in multiple specialties, most notably pediatrics and OB/GYN.

Over half of Idaho counties are “healthcare deserts,” meaning a person must travel to a different county for health care. Lack of health insurance and lack of healthcare availability are strongly correlated with poorer health and economic outcomes for children and their families, so expect all of the above to get worse.

If you want to see Idaho thrive instead of continuing on this destructive path, please call your local representative and let them know that you care about investing in healthy, educated kids with bright futures.

*Dr. Carrie Roller has decades of clinical and public health experience at a large surgical practice. She earned her doctor of medicine degree at Georgetown University, master of public health at the University of Washington and bachelor degree in astrophysics at the University of California, Berkeley.*
