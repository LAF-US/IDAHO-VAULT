**Joëlle Jones** (born February 20, 1980) is an American [comic book artist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comic_book_artist "Comic book artist") and writer, best known for her work on *[Lady Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Killer_\(comic_book\) "Lady Killer (comic book)")*, a series published in 2015–2017 by [Dark Horse Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Horse_Comics "Dark Horse Comics"),[^1] [^2] for her cover work on various [Marvel Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") series, and for her work writing and illustrating [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics "DC Comics") series including *[Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_\(comic_book\) "Batman (comic book)")* and *[Catwoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catwoman_\(comic_book\) "Catwoman (comic book)")*.

## Early life

Joëlle Jones is a native of [Boise, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho").[^3] She attended the [Pacific Northwest College of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest_College_of_Art "Pacific Northwest College of Art"),[^3] [^4] where she studied [oil painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_painting "Oil painting").[^5] She left school to pursue comic book illustration.[^6] She stayed in the [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon") area [^7] for several years, where she worked as a freelance artist for [Dark Horse Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Horse_Comics "Dark Horse Comics") and [Oni Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oni_Press "Oni Press").[^8]

## Career

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/3/31/Joelle_Jones_and_Jamie_S._Rich.jpg/250px-Joelle_Jones_and_Jamie_S._Rich.jpg)

Joëlle Jones and Jamie S. Rich at the 2007 San Diego Comic-Con

Jones's first credited work was as a colorist for [Harris Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris_Publications "Harris Publications") in 2004, back when the company had the rights to [Vampirella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vampirella "Vampirella"). In 2006, her illustrations appeared in the anthology *Sexy Chix*, published by Dark Horse Comics.[^3] The following year, she drew "How are the new Three Little Pigs adjusting to being pigs?", which appeared in *[Fables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fables_\(comics\) "Fables (comics)")* #59, a fantasy series published by the [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics "DC Comics") imprint [Vertigo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertigo_\(DC_Comics\) "Vertigo (DC Comics)").[^3] She returned to the *Fables* universe in 2015 with "The Last Geppetto Story" (which appeared in Issue 150) and *Fables: The Wolf Among Us* Digital Issue 26.

Jones and writer/editor [Jamie S. Rich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_S._Rich "Jamie S. Rich") collaborated on the 2006 [Oni Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oni_Press "Oni Press") book *12 Reasons Why I Love Her* (2006), her first long-form work.[^9] She and Rich would later collaborate on other Oni series, including *You Have Killed Me* (2009) [^10] and *Spell Checkers* (2010-2013).[^11] In 2008, [Minx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minx_\(comics\) "Minx (comics)") (a short-lived [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics "DC Comics") imprint) published the graphic novel *Token*, which was written by [Alisa Kwitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alisa_Kwitney "Alisa Kwitney") and illustrated by Jones.[^12] [^13] [^14]

In 2010, Dark Horse published *Troublemaker*, a graphic novel continuation of [Janet Evanovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Evanovich "Janet Evanovich") 's *Barnaby* series; Jones created the artwork.[^15] *Troublemaker* was released in two parts and then released as a collected trade paperback in 2011. In 2015 Dark Horse Comics published *[Lady Killer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_Killer_\(comic_book\) "Lady Killer (comic book)")*, a miniseries Jones illustrated and co-wrote with Rich about a 1960s housewife who moonlights as an assassin, later collected in trade paperback form. In 2016, Jones received three Eisner Awards nominations for her work on *Lady Killer* and on *Brides of Helheim*: Best Limited Series, Best Penciller/Inker, and Best Cover Artist.[^16]

During the 2010s, Jones continued illustrating titles published by Oni Press, including *House of Night*, *Helheim*, and *Brides of Helheim*.[^17] [^18] [^19] [^20] She also illustrated the graphic novel adaptation of *[The Girl Who Owned a City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Girl_Who_Owned_a_City "The Girl Who Owned a City")*, which was published in 2012 by Graphic Universe.[^21] [^22]

In 2016 Jones wrote *Lady Killer 2*, a five-issue sequel miniseries to the 2015 miniseries, taking on sole writing duties on the title.[^23] In September 2017, artwork inspired by Jones's style was used as part of the runway backdrop at [Prada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prada "Prada") 's spring 2018 exhibition in Milan.[^24]

In 2015 and 2016, Jones illustrated and sometimes drew covers for several issues of [Marvel Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Comics "Marvel Comics") 's *[Mockingbird](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mockingbird_\(Marvel_Comics\) "Mockingbird (Marvel Comics)")*, *[Spider-Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Woman "Spider-Woman")*, *[Scarlet Witch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_Witch "Scarlet Witch")*, and *[Ms. Marvel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ms._Marvel "Ms. Marvel")*.

Jones participated in DC Talent Development Workshop in early 2016 as a writer. *New Talent Showcase 2016* #1, which featured her [Harley Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley_Quinn "Harley Quinn") story, was published that November.[^25]

At the DC Press Breakfast held during the July 2016 [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con "San Diego Comic-Con"), DC announced that Jones signed an exclusivity contract with the publisher.[^1]

Between 2016 and 2017, Jones illustrated the four-part DC series *[Supergirl: Being Super](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergirl:_Being_Super "Supergirl: Being Super")* written by [Mariko Tamaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariko_Tamaki "Mariko Tamaki").[^26]

On March 30, 2017, DC announced that her artwork would be used as the basis for a new series of statues in their Cover Girls line, making her the first female designer of the line. The first four statues were released in 2018,[^27] depicting the characters [Harley Quinn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harley_Quinn "Harley Quinn"), [Batgirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batgirl "Batgirl"), [Supergirl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supergirl "Supergirl"), and [Mera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mera_\(character\) "Mera (character)").[^28] Subsequent statues were released of [Catwoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catwoman "Catwoman"),[^29] [Black Canary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Canary "Black Canary"),[^30] [^31] [Wonder Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Woman "Wonder Woman"),[^32] and [Huntress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntress_\(Helena_Bertinelli\) "Huntress (Helena Bertinelli)").[^33]

In July 2017, DC announced that Jones would draw both the covers and interior art for the [DC Rebirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Rebirth "DC Rebirth") version of *[Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_\(comic_book\) "Batman (comic book)")*, starting with issue #33,[^34] making her the first female artist to draw covers and interior pages on more than two consecutive Batman-centric issues in the character's history.[^35] [^36]

In April 2018, DC announced that Jones would write and draw a new *Catwoman* series.[^36] The first issue was released on July 4, 2018, the same day as *Batman* #50, which depicted Batman and Catwoman's wedding.[^37] The issue also featured the first appearance of the villain Raina Creel, the First Lady of Villa Hermosa.[^38] Jones is the regular writer on the series, and also drew the first six issues.[^39] [^40]

In 2021, following the events of *[Dark Nights: Death Metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Nights:_Death_Metal "Dark Nights: Death Metal")*, Jones created the character of [Yara Flor / Wonder Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yara_Flor "Yara Flor") who was featured in the [Future State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_State "Future State") event before making her appearance in the DC Universe proper in Wonder Girl #1 in 2021.[^41]

In February 2022, Jones came under fire for reportedly tracing Pepe Larraz's art for her work on *Trial of the Amazons*.[^42]

In April 2023, Jones became one of the founding members of the comic book company [DSTLRY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DSTLRY "DSTLRY").[^43]

In January 2024, it was announced that Jones would serve as one of the artist for the [2024 relaunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles_\(2024_comic_book\) "Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (2024 comic book)") of the [IDW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDW_Publishing "IDW Publishing") *[Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_Mutant_Ninja_Turtles "Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles")* series written by [Jason Aaron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Aaron "Jason Aaron").[^44]

## Personal life

As of 2017, Jones is based in Los Angeles.[^7] [^24]
