**John Houston Stockton** (September 23, 1901 – April 27, 1967) was a professional [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football "American football") player, a [back](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football_positions#Backs_and_receivers_\(R\) "American football positions") in the late 1920s in the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League "National Football League") (NFL). He played with the [Frankford Yellow Jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankford_Yellow_Jackets "Frankford Yellow Jackets") from [1925](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1925_NFL_season "1925 NFL season") until [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_NFL_season "1928 NFL season"), and was a member of Yellow Jackets' [1926 NFL Championship team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Frankford_Yellow_Jackets_season "1926 Frankford Yellow Jackets season"). During his final season in [1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_NFL_season "1929 NFL season"), Stockton split time between the [Boston Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bulldogs_\(NFL\) "Boston Bulldogs (NFL)") and the [Providence Steamroller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Steamroller "Providence Steamroller"). He was the grandfather of [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basketball "Basketball") [Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naismith_Memorial_Basketball_Hall_of_Fame "Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame") inductee, [John Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Stockton "John Stockton"), who played [point guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_guard "Point guard") for the [National Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_Association "National Basketball Association") 's [Utah Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Jazz "Utah Jazz") from 1984 to 2003.[^1] [^2]

## Early life

Born in [Parma, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parma,_Idaho "Parma, Idaho"), Stockton set [high school football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_football "High school football") scoring records as a back at [Columbia Prep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Portland "University of Portland") in [Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland,_Oregon "Portland, Oregon").[^3] Originally enrolled at [St. Mary's College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary's_College_of_California "Saint Mary's College of California"), then in [Oakland, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland,_California "Oakland, California"), he transferred as a sophomore in 1922 to [Gonzaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_University "Gonzaga University") in [Spokane, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spokane,_Washington "Spokane, Washington"),[^4] where he played [baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzaga_Bulldogs_baseball "Gonzaga Bulldogs baseball") and [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football "College football").[^5] A triple-threat halfback,[^6] [^7] Stockton threw passes to [Ray Flaherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Flaherty "Ray Flaherty"), a future member of the [Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_Football_Hall_of_Fame "Pro Football Hall of Fame"),[^8] and their head coach was [Gus Dorais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gus_Dorais "Gus Dorais"),[^1] who popularized the passing game as a player at [Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_Fighting_Irish_football "Notre Dame Fighting Irish football") with teammate [Knute Rockne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knute_Rockne "Knute Rockne").[^9] [^10] While in college, Stockton was compared to the top back of the era, [Red Grange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Grange "Red Grange") of [Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Fighting_Illini_football "Illinois Fighting Illini football").[^11]

## Professional career

During a [Thanksgiving Day game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_on_Thanksgiving_Day "NFL on Thanksgiving Day") in 1926, a pass from Stockton to [Two-Bits Homan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-Bits_Homan "Two-Bits Homan") netted the Yellow Jackets a 20–14 victory over the [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers "Green Bay Packers").[^12] Stockton made the same pass to Homan again that season, this time against the [Chicago Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cardinals "Chicago Cardinals") for a 7–6 Frankford win.[^13] In 1928, against the [Chicago Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Cardinals "Chicago Cardinals"), Stockton threw an 18-yard pass to [Carl Waite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Waite "Carl Waite") for a 19–0 win for the Jackets.[^14] At the start of his last season in 1929, Stockton and former Gonzaga teammate Flaherty were in training camp with the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants "New York Giants").[^15]

Stockton was an assistant coach at his alma mater Gonzaga during the 1927 season.[^16]

## After football

Following his football career, he was briefly a [professional wrestler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling "Professional wrestling") in Spokane,[^17] [^18] [^19] and worked for the [highway department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Transportation_Department "Idaho Transportation Department") in [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho").[^20]
