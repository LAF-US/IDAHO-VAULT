An abandoned industrial plant in Idaho could be turned into a $2.6 billion data center, if a proposal is approved.

[East Idaho News](https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/05/is-ai-data-center-coming-to-pocatello-weigh-in-on-thursday/) and KIFI-TV report that the City of Pocatello will hold a public hearing on Thursday, May 14, to convert the former Hoku Materials polycilicon plant into an AI data center on 49 acres of land on the west side of Pocatello near a rail line.

A company that calls itself Lex Developments LLC made the application, headed by Gus Shultz. The company is not licensed to do business in the State of Idaho. East Idaho News [said the company left out](https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/05/is-ai-data-center-coming-to-pocatello-weigh-in-on-thursday/) details about where it was based.

## Water, power

Hawaii-based Hoku declared bankruptcy in 2013, saying it had $1 billion in debt and idled the Pocatello plant, which made components for solar panels.

The former Hoku site is now owned by Portneuf Capital, LLC, and is part of an urban renewal district. The Pocatello Development Authority said it has been working to add a new rail crossing in the area of the Hoku plant and said Portneuf Capital has been working to clean up remaining remnants of the plant.

The Pocatello Hearing Examiner will hold a hearing Thursday evening at Pocatello City Hall to hear public testimony on Lex Developments’ proposal for a conditional use permit to build the so-called AI data center. The proposal includes a set of seven 100,000 square-foot buildings across the site, plus parking and retention basins.

In one paragraph, Lex touts that the project will create jobs, but not that many.

“Specifically, the proposed development is consistent with the intent and purpose of the applicable zoning district, which is to accommodate employment-generating, infrastructure-supported commercial and industrial activities,” the application noted. “The data center is functionally similar to permitted uses such as warehouse, light industrial, and utility-related facilities in that it operates within an enclosed structure, has limited on-site staffing, and generates minimal external impacts.”

In February, Idaho Power wrote Shultz a letter for the site for “Project IV West,” which the company said would lead to a “significant load increase” of more than 100 megawatts of energy. The Idaho Power document said it was expected to use 100 MW of power by 2031.

The City of Pocatello’s Water Pollution Control asked Lex how much wastewater would be discharged and several other questions. Lex didn’t respond directly, instead claiming the “data center can be adequately served by existing public facilities and services.”

## Could benefit from tax cap

[As BoiseDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/03/16/data-micron-exemption/) the Idaho Legislature considered a bill during the most recent session to roll back tax cuts for data centers and curb their use of water and power. The bill died under lobbying pressure, allowing any projects that start this year to benefit from state tax breaks. At least one other project, in the Magic Valley, [could be working to use the tax break structure](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/03/16/data-micron-exemption/).

The proposed Pocatello project would also qualify, as it caps property values for taxing purposes at $400 million when a business invests at least $1 billion in capital improvements.

The cap was formulated in 2008 for a French nuclear company that never ultimately moved to Idaho. It has been used by companies like Micron Technology, which has seen a significant break on its property taxes as it has poured billions into expanding its site in southeast Boise. The [tax cap shifts the burden](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/05/02/micron-tax-double/) to other taxpayers in the county. In Boise, Micron’s cap has cost every homeowner additional money paid in yearly property taxes.
