***Lana: The Lady, the Legend, the Truth*** is a 1982 [memoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoir "Memoir") by the American actress [Lana Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Turner "Lana Turner"), published by [Dutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E._P._Dutton "E. P. Dutton"). In it, Turner recalls her early life, her rise to stardom and personal troubles over the course of her career.[^2]

## Synopsis

Turner recounts her early life growing up in [Wallace, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace,_Idaho "Wallace, Idaho"), and [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco"), followed by her discover at age 16 in Hollywood by [William R. Wilkerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_R._Wilkerson "William R. Wilkerson"). She recalls various anecdotes and personal experiences as a rising star for [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer "Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer"), as well as her numerous romances, marriages and [miscarriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage "Miscarriage"). She also narrates the 1958 killing of [Johnny Stompanato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Stompanato "Johnny Stompanato") by her daughter, [Cheryl Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheryl_Crane "Cheryl Crane"), during a domestic struggle, followed by her later career in film and television.

## Critical response

Wayne Lawson of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* wrote of the book,

> As these books go, *Lana* is pretty bad, in fact often bad enough to be good. For example, she says of her most recent husband: "His name was Ronald Dante - and still is, I suppose. His real name was Ronald Pellar, and he called himself a doctor, although of what I'm not exactly sure." Now, is that supposed to be funny? Sarcastic? Ingenuous? All of these? None of these? Who can tell? Miss Turner is not solely at fault for this dreadfully written book; in the last paragraph she declares that it is a collaborative effort, and the copyright is held by Eltee Productions and Hollis Alpert, the film critic and editor. However, in the last sentence she insists on taking full responsibility for her life and her book. So be it.[^2]

*The New York Times* 's [Janet Maslin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Maslin "Janet Maslin") noted, "Lana Turner, though she isn't reflective or articulate even by movie star standards, has led a life that's a soap opera daydream. And she's got a one-of-a-kind Hollywood story to tell." [^3] *[Kirkus Reviews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkus_Reviews "Kirkus Reviews")* praised the book, writing, "Lana tells it all unpretentiously, unweepily; and the result--with nice little attacks along the way on [Richard Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Burton "Richard Burton"), [Ezio Pinza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezio_Pinza "Ezio Pinza"), [Otto Preminger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Preminger "Otto Preminger"), [Harold Robbins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Robbins "Harold Robbins") et al.--is a good old-fashioned celeb memoir, popcorn with spice, unashamedly Hollywood." [^1]
