**Genevieve Pezet**, born as **Genevieve Beatrice White,** and [mononymously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mononymous_person "Mononymous person") signed her work **Genevieve [^1]** (December 19, 1913 – January 23, 2009) was an American-born French artist, known for her paintings, ceramics, and sculptures.[^2] She was most active from around the 1940s until 2000.

## Life

Genevieve Beatrice White was born December 19, 1913,[^3] in [Sandpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpoint,_Idaho "Sandpoint, Idaho"), [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho") and she was raised in [Troy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy,_Montana "Troy, Montana"), [Montana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montana "Montana"). In 1928, she attended [Washington State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_University "Washington State University") in [Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pullman,_Washington "Pullman, Washington"), Washington.[^2]

Pezet started painting while studying philosophy at [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University "Columbia University").[^1] She continued her studies at the [Art Students League of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Students_League_of_New_York "Art Students League of New York"), while teaching at the [New York School of Interior Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_School_of_Interior_Design "New York School of Interior Design").[^2] In 1947, she moved to Paris and she studied painting with [André Lhote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Lhote "André Lhote") at the André Lhote Academy and sculpture with [Ossip Zadkine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossip_Zadkine "Ossip Zadkine") in 1956 at the [Académie de la Grande Chaumière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acad%C3%A9mie_de_la_Grande_Chaumi%C3%A8re "Académie de la Grande Chaumière").[^2] [^4] In 1948, she married Jacques Pezet at the [Saint-Sulpice church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Sulpice,_Paris "Saint-Sulpice, Paris") in Paris.[^5] Together they had two sons.[^5]

In 1954, she participated in the *Salon de la Jeune Sculpture* at [Musée Rodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_Rodin "Musée Rodin")*.*[^6]

She died in [Pénestin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A9nestin "Pénestin"), [Morbihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morbihan "Morbihan") in France, on January 23, 2009.
