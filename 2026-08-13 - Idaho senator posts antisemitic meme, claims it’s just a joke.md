Idaho state Sen. Camille Blaylock, R-Caldwell, recently posted an antisemitic meme on X, formerly known as Twitter, as journalist Heath Druzin [first pointed out on Blue Sky](https://bsky.app/profile/hdruzin.bsky.social/post/3msw6psygxs2m) on Wednesday.

The post was made in response to an X account labeled Mandy Arthur, which has a long history of posting a broad range of antisemitic attacks, including attributing Israeli government and war policy to Judaism, accusing Jews of being Satan’s chosen people and accusing Jewish bankers of exploiting Americans. Blaylock follows the account.

In response to a post from Arthur claiming that there were no Flock cameras in the Upper West Side neighborhood of New York City because many of the residents are Jewish, Blaylock posted a frequently recycled antisemitic meme, featuring a Hasidic Jewish man talking on a phone with the caption, “The goyim know … shut it down.”

“Dang! You’re my favorite legislator now,” replied David Reilly, Idaho’s most prominent antisemite, adding that Blaylock had surpassed Sen. Brian Lenney.

It was not the first time Blaylock had posted the meme. She posted it a day earlier in reply to Elon Musk.

Reached by phone, Blaylock said she is not an antisemite, that she supports Israel as a U.S. ally and that she was just joking.

“It was a joke,” she said. “It was just to be funny. I don’t know if you know the Jewish word for people who aren’t Jewish. They call it goyim so that’s where that comes from.”

Asked how she thought her Jewish constituents would feel having seen the meme she posted, Blaylock said they would think it’s funny. She said she would have made a similar joke about the “Mormon mafia” if maps of Flock cameras showed there were none near the LDS Temple.

“I made the joke, and I’m telling you it’s not antisemitic,” she said.

Except that it quite obviously is. It plays off a longstanding antisemitic trope that Jews somehow conspire to run the world, for example by putting Flock cameras in other people’s neighborhoods but not theirs.

“The language is typically used in references to antisemitic conspiracy theories depicting Jews as malevolent puppet-masters, manipulating the media, banks, and even entire governments to the benefit of themselves but to the detriment of other peoples,” [the Anti Defamation League wrote](https://www.adl.org/resources/hate-symbol/goyim-knowshut-it-down) about the meme Blaylock used. “The phrase is intended to be understood as spoken by a panicked Jew responding to some occurrence that would ostensibly reveal Jewish manipulations or deceit to non-Jews.”

“Typically paired with offensive images of a stereotypical Jew talking on a phone or holding a shocked expression, this meme is widely used by white supremacists and other antisemites,” [the American Jewish Committee wrote](https://www.ajc.org/translatehate/the-goyim-know) about the meme.

Blaylock’s post was made in response to a post by an antisemite, and cheered on by another. There is nothing ambiguous about it.

Surely, one of Idaho’s senators will stand up for our Jewish neighbors and file an ethics complaint. Will Senate Pro Tem Kelly Anthon convene the ethics committee under [Rule 52](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/senaterules/#:~:text=The%20President%20Pro%20Tempore%20shall%20receive%20complaints%20from%20any%20Senator) of the Senate rules to determine whether this is “conduct unbecoming a Senator?”

Idaho has thousands of Jewish residents. They have to live in a state where the laws are written by someone who portrays them in a bigoted way. That’s a disgusting mark on the character of Idaho and the Idaho Senate in particular.

If that isn’t conduct unbecoming an Idaho senator, the term has no meaning.

*Bryan Clark is an opinion writer for the Idaho Statesman.*
