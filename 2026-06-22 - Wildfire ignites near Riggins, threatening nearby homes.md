A wildfire southwest of Riggins is threatening nearby homes, although no evacuations have been ordered yet, according to the Idaho Department of Lands.

The Shingle Creek Fire, located about six miles southwest of Riggins, was reported on Sunday afternoon after igniting near homes on Shingle Creek Road, the Rapid River Fish Hatchery, and a popular trail that follows Rapid River.

An estimated 400 acres were burned as of Monday afternoon, with no containment reported yet. The cause of the fire is under investigation, said Jarel Bruce, an IDL spokesperson.

“Several structures are threatened, but no evacuations are in place at this time,” Bruce said. “Currently, the fire is advancing upslope toward Cannonball Mountain and away from developed areas.”

Five engines, a 20-person crew, a hot shot crew, and a helicopter are currently working the Shingle Creek Fire. A dozer, four single-engine air tankers, and two large air tankers have also been ordered, Bruce said.

“Ground crews and aviation resources continue to focus on slowing fire spread and protecting at-risk values,” he said.

The lands department is responding to the fire in tandem with the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forest. A spokesperson for the forest did not immediately respond to Valley Lookout’s request for comment on Monday.

The agencies expect to hand management of the fire off to [a regional incident management team](https://www.nifc.gov/resources/firefighters/incident-management-teams), citing “increasing complexity.”
