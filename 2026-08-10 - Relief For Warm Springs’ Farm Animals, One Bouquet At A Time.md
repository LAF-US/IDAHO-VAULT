As the Bench Fire surpassed 67,000 acres this past Sunday, the smoke from the Warm Springs reservation’s burning brush and timber hovered far from the forested region of Eugene’s South Hills area, more than three hours’ drive away.

At the Lox, Stocks & Bagels eatery, the doors frequently open and release the savory aroma of garlic, onion and asiago cheese as patrons come and go for their morning nourishment.

This past Sunday morning, dahlias, eukomis, hydrangeas and other flowers complemented the usual scents as volunteer fundraiser Lisa Smolenska hastily set up a stand with donated bouquets from Charles Little Flower Farm near Mount Pisgah.

“I have to be honest, it was a very impulsive idea,” said Smolenska, laughing. “I love flowers, and I love to do floral work, particularly for fundraising.”

Bouquets sold at $10 for a small and $20 for a large, with some patrons telling Smolenska not to bother giving back change. All sales were for the [Warm Springs Animal Relief Fund](https://www.zeffy.com/en-US/peer-to-peer/warm-springs-animal-relief-fund), a multi-group effort launched by [3 Sisters Equine Refuge](https://www.instagram.com/_u/3sistersequinerefuge?fbclid=IwcGRvZgVleHRuA2FlbQIxMABicmlkETEzYVlCN0xQbkdFMVZ0UVk4c3J0YwZhcHBfaWQQMjIyMDM5MTc4ODIwMDg5MgABHtrF2wwTjmNMu5iifwiUnB9NkgIH_2mczbD4MZ39c_zsnK-abNxjnSJwlh9A_aem_3YfyFSTkVpuWHw7Paba6UQ) to mitigate the effects of wildfires on animals in affected areas.

“Just the fact that so much forage land has been lost up there, and how important the animals are — the horses, the livestock, the companion animals, and wildlife — they’re just an integral part of the land and the people who live there,” said Smolenska.

The Warm Springs Animal Relief Fund had set an initial goal of $15,000 but has quickly blown past that. As of 10:00 p.m. PST Sunday, it had raised $26,719.

“We are incredibly grateful that the community has already helped us exceed our initial fundraising goal of $15,000,” Leslie Anglin, director of marketing for 3 Sisters Equine Refuge said in an email to Underscore Native News. “The original goal was established to support immediate emergency response efforts, but as we continue to assess conditions on the ground, it is becoming clear that the needs will extend well beyond this fire season and likely into 2027.”

Anglin added that a new fundraising target has not been determined and the focus will now be on making sure resources remain available as recovery needs from across Warm Springs are identified and prioritized. She said that one of the biggest concerns is the loss of foraging and grazing land that ranchers and horse owners have used to sustain their animals.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.underscore.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/IMG_3533-scaled.jpg?resize=2048%2C1536&ssl=1)

Lisa Smolenska holds up a large bouquet that was part of a floral fundraiser for the Warm Springs Animal Relief Fund held outside Lox, Stocks & Bagels on Sunday, Aug. 9, 2026. (Photo by Brian Bull) Credit: Brian Bull

“Many families in the Warm Springs area rely on tribal grazing lands and pasture resources that may take multiple growing seasons to recover.”

Anglin said she hopes people understand that the impact of a wildfire doesn’t end once the flames are extinguished, and the long-term consequences for animals — including wildlife — can continue for years.

Both Anglin and Smolenska said that for many families in Warm Springs, livestock and horses are more than economic assets. They represent family traditions, cultural heritage, and a way of life that’s connected to the land.

“When grazing areas burn, animals often require supplemental feed, alternative pasture, veterinary support, and additional care for months or even years while the landscape recovers,” wrote Anglin.

#### A timely response for one ranching family

Shana Johnson, a member of the Confederated Tribes of Warm Springs, manages a ranch with 15 horses and 300 cows. She said her family lost 99% of her 80,000-acre grazing district on the south end of the reservation due to the Beachcomb Fire several weeks ago (the Beachcomb Fire subsequently merged with the Bench Fire).

“We are looking for hay donations for animals,” Johnson told Underscore Native News. “We usually feed barley hay, but right now we’re kind of feeding a filler hay, which is Kentucky bluegrass. And then when it gets really cold, we usually feed alfalfa to our cows.”

Johnson said she had not heard about the Warm Springs Animal Relief Fund until asked about it for this story. She said it sounded like a great resource.

![](https://i0.wp.com/www.underscore.news/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/072526_JohnsonRanchHorses.jpg?w=1132&ssl=1)

Shana Johnson’s horses with the Beachcomb Fire in the background, on the Warm Springs reservation, July 25, 2026. (Credit: Una Johnson.)

“We are definitely going to need some assistance somewhere along the line here shortly,” she said, adding that the regional drought and dry winter didn’t help grazing conditions around the ranch. “We just didn’t expect this season’s fire to be so devastating.”

Money raised for the fund will provide hay and feed for livestock and horses, veterinary supplies and emergency animal care, shipment and delivery of critical supplies, and support for companion animals affected by the fire.

“I think we tend to focus immediately on the human need of course,” said Smolenska, on charitable responses to disasters, “But the need for the animals is also a need for the humans, because they’re so much an integral part of life.”

Before Smolenska could even make a second trip to her truck, Bill and Gretchen Poppie emerged from the bagel shop and relieved her of two large bouquets wrapped in brown paper and bursting with color.

“It’s wonderful that people like her are doing things for animals because we aren’t thinking about that,” said Bill Poppie. “Right now we’re thinking about our friends in Spokane who’ve been evacuated. We’re thinking about friends in Eastern Oregon.”

“We do have to look out for people and our critters as well,” said Gretchen Poppie. “So it’s great.”

Smolenska chatted through the next few hours with customers about the devastation of grazing and foraging lands in Warm Springs, as well as the generosity of Charles Little Flower Farm which turned 40 this year. Several patrons were drawn to the [art featured on the fundraiser’s flyer](https://www.instagram.com/p/DbtVvHimVAw/?img_index=1), designed by artist Suze Wehr, showing a horse and dog fleeing a burning landscape.

#### A bouquet for romance, money for relief

During a break from his morning shift at Lox, Stocks & Bagels, employee Fisher Albert worked out a payment with Smolenska’s Venmo account.

“I got a very pretty bouquet, they are very fresh and nice smelling, so I’m very happy to be picking these up for my lady,” said Albert, cradling the flowers in the crook of his arm. Like many Oregonians, he has kept up with the fires burning across Warm Springs. “It is definitely a shame what’s happening right now, so any way that we can help rehabilitate, I’m always on board for.”

Gretchen and Bill Poppie of Eugene were the first customers to buy bouquets from Lisa Smolenska on Aug. 9, 2026 to help animals affected by the wildfires affecting the Warm Springs area. (Photo by Brian Bull)

By the end of the flower drive, Smolenska said she managed to raise $673 which included extra donations on top of the bouquets sold.

“But this has got to be ongoing because the animals are going to need forage over the winter time as well and beyond,” said Smolenska, contemplating a return to the bagel shop in a week to raise more funds. “I don’t think we really know how long it’ll take to regenerate, and there’ll also be projects for wildlife habitat as well. So the more we can get, the better.”

Besides 3 Sisters Equine Refuge, other groups and partners involved with the Warm Springs Animal Recovery Fund are Central Oregon Equine Rescue, Dr. Cierra Buer of Central Oregon Community College in Bend, and other veterinary partners, Fences for Fido, Happy Trails Horse Rescue, Harmony Farms, Heart Space Horse Sanctuary, Lark Rescue, Mustangs to the Rescue, Rags to Rescues, Safe Acres, and Skydog Sanctuary.

Anyone who wants to support the fundraiser is encouraged to visit: [www.3sistersequine.org/warmsprings](http://www.3sistersequine.org/warmsprings).
