**Anthony Doerr** is an American author of novels and short stories. He gained widespread recognition for his 2014 novel *[All the Light We Cannot See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Light_We_Cannot_See "All the Light We Cannot See")*, which won the [Pulitzer Prize for Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Fiction "Pulitzer Prize for Fiction").

## Early life and education

Doerr grew up in [Cleveland, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Township,_Geauga_County,_Ohio "Russell Township, Geauga County, Ohio").[^1] He attended [University School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_School_\(Ohio\) "University School (Ohio)") in [Hunting Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting_Valley,_Ohio "Hunting Valley, Ohio"), an eastern Cleveland suburb, graduating in 1991. He majored in history at [Bowdoin College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowdoin_College "Bowdoin College") in [Brunswick, Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunswick,_Maine "Brunswick, Maine"), graduating in 1995. He earned an MFA from [Bowling Green State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_State_University "Bowling Green State University") in [Bowling Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green,_Ohio "Bowling Green, Ohio").[^2]

## Career

Doerr's first book was a collection of short stories called *The Shell Collector* (2002). His first novel, *About Grace*, was released in 2004. His memoir, *Four Seasons in Rome*, was published in 2007, and his second collection of short stories, *Memory Wall,* was published in 2010. Doerr's second novel, *[All the Light We Cannot See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_the_Light_We_Cannot_See "All the Light We Cannot See")*, is set in occupied [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France "France") during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") and was published in 2014. He laboriously worked on writing it for a decade in his [downtown Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Boise "Downtown Boise") office.[^3]

It received significant critical acclaim and was a finalist for the [National Book Award for Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award_for_Fiction "National Book Award for Fiction").[^4] The book was a [*New York Times* bestseller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Best_Seller_list "The New York Times Best Seller list"), and was named by the newspaper as a notable book of 2014.[^5] It won the [Pulitzer Prize for Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Fiction "Pulitzer Prize for Fiction") in 2015. It was the runner-up for the 2015 [Dayton Literary Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayton_Literary_Peace_Prize "Dayton Literary Peace Prize") for Fiction [^6] and won the 2015 Ohioana Library Association Book Award for Fiction.[^7] “It’s hard to think that I really belong on that list (he's the first Idahoan to win but a handful of writers including [Ernest Hemingway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Hemingway "Ernest Hemingway") and [Toni Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Morrison "Toni Morrison") have ties to [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho")),” he told the *[Idaho Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Statesman "Idaho Statesman")*. “I really haven’t had a chance to understand what this means. It’s so overwhelming. My editor worked with a bunch of great writers and told me that when [Frank McCourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_McCourt "Frank McCourt") (‘ [Angela’s Ashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela's_Ashes "Angela's Ashes") ’ in 1997) won he told her, ‘Now you know the first line of my obituary.’... that’s true. It’s this thing that will be forever attached to my name. You know, ‘Pulitzer Prize-winner Tony Doerr does something stupid at a BSU football game.’...Can’t do that anymore.” [^3]

Doerr writes a column on science books for *[The Boston Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boston_Globe "The Boston Globe")* and is a contributor to *[The Morning News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Morning_News_\(online_magazine\) "The Morning News (online magazine)")*, an online magazine. From 2007 to 2010, he was the Writer in Residence for the state of Idaho.[^8] [^9] Doerr's third novel, *[Cloud Cuckoo Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_Cuckoo_Land_\(novel\) "Cloud Cuckoo Land (novel)")*, has three story lines, scattered throughout time: 13-year-old Anna and Omeir, an orphaned seamstress and a cursed boy, on opposite sides of formidable city walls during the 1453 siege of Constantinople; teenage idealist Seymour and octogenarian Zeno in an attack on a public library in present-day Idaho; and Konstance, decades from now, who turns to the oldest stories to guide her community in peril.[^10] *Cloud Cuckoo Land* was released September 28, 2021. It was shortlisted for the 2021 [National Book Award for Fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award_for_Fiction "National Book Award for Fiction").[^11]

## Personal life

Doerr lives in the highlands of [Boise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise "Boise"), [Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho "Idaho"), with his wife Shauna Eastman and their two twin sons. He has coached [flag football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_football "Flag football") and he and his sons ski and hike.[^3]
