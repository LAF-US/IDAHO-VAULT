Some fans who got tickets to see Boise State take on the University of Nevada Las Vegas in the Mountain West Championship game got a rude surprise.

Their tickets were canceled. The impacted fans had nowhere to sit.

“Albertsons Stadium inadvertently made tickets available for sale that do not exist,” an automated email from Ticketmaster to an unknown number of fans read. “If you’d like to still purchase tickets, there are Standard Admission reserved seats available.”

![Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/54fe6a7b-4e48-4c07-b9ad-1f79c34fb57e.gif)

Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power

But tickets to the game are sold out.

Boise State made no initial public announcement about the issue.

The school’s Associate Athletic Director for Strategic Communications & Brand Advancement Chris Kutz told BoiseDev after the initial publication of this story that at least 230 “accounts” were impacted, with an unknown number of total tickets. He said the school was working to help all impacted ticketholders.

![Build Idaho - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/8679b89c-de2f-4b22-948e-bd39a76c4c7f.png)

Build Idaho - 2:1

Boise State’s athletic director Jeramiah Dickey took to X/Twitter, calling the issue an “opportunity.” Though the Ticketmaster email didn’t offer help beyond buying other sold-out tickets, Dickey said the school would try to assist.

“Anyone who had issue received email…you’ll (sic) have access to tickets in other sections. I apologize and appreciate your patience 🙏🏼 Please email ticketoffice@boisestate.edu for customer support.”

Kutz said they’d whittled down the list to about 75 people who they had not helped as of 4:30pm Tuesday.

![Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/54fe6a7b-4e48-4c07-b9ad-1f79c34fb57e.gif)

Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power

The Ticketmaster email attributed the mistake to “Albertsons Stadium,” and “the venue.” The Mountain West Championship game is not technically a home game for Boise State, but instead administered by the Mountain West Conference using Boise State’s facilities.

Kutz said the Ticketmaster sold seats that did not actually exist.

“We feel like we’ll make people right, and it was on our watch and we own it,” Kutz said.

This is not the first time this season that the issue has cropped up. A BoiseDev reporter witnessed two sets of people show up for the same seats at the Boise State vs. Nevada game on November 9, both with tickets showing the same numbers.

Fans of both Boise State and UNLV were impacted.

“And the small town shenanigans begin,” Michael Knight wrote on Twitter. “Bought a ticket last week from @BoiseState athletics website and they just cancelled it. Albertsons Stadium ‘sold tickets that didn’t exist.’”’ My trip accommodations have been booked for over a week. They’re legit scared.”

![Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/54fe6a7b-4e48-4c07-b9ad-1f79c34fb57e.gif)

One fan who said she’d received the email told BoiseDev she reached out to the ticket office. After about four hours, the ticket office offered her help.

“The seats are actually much better than my original tickets and I paid less! In my first email I expressed how devastated and upset my kids and I were,” Kailyn Noble Fuller said.

But Knight the UNLV fan, told BoiseDev on Twitter that he was not helped directly, but instead “given the option to buy elsewhere.”

![Weather with Sarah - Idaho Power](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/54fe6a7b-4e48-4c07-b9ad-1f79c34fb57e.gif)

“It was a polite email exchange,” Knight said. “No call back received. Not a great alternative offered and no reason as to why it happened. Not a very good experience so far…”
