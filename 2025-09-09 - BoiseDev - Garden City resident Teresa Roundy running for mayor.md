A candidate for Garden City’s highest office hopes to put all of her focus on changes at the Garden City Police Department.

Teresa Roundy, a political newcomer, is running for mayor in the Boise suburb with the aim of to put all of her energy into correcting what she sees as shortfalls in the city for police to adequately investigate human trafficking and other crimes against children. In an interview with BoiseDev, she spoke at length about her general frustrations with the Boise School District, the family court system in Idaho, and nationwide for not doing enough to protect children.

She is running against [Bill Jacobs, a current Garden City city council member](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/08/29/jacobs-mayor-gardencity/), and Molly Lenty, a College of Western Idaho trustee.

When asked why Roundy, who has never held public office and has not raised any campaign funds, is running for mayor instead of a school board position or a state legislature seat to address problems in the court system, she said “the mayor enforces laws” and her push would be to use her personal experience with abuse and trauma to educate police officers on how to protect children more adequately.

“I’m running on family and child safety first,” she said. “The adults in this town are not protecting the children, and if they’re not protecting the kids then I will teach the children how to protect themselves.”

Roundy said she is a former educator with the Tracy Unified School District in California and was last in the classroom in 2007 and also held part-time work as a service coordinator at an unnamed organization. When asked what her current occupation is, she told BoiseDev she’s currently living on government food stamp benefits while she recovers from trauma she experienced in family court.

Focusing on criminal justice issues and how Garden City’s policing would impact outcomes in family court mediation sessions is Roundy’s sole focus running for public office in the city. She was unfamiliar with the proposal to establish a new urban renewal district on the south side of Chinden Boulevard or the details of Garden City’s current zoning and development policies. When asked about the city’s tight budget and how she would keep costs down, she pitched a city-wide gardening initiative.

“We need to grow gardens in Garden City,” she said. “We can make Garden City and teach the children. I want to teach the elderly and preschool kids to do gardens along the stretch of the walking path across from my house.”
