# Alchemical symbols

**Alchemical symbols** are the glyph-language of [[Alchemy]] — the compact
signs alchemists used to denote elements, substances, apparatus, and processes.
They were in use from roughly the 5th century CE (developed first by Greek,
Syriac, and Egyptian writers) until the 18th century, when modern chemical
notation displaced them. This node is the companion glyph-key spun out of the
main [[Alchemy]] node. It is an original synthesis grounded in the cited
sources, not a verbatim copy.

## Why a glyph language at all

Alchemy ran on secrecy, danger, and contested authority, so its practitioners
wrote in signs rather than plain words. A glyph does three jobs at once:

- **Compression** — one mark stands for a whole substance or operation.
- **Concealment** — the uninitiated cannot read the recipe.
- **Correspondence** — the *same* mark can carry the metal, its planet, and the
  faculty of the self it answers to, encoding the doctrine "as above, so below"
  into the very characters.

That makes the notation itself a coded language, kin to the
[[Language of flowers]]: in both, *the mark is the message*.

## The core lexicons

### The four elements

Written as triangles, distinguished by direction and a crossing bar:

| Element | Glyph | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fire | 🜂 | upward triangle |
| Water | 🜄 | downward triangle |
| Air | 🜁 | upward triangle + bar |
| Earth | 🜃 | downward triangle + bar |

The up/down direction tracks "ascending" (Fire, Air) versus "descending"
(Water, Earth); the bar distinguishes the second element of each pair — Air from
Fire, Earth from Water — rather than marking wet against dry.

### The three primes (*tria prima*)

Paracelsus's principles of which all substances are composed:

| Prime | Glyph | Principle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sulfur | 🜍 | soul; combustibility, the active |
| Mercury | ☿ | spirit; fusibility/volatility, the fluid |
| Salt | 🜔 | body; fixity, the solid |

### The seven metals and their planets

The metals known since antiquity, each fused to a classical planet (the
associations had stabilized by the 15th century):

| Planet | Glyph | Metal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sun | ☉ | Gold |
| Moon | ☽ | Silver |
| Mercury | ☿ | Mercury (quicksilver) |
| Venus | ♀ | Copper |
| Mars | ♂ | Iron |
| Jupiter | ♃ | Tin |
| Saturn | ♄ | Lead |

Beyond these, glyphs existed for compounds and reagents (vitriol, sal ammoniac,
aqua fortis, antimony, arsenic…), for apparatus (alembic, retort, crucible,
bath), and for operations (sublimation, distillation, putrefaction,
amalgamation, the times of day and "the hour").

## Why it was never a clean alphabet

The notation was only *partly* standardized; style and sign varied from
alchemist to alchemist, and the same glyph could mean different things in
different hands. The scale of the drift is striking: the Swiss scholar
**Lüdy-Tenger** catalogued **3,695 symbols and variants** — and even that was
not exhaustive (it omitted many of **Isaac Newton's** signs). Like the
[[Language of flowers]], it is best read as a *living convention with regional
dialects*, not a fixed cipher with one correct key.

## The glyphs today — Unicode

Modern text can carry the historical signs directly: the Unicode
**Alchemical Symbols** block (range **U+1F700–U+1F77F**, 128 code points, in the
Supplementary Multilingual Plane) was introduced in **Unicode 6.0 (2010)**,
drawing on a proposal tied to the digitization of Newton's alchemical papers.

This matters for the vault specifically: emoji and Unicode symbols are
**first-class vault syntax** (per [[VAULT-CONVENTIONS]], 2026-04-02), with the
[Unicode Consortium](https://unicode.org) as the naming authority. The
alchemical block is therefore a legitimate, governable character set for use in
notes — a symbol language the vault can actually *type*.

## Relationship to alchemy and floriography

These glyphs are the **surface** of [[Alchemy]] — the layer where its doctrine
becomes writable. They are the alchemical counterpart to floriography's
"vocabulary" of blooms: a finite set of signs whose meanings are conventional,
contested, and meaning-bearing only to a reader who knows the code. Where the
[[Language of flowers]] hands you a *flower*, alchemy hands you a *mark*; both
ask to be *read*.
