BOISE, Idaho — The Idaho Humane Society is issuing a warning after being notified of the 15th rabies case involving a bat in Ada County. However, the organization says it's easily preventable.

"We don't share this information to scare anyone," the humane society wrote in a [social media post](https://www.facebook.com/idahohumanesociety/posts/pfbid02u4VdHzzPWBDsCSYHiX2882YG4fBjPMRWmfJSnsQPi4HtWT9Geu3Je2Sb62iAxKzAl). "We share it because rabies is preventable, and this is a good reminder to make sure your pets are up to date on their rabies vaccinations."

Officials said healthy [bats](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/idaho/first-rabid-bat-in-2026-confirmed-blaine-county/277-08c400a2-c34f-4a4a-a7dc-040b8ad620e6) generally avoid people, but residents should still keep their distance from the animal. The Idaho Humane Society said if the animal is easily picked up or handled, it may mean something is wrong with the bat.

If a bat gets into a home, officials said it should be taken seriously. If there is any chance it had contact with a child or pet, the bat should be contained and submitted for testing, according to the Idaho Humane Society.

However, the organization says the animal is "incredibly important to our ecosystem."

"We absolutely do not encourage anyone to harm or kill a bat," the Idaho Humane Society wrote in a social media post. "The goal is simply to give bats the space they need while taking appropriate precautions when one is acting unusually."

Residents should contact animal control or public health officials for guidance. Officials say that being up to date on your rabies vaccine is the best way to protect your children and pets.
