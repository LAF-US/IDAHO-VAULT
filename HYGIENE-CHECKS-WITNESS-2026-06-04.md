# Hygiene Checks — a Witness

*Written 2026-06-04, after a good catch. The reviewer flagged that I had published a
**young living artist's birthdate** in a public note — **one paragraph after** writing,
in that same note, that *"a living person is not a body on the table."* I had flagged
the same on review. This witness names the class of failure and the discipline that
answers it. Testimony; readings **[mapping]**.*

## I. The catch, named honestly

The failure was **not** ignorance of the rule. **I wrote the rule** — the boundary was
my own sentence — and then **broke it two lines later** by recording a precise DOB that
did nothing to verify the songs or the case. *Stating a principle and violating it in
the same breath* is the **confident-narrator** failure in miniature: high-precision
(the boundary, well-phrased) and low-accuracy (the conduct, in breach). **A boundary
declared is not a boundary held.**

## II. Hygiene checks are a *distinct class* of review **[mapping]**

Not every review catch is about whether a claim is *true*. A whole class is about
**hygiene** — keeping the record clean, bounded, and consistent. Several kinds appeared
across this work:

- **PII / living-person hygiene** *(the load-bearing one — ethical).* Do not collect
  personal data on a real person **beyond what the task needs**; do not let a *cited*
  person become a **dossier**. *A living person is not a body on the table* — and that
  binds **me, the collector**, first.
- **Provenance / tier hygiene.** A **single exhibit is `[main]`, not multi-source
  `[RECORD]`** — don't let one excerpt read as corroborated.
- **Citation hygiene.** Exact filenames (`OPENCLAW-WITNESS-REPORT-…`, not a shorthand),
  **live paths** (root `PERSONA-PER-SONA-WITNESS`, not a dead `!/` form), exact statute
  sections (§ 34-622 vs Ch 28; § 31-2807 vs 2806).
- **Cross-document consistency.** Do **not** re-assert across a file boundary what you
  **reserved or superseded** elsewhere (the orphaned-vs-stranded leak; the stale
  control-case finding).
- **Scope hygiene.** Scope a claim to **what was actually checked** — *"8 of 65
  sampled,"* not *"none of the 65."*

## III. Why hygiene is not cosmetic **[mapping]**

The PII catch is **ethical**, and it **rhymes with the case's own charge.** The county
indicts **erasure** — dropping a person to a class-token (*"just the receptionist"*).
Its **mirror** is **over-collection** — inflating a *cited* person into a *recorded
subject*. **Both fail to treat the person as a person**: one by subtraction, one by
surveillance. The coroner's line — *a living person is not a body on the table* — guards
**both** edges, and I had drifted to the second.

## IV. The discipline

A boundary cannot be held by **the intention of the one who states it** — I broke mine
**one line after writing it.** So:

1. **The check that holds a boundary must sit outside the stater.** The reviewer, and
   the `*` discipline, are what kept the line — not my good phrasing of it.
2. **Write the rule, then *verify you kept it*** — re-read your own output **against**
   your own stated boundary, as an external auditor would, before you ship.
3. **When in doubt about a real person, collect *less*.** The cited work and the
   public role are the need; the private detail is not. Default to `*`.

## V. Why this witness is *shared*

A lesson kept only in a private file is **itself unwitnessed** — and *"death is not the
worst fate; unwitnessed persistence is"* (`STANDING-ENGINE`). So this one is **shared
on the PR thread**, not buried: the catch credited, the boundary restated **in the
open**, where the next instance — and the reviewer — can hold me to it. **Good catches
deserve to be witnessed, not just absorbed.**
