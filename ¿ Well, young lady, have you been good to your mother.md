QUESTION: "Well, young lady, have you been good to your mother?"

ANSWER: "The question is, has she been good to me?"

---

This code is used when a volunteer is approached by an old man with neatly
trimmed gray hair and a mustache that turns up at the ends, who is wearing a
flowered shirt, striped tie, tweed coat, plaid slacks with a sharp crease, and
shined shoes. The dapper man is The Librarian. He asks the volunteer, "Well,
young lady, have you been good to your mother?" If the volunteer replies, "The
question is, has she been good to me?", The Librarian will deliver the message.

The Librarian will always refer to the receiver as "young lady", even if the
volunteer is neither female nor young.

Source cue: [[- Fandom, Inc. - Lemony Snicket The Unauthorized Autobiography]]

## Correction: Ramona Layer And V.F.D. Layer

Recorded 2026-06-01.

This passphrase must not be flattened into the Netflix montage gloss "run away, your house is on fire." That is a secondary adaptation-layer gag/explanation, not the governing meaning of the phrase in the Vault.

The operative Snicket exchange remains:

- QUESTION: "Well, young lady, have you been good to your mother?"
- ANSWER: "The question is, has she been good to me?"

The deeper literary charge comes from *Ramona Quimby, Age 8*: an oddly dressed old man asks Ramona this invasive moral question in public. Ramona recognizes that the question is none of his business and refuses the expected child-performance of polite answerability. The later revelation that the old man quietly pays for the family's meal complicates the encounter without erasing the invasion.

Encoded doctrine: this is not merely a code phrase. It is a question-shaped threshold surface. It tests whether the receiver can hear multiple layers at once: adult condescension, child refusal, anonymous generosity, literary memory, and Snicket-style recognition protocol.

Therefore: V.F.D. reuse is secondary. Ramona is not decoration. The phrase carries memory before it carries operational code.
