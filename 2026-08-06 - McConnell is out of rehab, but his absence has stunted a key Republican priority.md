#### Mitch McConnell reveals reason for hospitalization, Senate absence

Fox News senior medical analyst Dr. Marc Siegel discusses Sen. Mitch McConnell's, R-Ky., update on his health nearly a month after his hospitalization on ‘The Big Weekend Show.’

Sen. [Mitch McConnell,](https://www.foxnews.com/category/person/mitch-mcconnell) R-Ky., is officially back home after weeks of receiving medical care, but it’s still not clear when he will return to the Senate.

McConnell, in a statement released by his office Thursday afternoon, revealed that he had been "discharged from the rehabilitation center to continue my recovery at home" with his wife, former Transportation Secretary Elaine Chao.

The longtime lawmaker has been absent from the public eye since June after suffering a fall at his Washington, D.C. Late last month, he disclosed that he had been discharged from the hospital, but was not yet "medically cleared" to return to work.

"Elaine and I are grateful for the many well-wishes and support from friends, colleagues, and Kentuckians, and for the attentive care I’ve received from excellent doctors, nurses, therapists, and hospital staff," McConnell said.

"On the advice of my doctors, I’ll maintain an intensive regimen of physical therapy from home during the state work period, and I’ll continue to engage with my staff and colleagues on important [Senate business,"](https://www.foxnews.com/category/politics/elections/senate) he continued.

His return to the Senate, however, remains a mystery.

And the upper chamber is still currently in session, mired in negotiations moving at a glacial pace to pass a series of key agenda items before jet-setting from Washington for the rest of August. Whether [McConnell returns](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/kentucky-governor-calls-mcconnell-prove-he-fit-serve-resign-address-people) in early September when the Senate is set to clock back in remains in the air, too.

Fox News Digital did not immediately receive comment on McConnell's return to the upper chamber.

For weeks, his absence has had little effect on Senate business other than speculation that the day would come when his vote was needed, and he wasn't there.

That changed Thursday morning when Senate Republicans on the Senate Agriculture Committee, which McConnell is a member of, tried to move a revamped, sweeping farm package through committee. Senate Democrats voted against the package over concerns that the food stamp provisions in the legislation would put more of the cost of the program onto states.
