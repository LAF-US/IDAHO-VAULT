Interfaith Sanctuary’s State St. shelter will continue operations under the same set of conditions following a decision by the Boise City Council on Tuesday evening.

The council voted 4-1 to uphold [its decision in September 2025](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/10/01/ifs-approval-2025/) that approved a conditional use permit for the shelter’s relocation to State St. from Shoreline Dr. Council Member Luci Willits cast the sole dissenting vote. Council President Pro Tem Kathy Corless was absent.

Following the September decision, the Veterans Park Neighborhood Association filed for judicial review, arguing that certain evidence was not available to the council for review ahead of the September hearing. Ada County District Court Judge Lynn Norton ordered that the case be remanded to the council so that its members could decide whether to augment the record with the additional materials.

The items for the council’s consideration Tuesday included correspondence between VPNA and Boise Planning and Development Services staff; information on crime from the Boise Police Department; and spreadsheets showing calls for service within specific areas of the city.

The council’s September vote followed the conditional use permit’s [approval by the planning and zoning commission in July 2025](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/07/08/interfaith-sanctuary-approved-state-street-2025/). In July, the commission approved the conditional use permit and denied a requested landscaping variance, according to a memo from the city. VPNA then appealed the CUP approval to the council.

## Why weren’t certain documents available ahead of the hearing?

Boise Senior Planner Josh Wilson told the council Tuesday evening that some of the materials the court required for council review were not available before the September hearing due to an error in a hyperlink in the council report for the meeting.

To share the more than 24,000 pages of documents associated with the September hearing, city staff uploaded them to Google Drive and created hyperlinks in the report linking to specific documents. However, the hyperlink for Appendix E in the report inadvertently linked to materials in Appendix D, Wilson said.

That prompted the court to remand the case to council to consider augmenting the record with materials from the accidentally omitted materials in Appendix E, he said.

## What did VPNA and Interfaith say?

Tuesday’s proceedings were not a public hearing, and as a result, only a representative from VPNA and one from the shelter were allowed time to present information.

VPNA President Katy Decker said the materials missing for the council’s consideration in September 2025 included information about how Interfaith Sanctuary’s Shoreline Dr. location had impacted surrounding areas. Decker said that this included the number of calls for service within 300 feet of the shelter related to individuals who had sought the shelter’s services.

Decker also presented information about the types of crimes associated with calls to police while homeless individuals were temporarily housed at downtown hotels during the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020. Some of these crimes included theft of hotel materials, drug use and possession, and reports of verbal abuse.

Decker said that while shelter representatives have indicated they are implementing an array of mitigation measures to protect the surrounding community, she said the hotel issues indicate a need for more stringent requirements in the shelter’s conditional use permit for State St.

“The omitted data underline that there’s ample evidence for the potential of Interfaith’s operation to impact the surrounding area,” Decker said. “We’re asking for the conditions of approval to be updated to at minimum reflect the mitigation measures that Interfaith has claimed they are implementing, which are not reflected in the conditions of approval.”

Decker expressed concern that the shelter’s permit does not limit it from expanding its population density in the future or restricting it to men only in the future, which she said is associated with higher levels of crime. She also said that the shelter only encourages guests to participate in certain programs, but does not require it.

Geoff Wardle, legal counsel for Interfaith Sanctuary, said the matter before the council was an attempt to relitigate the permit.

![Title One - Hallow - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/54e8aba3-801f-4333-acda-e6692d3ad845.gif)

Wardle said that although some 300 pages of spreadsheets weren’t available ahead of the fall council hearing, the council made a sound decision after considering testimony from Decker and other members of the public about how the shelter would impact the surrounding area. The council then “articulated why you didn’t accept that argument,” and adopted conditions when approving the project, he said.

“There is no reason to reopen this matter,” Wardle said. “There is nothing that has been presented to you tonight in the augmented materials that dictate any response, any change to the decision you made.”

Conditions approved by the council at the September hearing include having the shelter open 24 hours rather than requiring guests to leave during the day, a requirement to provide case management to connect guests to other social services, and preventing the discharge of shelter guests from 7 p.m. to 7 a.m., [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/10/01/ifs-approval-2025/).

## What did council members say?

The council voted 4-1 to uphold the decision and keep the associated record as is.

Two council members offered brief comments ahead of the vote.

Willits, who also voted against approving the conditional use permit in September 2025, said that VPNA members had “done all you can do.”

![Zamzows 116 - July - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/46bf9fe4-7aa0-4f20-a644-e8911393869f.jpg)

“You’ve kept the city honest. You made sure everything was in that record,” Willits said.

Council Member Jimmy Hallyburton said that he seconded the motion because after reviewing all of the previous materials and the new information, he “didn’t see any information in there that I don’t feel like was covered during our meeting.”
