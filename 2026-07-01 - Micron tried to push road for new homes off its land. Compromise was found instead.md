As Micron Technology’s large-scale expansion in Boise moves forward, both the City of Boise and the local highway district have eyes on the roadways to support residents and the future thousands of jobs expected to come to the area.

Last week, Micron representatives went before the Ada County Highway District’s Board of Commissioners seeking a recommendation of approval for a new master street map. But the process wasn’t so cut-and-dried.

## The need for connectivity

[As BoiseDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/2026/06/24/aerial-video-see-microns-expansion-progress-from-2024-to-now/) construction on Micron’s first Boise fabrication plant at Boise’s Southeastern edge is in full swing and progressing. According to Micron’s Senior Director of US Expansion Planning, Jeff Binford, construction on the second fab is expected to start later this year.

Watkins Carter - 2:1

With the construction of the second fab, Micron needs to annex a portion of property it owns into the City of Boise’s limits. As part of the process, the company has been asked to work with local entities and update its master street map — a 2010 document that acts as a tool for identifying needed right-of-way and maps out future collector roads in the area.

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/relative-alignment-and-consensus-achd-votes-on-micron-roadway-plan-Screenshot-2026-06-2.jpeg)

Illustration of the original 2010 master street map by Micron. Map: Courtesy ACHD

The current master street map plots a roadway through Micron’s property where the first fab is being built, and is thus no longer feasible.

[As BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/04/10/no-more-housing-can-be-built-on-the-columbia-bench-without-a-second-road-due-to-fire-danger-neither-achd-or-the-city-of-boise-required-micron-to-build-one/), while the area has long been eyed as a place to house residents, its further development has largely been blocked. Boise’s fire code says there can’t be more than 60 homes on a road without a secondary access point, so residents could escape in the event of a wildfire. There is no secondary access point on Columbia Rd.

## Multiple map suggestions

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/relative-alignment-and-consensus-achd-votes-on-micron-roadway-plan-columbia-rd-area-map.png)

Map shows proposed routes for new road near the area around Micron. Map: Micron Technology/BoiseDev

Micron’s new map proposed a few changes to the current plan, including pushing the Columbia connection further east past the two existing subdivisions. ACHD staff weren’t on board with this plan.

“Essentially, we believe that a more direct connection between Eisenman Interchange/Memory Rd. and Colombia Rd., essentially through more of the Micron property, provides overall better connectivity and future opportunities for network development in this area,” Justin Lucas, ACHD Chief of Operations for Infrastructure, said.

ACHD staff proposed an alternative alignment, one that was further west and bordering land owned by Micron. Lucas said that, in addition to providing better connectivity, ACHD’s proposed map would better position Micron to build out part of the roadway. The idea of Micron being responsible for building the infrastructure in the area has been a topic of debate, [as BosieDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/04/10/no-more-housing-can-be-built-on-the-columbia-bench-without-a-second-road-due-to-fire-danger-neither-achd-or-the-city-of-boise-required-micron-to-build-one/) and it was again on Wednesday night.

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

“It’s not clear it was going to be a Micron developer project to begin with,” Micron representative Deborah Nelson of Givens Pursley said. “It’s not designed or intended to really serve the industrial campus.”

ACHD Commissioner Alexis Pickering pointed out that other developers in the area frequently build roads and infrastructure where they have projects. The highway district had received two letters from Boise developer Hawkins urging ACHD and the City of Boise to require Micron to dedicate right-of-way for the roadway and construct a portion of it as a condition of their annexation approval.

Hawkins Pre-development Manager Ethan Mansfield noted that the upcoming July 6 hearing, where Boise will make a decision on the annexation, would be the last opportunity for Micron to be held to any such agreements.

Binford said ACHD’s proposed alignment recommendation posed an additional issue, as it goes right through the company’s planned electrical yard. He said this would “create a significant challenge” for Micron. Binford raised concerns about employee safety and campus security, saying the campus could face challenges with a public roadway running directly through it.

Binford said instead, the company is working with the city to allow emergency access to vehicles through the campus. He said they would extend their campus roads and construction roads up to the neighborhoods to provide emergency access connectivity.

ACHD Commissioner Dave McKinney proposed another alternative at the meeting, a connection to Columbia located midway between where ACHD staff and Micron had both proposed roadways. Micron representatives said they would be open to this connection, but Lucas said a five-lane road in that area would be difficult due to existing entitlements and a preliminary plat for a future subdivision in the area. Lucas said it would be difficult but “not impossible.”

## Looking forward

In the end, Pickering proposed that the body recommend a new master street map to the City of Boise that plots future roadway points where both the highway district and Micron agreed the road should go on the southern portion of the property, and then make another hypothetical line on the map for the portion that connects to Columbia, with a note that says “to be studied further.”

“My motion is to kind of define what we have that alignment on, and we have consensus on, but allowing additional time for us to kind of talk about and review the proper connection,” Pickering said. “Because what I don’t want to do is make decisions from a dais where we have very limited information. We are not the experts, and I’d rather the Micron team and our staff make some of those determinations for that additional connection.”

This recommendation passed the commission on a 3-1 vote, with Commissioner Kent Goldthorpe being the lone opposition. ACHD’s recommendation will be sent to the City of Boise to consider when it hears Micron’s application in early July.
