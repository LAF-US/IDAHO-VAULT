A major roadway project south of the Boise Airport is now much closer to becoming reality thanks to federal dollars.

The City of Boise was recently awarded a $5.6 million federal Better Utilizing Investments to Leverage Development grant to go toward the Lake Hazel Rd. extension.

The Lake Hazel Rd. extension would connect Southwest Boise to Southeast Boise while simultaneously unlocking swaths of potential industrial land for development. The project would take Lake Hazel Rd, which currently terminates near Orchard St, and extend it out southeast across undeveloped rangeland to the southern end of Eisenmann Rd.

[As BoiseDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/09/24/lake-hazel-extension/) the Lake Hazel Rd. extension project has been a topic of discussion for multiple years, but progress has been largely stagnant due to a lack of funding and necessary studies.

[BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/04/13/boise-funding-roadway-extension-april-13-2026/) the city applied for the grant in April.

According to City of Boise Transportation Advisor Bre Brush, the awarded funds will allow the city and ACHD to essentially get the project shovel-ready. Brush said the funds will be used to determine the exact location of the road, create conceptual engineering, perform needed environmental studies, look at potential railroad crossings, and develop a phased implementation strategy with updated cost estimates.

![Freeform - Invested in Boise - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/8d57412c-01ee-47ef-b704-9f71ec57d3ba.png)

Freeform - Invested in Boise - 2:1

The project has remained on long-range planning models for decades, and thus many traffic studies, like the recent ITD beltway study, have assumed its buildout when looking at future roadway needs for the area.

Construction of the actual extension is currently estimated at over $75 million, according to Brush.

Funding transportation projects is an ever-complex issue due to dollar shortages on both state and local levels. [As BoiseDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/07/02/south-ada-canyon-county-connectivity-study-highway-16-extension/) ITD recently delayed over $1 billion in planned road construction projects across the state after the legislature cut the department’s funding in the most recent legislative session.

And ACHD [recently delayed a batch of roadway widening projects](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/12/01/southwest-boise-widening/) in southwest Boise due to inflation increasing project costs.

The City of Nampa was also awarded a BUILD grant for $22.99 million to replace the Broadmore Way bridge over Indian Creek and add new traffic signals, pedestrian crossings, and lighting along the Northside Corridor. However, COMPASS and ITD’s BUILD grant application for the South Ada and Canyon County Connectivity Study [was denied.](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/07/02/south-ada-canyon-county-connectivity-study-highway-16-extension/)
