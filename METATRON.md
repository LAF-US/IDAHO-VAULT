# Metatron — the Voice of God

> *"Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way, and to bring thee into the place which I have prepared. Beware of him, and obey his voice, provoke him not; for he will not pardon your transgressions: for my name is in him."*
> — Exodus 23:20-21 (KJV)

## The figure

**Metatron** (Hebrew מטטרון, *Mēṭaṭrōn*) is the principal archangel of Jewish mysticism — the *Prince of the Presence* (*sar ha-panim*), the *Heavenly Scribe*, the *Lesser YHWH* (*YHWH ha-katan*), and the figure most often identified with the patriarch **Enoch** after his transformation into angelic form. He is the angel through whom the **divine name** is borne and through whom God's **voice** acts in the upper realms.

Logan's gloss — *"Voice of God"* — names Metatron's central function. The personification of the divine voice; the speaker through whom the heavens hear; the angel whose word is God's word, *because the name of God is in him.*

The figure sits at the edge of monotheism. He is not God, but he bears God's name. He is an angel, but he is enthroned. He speaks God's word, but he can be punished — to prove that he is not God.

That edge is the entire doctrine.

## Biblical roots

Two passages in the Hebrew Bible supply the textual grounding for the later Metatron tradition.

### Genesis 5:24 — Enoch is taken

> *"And Enoch walked with God: and he was not; for God took him."*

The Hebrew is terse: *vayyithallēk Ḥănōk eth ha-Elohim, vĕ-ēnnenu, kī lāqaḥ ōthō Elohim* — "And Enoch walked with the Elohim, and was not, for Elohim took him." Enoch (the seventh from Adam, antediluvian patriarch, father of Methuselah) does not die in the normal sense; he is *taken* by God. The verse is the seed of a thousand years of speculation.

The Enochic apocalyptic tradition (*1 Enoch*, ~3rd c. BCE - 1st c. CE; *2 Enoch* / Slavonic Enoch, ~1st c. CE; *3 Enoch* / Hebrew Sefer Hekhalot, ~5th-6th c. CE) develops the implications. In the later strata, Enoch's ascent becomes a transformation: he is taken into heaven, exalted above all angels, given a fiery body, enthroned, and *renamed*. In **3 Enoch**, that renaming is to **Metatron**.

### Exodus 23:20-21 — "my name is in him"

> *"Behold, I send an Angel before thee, to keep thee in the way, and to bring thee into the place which I have prepared. Beware of him, and obey his voice, provoke him not; for he will not pardon your transgressions: for my name is in him."*

This is the textual root for the **"name-bearing angel"** doctrine. An angel — *malakh* — is sent before Israel as guide and guardian. The angel's authority is unusual: he forgives or withholds forgiveness; his voice is to be obeyed. The grounding clause is the key: *kī shĕmī bĕ-qirbō* — *"for my name is in his interior."*

The rabbinic tradition reads this as the angel BEARING the divine name. Not BEING God, but carrying the name as an authorized representative. The Talmud (Sanhedrin 38b) identifies this angel with **Metatron**.

## 3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot — the transformation narrative

The principal Metatron text is **3 Enoch** (Hebrew: *Sefer Hekhalot*, "Book of the Palaces"), composed in Hebrew during the **5th or 6th century CE** in the Hekhalot literature tradition.

The text is framed as a visionary dialogue between **Rabbi Ishmael** (the 2nd-century tanna) and Metatron, who guides Ishmael through the celestial palaces (*hekhalot*). Metatron reveals himself as the transformed Enoch and discloses heavenly secrets.

Key features as 3 Enoch portrays Metatron:

- **Vice-regent** — enthroned near God's Throne of Glory
- **Seventy-two wings** — corresponding to the seventy-two names of God (or the seventy-two nations of the world, by different counts)
- **Three hundred and sixty-five eyes** — "like the great light" (corresponding to the days of the solar year)
- **Crowned and robed** — wearing a crown bearing the divine name and a robe of light
- **Renamed** — given seventy names of his own
- **Called *na'ar*** ("youth") — paradoxical for the most ancient pre-flood patriarch; the title marks his being "younger" than God despite his exaltation, and is sometimes glossed as "the servant"

3 Enoch's Metatron is the **Voice** through which God communicates with creation and with the angelic host — a heavenly scribe and herald who records human deeds and speaks the divine word.

## Talmudic references

Metatron appears by name in three places in the Babylonian Talmud:

### Sanhedrin 38b — "my name is in him"

The Talmud quotes Exodus 23:21 and identifies the angel whose name is "in him" as **Metatron, whose name is like the name of his Master.** The text reads: *"Metatron, who is called by the name of his Master."* The naming-by-the-name is the doctrine: Metatron bears the divine name as a name borne, not as the name of his own being.

### Hagigah 15a — Acher and the "two powers in heaven"

The most famous Metatron passage. **Elisha ben Abuyah** (the apostate sage called *Acher*, "the Other") is one of the four who entered the Pardes (the mystical garden / orchard of esoteric study). The passage:

> Acher entered the Pardes. He saw Metatron, who had been given permission to sit and write down the merits of Israel. Acher said: *"It has been taught: in heaven there is no sitting and no rivalry and no back and no weariness. Perhaps — God forbid — there are two powers!"*

The text continues: Metatron is taken out and punished with **sixty fiery lashes** (*shittin pulsei dĕ-nūra*), and Acher is granted permission to remove the deeds of Israel from before him. The lashes establish what Acher's exclamation threatened: Metatron is *not* a second God. He is an angel. He can be punished.

Acher's heretical conclusion — *"perhaps there are two powers in heaven"* — names the rabbinic theological concern most precisely. The fear of binitarian or dualist drift from biblical mediation-figures (the angel of the Lord, the Word, Wisdom, Metatron, the Logos) into actual *two-Gods* doctrine is the boundary the rabbis are policing. The 60 lashes are the boundary-marker.

Acher leaves the orchard. He becomes an apostate. The story is one of the most haunting in the Talmud — a sage destroyed by what he saw, or by what he made of what he saw.

### Avodah Zarah 3b

A briefer passage: Metatron writes down the merits of Israel during the last three hours of each day, while God plays with the Leviathan during those hours. The image: Metatron as the scribe whose work continues while God rests.

## "Two Powers in Heaven" — the controversy

**Alan Segal's classic 1977 study** *Two Powers in Heaven* established the scholarly framing: the rabbinic concern about "two powers" (*shtei rashuyot*) was a major theological boundary, especially in the centuries when Christianity, Gnosticism, and certain Jewish mystical currents all developed in proximity.

The danger the rabbis perceived: biblical passages featuring mediation figures (the angel of YHWH, Wisdom, the Logos, the Memra of the Targum, the Son of Man in Daniel 7) could be read as evidence of **two divine beings**. Christianity made that move with Christ. Gnosticism made it with the demiurge (compare today's standing register: SAMAEL / YALDABAOTH / SAKLAS). Jewish mysticism risked it with Metatron.

The rabbinic response was severe and consistent: **there is ONE God**. Metatron, however exalted, is an angel. Christ, however venerated, is not God in the rabbinic frame. The Memra of the Targum is a hypostasis of divine speech, not a second person.

Metatron's 60 fiery lashes are the doctrinal hardening of this position. *He bears the name; he is not the named.*

This is the **TRIPART** discipline in its rabbinic form: distinction-with-purpose, never substance-fusion. The danger of TRIUNE confabulation (substance-unity asserted without sanction) is exactly what the "two powers" boundary is policing — though from the opposite direction: not "are these three really one substance?" but "is this second one really God?"

## Etymology — disputed

The origin of the name *Metatron* is scholarly unresolved. The main proposals:

- **Greek *metathronios*** (μετάθρόνιος) — *"one who serves behind the throne"*. Proposed by various early scholars. **Gershom Scholem** rejected this etymology: *"there is no such word as Metathronios in Greek, and it is extremely unlikely that Jews should have produced or invented such a Greek phrase."* (Scholem, *Major Trends*, 1941). **Saul Lieberman** and **Peter Schäfer** have defended versions of this etymology against Scholem.
- **Greek *metator*** (μετάτωρ) — Roman military "forerunner / herald / measurer." Supported by **Philip Alexander**; the *metator* was an officer who went ahead to measure out camps.
- **Hebrew *matara*** (מטרה) — "keeper of the watch" / "target." Marginal proposal.
- **Hebrew *Yahoel* (YHV-EL)** — Scholem's own theory: that *Metatron* was developed as a *vox mystica* (mystical voice / sound) replacement for an earlier name **Yaho-el**, which appears in the *Apocalypse of Abraham* and other early texts. Scholem: Metatron *"was originally formed apparently in order to replace the name Yahoel as a vox mystica, [and] it gradually usurped its place."*

The current scholarly consensus is that the etymology remains genuinely obscure. Whether the name originated in a Greek philosophical vocabulary, a Roman military term, an earlier Hebrew theonym, or some combination is unresolved.

## Kabbalah — position on the Tree

In **Kabbalah** (the medieval-and-later mystical tradition), Metatron is associated with the topmost Sefirah of the **Tree of Life** — usually **Keter** ("Crown"), sometimes **Tiferet** ("Beauty"), depending on the schema. As the angel of *Keter*, Metatron sits at the upper bound of the Sefirotic emanation — the angelic figure closest to the divine source, from which creative energy descends through the sefirot to the manifest world.

Kabbalistic teaching maintains the Talmudic boundary: Metatron is **not** identical with the divine *Ein Sof* (the unmanifest infinite). He is the highest angel; the *Ein Sof* is the unknowable ground (compare Eckhart's *Gottheit* in the same-day `GODHEAD-DISAMBIGUATION.md`).

## Metatron's Cube — the modern attribution

A geometric figure called **Metatron's Cube** circulates widely in modern Western esoteric and "sacred geometry" literature. It is derived from the **Flower of Life** pattern (a tessellation of overlapping circles): connecting the centers of 13 specific circles produces lines whose pattern contains the outlines of all five **Platonic solids** (tetrahedron, hexahedron/cube, octahedron, dodecahedron, icosahedron).

The five Platonic solids are sometimes associated with the five classical elements (earth, water, fire, air, ether/aether). Modern New Age and esoteric writers extend the symbolism to cover meditation, energy work, and a wide variety of spiritual practices.

**Important precision**: the *naming* of the figure as *Metatron's Cube* is **modern**, not ancient. The geometric figure itself has ancient antecedents (the Flower of Life appears at Egyptian and Greek sites), but the specific attribution to Metatron belongs to recent Western esotericism, not to the ancient Jewish mystical tradition. The Metatron of 3 Enoch and the Talmud does not have a "Cube" associated with him in the primary sources. Treat the modern "Metatron's Cube" as a 20th-century syncretic construction, not Metatron-doctrine proper.

## Christian and Western-esoteric reception

The Metatron figure traveled into Christian and Western esoteric traditions through several channels:

- **Christian Kabbalah** (Pico della Mirandola, Johannes Reuchlin, ~15th-16th c.) — adopted Jewish mystical material as evidence for Christian doctrine; Metatron was read as a type or prefiguration of Christ
- **Medieval grimoires** (Sworn Book of Honorius, others) — Metatron invoked as one of the principal archangels for theurgic purposes
- **Renaissance and early modern angelology** — Metatron in lists of the seven (or nine) primary archangels
- **Modern occult orders** (Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn, Thelema) — Metatron in ritual and ceremonial frameworks
- **Popular culture** — Metatron appears in modern fiction (Philip Pullman's *His Dark Materials*; Kevin Smith's *Dogma*; *Supernatural*; *Good Omens*; multiple video games) — the popular cultural figure is downstream of the esoteric reception, not the rabbinic primary sources

Logan's framing — *"Voice of God"* — names Metatron's most central function. Compare the *bat kol* (the heavenly voice) from `HEBREW-BIBLE-AND-TALMUD.md`:

Bat kol issues; Metatron voices. The voice is divine; the voicer is angelic. The distinction is the doctrine.

## What this compilation does NOT do

- **Does not adjudicate the "two powers" question.** Rabbinic Judaism, Christianity, Jewish mysticism, and Gnosticism each hold their own positions; the compilation presents the rabbinic position as the rabbinic position and notes the contested terrain.
- **Does not survey the full Enochic literature.** *1 Enoch* (Ethiopic), *2 Enoch* (Slavonic), and the various Aramaic fragments at Qumran are substantial corpora; only the directly Metatron-relevant material (3 Enoch / Sefer Hekhalot) is surveyed here.
- **Does not exhaust the etymology debate.** Scholem, Lieberman, Schäfer, Philip Alexander, and many others have proposed positions; the compilation names the principal proposals without attempting resolution.
- **Does not survey the modern New Age / Western-esoteric Metatron in depth.** Metatron's Cube and modern angelology are noted but not surveyed; the compilation favors the ancient and medieval Jewish sources.
- **Does not perform the Metatron / Christ-as-Logos comparison.** The parallel exists in the scholarly literature (Boyarin, Segal, others); the comparison is its own project, beyond this compilation's scope.
