In May, the City of Pocatello’s hearing examiner nixed plans for a $2.6 billion data center proposed in the city. Now, the developer’s attorneys are appealing the decision.

[As BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/05/14/lex-portneuf-data-center/), Lex Developments has applied to convert the former Hoku Materials polysilicon plant into an AI data center on 49 acres of land on the west side of Pocatello near a rail line.

[According to EastIdahoNews](https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/06/lex-developments-appeals-pocatellos-denial-of-application-to-build-controversial-ai-data-center/), the city’s hearing examiner heard hours of testimony, most of which was negative, complaining about the proposal. Of the 90 people who testified, only four spoke in favor, according to East Idaho News.

Lex Developments’ principal Gus Shutlz gave a short presentation before testimony and, at the end of the public hearing, told the hearing examiner, “At the end of the day, the city gets to tell us what we can and can’t do.”

Katleen Lewis, Pocatello’s hearing examiner, [ultimately found](https://bloximages.chicago2.vip.townnews.com/idahostatejournal.com/content/tncms/assets/v3/editorial/d/fe/dfe7f11d-fc09-4335-b994-bfa866ac6f63/6a21c9615fecc.pdf.pdf) the developer’s application did not “meet the standards for approval” for a conditional use permit. Her findings echoed a key complaint from the public during the hearing: the application needed more detail.

“The hearing examiner finds that the application does not adequately address that the project would not be detrimental to  
the public interests, health, safety, or welfare of the city,” Lewis wrote. “The applicant should conduct studies/impact analysis for power, water and wastewater capacity, and air quality analysis prior to submitting another application. These studies should include at a minimum: wastewater discharge quantities; identification of pretreatment systems if needed; potential chemical discharge; water demand calculations; long-term electrical infrastructure requirements and environmental impacts related to emissions, water utilization and wastewater discharge.”

Lex’s attorney, Jon A. Stenquist with Parsons Behle & Latimer, filed an extensive appeal, which will move the decision to Pocatello’s city council for a decision. Stenquist’s letter contends the city’s process was flawed and that the project does not require a conditional use permit. He also notes that the former Hoku project was used as an industrial plant and calls it a “far more intensive use” than the proposed data center.

Stenquist also claimed that Idaho’s law called for the application to be approved, with conditions — instead of denial.

“If the hearing examiner genuinely believed additional technical information was necessary, the appropriate course was straightforward,” Stenquist argued. “That is, approve the application subject to conditions requiring completion of those studies before commencement of construction or issuance of final permits. The complete denial instead transformed the CUP process into an open-ended and prohibitively expensive prerequisite regime far removed from the ordinary sequencing of land use and permitting review.”

The Pocatello City Council has not yet set a date for reconsideration of the proposal.

Lex Developments now owns the property, according to Idaho commercial real estate firm CBRE. Portneuf Capital sold the site to Lex earlier this spring, CBRE said.
