**John Gutzon de la Mothe Borglum** (March 25, 1867 – March 6, 1941) was an American [sculptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture "Sculpture") best known for his work on [Mount Rushmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rushmore "Mount Rushmore"). He is also associated with various other public works of art across the U.S., including [Stone Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Mountain "Stone Mountain") in Georgia, statues of Union General [Philip Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sheridan "Philip Sheridan") [in Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue_of_Philip_Sheridan_\(Washington,_D.C.\) "Equestrian statue of Philip Sheridan (Washington, D.C.)"), and [in Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue_of_Philip_Sheridan_\(Chicago\) "Equestrian statue of Philip Sheridan (Chicago)"), as well as [a bust of Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bust_of_Abraham_Lincoln_\(Borglum\) "Bust of Abraham Lincoln (Borglum)") exhibited in the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") by [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") [^6] and now held in the [United States Capitol crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol_crypt "United States Capitol crypt") in Washington, D.C.[^7]

## Early life

The son of [Danish immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_Americans "Danish Americans"), John Gutzon de la Mothe Borglum was born in 1867 in [St. Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Charles,_Idaho "St. Charles, Idaho"), in what was then thought to be in Utah but was later determined to be in [Idaho Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idaho_Territory "Idaho Territory"). Borglum was a child of [Mormon polygamy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism_and_polygamy "Mormonism and polygamy"). His father, Jens Møller Haugaard Børglum (1839–1909), came from the village of [Børglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8rglum "Børglum") in northwestern Denmark. He had two wives when he lived in Idaho: Gutzon's mother, Christina Mikkelsen Børglum (1847–1871), and her sister Ida, who was Jens's first wife.[^8]

Jens Borglum decided to leave the [LDS Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints") and moved to [Omaha, Nebraska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaha,_Nebraska "Omaha, Nebraska"), where polygamy was both illegal and taboo.[^9] Jens Borglum had worked mainly as a woodcarver before his decision to attend the Saint Louis Homeopathic Medical College [^10] in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), Missouri. At this point "Jens and Christina divorced, the family left the LDS church, and Jens, Ida, their children, and Christina's two sons, Gutzon and Solon, moved to St. Louis, where Jens earned a medical degree." Upon his graduation from the Missouri Medical College in 1874, Dr. Borglum moved the family to Fremont, Nebraska, where he established a medical practice.[^11] [^12] Gutzon Borglum remained in Fremont until 1882, when his father enrolled him in [St. Mary's College, Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary's_Academy_and_College "Saint Mary's Academy and College").[^13]

After a brief stint at Saint Mary's College, Gutzon Borglum moved to Omaha, Nebraska, where he apprenticed in a machine shop and graduated from [Creighton Preparatory School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creighton_Preparatory_School "Creighton Preparatory School").

## New York City

In New York City, he sculpted saints and apostles for the new [Cathedral of St. John the Divine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._John_the_Divine "Cathedral of St. John the Divine") in 1901; in 1906 he had a group sculpture accepted by the [Metropolitan Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Museum_of_Art "Metropolitan Museum of Art") [^14] —the first sculpture by a living American the museum had ever purchased—and made his presence further felt with some portraits. He also won the [Logan Medal of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Medal_of_the_Arts "Logan Medal of the Arts"). His reputation soon surpassed that of his younger brother [Solon Borglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon_Borglum "Solon Borglum"), already an established sculptor.

## Family

In 1889, Borglum married his painting instructor, [Elizabeth Putnam Janes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Borglum "Elizabeth Borglum"), who was 18 years his senior.[^15] After divorcing his first wife, Borglum married Mary Montgomery Williams, on May 20, 1909, with whom he had three children,[^8] including a son, [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Borglum "Lincoln Borglum"), and a daughter, Mary Ellis (Mel) Borglum Vhay.

## Commissioned to design David Livingstone Centenary medal

According to the American Geographical Society website, "The [David Livingstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Livingstone "David Livingstone") Centenary Medal was founded at the initiative of the [Hispanic Society of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_Society_of_America "Hispanic Society of America") in March 1913". The site goes on to state "Gutzon Borglum, the sculptor of Mt. Rushmore, was the designer of this unusually beautiful example of medallic art." The same website page notes that [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") was awarded the medal in 1917.[^16]

## Public life

Borglum was active in the committee that organized the New York [Armory Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armory_Show "Armory Show") of 1913, the birthplace of [modernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism "Modernism") in American art. By the time the show was ready to open, however, Borglum had resigned from the committee, feeling that the emphasis on avant-garde works had co-opted the original premise of the show and made traditional artists like himself look provincial. He moved into [an estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgland "Borgland") in [Stamford, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamford,_Connecticut "Stamford, Connecticut"),[^17] in 1914 and lived there for 10 years. He sheltered [Czechoslovak Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Legion "Czechoslovak Legion") members on his land at Stamford in 1917.[^18]

Borglum was an active member of the Ancient Free and Accepted Masons (the [Freemasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemasonry "Freemasonry")), raised in Howard Lodge #35, New York City, on June 10, 1904, and serving as its Worshipful Master 1910–11. In 1915, he was appointed Grand Representative of the Grand Lodge of Denmark near the Grand Lodge of New York. He received his Scottish Rite Degrees in the New York City Consistory on October 25, 1907.[^19] He was friends with [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") for many years [^20] [^21] and during the [1912 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_United_States_presidential_election "1912 United States presidential election") Borglum was a very active campaign organizer and member [^22] of the [Bull Moose Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull_Moose_Party "Bull Moose Party").[^23] [^24]

While it has been claimed that Borglum was a member of the [Ku Klux Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan "Ku Klux Klan"),[^7] an article in the *Smithsonian Magazine* denies that there is proof that he officially joined the KKK.[^25] That said, he became "deeply involved in Klan politics", attending Klan rallies and serving on Klan committees.[^23]<sup><span title="Page / location: 186">: 186</span> </sup> In 1925, having only completed the head of Robert E. Lee, Borglum was dismissed from the Stone Mountain project, with some holding that it came about due to infighting within the KKK, with Borglum involved in the strife.[^26] Later, he stated "I am not a member of the Kloncilium, nor a knight of the KKK", but Howard Shaff and Audrey Karl Shaff claim that "that was for public consumption".[^27] The museum at Mount Rushmore displays a letter to Borglum from [D. C. Stephenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._C._Stephenson "D. C. Stephenson"), the infamous Klan Grand Dragon who later was convicted of the rape and murder of [Madge Oberholtzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madge_Oberholtzer "Madge Oberholtzer"). The 8 ft × 10 ft (2.4 m × 3.0 m) portrait contains the inscription "To my good friend Gutzon Borglum, with the greatest respect." Correspondence from Borglum to Stephenson during the 1920s detailed a deep [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_the_United_States "Racism in the United States") conviction in [Nordic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordicism "Nordicism") moral superiority and strict immigration policies.[^28]

## Monuments

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a8/General_Philip_Sheridan_Memorial_-_Rienzi.JPG/250px-General_Philip_Sheridan_Memorial_-_Rienzi.JPG)

General Philip Sheridan, sculpted by Borglum in 1908, in Washington, D.C.

A fascination with gigantic scale and themes of heroic nationalism suited his extroverted personality. His [head of Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bust_of_Abraham_Lincoln_\(Borglum\) "Bust of Abraham Lincoln (Borglum)"), carved from a six-ton block of marble, was exhibited in [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt") 's [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House") and can be found in the [United States Capitol Crypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Capitol_crypt "United States Capitol crypt") in [Washington, D.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C.") A "patriot", believing that the "monuments we have built are not our own", he looked to create art that was "American, drawn from American sources, memorializing American achievement", according to a 1908 interview. Borglum was highly suited to the competitive environment surrounding the contracts for public buildings and monuments, and his public sculptures are found all around the United States.

In 1908, Borglum won a competition for an [equestrian statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue_of_Philip_Sheridan_\(Washington,_D.C.\) "Equestrian statue of Philip Sheridan (Washington, D.C.)") of the Civil War General [Philip Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Sheridan "Philip Sheridan") to be placed in [Sheridan Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan_Circle "Sheridan Circle") in Washington, D.C. A second version of *General Philip Sheridan* was erected in [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois "Chicago, Illinois"), in 1923. Winning this competition was a personal triumph for him because he won out over sculptor [J. Q. A. Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Quincy_Adams_Ward "John Quincy Adams Ward"), a much older and more established artist and one whom Borglum had clashed with earlier in regard to the [National Sculpture Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sculpture_Society "National Sculpture Society"). At the unveiling of the Sheridan statue, one observer, President Theodore Roosevelt (whom Borglum was later to include in the Mount Rushmore portrait group), declared that it was "first rate"; a critic wrote that "as a sculptor Gutzon Borglum was no longer a rumor, he was a fact".[^13]

President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt "Franklin D. Roosevelt") delivered an address on May 3, 1934, dedicating a statue of [William Jennings Bryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Jennings_Bryan "William Jennings Bryan") created by Borglum. This Bryan statue by Borglum originally stood in Washington, D.C., but was later displaced by highway construction and moved by an Act of Congress in 1961 to [Salem, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Illinois "Salem, Illinois"), Bryan's birthplace.[^29] [^30]

In 1925, the sculptor moved to Texas to work on the monument to trail drivers commissioned by the Trail Drivers Association. He completed the model in 1925, but due to lack of funds it was not cast until 1940, and then was only a fourth its originally planned size. It stands in front of the Texas Pioneer and Trail Drivers Memorial Hall next to the [Witte Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witte_Museum "Witte Museum") in [San Antonio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio "San Antonio"). Borglum lived at the historic [Menger Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menger_Hotel "Menger Hotel"), which in the 1920s was the residence of a number of artists. He subsequently planned the redevelopment of the Corpus Christi waterfront; the plan failed, although a model for a statue of Christ intended for it was later modified by his son and erected on a mountaintop in South Dakota. While living and working in Texas, Borglum took an interest in local beautification. He promoted change and modernity, although he was berated by academicians.[^31]

### Stone Mountain

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ee/Stone_mountain_memorial_half_dollar_commemorative_obverse_reverse.jpg/250px-Stone_mountain_memorial_half_dollar_commemorative_obverse_reverse.jpg)

Stone Mountain Memorial half dollar, 1925 (design by Borglum)

Borglum was initially involved in the carving of [Stone Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Mountain "Stone Mountain") in [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_\(U.S._state\) "Georgia (U.S. state)"). Borglum's [nativist stances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nativism_\(politics\)#20th_century "Nativism (politics)") made him seem an ideologically sympathetic choice to carve a memorial to heroes of the [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America "Confederate States of America"), planned for [Stone Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Mountain "Stone Mountain"), Georgia. In 1915, coinciding with the Klan-glorifying, highly successful *[The Birth of a Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Birth_of_a_Nation "The Birth of a Nation")*, he was approached by the [United Daughters of the Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Daughters_of_the_Confederacy "United Daughters of the Confederacy") with a project for sculpting a 20-foot (6 m) high bust of General [Robert E. Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_E._Lee "Robert E. Lee") on the mountain's 800-foot (240 m) rockface. Borglum accepted, but told the committee, "Ladies, a twenty-foot head of Lee on that mountainside would look like a postage stamp on a barn door." [^32]

Borglum's ideas eventually evolved into a high relief [frieze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieze "Frieze") of Lee, [Jefferson Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Davis "Jefferson Davis"), and [Stonewall Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_Jackson "Stonewall Jackson") riding around the mountain, followed by a legion of artillery troops. Borglum agreed to include a [Ku Klux Klan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan "Ku Klux Klan") altar in his plans for the memorial to acknowledge a request of [Helen Plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Plane "Helen Plane") in 1915, who wrote to him: "I feel it is due to the KKK that saved us from Negro domination and [carpetbag rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpetbagger "Carpetbagger"), that it be immortalized on Stone Mountain".[^26]

After a delay caused by [World War I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), Borglum and the newly chartered Stone Mountain Confederate Monumental Association set to work on this monument, the largest ever attempted. Many difficulties slowed progress, some because of the sheer scale involved. After finishing the detailed model of the carving, Borglum was unable to trace the figures onto the massive area on which he was working, until he developed a gigantic [magic lantern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic_lantern "Magic lantern") to project the image onto the side of the mountain.

Carving officially began on June 23, 1923, with Borglum making the first cut. At Stone Mountain he developed sympathetic connections with the reorganized Ku Klux Klan, who were major financial backers of the monument. Lee's head was unveiled on Lee's birthday January 19, 1924, to a large crowd, but soon thereafter Borglum was increasingly at odds with the officials of the organization. His domineering, perfectionist, authoritarian manner brought tensions to such a point that in March 1925 Borglum smashed his clay and plaster models. He left Georgia permanently, his tenure with the organization over. None of his work remains, as it was all blasted off the mountain's face for the work of Borglum's replacement [Henry Augustus Lukeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Augustus_Lukeman "Henry Augustus Lukeman"). In his abortive attempt, however, Borglum had developed the necessary techniques for sculpting on a gigantic scale that made Mount Rushmore possible.[^33]

### Mount Rushmore

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/1/19/Mount_Rushmore_Closeup_2017.jpg/250px-Mount_Rushmore_Closeup_2017.jpg)

Mount Rushmore, located in the Black Hills of South Dakota

His Mount Rushmore project, 1927–1941, was the brainchild of South Dakota state historian [Doane Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doane_Robinson "Doane Robinson").[^34] His first attempt with the face of [Thomas Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Jefferson "Thomas Jefferson") had to be redone when it was determined that there was not enough stone to complete it.[^35] Dynamite was used to remove large areas of rock from under Washington's brow. The initial pair of presidents, [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington "George Washington") and Thomas Jefferson, was soon joined by [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln "Abraham Lincoln") and [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt").[^13]

Ivan Houser, father of [John Sherrill Houser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Sherrill_Houser "John Sherrill Houser"), was assistant sculptor to Gutzon Borglum in the early years of carving; he began working with Borglum shortly after the inception of the monument and was with Borglum for a total of seven years. When Houser left Gutzon to devote his talents to his own work, Gutzon's son, Lincoln, took over as assistant sculptor to his father.[^36]

Borglum alternated exhausting on-site supervising with world tours, raising money, polishing his personal legend, sculpting a [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine "Thomas Paine") memorial for Paris and a [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson") memorial for [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84 "Poznań"), Poland (1931).[^37] In his absence, work at Mount Rushmore was overseen by Bill Tallman [^38] and later his son, [Lincoln Borglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Borglum "Lincoln Borglum").[^39] During the Rushmore project, father and son were residents of Beeville, Texas. When he died in Chicago, following complications of surgery, his son finished another season at Rushmore, but left the monument largely in the state of completion it had reached under his father's direction.[^40]

### Other works

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/90/John_William_Mackay_statue.jpg/250px-John_William_Mackay_statue.jpg)

Statue of Comstock Lode silver baron John William Mackay (1831–1902), Mackay School of Earth Sciences and Engineering, University of Nevada, Reno (1908)

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/c/cf/%22The_Aviator%22_%28of_1919%29_by_Gutzon_Borglum.jpg/250px-%22The_Aviator%22_%28of_1919%29_by_Gutzon_Borglum.jpg)

Aviator, Borglum, 1919, University of Virginia

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b5/William_Dempster_Hoard_Sculpture_-_panoramio.jpg/250px-William_Dempster_Hoard_Sculpture_-_panoramio.jpg)

William Dempster Hoard Sculpture in Henry Mall in front of Agriculture Hall, University of Wisconsin-Madison (1922)

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/b/b0/BorglumNC_monument.jpg/250px-BorglumNC_monument.jpg)

North Carolinian soldiers at the Battle of Gettysburg

In 1909, the sculpture *[Rabboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabboni_\(sculpture\) "Rabboni (sculpture)")* was created as a grave site for the Ffoulke Family in Washington, D.C., at [Rock Creek Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_Creek_Cemetery "Rock Creek Cemetery").[^41]

Four public works by Borglum are in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey "Newark, New Jersey"): *[Seated Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seated_Lincoln_\(Borglum\) "Seated Lincoln (Borglum)")* (1911), *[Indian and the Puritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_and_the_Puritan "Indian and the Puritan")* (1916), *[Wars of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_America "Wars of America")* (1926), and a [stele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stele "Stele") with bas-relief, *[First Landing Party of the Founders of Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Landing_Party_of_the_Founders_of_Newark "First Landing Party of the Founders of Newark")* (1916). No other U.S. city holds as many public displays by Borglum.[^42] [^43]

In 1912, the *[Nathaniel Wheeler Memorial Fountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Wheeler_Memorial_Fountain "Nathaniel Wheeler Memorial Fountain")*, designed by Borglum, was dedicated in [Bridgeport, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport,_Connecticut "Bridgeport, Connecticut").

A memorial to [Robert Louis Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Louis_Stevenson "Robert Louis Stevenson") at Baker Cottage, [Saranac Lake, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saranac_Lake,_New_York "Saranac Lake, New York"), was unveiled in 1915.

In 1916, he overhauled the design of the torch for the [Statue of Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Liberty "Statue of Liberty") in New York City.[^44]

In 1918, he was one of the drafters of the [Czechoslovak declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_declaration_of_independence "Czechoslovak declaration of independence").[^45]

One of Borglum's more unusual pieces is *[The Aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Aviator_\(Charlottesville,_Virginia\) "The Aviator (Charlottesville, Virginia)")*, completed in 1919 as a memorial for [James Rogers McConnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Rogers_McConnell "James Rogers McConnell"), who was killed in World War I while flying for the [Lafayette Escadrille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Escadrille "Lafayette Escadrille"). It is located on the grounds of the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia") in [Charlottesville, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlottesville,_Virginia "Charlottesville, Virginia").[^46]

In 1922, he crafted the [William Dempster Hoard Sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Dempster_Hoard_Sculpture "William Dempster Hoard Sculpture") in the north end what is now the [Henry Mall Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Mall_Historic_District "Henry Mall Historic District") at the [University of Wisconsin-Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison").[^47]

His statue of Collis P. Huntington was completed in 1924 and stands at the entrance of the CSX Huntington headquarters building located in the 900 block of Seventh Avenue, [Huntington, West Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington,_West_Virginia "Huntington, West Virginia").

His [statue of Harvey W. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Harvey_W._Scott "Statue of Harvey W. Scott") was completed in 1933 and stood at the peak of [Mount Tabor, Portland, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor,_Portland,_Oregon "Mount Tabor, Portland, Oregon"), until it was toppled by protestors in 2020.

Borglum sculpted the *[Memorial to the "Start Westward of the United States"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Start_Westward_Memorial "Start Westward Memorial")*, which is located in [Muskingum Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muskingum_Park?action=edit&redlink=1 "Muskingum Park (page does not exist)"), [Marietta, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marietta,_Ohio "Marietta, Ohio") (1938).[^48] The work was featured on a 1938 3¢ US postage stamp.[^49]

He built the statue of [Daniel Butterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Butterfield "Daniel Butterfield") at [Sakura Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakura_Park "Sakura Park") in Manhattan (1918).[^50]

He created a memorial to [Sacco and Vanzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacco_and_Vanzetti "Sacco and Vanzetti") (1928), a plaster cast of which is now in the Boston Public Library.[^51] [^52] [^53] [^54] [^55]

Another Borglum design is the [North Carolina Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_State_Monument_\(Gettysburg,_Pennsylvania\) "North Carolina State Monument (Gettysburg, Pennsylvania)") on [Seminary Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary_Ridge "Seminary Ridge") at the [Gettysburg Battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Battlefield "Gettysburg Battlefield") in south-central [Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania "Pennsylvania"). The cast bronze sculpture depicts a wounded [Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_Army "Confederate States Army") officer encouraging his men to push forward during [Pickett's Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickett's_Charge "Pickett's Charge"). Borglum had also made arrangements for an airplane to fly over the monument during the dedication ceremony on July 3, 1929. During the sculpture's unveiling, the plane scattered roses across the field as a salute to those North Carolinians who had fought and died at Gettysburg.

In 1939 when German troops marched into Poland, they destroyed Borglum's statue of [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson "Woodrow Wilson") located in [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84 "Poznań").[^56]

## Death

While visiting Chicago in 1941 to give a talk, Borglum began feeling ill and underwent routine [prostate surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostate_surgery "Prostate surgery"). However, [blood clots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_clots "Blood clots") resulting from the surgery led to a series of [heart attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack "Heart attack"), with the final one resulting in his death on March 6. He was initially buried in a Chicago cemetery, with plans to later [reinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinter "Reinter") his body elsewhere. The Rushmore Commission recommended that his body be interred in a specially constructed tomb at Mount Rushmore. However, there was no approval for his wife to be buried alongside him there, and several years after his death, his body was interred at its final resting place at [Forest Lawn Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Lawn_Memorial_Park_\(Glendale\) "Forest Lawn Memorial Park (Glendale)") in Glendale, California.[^13] [^57] [^58]

His son [Lincoln Borglum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Borglum "Lincoln Borglum") completed the Mount Rushmore memorial and served as the memorial's first superintendent.[^13] [^57]
