Skiers, snowboarders, and mountain bikers rejoice: Bogus Basin has some new projects in the works.

Bogus Basin Director of Mountain Operations Nate Shake said that Bogus Basin recently received approval for four new projects at the recreation area — two new alpine trails, a chairlift realignment, and two mountain bike trail extensions.

## What’s coming?

Because the recreation area operates on U.S. Forest Service land with a special use permit, that agency had to sign off on upgrades and changes to the organization’s land use within that special use permit boundary, Shake said.

![Build Idaho - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/8679b89c-de2f-4b22-948e-bd39a76c4c7f.png)

Shake said that this summer, crews will clear and grade two new groomed runs in Pine Creek. Pine Creek sits on the back side of Shafer Butte.

The organization also received approval to realign the Showcase chair lift up the south face of Deer Point Mountain to Cabin Traverse. That change will serve the back side of the mountain, Shake said. He estimated that the work could be done in the next 2-5 years, depending on snowfall in the next several winters, which tends to drive revenue that the organization can put toward new projects.

The 2025-2026 winter season brought an unusually low snowpack to many of Idaho’s watersheds, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/newsletter/04-01-26-2-2-2-2-2/). On April 1, the point at which basins transition from peak annual snowpack to spring melt-off, Bogus Basin sat at 62% of normal snow water equivalent, a measurement of water storage, BoiseDev reported[.](https://boisedev.com/newsletter/04-01-26-2-2-2-2-2/)

“If we go into another winter similar to this one, it slows our ability to pay for these improvements,” Shake said.

The next projects in the works are two mountain bike trail extensions, Shake said. The first would extend Deer Point Trail to Around the Mountain Trail. The second would extend Elk Meadows Trail to the top of the Morning Star Express chair lift. Connecting Elk Meadows to the top of the lift completes Elk Meadows as a single track trail all the way around Shafer’s Butte, he said.

## When will Bogus Basin have a new master plan?

So how does Bogus Basin decide which projects to move forward with?

![Boise Parks & Rec - Activity Guide August - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/2a472358-d841-43cb-92a9-38111f86cacf.png)

Boise Parks & Rec - Activity Guide August - 2:1

Shake said that Bogus Basin developed its first master plan in 1996, and created a new one in 2016. Master plans tend to outline an organization’s plans for development over a set period of time, such as ten years. He said that the organization is currently operating off of its 2016 master plan. Bogus Basin leadership used the organization’s strategic business plan to create the master plan, with each proposed project corresponding to a revenue increase, he said.

Since 2016, Bogus Basin has spent $80 million on projects, all paid for without the organization taking on debt, Shake said. One project included installing the mountain’s original snowmaking pond, and installing snowmaking infrastructure on about 100 acres, Shake said.

The plan was amended in 2023 to allow for several improvements, including expanding night skiing terrain on the Superior and Triumph runs, the installation of the Coach’s chairlift, doubling the length of Coach’s Corner run, and adding snowmaking devices and night lighting.

Shake said that the organization might amend the plan one more time, likely in 2026, before developing a new master plan. An amendment would include expanding the recreation area’s operating terrain, including adding new lifts and trails outside of the recreation area’s current permitted area with the Forest Service, he said.

Following that, it could take two to five years to develop and put a new master plan in place, according to Shake.

As leaders contemplate changes, they are also thinking of infrastructure improvements, like parking and lodges, to accommodate additional visitors. [Pioneer Lodge underwent a $5 million remodel](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/05/23/bogus-basin-pioneer-lodge-upgrade-rennovation-boise/), and next winter, visitors will be able to enjoy a new 10,000 sq. ft. food, beverage, and event facility with capacity for 250 people near the Pioneer base area, Shake said. [BoiseDev shared details about the new facility earlier this year](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/01/13/bogus-basin-new-food-venue-the-outpost/).
