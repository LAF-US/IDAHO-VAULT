A new, faster speed limit is planned to replace the 25 mph limit on Highway 55 in Horseshoe Bend.

The Idaho Transportation Department, in partnership with the City of Horseshoe Bend, is working on roadway improvements that will lead to the elimination of the city’s current 25 mph speed limit through town.

According to ITD Outreach Coordinator Matt Kreizenbeck, the department is adding drainage improvements with a curb gutter and sidewalk on the east side of the highway, as well as an enhanced crosswalk with flashing beacons — called a pedestrian hybrid beacon — near the main entrance of the high school. Drainage improvements and a sidewalk were previously installed on the west side of the highway.

The project will start at the Payette River bridge and extend to a small access road just south of Horseshoe Bend High School’s baseball field.

ITD Public Information Officer Jill Youmans said the new 35 mph speed limit will be in effect except during morning and afternoon school commute times, when new flashing beacons will alert drivers to the slower speed limit — much like a classic school zone. Horseshoe Bend School District holds school Monday through Thursday.

“That doesn’t seem like a big change, but from a traffic congestion standpoint, that makes a very large difference in the large queues that we form on weekends in Horseshoe Bend. So we’re very excited to be making these community-focused improvements,” ITD District Engineer Jason Brinkman told the ITD Board in a presentation on the project June 17.

![Morrison Center - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/37e7ef09-855c-4668-ba54-b9eb50afe557.png)

Kreizenback said the department hasn’t determined exactly what the school zone speed limit will be, but it will be no more than 25 mph.

Youmans said she is “excited” for the project because it will help alleviate some of the Highway 55 traffic congestion on summer weekends. She said the pedestrian safety upgrades were a “mutually beneficial solution” to the issue because the department and city felt it could increase traffic speeds without worrying about children walking along the edge of the highway and being unsafe.

The project is slated to begin this fall, sometime after Labor Day, and will be finished before winter, Youmans said. Once the upgrades are complete, the speed limit will be raised.

Kreizenback said the project cost ITD approximately $700,000. He said Horseshoe Bend also received a $500,000 grant to extend the sidewalk from the northern limit of ITD’s portion of the project to “as close to the Chevron on the north side of town as possible.”
