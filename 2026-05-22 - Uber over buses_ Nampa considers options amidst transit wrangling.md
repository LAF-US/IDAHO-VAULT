The abrupt cancellation of a presentation planned as part of a city budget workshop prompted concerns of political foul play early Thursday morning.

The Nampa City Council was scheduled to hear two presentations during its city council workshop Thursday morning: one from Valley Regional Transit, the Treasure Valley’s public transit agency, and one from Uber, the private ride-hailing company, about “providing public transportation.” The workshop is part of the city’s annual series of budget-setting workshops for the upcoming fiscal year.

But Nampa Council President David Bills announced at the beginning of the meeting that the council had received an email that the person presenting for Uber would not present after all. Bills said he was concerned about political meddling and initially said he was not interested in hearing VRT’s presentation.

“I would say politics are at work,” Bills said. “Uber has provided us with communication as of this morning that their senior leadership has said they are not coming to Nampa, and they are going to support VRT … we’re going to suspend any presentation, because Elaine, your presentation says you can do all kinds of things that we haven’t been presented with in the last two years, so to walk through this, that you can now provide these services, is almost offensive for me personally.”

Bills said that Uber had put together a proposal, and even flew someone into town to speak, but then decided not to go forward with the presentation.

Bills left it to the council to decide whether to allow VRT CEO Elaine Clegg to present to the council as scheduled. Clegg requested a moment to speak, and said that she and VRT had nothing to do with Uber’s cancellation.

“It’s a curveball for me too,” Clegg said. “If you think we organized this, we did not. I’m just as surprised as you are. I hope you know that.”

Council Member Dale Reynolds and Council Member Sebastian Griffin said they still wanted to hear Clegg’s presentation.

Bills told Clegg that he “jumped the gun in saying that you weren’t going to present. My apology from the bench and personally,” and allowed Clegg’s presentation to proceed.

A statement provided to BoiseDev by Uber said that the company sometimes works with cities and transit entities on expanding mobility. Following discussions with Nampa stakeholders, the company will focus on working with Valley Regional Transit to support existing services, the company said.

## What is VRT proposing?

VRT offers two kinds of service in Nampa: fixed route buses, and on-demand service, in which customers have to call and schedule rides ahead of time.

Clegg said that VRT’s proposal is different compared to ideas shared at [a joint meeting with the Nampa and Caldwell city councils in February](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/02/26/van-pools-event-service-levy-power-with-bus-ridership-up-canyon-co-leaders-discuss-vrt-services-funding-streams/). Clegg said she had never been satisfied with the agency’s on-demand system, which was in place when she took the role as CEO.

![Western States - 2026 Q2 - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/77f3a89a-125c-4f85-be07-1598966cc675.jpg)

She said that the on-demand system relies on drivers who have commercial drivers licenses, and the vehicles are large and can’t offer curb-to-curb service. Though she had pushed staff for ways to update the on-demand service, they were not ready to preview a new path forward in February.

Uber is a type of Transportation Network Company, or TNC. Clegg said that some parts of the country integrate TNCs into their public transportation, and that this could be part of a strategy for Nampa — fixed route service could serve much of the city, with TNC service supplementing peak hours and customers at the edges of the service area.

A slide presented by Clegg estimated that funding the Micro Transit option would result in a decrease for VRT in the cost per boarding of 59%, while funding fixed route service would decrease the cost per boarding by 38%.

Clegg dubbed the better on-demand system combined with TNC as “Micro Transit,” with a price tag of $353,600. Continuing fixed route bus service would cost approximately $375,100, according to the presentation. When combined with other smaller costs, the total price tag would be $857,794, a decrease from $862,948 in the current fiscal year.

## What could a partnership with Uber look like?

Mark Steuer, Nampa’s director of development service, said that staff had worked with Uber to better understand the services they provide to cities and towns across the country.

Some cities or transit authorities contract with Uber to subsidize part of a rider’s fare. Steuer said that the city has a lot of flexibility in deciding what goes into the program, such as whether to subsidize rides with destinations in the city or elsewhere, and limiting the time of day subsidized rides are available.

![Zamzows - Class - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/299f1d40-a259-40e1-a36e-e3c98ae2cb8d.png)

Cities or transit entities also have the ability to adjust how much they subsidize rides or the destinations they subsidize rides for, even during the middle of the year, Steuer said.

“So it’s a very flexible program, and then there’s some local employment opportunities for people — more Uber drivers needed, some people might take advantage of that,” Steuer said.

One key logistical need would be either a part-time person or 50% of an employee’s time for staffing and administering the contract, maintaining the website, and other tasks, Steuer said. He estimated the cost at $45,000 per year.

Clegg said that if the council opts to make a drastic change in service, such as only funding an Uber rides program but not funding bus service, VRT would be required to hold a public hearing to explain the change to constituents. She asked that if the council goes that route, that they host such a public hearing jointly with VRT.

## What is the ‘required role of government?’

Council voiced concerns about at what level to fund VRT, saying they grappled with the proper role of city government.

Griffin said he believed that a city’s role was to fund public safety and infrastructure. He said the city’s department directors estimate that the city has $600 million in deferred maintenance, and that even if they started today, they could still only complete about $25 million in work per year if the city used “every contractor and every city resource we have available.” Projects Griffin said he is interested in funding include replacing aging sewer and water pipes, one of which burst a couple of days prior to the meeting. He also said he’d be interested in discussing a levy override to support paying for infrastructure improvements.

“The required role of government is that we have cops that show up when you get stabbed, and they have a road to get to you when that happens,” Griffin said. “Beyond that, it’s fluff, and it’s necessary fluff for the quality of life we have here, but it’s still fluff.”

Council Member Natalie Jangula said that public transportation is a service generally provided by cities.

“Part of having a community is what amenities does a community provide that make life better and bring a quality of life,” Jangula said. “ … I think it’s still a need. I still want to tailor it a bit. I think it needs to be more centralized to Nampa, but it is a public service, and it is something that is part of things we have to work through.”

Council Member Debbie Skaug said that the city’s decision to spend less on transportation could motivate private companies that VRT has attempted to partner with to step up to the plate.

“If we limit this, as we have to, it will give Elaine more sway with the private sector (VRT) services, the businesses,” Skaug said. “Micron should not be holding out. They should be doing more than anything we can do as a city, as well as Amazon and all these big companies coming here,” she said, noting that entities like hospitals pay VRT for programs getting patients to healthcare appointments.

Nampa City Attorney Preston Rutter said that with the workshop, the city had satisfied the public meeting requirement for the budget item. Bills said that additional discussions on funding for VRT would be noted for future budget hearings.
