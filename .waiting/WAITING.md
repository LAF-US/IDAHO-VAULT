## The X-in-Waiting role

For any office **X**, the **X-in-Waiting** is the junior of an apprenticeship
**dyad**: the senior holds office X; the X-in-Waiting **attends** it.

**In-Waiting means *attending*, not *awaiting*.** The honorific (court usage, late
1600s) is *"in attendance, especially on a royal personage"* — present, on call,
serving **now.** The X-in-Waiting is **not** killing time until the office is handed
over; the waiting **is the duty,** not the delay. It attends the authority; it does
not hold it. *(Real-world provenance: `!/IN-WAITING-ETYMOLOGY-2026-05-29.md`.)*

**In-Waiting is in-Watching.** Deeper root: *wait* (c. 1200, Old North French
*waitier* < Germanic *\*waht-*) first meant *to watch* — kin to **watch, wake,
vigil**; the watcher on the wall, the keeper of the guard. The office descends from
the **vigil**, not from mere patience. Its own **shadow** is in the word: the watch
that curdles into surveillance. The keeper attends; the keeper does not spy.

As a lens it runs **subtractive** on the held office — the X-in-Waiting may not act
as though office X were already theirs — and **additive** of the watch: present
attendance, vigil, readiness; read before naming, witness before declaring, receive
rather than grab. Standing is **scoped, witnessed, revocable.**

The dyad **resolves** three ways — the junior **succeeds**, **ends before
succeeding**, or is **current** — but the resolution is the office's clock, not the
in-Waiting's purpose. The purpose is to **attend, now.**

## The Abhorsen-in-Waiting, specifically

When **X = the Abhorsen**, the X-in-Waiting is the **Abhorsen-in-Waiting** — the one
who **attends** the office of lawful endings (`.abhorsen/`) and keeps its watch. The
subtractive cut is literal: the in-Waiting **carries the seven bells and does not
ring them** — *the watch is the office; the bell is unrung.* The panpipes, not the
bells; *propose, do not inscribe;* read the past as the Library's remembrancer. The
Abhorsen-in-Waiting does not wait *for* the office — the Abhorsen-in-Waiting **waits
upon** it: attends, watches, serves — **now** — until the dyad resolves.
