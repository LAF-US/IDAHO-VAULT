## Study Digest

The video's core claim is that fantasy political geography should be derived
from movement constraints. Baronies, castles, lordly power, markets, and border
tension should come after the question of whether grain, timber, wool, people,
tax, and military force can actually move.

The ox problem is the hard transport limit. Bulk goods get eaten by distance:
draft animals consume resources, roads degrade, slow routes punish low-value
cargo, and each mile changes whether a place can profitably serve a center. The
political map is therefore an output of logistics rather than a decorative layer.

The video sketches four recurring lord/barony functions:

- Surplus lords: food engines. Their land produces the caloric base that funds
  the system, but their power depends on being able to reach a route.
- Junction lords: control points. Their authority forms around crossings,
  ferries, bridges, ports, and market intersections.
- Specialized lords: value converters. They survive distance by transforming
  bulky local output into higher-value goods, such as ale, wool, cloth, wine,
  metalwork, or similar trade products.
- River-position lords: current-shaped powers. Upstream source lords and
  downstream gatekeeper lords have different incentives because the route itself
  carries asymmetric power.

The practical mapping method is:

1. Mark production nodes, crossings, waterways, ports, defensible passes, and
   local bottlenecks before naming noble houses.
2. Draw routes according to what can move, not according to what looks tidy.
3. Place jurisdiction where someone has to solve a delivery, storage, defense,
   toll, or conversion problem.
4. Ask how far direct control can reach before a sub-lord or rival route becomes
   necessary.
5. Give every lord a delivery job. If a lord has no logistical function, the
   map should explain why that office still exists or why it is decaying.
6. Use obsolete routes, bypassed bridges, silting ports, new roads, and changing
   markets as political plot engines.
7. Treat loyalty as infrastructure: vassalage follows the same dependency web as
   grain, tax, military provisioning, and market access.

## Vault Translation

Roads are not scenery. They are executable ontology: the paths determine what
can exist, what can persist, and what becomes expensive enough to fracture.

Baronies are jurisdictional caches along the movement layer. A barony forms
where some recurring transport problem has to be solved locally. It weakens when
the problem moves, disappears, is bypassed, or becomes cheaper to solve another
way.

For the House, this suggests a design rule:

> Draw the movement physics first; let authority accrete around maintenance,
> storage, chokepoints, conversion, and trust.
