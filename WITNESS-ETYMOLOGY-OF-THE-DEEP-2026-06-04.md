# Witness: Etymology and Cultural Connotations of "the Deep"

## Etymology

**deep** (adj.) ← Old English *dēop* "profound, vast, extending far down"
← Proto-Germanic **deupaz*
← PIE **dʰewb-* "deep, hollow"

| Language | Cognate | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Lithuanian | *dubus* | deep, hollow |
| OCS | *duno* | bottom, foundation |
| Welsh | *dwfn* | deep |
| Old Irish | *domun* | world (bottom → foundation → earth → world) |

Related English words from the same root: **dip**, **dive**, **depth**.

**deep** (n.) — Old English *dēop* "deep water, especially the sea."

**abyss** — Greek **ábyssos* = *a-* (not, without) + *byssós* (deep place) →
"bottomless." Latin *abyssus* "bottomless pit."

The word "deep" as a noun refers first to the sea — the archetypal deep. The
sea is not *deep* as a property; it is *the deep* as a location.

## Cultural Connotations

### 1. Hebrew Bible — [[tehom]] (תְּהוֹם)

The primordial deep, the chaotic waters before creation. In Genesis 1:2,
*darkness was over the face of the deep* — the [[tehom]] that God separates
and contains to make the world. Also the dwelling of Leviathan (Job, Psalms).

The deep is *chaos contained by divine order*. It precedes creation, it
surrounds creation, it must be held back.

### 2. Greek Mythology — [[tartarus]]

[[tartarus]] is the deep abyss beneath Hades, "as far beneath Hades as heaven is
above earth" (Homer, Iliad). A dungeon for the Titans, a place of torment.
Also one of the primordial beings — third to exist after [[chaos]] and Gaia.

The deep as *the lowest prison*, the *dungeon beneath the underworld*.

### 3. Greek — [[bythos]] (Βυθός)

Personified depths of the sea. Also used in [[gnostic]] cosmology: [[bythos]] is
the primordial depth, the first emanation, the source from which all aeons
descend.

The deep as *the origin*, the *source that cannot be fathomed*.

### 4. Norse Mythology — [[ginnungagap]]

The primordial void, the "yawning gap" between fire and ice, from which
all creation emerged. The deep as *the generative nothing*.

### 5. Biblical/Christian — [[abyss]]

In Revelation, the [[abyss]] (abyssos) is the bottomless pit from which the
Beast ascends. The prison of demons. The place that Christ descended into
([[harrowing of hell]]).

The deep as *the prison of what must not be released*.

See also [[WITNESS-ETYMOLOGY-ABYSS-2026-06-04]].

### 6. Sunken Cities

Atlantis (Plato), Ys (Brittany), Kumari Kandam (Tamil), R'lyeh (Lovecraft).
The deep as *what was lost and lies preserved beneath*.

### 7. [[depth psychology]]

Freud's "depth psychology" — the [[unconscious]] as the submerged part of the
mind. Jung's collective [[unconscious]] — the deep structure shared by all.
The deep as *the psyche below awareness*.

### 8. [[lovecraft]]

The deep as the abode of Cthulhu (R'lyeh), Dagon, the Deep Ones
(Y'ha-nthlei). Not just physical depth but *ontological depth* — the
universe is deeper than humanity can measure, and something lives in
that depth.

## Synthesis: The Deep as Archetype

| Connotation | What the deep is |

| **Unfathomable** | That which cannot be measured or bottomed |
