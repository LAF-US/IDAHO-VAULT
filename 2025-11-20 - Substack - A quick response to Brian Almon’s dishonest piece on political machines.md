I want to take a moment to respond to Brian Almon’s latest piece, which is nothing more than revisionist-history rubbish paired with more dishonest calls for unity on the right.

In the piece, which I encourage you not to read, Almon slobbers over the political machine the Idaho establishment has built over the years. That machine, now controlled by Brad Little, gets to pick legislative replacements, among other sweet perks that come with power.

Sure.

Things get odd when Almon starts postulating that the principled conservatives need to build a machine of their own to go toe to toe with Little’s weaponry.

Say what?

He also tells us that, in his mind, one cannot win elections and take principled stands. “Principled stands get headlines, but political machines win,” he wrote.

Uh. That’s absolutely not true, but OK. It can’t get weirder, right?

“If conservatives actually want to implement policy and change society, we need to learn to play the game,” he continued.

Oh, it did. Cool.

I’ll likely say more on this later, but I need to point out a few problems here.

First, I’ll gladly attack the messenger. Almon has a lane in the political arena, but it’s not the winning-elections one. He is fresh off losing a city council contest, and it’s a little odd that he thinks he’s in any sort of place to tell conservatives how to win anything. He’s not. Win a few races of significance, Brian, and then preach.

Next, I’ll point out that Idaho has had a political machine for nearly two decades. The Idaho Freedom Foundation has, since late 2008, developed good policy ideas and held lawmakers accountable for their votes. Since 2016, Idaho Freedom Action put a little more, ahem, political muscle into it, taking the fight right to lawmakers in their home districts.

Conservatives were and are winning. And they were doing it while taking principled stands. IFF and IFA never compromised, but they excelled at telling Idahoans the truth about their lawmakers.

Progress has been coming slowly but, as they say, Rome wasn’t built in a day. Since 2016, the IFF-led conservative machine has expelled dozens of liberal Republicans from the state, which has led to a far more conservative lawmaking body.

Lawmakers like Jeff Agenbroad, the late Carl Crabtree, Greg Chaney, Scott Syme, Jim Patrick, Rich Wills, and many, many more were shown the exit because IFF and IFA, working hand in hand, told the truth about their liberal voting records.

Wannabe pundits like Almon have suggested that 2025’s legislative session was one of the most conservative in the state’s history. If Almon believes that, it’s a serious blow to today’s piece because that didn’t come in a single election. In the 2024 elections, conservatives again routed the establishment, finally tipping the scales in conservatives’ favor. How did that come about? The IFF-led conservative machine.

I cannot understand how or why Almon ignores the movement’s rise in his piece.

Perhaps he hasn’t been around to understand how far Idaho’s grassroots conservative movement has come since 2008. If that’s the case, he might take some extra time to research things before posing as some sort of authority on state politics.

That, though, leads to another point I need to make. IFF and IFA have been instrumental in righting the ship, so to speak. Yet both organizations routinely come under attack from Lauren Walker and Brandy Paradee, two of Almon’s X (formerly Twitter) pals. Both women, desperate to control the political sphere and do Mike Moyle’s dirty work, routinely lie about IFF in a bid to, uh, sell $3 monthly subscriptions.

Almon preaches unity in his pieces but has said exactly nothing about Walker and Paradee’s near-constant attacks on the very conservative machine he says doesn’t exist and dreams of building. Almost daily, the two X screamers attack IFF, IFA, and a number of principled conservative lawmakers. That means he either agrees with their unfounded and dishonest criticism, or he lacks the courage to address it head-on. I’m not sure which is worse.

In the end, this piece is Almon’s latest attempt to stand on the moral high ground. He poses as a uniter, calling for peace among the factions. Yet he’s a wizard of doublespeak as he pals around with two of the most divisive voices in the political arena.

I’ll reshare here what I wrote for my Freedom Bros Podcast a while back. Every word remains true to this day.

Here it is:

*He doesn’t want unity. He wants unilateral disarmament—while his friends continue to lob personal attacks at some of the best conservatives in the Statehouse and the groups that helped make Idaho a conservative stronghold.*

*Unilateral disarmament is no longer an option.*

*If Almon and others sincerely desire unity, they ought to seek it from all parties in this factional conflict.*

Almon needs a reality check. The machine absolutely exists, but his pals are working tirelessly to tear it down so they can sell X subscriptions and please Mike Moyle.

Brian, once you address that problem, we’ll take you seriously as a writer and authority in Idaho politics. Until then, you keep showing you’re nothing more than a shill for one faction and a propagandist worshipping power and access.

Either way, the IFF-led machine will move on and keep doing what it has always done.
