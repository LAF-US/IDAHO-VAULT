| ### Description | Landscape, Pastel on paper.      Raised on a farm near [[Idaho]] Falls, Idaho, Helen Aupperle became a painter of Indian subjects and had begun sketching Western scenes as a child when her family moved her to California. She worked in watercolor, oils, and then pastels which became her medium of choice. She was also a highly respected art teacher, being voted in 1966 one of the nation's top 25 art teachers in ceremonies at the National Gallery in Washington DC.      She graduated from U.C.L.A. and earned an M.A. Degree from Claremont College. Then she was a teacher of art and costume design in the Long Beach schools but did have sabbaticals of six months each at the Royal Academy of Art in Stockholm, Sweden, and the Royal Academy of Art at Copenhagen, Denmark.      In 1940, after she married Don Aupperle, she returned to Idaho Falls and became an art supervisor for the Idaho Falls School System and renowned painter of Northwest subjects including Indians, a subject that first got her attention in 1939 when she was a spectator at the Sun Dance ceremonies of the Bannock and Shoshone at the Fort Hall reservation. Eventually she visited tribes, painting portraits and genre scenes, all over the West including Idaho, Montana, Washington, and Wyoming. She also did landscape painting.
