# Moscow School District explores levy for major building changes

## Possible options include converting the middle school into a high school

[

![Tiegs](https://public-assets-prod.pubgen.ai/brand_4fc3eb37-4c8a-4951-9324-365f6e4b53fd/asset_b2f2f9d1-d70e-5d62-b1b8-16ed6353931a.jpg?w=3840)

Tiegs

The Moscow School Board will decide next month whether to place a bond or levy on the November election ballot to fund significant construction projects, including a relocation of the high school.

The board met Wednesday to hear presentations about these projects. The board did not vote on these items, but will make a decision in August.

Superintendent Shawn Tiegs laid out a number of project ideas that could happen with voter approval. The Moscow School District has long discussed ideas to upgrade its aging facilities and accommodate potential enrollment growth.

For example, the district will consider relocating the high school into the existing Moscow Middle School building on D Street. The district would then find a new location for the middle school.

The existing middle school campus would be renovated and expanded to meet the needs of the Moscow High School. This includes adding a career and technical education space, as well as a new performing arts center, Tiegs said.

Tiegs said this is more cost-effective and realistic than purchasing a property to build a brand-new high school building.

Tiegs said he recommends the district go forward with this plan. He said based on community feedback he’s received, Moscow residents have asked the district to prioritize finding a new location for Moscow High School.

Building a career technical education space is a priority for the district. On Wednesday, Tiegs said he envisions the high school developing strong CTE programs in agriculture, construction, family and consumer sciences, and, particularly, health.

“We can be the leader in Idaho with a career technical center that is focused on health professionals,” he said.

Other options on the table include building a new elementary school on West Palouse River Drive, and renovating the former Russell Elementary building to make it the new home of Paradise Creek Regional High School.

Tiegs said the old Russell Elementary is not suitable for a large elementary school, but can serve a smaller alternative high school that can use the asphalt playground behind the school as a parking lot. The lease for the current Paradise Creek Regional High School building on South Main Street ends in two years.

He said the new elementary school on West Palouse River Drive, which would be built on city-owned land, would replace West Park Elementary if built.

The district is also looking to replace the HVAC systems at Lena Whitmore Elementary and install lighting for the community playfields.

Tiegs emphasized that the district is not trying to complete all these projects at once under the same levy or bond. He said it would be too expensive for residents.

“There’s no single-fix option,” he said. “I’m not going to stand here as a superintendent and say we need to run a $158 million bond and we need to get all this stuff done. Ultimately for me that is just not digestible in this community nor any community. The price tag is too high.”

Tiegs said that while enrollment either declined or remained flat in recent years, he expects that to change as Schweitzer Engineering Laboratories brings more jobs to its Moscow facilities.

Tiegs said he does not recommend trying to pass a bond, which he said would be more expensive and likely less popular among taxpayers. A bond would likely be set at more than $100 million and could run for up to 30 years. It would require two-thirds of the vote to pass.

“A bond has not passed in the state of Idaho for a school district in more than two years,” Tiegs noted.

He is instead recommending a plant facilities levy that could run from seven to ten years. This would require 60% of the vote. A draft of this levy, which can be found on Wednesday’s meeting agenda, shows the amount to be $9,370,000.

The school board has tentatively scheduled a workshop on this issue Aug. 10. They will have a school board meeting Aug. 26, which is the deadline for placing a bond or levy on the November ballot.

A draft of the levy can be viewed at bit.ly/4wf0gOw.
