Forging relationships with content creators and local influencers can help public media producers and social media managers make the most of their local communities. But getting started can feel daunting, especially when budgets are tight. So how can stations of all sizes build their social media presence with limited resources? **Claribel Rivas**, Sr. Social Media Manager of PBS Digital Studios, was recently joined by **Lauren Donia** (PBS SoCal), **Kirby Calberg** (Austin City Limits), and **Alundre Jones** (Austin PBS) on Station Services’ [Summer School](https://hub.pbs.org/summer-school) webinar “ [Make the Most of What’ve You’ve Got: Social Media Success from Scratch](https://hub.pbs.org/posts/make-the-most-of-what-youve-got-social-media-success-from-scratch-2026-summer-school) ” and shared six tips growing your engagement by working with social media influencers.

## 1\. Transform Connection into Collaboration

A simple TikTok comment can go a long way. Claribel Rivas used the [PBS Digital Studios (PBSDS) account](https://www.tiktok.com/@pbsdigitalstudios) to leave one on another creator's post: "we wouldn't be here without viewers like you," and it helped increase viewership on PBSDS's TikTok account by 60%, while also gaining a new creator for the PBS system. That creator, [Sidney Raz](https://www.tiktok.com/@sidneyraz), had posted a video about the trusted and informative nature of PBS News Hour. By interacting with his post, Claribel initiated the connection needed for Sidney to later become a key player in the Protect My Public Media campaign.

## 2\. Treat Creators as Future Collaborators, not Competitors

Lauren Donia’s biggest piece of advice to other stations: consider external creators as “future collaborators,” not competitors. Even though PBS SoCal’s team didn’t have an initial project in mind for Sidney Raz, they still took the initiative to lay the groundwork for a relationship by reaching out to him for a Zoom call. That proved to be crucial; soon after, they called on him in to support projects for the [My Public Media Campaign](https://www.tiktok.com/@sidneyraz/video/7500969350552096043).

## 3\. Look for Brand Alignment

Not every popular creator is the right fit, so finding common themes and content is critical to a successful collaboration. PBS SoCal intentionally built a from-scratch partnership with someone who they believed strongly aligned with their brand: Bennet Rae, host of the popular YouTube show “ [Cooking with Congress](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6pqK1nkvMqk).” In the show, Bennet recreates recipes tied to real politicians and historical figures. Lauren’s team pitched Bennet on a collaboration with Ken Burns to recreate Revolutionary War-era recipes, which Bennet enthusiastically agreed to! The resulting video became one of Ken Burns’s most-viewed interviews. Lauren attributed the partnership’s success to how naturally it aligned with PBS’s ‘edutainment’ brand, which helped the collaboration feel authentic rather than forced.

## 4\. Don’t Let Budget be the Deciding Factor if the Fit is Right

Sometimes all it takes is “a shout out for a shout out” to make a collaboration happen. [Austin PBS](https://www.instagram.com/austinpbs/) partnered with local nonprofits and museums on social media with no official budget, a decision that ultimately helped them reach a larger audience. PBS’s and member station brands carry significant weight, so reaching out to other influencers and organizations who care about the brand rarely hurts. Creators who align with PBS’s mission are often willing to collaborate even without formal compensation.

## 6\. Go Local

To celebrate ACL’s 50th anniversary, Kirby’s team also tapped into working with hyper-local influencers. When choosing who to approach, Kirby searched for an influencer who already spotlighted local niches around Austin, aligned with PBS’s brand, and represented a younger generation.

---

Growing a social media presence doesn't follow one strict formula. Whether it's a TikTok comment, a fan callout, or a from-scratch creator partnership, the common thread is a willingness to think outside the box and find creators who align with PBS's mission and values, no matter the budget.
