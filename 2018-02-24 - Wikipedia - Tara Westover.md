**Tara Westover** [^1] (born September 27, 1986) [^2] is an American [memoirist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memoir "Memoir"), [essayist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essay "Essay") and [historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historian "Historian"). Her memoir *[Educated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educated_\(memoir\) "Educated (memoir)")* (2018) debuted at No. 1 on *The [New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* bestseller list and was a finalist for a number of national awards, including the [*Los Angeles Times* Book Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times_Book_Prize "Los Angeles Times Book Prize"), [PEN America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEN_America "PEN America") 's [Jean Stein Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEN/Jean_Stein_Book_Award "PEN/Jean Stein Book Award"), and two awards from the [National Book Critics Circle Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Critics_Circle_Award "National Book Critics Circle Award"). The *New York Times* ranked *Educated* as one of the 10 Best Books of 2018.[^3] Westover was chosen by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\) "Time (magazine)")* as one of the 100 most influential people of 2019.[^4]

## Early life and education

Westover was the youngest of seven children born in [Clifton, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifton,_Idaho "Clifton, Idaho") (population 259) to Mormon [survivalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivalism "Survivalism") parents, whom she [pseudonymously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudonym "Pseudonym") refers to as Gene and Faye. She has five older brothers and an older sister.[^5] [^6] Her parents were suspicious of doctors, hospitals, [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school#United_States "State school"), and the [federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States"). Westover was born at home, delivered by a [midwife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwife "Midwife"), and was never taken to see a doctor or nurse.[^7] She was not registered for a [birth certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_certificate "Birth certificate") until she was nine years old. Their father resisted getting formal medical treatment for any of the family. Even when seriously injured, the children were treated only by their mother, who had studied [herbalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbal_medicine "Herbal medicine") and other methods of [alternative healing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_medicine "Alternative medicine").

All of the siblings were loosely [homeschooled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling "Homeschooling") by their mother. Westover has said an older brother taught her to read, and she studied the scriptures of the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints "The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints"). But she never attended a lecture, wrote an essay, or took an exam. There were few textbooks in their house. As a teenager, Westover began to want to enter the larger world and attend college. She purchased textbooks and [studied independently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autodidacticism "Autodidacticism") in order to score well on the [ACT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACT_\(test\) "ACT (test)"), following the suggestion of Tyler, one of her older brothers. She gained admission to [Brigham Young University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University "Brigham Young University") in [Provo, Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provo,_Utah "Provo, Utah") and was awarded a scholarship, although she had no high school diploma. After a difficult first year, in which Westover struggled to adjust to [academia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy "Academy") and the wider society at university, she became more successful and graduated with honors in 2008.

Next, she earned a [master's degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master's_degree "Master's degree") from the [University of Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cambridge "University of Cambridge") at [Trinity College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_College,_Cambridge "Trinity College, Cambridge") in [Cambridge, England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge "Cambridge") [^8] as a [Gates Cambridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gates_Cambridge_Scholarship "Gates Cambridge Scholarship") scholar, and was a visiting fellow at [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University "Harvard University") in [Cambridge, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge,_Massachusetts "Cambridge, Massachusetts") in 2010. She returned to Trinity College earning a [doctorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctorate "Doctorate") degree in [intellectual history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_history "Intellectual history") in 2014. Her thesis is entitled "The Family, Morality and Social Science in Anglo-American Cooperative Thought, 1813–1890".[^9] [^10] In 2009, while a [graduate student](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postgraduate_education "Postgraduate education") at Cambridge, Westover told her parents that for many years (since age 15), she had been physically and psychologically abused by an older brother, Shawn. Her parents denied her account and suggested that Westover was under the influence of [Satan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satan "Satan"). The family split over the events. Westover wrote about the estrangement, and her unusual path to and through university education in her acclaimed 2018 memoir, *[Educated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educated_\(memoir\) "Educated (memoir)")*.

## Career

Westover was an [A. M. Rosenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._M._Rosenthal "A. M. Rosenthal") Writer in Residence at the [Shorenstein Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorenstein_Center_on_Media,_Politics_and_Public_Policy "Shorenstein Center on Media, Politics and Public Policy") at [Harvard Kennedy School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_Kennedy_School "Harvard Kennedy School") at Harvard, in the fall of 2019. She was then selected as a Senior Research Fellow at the school for the spring of 2020.[^11] Westover has written for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* [^12] and [BBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_News "BBC News").[^13]

## Works

### Educated: A Memoir

In 2018, [Penguin Random House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penguin_Random_House "Penguin Random House") published Westover's *[Educated: A Memoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educated_\(memoir\) "Educated (memoir)")*, which recounts the story of her struggle to reconcile her desire for education and autonomy with her family's rigid [ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideology "Ideology") and isolated life.[^6] [^14] [^15] [^16] [^17] The coming-of-age story was a No. 1 *New York Times* bestseller, and was positively reviewed by the *New York Times*,[^18] [^19] *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic "The Atlantic")*,[^20] *[USA Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Today "USA Today")*,[^21] *[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_\(magazine\) "Vogue (magazine)")*,[^22] [^23] and *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist "The Economist")* [^24] among others.

As of February 2020, *Educated* in hardcover has spent two years on the *New York Times* bestseller list [^25] and has been translated into 45 languages.[^26] The book was voted the No. 1 Library Reads pick by American librarians and in August 2019, it had been checked out more frequently than any other book through all of [New York Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Public_Library "New York Public Library") 's 88 branches.[^27] As of December 2020, *Educated* has sold more than 8 million copies.[^28] Through their attorney, the family has disputed some elements of Westover's book, including her suggestion that her father may have [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder "Bipolar disorder") and that her mother may have suffered a brain injury which resulted in reduced motor skills.[^29] [^30]
