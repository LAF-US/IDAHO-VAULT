# CORRECTIONS

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This is Vaulted Syntax operational doctrine, recorded at Logan's direction.
> It governs how correction work is classified. It is distinct from the
> review of any specific currently circulating heresy.

## Rule

A correction must be named according to the work it performs. Do not promote
an ordinary typo into an adjudication. Do not reduce a witnessed transmission
repair to mere spelling cleanup. Do not let a codifier's assembly silently
create doctrine.

## Three Classifications

### 1. Typographical Errors / Typos

An ordinary mistake in written form: misspelling, transposition, omitted
character, repeated word, or similar surface error.

**Authority and boundary:** The intended form may be corrected when supplied
by the author or otherwise unambiguous in the record. A typo correction does
not by itself alter meaning, standing, office, or doctrine.

**Example:** Logan's correction of `epistimelogical` to `epistemological` is
a typo correction.

### 2. Scrivener's Corrections

A witnessed repair of a transmission, copying, drafting, or inscription
defect: an error in carrying an intended text or operation into its recorded
form.

**Authority and boundary:** A Scrivener's Correction requires a witness to
the intended text or operation, such as Logan's direction, a controlling
source, or a clear comparison between source and copy. It may restore what
was meant to be carried; it may not select among disputed meanings, invent an
office, or manufacture doctrine.

**Legal analogue:** Legal sources use the related phrase `scrivener's error`
for certain mistakes in drafting or copying. Vaulted Syntax retains
**Scrivener's Corrections** as its operational classification; the analogue
informs the doctrine but does not limit it to a single external procedure.

### 3. Codifier's Corrections

The collected work of maintaining an accumulated body: reconciling,
annotating, indexing, restating, or proposing technical repairs across one or
more existing surfaces.

**Authority and boundary:** A Codifier's Correction must preserve provenance
and state whether the repair is proposed or promoted. It may mark conflict,
quarantine mixed evidence, and propose replacement wording. It may not erase
the evidence that explains drift or silently elevate a proposed repair into
governing doctrine.

**Idaho analogue:** The Idaho Legislature's annual `CODIFIER'S CORRECTIONS`
bill is the direct civic analogue identified by Logan. Legislative Services
Office drafters prepare the annual technical-corrections bill; the Idaho Code
Commission separately maintains and supervises the Idaho Code compilation.

## Classification Test

1. If the defect is merely an ordinary error in written form, classify the
   repair as a **Typographical Error / Typo** correction.
2. If the defect occurred in transmitting, copying, drafting, or inscribing
   intended language or operation, and the intended form is witnessed,
   classify the repair as a **Scrivener's Correction**.
3. If the work assembles, reconciles, annotates, or restates an accumulated
   body of records, classify it as a **Codifier's Correction**.
4. If the proposed change would decide contested meaning, alter authority or
   standing, promote doctrine, or erase material evidence, the correction
   class does not supply authorization. Route the question to Logan or to an
   already lawful governance process.

The classifications describe correction work, not moral severity. One review
may contain more than one classification, but each repair should say which
classification applies and why.

## Annotation Requirements

For a correction with doctrinal or operational significance, record:

- the observed defect
- the correction classification
- the intended or corrected form
- the witness or authority for the correction
- whether the result is proposed, promoted, or only preserved as evidence

Where a contaminated document remains evidentially useful, annotate it rather
than destroying the trace.

## Recorded Example

- `epistimelogical` to `epistemological` in the dated census discussion is a
  **Typographical Error / Typo** correction.

## Civic Witnesses

- Idaho House State Affairs Committee, minutes for February 8, 2024,
  [`H 455` annual codifier's bill discussion](https://lso.legislature.idaho.gov/MediaPub/2024/AgendaMinutes/240208_hsta_0900AM-Minutes.pdf):
  official committee minutes identify LSO drafters as creating the annual
  bill for technical corrections arising as code changes.
- Idaho Attorney General, [*2019 Annual Report*](https://ag.idaho.gov/content/uploads/2021/03/2019-Annual-Report.pdf),
  Certificates of Review at printed pages 110-111: an initiative's accidental
  duplicated statutory language is identified as a `scrivener's error` and a
  mistake in copying the language of the statute.
- Idaho Secretary of State,
  [*Publications & Idaho Code Commission*](https://sos.idaho.gov/publications-idaho-code-commission/):
  the Commission's published mission is maintaining the Idaho Code
  compilation under title 73, chapter 2, Idaho Code.
- Idaho Supreme Court, [*Peterson v. Peterson*, Docket No.
  41017-2013](https://isc.idaho.gov/opinions/41017.pdf):
  Code compilation is evidence of enacted law, not an exercise of legislative
  power by the Code Commission.
- Idaho Supreme Court, [*TracFone Wireless, Inc. v. State of Idaho*, Docket
  No. 41868-2014](https://isc.idaho.gov/opinions/41868.pdf):
  an obvious clerical error may be read correctly where its true meaning is
  obvious; an unambiguous technical term is not rewritten.

### Secretary Of State Caption Trail

The Idaho Code Commission is in the office of the Secretary of State, but a
case caption naming a Secretary does not by itself concern codification or
compilation authority. The caption lineage examined in establishing this
boundary is `Pete T. Cenarrusa`, `Ben Ysursa`, `Lawerence Denney`, and `Phil
McGrane`; `Cenarrusa` is the spelling used in the reported Idaho case caption.

- *Nez Perce Tribe v. Cenarrusa*, 125 Idaho 37, 867 P.2d 911 (1993),
  concerns a constitutional amendment and the Secretary's record or ballot
  function, not the Code Commission's compilation power.
- Idaho Supreme Court, [*Coeur d'Alene Tribe v. Denney*, Docket No.
  43169](https://isc.idaho.gov/opinions/DENNEYFINAL.pdf), concerns the
  Secretary's duty under Idaho Code section 67-505 to certify a bill that
  became law after an untimely veto. Certification of enacted law is distinct
  from compilation.
- Idaho Supreme Court, [*BABE VOTE v. McGrane*, Docket No.
  51227](https://isc.idaho.gov/opinions/51227.pdf), and [*Labrador v.
  Idahoans for Open Primaries*, Docket No.
  52089](https://isc.idaho.gov/opinions/52089A.pdf), concern election or
  initiative administration, not codifier or compilation power.
- The Ysursa-captioned matters located in the doctrinal review arise from
  election or political-process administration, including the federal
  *Ysursa v. Pocatello Education Association* litigation. No Idaho Code
  compilation holding is attributed to that line without further evidence.
