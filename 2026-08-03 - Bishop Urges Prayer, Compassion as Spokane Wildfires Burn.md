*Editor’s note: Bishop Megann Manlove wrote this as a note to those in the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America’s Northwest Intermountain Synod*

**Guest Column By Bishop Megann Manlove | [ELCA Northwest Intermountain Synod](https://nwimsynod.org/)**

*The views expressed in this opinion column are those of the author. They do not necessarily reflect the* [*views*](https://favs.news/about-favs-news/) *of* [*FāVS News.*](https://favs.news/)

My heart breaks for the greater Spokane community in the face of wildfires, which are still burning as I write this. I remember wildfires growing up in the Black Hills of South Dakota. My half-brother, a Forest Service ranger in Northern Minnesota, spent most of his summers fighting fires out west. All of that makes some of this familiar, but no less hard to take in.

I know you are already praying for community members in Spokane, and all across our region, who have lost their homes, and for those who have evacuated and are still waiting for word. We give thanks for all who are providing shelter, including some of you reading this, and we pray for continued welcome and hospitality. Finally, we pray for firefighters and those coordinating the response and planning for recovery.

We pray all of this recognizing it is only Aug. 3, and wildfire season is not over. I recently read that the Holden Village Bed and Breakfast was evacuated because of a wildfire. I’m watching the fire on the edge of Yakima and the wildfire north of Davenport. And there are large fires in North Central Washington and along the Oregon-Idaho border that are very concerning.

This afternoon I spoke with Ray Sherjven, our Washington Voluntary Organizations Active in Disaster representative and Lutheran Disaster Response liaison. He’s hoping to hold a video meeting with some of our Spokane pastors later this week.

In consultation with Sherjven earlier this summer, synod staff identified the following organizations that can receive financial donations:

- ELCA Disaster Response — there for the long haul, after the dust, or ash, settles
- United Way — often on the ground early
- American Red Cross — on the ground during emergencies

Feel free to give to the United Way or American Red Cross chapter in your state. You may also find a local agency doing excellent work. As a synod, we don’t have the resources to handle disaster relief donations or their distribution ourselves.

We will, as we always do, partner with local organizations. We will care for our individual neighbors. We will step into the disaster response and recovery as each of us is able. We will pray — not as an empty gesture, but because prayer allows us to take a breath, and because prayer, especially prayer together, can help us discover the next faithful step we’re being called to.

Let us pray: Holy God, in you alone we find safety and shelter. Be a sure refuge to all suffering from wildfires. Grant courage, protection and favorable weather to firefighters and emergency responders; embrace with supportive communities all who are newly displaced or homeless; comfort those grieving all that has been destroyed and give patience to those who are anxious, not knowing when relief will come. Heal your whole creation, that out of flame and ashes new growth will spring forth. We pray through Jesus Christ, our life and our strength. Amen.

*(From “All Creation Sings,” page 50, a worship resource used in ELCA congregations)*

## What else is the church called to right now?

I want to remind you that showing up for those who have experienced fear and loss is valuable, and it’s something you can do. You can see Jesus in the face of every neighbor you encounter. You do not have to have answers. Simply remind them that you love them and, if appropriate, that the God we worship loves them too and is big enough to hold all of their sorrow and anger. Sitting with someone in silence, listening deeply and just being present is important work, and it’s not something everyone is comfortable doing. God equipped you for this holy work through baptism.

Let me close by leading you in this litany for times of wildfire, from ELCA Worship Resources:

Holy God, in the wake of fires that devour and consume,  
Refresh us with the water of life.  
In the barren places of ash and dust,  
Restore us with the water of life.  
When all we can see is devastation and despair,  
Renew us with the water of life.  
In Christ, we are washed clean.  
We are refreshed.  
In Christ, we drink from the wellspring of hope.  
We are restored.  
In Christ, we dance in the fountain of life.  
We are renewed.  
Thanks be to God. Amen.

Thank you for continuing to be wellsprings of God’s love. God be with each of you and your neighbors, near and far.
