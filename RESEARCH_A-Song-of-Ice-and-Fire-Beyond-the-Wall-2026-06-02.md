# Beyond the Wall

> The unmapped country north of the Wall, where the map becomes rumor: a free, lordless people; giants and the children of the forest; and refugees fleeing south from something worse. One of a trilogy with `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Wall-and-the-Watch-2026-06-02]]` and `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Others-and-the-Long-Night-2026-06-02]]`.
>
> **Provenance.** Canon from this session's geography research + established lore. Tiers: **[canon]**, **[legend]**, **[theory]**, **[show]**, **[reading]**. ⚠️ Verify before publication.

## The land

- **[canon]** Past the Wall lies the **Haunted Forest** — a vast cold taiga running from the eastern coast inland to the mountains. Landmarks: the **Fist of the First Men** (an ancient ring-fort on a bald hill, a relic of the last preparation against the dark); **Craster's Keep**; the rivers **Milkwater** and **Antler**; the ruined coastal town of **Hardhome** (destroyed centuries ago in an unexplained cataclysm; its bay still shunned).
- **[canon]** Westward rise the **Frostfangs**, brutal mountains hiding secret valleys such as **Thenn**; beyond them, at the roof of the world, the **Lands of Always Winter** — the cold, unmapped country from which the Others come (→ the Others node).
- **[canon]** A great **weirwood cave** beyond the Wall holds the **three-eyed crow** (below). The **Frozen Shore** lies on the northwest coast.

## The free folk

- **[canon]** The people north of the Wall call themselves the **free folk**; the south calls them **wildlings**. They descend from the same **First Men** as the North — left on the far side when the Wall went up. (→ the North node.)
- **[canon/reading]** Their creed is **no kneeling**: no lords, no inherited kings, no law but custom and strength. (They call southerners "kneelers.") They take wives by **"stealing,"** their women fight as **spearwives**, and they are a patchwork of many peoples — the disciplined **Thenns** (ruled by a *Magnar*, "lord"), the **Hornfoots**, the cannibal **ice-river clans**, cave-dwellers, the dog-sledding folk of the Frozen Shore, the Nightrunners.
- **[reading]** The south imagines them as raiders, and they do raid — but the deeper truth is that **they are refugees.** They mass at the Wall not to pillage but to get *south of it*, because the Others are coming behind them. The realm meets its fleeing neighbors with swords.

## The old powers of the north

- **[canon]** **Giants** — real: twelve feet and taller, riding **mammoths**, speaking the **Old Tongue**, and dwindling toward extinction (e.g., Wun Wun).
- **[canon]** **The children of the forest** — the non-human First inhabitants of Westeros, believed extinct for millennia, who in fact **survive in caves beyond the Wall**. They carved the weirwoods and **knapped dragonglass into blades** (giving obsidian weapons to the first Watchmen) — the volcanic glass itself is not their creation. ⚠️ **[show]** The HBO series has the children *create* the Others; this is **not confirmed in the books.**
- **[canon]** **The three-eyed crow** — the last **greenseer** (Brynden Rivers, "Bloodraven"), fused into the roots of a great weirwood in a hidden cave, who reaches south through dreams to wake Bran Stark (→ the old gods, religions node).

## Mance Rayder & the Horn

- **[canon]** **Mance Rayder** — a former Night's Watch ranger who deserted, went north, and did the impossible: **united the free folk under one King-Beyond-the-Wall**, for the single purpose of bringing them through the Wall before winter falls.
- **[canon/legend]** He searches for the **Horn of Joramun** (the **"Horn of Winter"**), said to **wake giants from the earth and bring the Wall crashing down** — the one thing that could undo the realm's ancient ward.

## Craster — a hint of the worst

- **[canon]** **Craster**, a wildling living north of the Wall, **weds his own daughters** and **leaves his infant sons in the woods** as offerings to **"the cold gods."** ⚠️ The strong implication is that the **Others take and transform the boys** — one of the books' rare, oblique glimpses of what the Others *want*.

## Theme — the lordless mirror

**[reading]** Beyond the Wall is the saga's **anti-feudal mirror**: a society with **power but no thrones**, freedom bought at the price of safety and order. It reframes the whole Game of Thrones — here are people who *chose* to have no king, now begging to pass a wall built to keep them out, fleeing the one enemy that makes the southern squabbles look like children at play. They share the North's **First Men blood and old gods** and its long memory of the dark — which is why the free folk, like the North, *remember* what the curated southern record forgot (→ the North node, the Others node).
