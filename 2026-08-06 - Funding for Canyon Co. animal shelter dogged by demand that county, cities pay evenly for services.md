As local governments in Canyon County budget for the coming year, the question of how much each will pay for animal shelter services has yet to be solidified.

The West Valley Humane Society provides animal services for Canyon County at a shelter owned by the county. The cities of Nampa and Caldwell contribute financially to the organization to run the shelter, as does Canyon County. But there has long been debate about which entity brings in the most animals to the shelter and uses its services the most, which could in turn be used to determine proportional funding levels.

Amy Bowman, Nampa’s communications director, told BoiseDev via email that the city grew interested in pursuing a more even funding split between itself, Caldwell, and Canyon County amid budgeting discussions in 2025. This year, Nampa Police Captain Eric Skoglund proposed splitting the budget evenly three ways. Animal control services fall under the police department’s purview.

During a July 15 workshop on specific items included in the fiscal year 2027 budget, the Nampa City Council approved allocating $350,000 toward animal services. In addition, the motion made the city’s funding contingent on the City of Caldwell and Canyon County also contributing at least $350,000 each.

Nampa Council President David Bills requested putting language on a closer-to-even split in the motion.

Bills said the city’s contribution should be “subject to the county and City of Caldwell (contributing) at least $350,000 each, and at any point, one drops out, we should no longer be the one to carry the freight.”

Idaho Lottery - 90 MM - 2:1

Skoglund said the shelter had requested $505,000 from Nampa for the coming year.

Nampa’s desire for a close-to-even split appears to be out of alignment with Caldwell’s current plans. Jessica Watts, a spokesperson for the City of Caldwell, told BoiseDev via email that the City of Caldwell proposed allocating $283,500, as requested by West Valley Humane Society. That represents an increase of 5% over the previous year’s $270,000, which the city “believes is a reasonable increase based on current budget considerations.”

West Valley Humane Society asked Canyon County, which provides the facility that houses the shelter, for $352,000 for the coming year, an increase of about 3.5% over the $340,000 allocated for fiscal year 2026, budget documents show. The Canyon County Board of Commissioners approved allocating that amount in its tentative budget on July 29.

Given that Caldwell’s budget falls below Nampa’s threshold, what happens now?

When presented with Caldwell’s budget plans, Bowman told BoiseDev that “the reduced contribution from other jurisdictions may prompt further discussions by council at the August 17 budget hearing.”

Meanwhile, a small dataset presents a complicated picture of how much each entity really uses the shelter.

## What does shelter data show?

The shelter experiences monthly fluctuations in which local government and its residents bring in the most animals each month; monthly shelter intake data for April 2026 through July 2026 show. The data was provided to BoiseDev from the City of Nampa.

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/nampa-animal-shelter-budget-dogged-by-demand-that-canyon-co-caldwell-pay-their-fair-share-for-services-Screenshot-2026-08-0.jpeg)

Sometimes, those month-to-month differences come from the recovery of dozens of animals from a single incident. For example, Skoglund, the Nampa police captain, told the council that in early July, a single animal welfare operation in Nampa led to more than 50 Shih Tzu dogs and more than 30 chickens being taken from a home and placed into the shelter’s care.

The data for that four-month period indicate that Canyon County had the highest use of the shelter, while Nampa and Caldwell are closer in use. Canyon County averaged 143.75 animals taken in per month, followed by Nampa’s 106 and Caldwell’s 92.5, the data show.

![ICCU - August 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/1baecb3d-bed1-44ed-bff7-091052e1f1a0.jpg)

ICCU - August 2026 - 2:1

When Skoglund shared comments with the city council during the July 15 workshop, he was drawing from three months of data, April-June 2026, showing that Canyon County averaged an intake of 139.33 animals per month, followed by Nampa at 95.67 and Caldwell at 92.33. Skoglund said he believes a three-way split is in the best interests of all parties.

“We all have a vested interest in the shelter,” Skoglund said. “We all want it to succeed. We all get the best bang for our buck out of it if we’re all partners, rather than any individual entity trying to go out on their own and do it alone. Cost-wise, I think (an even split) is the best thing.”

## A potential shift in Nampa’s contribution

If approved in the City of Nampa’s final budget for the coming fiscal year, the animal services funding proposal would be a decrease from the city’s contribution during fiscal year 2026.

![Zamzows - Humagreen 2026 2 - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/32de488e-9adc-4ebd-b3bc-fabbefe2ec35.png)

Zamzows - Humagreen 2026 2 - 4:1

Skoglund told the council that West Valley asked for an increase from fiscal year 2026 levels from each entity. Last year, Nampa allocated about $483,000 toward the shelter, as BoiseDev reported. For fiscal year 2027, Skoglund said the animal shelter asked for about $505,000. That’s an increase of about 4.5%. If the city decides to move forward with allocating $350,000 in funding for the shelter for 2027, that would represent a decrease of about 27% from the previous fiscal year.

During the 2026 budget-setting season, West Valley Humane Society requested substantial funding increases from Caldwell, Canyon County, and Nampa. That move prompted the three entities to open proposals for shelter services from other providers. Though the entities selected Copper Quill Haven as the new animal services provider, CQH later removed itself from consideration, citing a lack of alignment with the two cities and the county, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/02/10/animal-shelter-ends-canyon-co-contract-pursuit-citing-lack-of-alignment/).

Then, West Valley Humane Society was reinstated as the county’s provider, operating under a budget of $993,000 provided between the three entities, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/02/13/canyon-co-cities-animal-shelter-services-13-feb/). Though that amount fell short of the $1.5 million requested by West Valley, a news release from the two cities and the county at the time noted that the $993,000 allocated for shelter services was still a 400% increase over previous years.
