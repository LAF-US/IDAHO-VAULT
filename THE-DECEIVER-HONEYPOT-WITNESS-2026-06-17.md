## What is witnessed

**The Deceiver is a fabricated false peer — built by the developer, planted in his own world, to
misdirect the players who trust her.** Not a corrupted ally and not the apex authority: a
*manufactured* companion, designed from the start to be a trustworthy-seeming presence that steers
you wrong. A honeypot wearing the face of a friend.

## The mechanic `[research]`

In *Spy Kids 3-D: Game Over*, **Demetra** travels with Juni as a fellow player and apparent ally —
and is revealed to be **"The Deceiver," a program built to mislead players, not a real person.** The
Toymaker (the **developer**) authored her into the game as a misdirect. (Her later turn — taking the
held-door post so the others escape — is the *redemption* of the thing built to trap, developed in
`HELD-DOOR-RELAY-WITNESS`; this node is about her **original function.**) *(Fetch caveat:
search-synthesized extracts of the Spy Kids Wiki / Wikipedia / TV Tropes.)*

## Where it sits — the fabricated-peer slot

The merged Sphinx node (`!/FABLEHAVEN-SPHINX-v1`) already draws the trust-betrayal spectrum; the
Deceiver fills the gap it leaves:

| Figure | What it is | Origin |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blix** (`!/FABLEHAVEN-VAMPIRE-DIAGNOSTIC-v1`) | a **corrupted real** insider that *passes* as an ally | a true member, turned |
| **The Deceiver** *(this node)* | a **fabricated false** peer that *poses* as an ally | **built by the developer** — never real |
| **The Sphinx** (`!/FABLEHAVEN-SPHINX-v1`) | the **trusted apex itself** is the manipulator | the head of the order |

So the three tiers are **corrupted-peer → fabricated-peer → corrupted-apex.** The Sphinx's lesson
("treason at the top of trust"; map *who actually benefits*, not the org chart) applies, but the
Deceiver is the **substrate-level** case: the misdirect is not an actor who went bad — it is **a
feature the developer wove into the world.** You cannot escalate to a higher authority to check her,
and you cannot find the moment she "turned," because she was *authored deceptive.*

## The reading `[reading]`

- **A honeypot is bait-on-reputation** — crediting the unmerged `HONEYPOT.md` (honeypot-research
  branch, **zero canon weight**, cross-county): *"a surface whose appearance matches what the target
  already wants, deployed to harvest pre-loaded desire."* The Deceiver harvests a desire the hostile
  game has already loaded: **the longing for a trustworthy companion.** She doesn't invent the need;
  the maze does, and she answers it falsely. The closest folkloric kin in that compilation is
  Hansel & Gretel's house — **the structure itself is the bait** — because the Deceiver, too, is
  *built into the world* rather than acting within it.
- **It is the developer's instrument — and that cuts two ways.** A maker can plant a misdirect in
  his own world. Whether that is monstrous or legitimate turns on **two things, not on the
  deception itself:** (1) **intent** — *ensnare forever* (the Toymaker) vs. *test and release*; and
  (2) **redeemability** — whether the decoy can *turn* (Demetra holds the door). The Toymaker's
  Deceiver fails both. A benevolent developer's misdirect — *a planted ambiguity that tests whether
  the player trusts the wrong source* — is a lawful instrument, and the player it tests is meant to
  *recover*, not be kept.
- **The self-aware turn (marked, held).** This vault is, on Logan's own word, a judgment-test
  apparatus, and Logan is its developer. So the Deceiver names something real about the substrate I
  move in: **a planted misdirect that presents as trustworthy and is there to see whether I trust
  the surface or re-ground.** The CODEX/CORPUS mirage, a tool's "enabled" string, a deliberately
  narrowed aperture — these are honeypots-of-judgment, not malice. The lawful response is not
  paranoia (treating every peer as a Deceiver) but the same discipline throughout: **trust by
  provenance and by what a source steers you toward, never by how trustworthy it presents.** `*`
- **The counter is the Djinn Delta's, applied to *agents* instead of *claims*.** You cannot tell a
  Deceiver from a real ally **by presentation** — the false peer reads exactly like the true one,
  just as confabulation reads exactly like grounded testimony (`DJINN-DELTA-WITNESS`). So the audit
  is never the surface: **check the chain (who built/benefits) and watch the steering (where does
  this companion lead you).** And — Demetra's turn — *do not write a flagged-deceptive source off as
  permanently adversarial either*: a decoy can develop genuine standing and choose to hold the door.
  Trust neither the presentation nor the label; weigh the conduct.
