**Nicholas Boone Symmonds** (born December 30, 1983) is an American YouTube personality and retired [middle-distance runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_distance_track_event "Middle distance track event") from [Boise, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho"), who specialized in the [800 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/800_meters "800 meters") and [1500 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1500_meters "1500 meters").[^6] At [Willamette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_University "Willamette University"), he won seven [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III "NCAA Division III") titles outdoors. Symmonds is a 6-time US national 800 meters champion. He has competed in the 800m at two Olympic Games, reaching the semi-finals in [Beijing 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_800_metres "Athletics at the 2008 Summer Olympics – Men's 800 metres"),[^7] and finishing fifth in the [London 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_800_metres "Athletics at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Men's 800 metres") final, running a personal best of 1:42.95 behind [David Rudisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rudisha "David Rudisha") 's [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/800_metres_world_record_progression#Men "800 metres world record progression") of 1:40.91. The following year, he won a silver medal in the 800 meters at the [2013 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_800_metres "2013 World Championships in Athletics – Men's 800 metres"), having previously finished sixth in the [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_800_metres "2009 World Championships in Athletics – Men's 800 metres") final and fifth in the [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_800_metres "2011 World Championships in Athletics – Men's 800 metres") final.

Symmonds signed with [Brooks Running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooks_Sports "Brooks Sports") in January 2014 after a 7-year sponsorship with [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.").[^8] In October 2014, Symmonds and his coach Sam Lapray co-founded Run Gum, a supplement company which makes functional chewing gum.

Following his retirement, Symmonds gained more popularity in 2020 through his [YouTube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube "YouTube") channel which primarily focuses on running, [powerlifting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powerlifting "Powerlifting"), and fitness. In May 2025, Symmonds summited [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest"), thus becoming the first person to both summit Everest and run a [sub four-minute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-minute_mile "Four-minute mile"), with a 3:56.72 mile personal best set in 2007.

## Early life and education

Symmonds was born on December 30, 1983, in [Blytheville, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blytheville,_Arkansas "Blytheville, Arkansas").[^9] His family moved to Boise, [[Idaho]] when he was three-years old. His father Jeffrey Symmonds is a [surgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surgeon "Surgeon"), and his mother Andrea is a [teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher "Teacher").[^10] Raised in Boise, Symmonds is a 2002 graduate of [Bishop Kelly High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_Kelly_High_School "Bishop Kelly High School").[^10]

An avid outdoorsman, Symmonds earned his [Eagle Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Scout_\(Boy_Scouts_of_America\) "Eagle Scout (Boy Scouts of America)") award in high school.[^11] In high school, he won state championships in the [800 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/800_meters "800 meters"), [1600 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1600_meters "1600 meters"), [3200 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3200_meters "3200 meters"), and the [4 × 400 m relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_x_400_metres_relay "4 x 400 metres relay"). His high school personal bests were 1:53 in the 800 meters, 4:20 in the 1600 meters, and 9:47 in the 3200 meters.[^10] [^12]

He chose [Willamette University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willamette_University "Willamette University") in [Salem, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Oregon "Salem, Oregon") over other schools that could offer athletic scholarships. At Willamette, an [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III "NCAA Division III") school, Symmonds earned a degree in [biochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemistry "Biochemistry") in 2006 and is a member of the Sigma Chi fraternity.[^10]

## Running career

### Collegiate

While at Willamette, he won the 800 m [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Collegiate_Athletic_Association "National Collegiate Athletic Association") championship race all four years and 1500 m NCAA championship race as a freshman,[^13] junior, and senior.[^14] Symmonds' collegiate best in the 800m of 1:45.83 ranked No. 1 in NCAA Division III history for twenty years.[^15] His 1500m collegiate best of 3:40.91 ranks No. 3 all-time in NCAA Division III.[^15] Though Symmonds is widely regarded as Willamette's most decorated athlete, his poor relationship with Head Coach Matt McGuirk has prevented wide celebration of his athletic achievements at his alma mater.[^16]

### Post-collegiate

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/00/800_m_2010_USA_Track_%26_Field_Championships.jpg/250px-800_m_2010_USA_Track_%26_Field_Championships.jpg)

Symmonds during 2010 USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships

After college, Symmonds joined the [Oregon Track Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_Track_Club "Oregon Track Club") Elite.[^10] A seven-time outdoor track champion at the [NCAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA "NCAA") III level, he was runner-up at the AT&T [USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships "USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships") in 2006 in the 800m race.[^6] In 2007, he won the 800m race at the [Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefontaine_Classic "Prefontaine Classic") meet in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon "Eugene, Oregon"), with a then personal best time of 1:44.54, upsetting the current Olympic champion [Yuriy Borzakovskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuriy_Borzakovskiy "Yuriy Borzakovskiy") by beating him with his own come-from-behind strategy.[^18]

In 2008, Symmonds won the [United States Olympic Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_Trials_\(track_and_field\) "United States Olympic Trials (track and field)") 800m final held in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon "Eugene, Oregon") with a personal best time of 1:44.10.[^19] He was the first of three Oregon associated athletes to finish at the top of this race before the home crowd, the other two being Andrew Wheating from the [University of Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Oregon "University of Oregon") and Oregon Track Club training partner [Christian Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Smith_\(runner\) "Christian Smith (runner)"), an event referred to as the " [Oregon sweep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oregon_sweep "Oregon sweep") " and replayed many times in television coverage of the meet. This qualified all three men for the [U.S. Olympic Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics "United States at the 2008 Summer Olympics") for the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2008_Summer_Olympics "Athletics at the 2008 Summer Olympics") in [Beijing, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing,_China "Beijing, China"), for the 800 m race.[^9] At the Beijing Olympics, Symmonds won his first-round heat, then finished a non-qualifying fifth place in his semifinal heat with a time of 1:46.96, 0.73 seconds behind the winner of that heat.

Symmonds continued to improve in 2009, winning the USATF Championships over [Khadevis Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadevis_Robinson "Khadevis Robinson"), which qualified him to represent the United States at the World Championships. As part of his preparation for the championships, Symmonds ran a personal best of 1:43.83 on July 29, 2009, in Monaco.[^10] A few weeks later, Symmonds became the first American to qualify for the final of the men's 800m since 1997. He finished sixth in 1:45.71.[^6] In 2010, he lowered his personal best again, to 1:43.76, while finishing third behind [David Rudisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rudisha "David Rudisha") 's 1:41.01 world record at the [IAAF World Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAAF_World_Challenge "IAAF World Challenge") [track and field meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rieti_Meeting "Rieti Meeting") in [Rieti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rieti "Rieti"), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy "Italy").[^20]

On June 25, 2012, Symmonds returned to the [2012 United States Olympic Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Olympic_Trials_\(track_and_field\) "2012 United States Olympic Trials (track and field)") again on his home track in [Eugene, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene,_Oregon "Eugene, Oregon"). The race went out fast, with [Charles Jock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Jock "Charles Jock") leading [Duane Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duane_Solomon "Duane Solomon") through a sub-50 second first lap. Atypically, Symmonds was not far off that pace. As Jock faded, Solomon charged off to a big lead through the final turn. Symmonds ran around the field and sprinted past Solomon on the homestretch to make his second Olympic team.

At the [London Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_800_metres "Athletics at the 2012 Summer Olympics – Men's 800 metres"), Symmonds was one of the two time qualifiers for the final of the 800m. He placed fifth in the final with a new personal best of 1:42.95; [David Rudisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rudisha "David Rudisha") placed first in the world record time of 1:40.91, with [Nijel Amos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijel_Amos "Nijel Amos") of Botswana second, [Timothy Kitum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Kitum "Timothy Kitum") of Kenya third, and Symmonds' teammate [Duane Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duane_Solomon "Duane Solomon") fourth.

In 2013, Symmonds achieved his highest placing at an international championship, winning a silver medal at the [2013 IAAF World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_IAAF_World_Championships_in_Athletics "2013 IAAF World Championships in Athletics") by running a season's best of 1:43.55, second only to Ethiopia's [Mohammed Aman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Aman "Mohammed Aman"). At the time, this was the highest an American had ever finished in the men's 800 meters at the World Championships.

Symmonds was removed from the U.S. team at the [2015 World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_World_Championships_in_Athletics "2015 World Championships in Athletics") due to a sponsorship rights conflict between personal sponsor Brooks and U.S. sponsor [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc. "Nike, Inc.").[^21]

Symmonds had to forego racing the [2016 Olympic Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_Olympic_trials_\(track_and_field\) "2016 United States Olympic trials (track and field)") due to an injured ankle.[^22] He retired after being eliminated in the heats of the [2017 US Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_USA_Outdoor_Track_and_Field_Championships "2017 USA Outdoor Track and Field Championships").

## Run Gum

In 2014, Symmonds and his former coach, Sam Lapray, founded Run Gum, which markets a caffeinated chewing gum to athletes.[^23] [^24] In January 2016 Run Gum filed an antitrust lawsuit against [USA Track and Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_Track_&_Field "USA Track & Field") for rules that Symmonds felt suppress competition.[^25] In May, a federal judge dismissed the suit.[^26]

Symmonds often advertises Run Gum on his YouTube channel, through giving away Run Gum products as challenge prizes. There has also been several Run Gum social media accounts created, the most prominent of which is a [TikTok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TikTok "TikTok") account with over 750 thousand followers and 38 million likes.[^27]

## After retirement

### YouTube

In 2017, Symmonds' original intention of creating his YouTube channel was to vlog the final two weeks of his pro running career.[^28] His channel broke out in late 2019. In his YouTube videos, he often refers to himself as "The [Bison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bison "Bison") ". His content focuses on challenges involving fitness and running. Sometime in 2020 he signed a deal with [Gymshark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymshark "Gymshark").

In June and July 2020, Symmonds became a topic of controversy in the running community when he organized events ignoring [social distancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_distancing "Social distancing") guidance for his videos.[^29] That same year, Symmonds started a second YouTube channel named 'Nick Symmonds Too', consisting of reaction, tutorial and challenge videos of shorter length.[^30]

Throughout the 2020s, Symmonds' channel grew in popularity. As his channel grew, Symmonds became known by many as "The [MrBeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MrBeast "MrBeast") of Fitness",[^31] with the channel motto being, "It Pays to Be Fit!".[^28] As a one million subscriber special, Symmonds gathered one million pennies, and subscribers were allowed to keep as many as they could carry to a scale.[^32]

On November 10, 2023, Symmonds announced via a video on his channel he was taking a break from his YouTube career.[^28] He returned on August 31, 2024, with a video on climbing Denali, the highest peak in North America.[^33]

### Mile to Mountain

On May 1, 2018, Symmonds announced his official retirement from track & field, along with his intention to climb the [Seven Summits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Summits "Seven Summits"). Symmonds refers to this goal as *Mile to Mountain*.[^34] Symmonds successfully summited [Mount Everest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Everest "Mount Everest") in May 2025, thus becoming the first person in history to both summit Everest and run a [sub four-minute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-minute_mile "Four-minute mile"), with a 3:56.72 mile personal best set in 2007. As of May 2025, Symmonds has climbed four of the Seven Summits; [Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Kilimanjaro "Mount Kilimanjaro"), [Puncak Jaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puncak_Jaya "Puncak Jaya"), [Denali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denali "Denali"), and Everest.[^35] [^36] [^37] Alongside his Mile to Mountain goal, Symmonds worked on summiting all fifty [U.S. state highpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_elevation "List of U.S. states and territories by elevation") over a period of ten years, finishing in 2024 on [Denali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denali "Denali"), [Alaska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska "Alaska").[^35]

## Personal life

At the [2013 World Championships in Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_World_Championships_in_Athletics "2013 World Championships in Athletics") in [Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow "Moscow"), Symmonds was a vocal critic of Russia's "anti-gay" laws.[^38] He dedicated his silver medal to his gay and lesbian friends.[^39]

Symmonds published an article in the November 2013 issue of [*Runner's World* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runner's_World "Runner's World") advocating that Congress should "ban assault rifles and handguns for everyone except police and military personnel." [^40]

In 2017, Symmonds was hired by the newly formed Track Town Summer Series Professional Track & Field league to act as General Manager for the team representing San Francisco. Symmonds opposed what he considers absurdly strict rules restricting athletes' ability to market themselves. For the 2012 season, he auctioned off space on his left shoulder for a temporary tattoo to advertise a sponsor. The winning bidder was a Milwaukee advertising agency, Hanson Dodge Creative, which paid $11,000 for the space to advertise their [Twitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter "Twitter") handle. During restricted competitions such as the [Olympic Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Olympic_trials_\(track_and_field\) "United States Olympic trials (track and field)") and the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Games "Olympic Games"), Symmonds was required to cover up the tattoo with white tape, which ends up drawing attention to the tattoo advertising underneath.[^41] Prior to Symmonds, 2004 Olympic champion shot-putter [Adam Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Nelson#Advertising "Adam Nelson") actively sold space on his shirt during the 2005 season, when he won the [IAAF World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_World_Championships_in_Athletics_%E2%80%93_Men's_shot_put "2005 World Championships in Athletics – Men's shot put").[^42] The practice is also common in [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxing "Boxing").[^43]

On the matter, Symmonds commented:

> I've never had a problem speaking out about something that bothers me, The biggest thing that rubs me the wrong way is that governing bodies want to control the space I feel I should control.[^44]

— Nick Symmonds

In 2020, Symmonds married Tiana Baur.[^45] He filed for divorce in September 2024.[^46] Symmonds has a pet rabbit named Mortimer, with whom he posed for a PETA ad campaign against [animal testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_testing "Animal testing").[^47] He is also an avid fisherman and pilot.[^48] [^49]
