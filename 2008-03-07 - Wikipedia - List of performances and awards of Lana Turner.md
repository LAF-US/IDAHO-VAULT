[Lana Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Turner "Lana Turner") (1921–1995) was an American actress who appeared in over fifty films during her career, which spanned four decades. Discovered in 1937 at age 16, she signed a contract with [Warner Bros.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros. "Warner Bros.") but soon transferred to [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer "Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer").[^7] The studio's co-founder, [Louis B. Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_B._Mayer "Louis B. Mayer"), helped further her career by casting her in several youth-oriented comedies and musicals, including *[Dancing Co-Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dancing_Co-Ed "Dancing Co-Ed")* (1939) [^8] and *[Ziegfeld Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziegfeld_Girl_\(film\) "Ziegfeld Girl (film)")* (1941), the latter of which was a commercial success and helped establish her as one of the studio's leading performers.[^9] Turner subsequently co-starred with [Clark Gable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Gable "Clark Gable") in the drama *[Somewhere I'll Find You](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somewhere_I'll_Find_You "Somewhere I'll Find You")* (1943), the first of four films she would appear in with him.[^10]

Turner's role as a [femme fatale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Femme_fatale "Femme fatale") in the film noir *[The Postman Always Rings Twice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Postman_Always_Rings_Twice_\(1946_film\) "The Postman Always Rings Twice (1946 film)")* (1946) advanced her career significantly and established her as a dramatic actress.[^11] It earned her acclaim with [Bosley Crowther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosley_Crowther "Bosley Crowther") of *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")* deeming it "the role of her career." [^12] In addition to her film roles, Turner frequently appeared on radio programs throughout the 1940s, including *[Suspense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspense_\(radio_drama\) "Suspense (radio drama)")* and *[The Orson Welles Almanac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Orson_Welles_Almanac "The Orson Welles Almanac")*.[^13] [^14] In 1952, she co-starred in the drama *[The Bad and the Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bad_and_the_Beautiful "The Bad and the Beautiful")* (1952) opposite [Kirk Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirk_Douglas "Kirk Douglas"), portraying an alcoholic actress.[^15] Turner made her final film appearance with Gable [^16] in the drama *[Betrayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betrayed_\(1954_film\) "Betrayed (1954 film)")* (1954).[^17] After the critical and commercial failure of *[Diane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_\(1956_film\) "Diane (1956 film)")* (1956), MGM opted not to renew Turner's contract.[^18] At the time, her films with the studio had collectively earned over $50 million [^19]

In 1957, she took a leading role portraying [Constance MacKenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constance_MacKenzie "Constance MacKenzie") in [20th Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Century_Fox "20th Century Fox") 's *[Peyton Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Place_\(film\) "Peyton Place (film)")*, a film adaptation of the [Grace Metalious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Metalious "Grace Metalious") [novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peyton_Place_\(novel\) "Peyton Place (novel)").[^20] The film was a major box office success,[^21] and Turner earned her first and only [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award "Academy Award") nomination for [Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress "Academy Award for Best Actress") for her performance.[^20] In 1959, she accepted the lead role in [Douglas Sirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Sirk "Douglas Sirk") 's remake of *[Imitation of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imitation_of_Life_\(1959_film\) "Imitation of Life (1959 film)")*, a drama for [Universal Pictures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Pictures "Universal Pictures") in which she portrayed a struggling stage actress, which was another commercial success at the box office.[^22] Turner's final leading role was in 1966's *[Madame X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madame_X_\(1966_film\) "Madame X (1966 film)")*, for which she earned a [David di Donatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_di_Donatello "David di Donatello") award for Best Actress.[^23] She spent the majority of the 1970s in semiretirement, appearing in touring stage plays, such as *[Forty Carats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forty_Carats "Forty Carats")* [^24] and *[Bell, Book and Candle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell,_Book_and_Candle "Bell, Book and Candle")*.[^25] In 1982, she was cast in a recurring guest role on the television [soap opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_opera "Soap opera") *[Falcon Crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon_Crest "Falcon Crest")*.[^26] She made her final film appearance in the [comedy horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_horror "Comedy horror") film *[Witches' Brew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witches'_Brew_\(film\) "Witches' Brew (film)")* in 1980.[^27]
