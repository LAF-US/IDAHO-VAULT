Idaho Power’s PR team has been working hard lately to put a positive spin on the new gas plants it’s trying to build in southern Idaho — [one near Mountain Home, one near Twin Falls](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/business/article315647760.html) — but Idahoans won’t be fooled. There’s nothing positive about these gas plants. They’re expensive, risky, dirty, and unnecessary. Idaho Power should invest in real energy solutions that actually benefit our state. And the [Idaho Public Utilities Commission](https://puc.idaho.gov/Case?util=1&closed=0&pn=1&ps=100&sc=1&sd=1&fc=2&fv=idaho+power) needs to do its job by holding Idaho Power accountable and saying no to these gas plants.

Let’s start by looking at why Idaho Power says it needs these new power plants, anyway.

Idaho Power predicts that energy demand will skyrocket in the next few years due to growth in southern Idaho. This includes some growth from homes and businesses, but, according to [Idaho Power’s own analysis](https://docs.idahopower.com/pdfs/AboutUs/PlanningForFuture/2025IRP/2025%20IRP%20Appendix%20A.pdf), it’s primarily from Big Tech, like new data centers and Micron. Some of this new energy demand will come to fruition, but some won’t. The city of Pocatello [just put the kibosh](https://www.eastidahonews.com/2026/07/pocatello-city-council-upholds-decision-to-deny-ai-data-center/) on a new data center, and the same could happen to others, especially as data centers steadily [fall out of favor](https://news.gallup.com/poll/709772/americans-oppose-data-centers-area.aspx) with communities and decision makers alike. Idaho Power should be careful not to put ratepayers on the hook for new power sources that we may not even need.

Now, let’s say we do need some, if not all, of this new power in the coming years, and Idaho Power needs to invest in new power sources. Are gas plants the best choice?

In this day and age — with advancements in [alternative energy sources](https://www.climaterealityproject.org/blog/how-do-renewables-lower-cost-electricity) that have led to low costs, fast installations, and reliable performance — it would be truly laughable to invest in building old-fashioned gas-fired power plants. These plants would require building new gas pipelines in Idaho, waiting years to [secure the right equipment](https://www.eenews.net/articles/want-to-build-a-gas-plant-get-in-line/), and locking us into paying [volatile gas prices](https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/analysis/publications/renewable-electricity-buffering-gas-prices) for decades to come. Not to mention adding even more [air pollution](https://www.selc.org/topic/methane-gas-threatens-southern-people-and-places/) at a time when Idahoans are already struggling to breathe.

So, are there other ways for Idaho Power to meet increasing power needs in a more affordable, effective, and stable way? Of course.

First, Idaho Power can always increase investments in proven strategies for helping our state use energy in smarter ways. I’m not talking about telling homeowners to change their lightbulbs. I’m talking about large-scale approaches, like requiring data centers and other industries to use energy at specific times of day and build their own energy sources; along with system-wide programs like Idaho Power’s Irrigation Peak Rewards and [A/C Cool Credit](https://www.idahopower.com/energy-environment/ways-to-save/savings-for-your-home/rebates-and-offers/ac-cool-credit/), which save as much energy as a gas plant could produce. If Idaho Power expanded these strategies, it could help avoid building new power plants and support Idahoans with more efficient facilities and lower power costs. These are Idaho-based investments that benefit Idaho’s economy. Gas plants just funnel money to out-of-state gas companies.

Second, if Idaho Power really needs more electricity sources, it can choose to build battery plants instead of gas plants. Idaho Power’s forecasts show that it primarily needs extra power during peak hours of high demand, when everyone is running their A/C, irrigation, and businesses during the heat of the summer. Batteries offer the same quick-ramping power capabilities as gas plants, but without the need to rely on a risky, pricey and polluting fuel source, and Idaho Power’s own [bids](https://lf-puc.idaho.gov/WebLink/DocView.aspx?dbid=0&id=156095) show that costs for new battery projects are on par with new gas plants. Especially with a public commitment to be [coal- and gas-free in less than 20 years](https://lf-puc.idaho.gov/WebLink/DocView.aspx?dbid=0&id=156095), Idaho Power’s choice to invest in new gas infrastructure instead of clean energy sources is baffling.

In short, it’s clear that Idaho Power’s new gas plants are not in the public interest, and our state’s PUC should deny their approval. Please submit a [public comment](https://puc.idaho.gov/Form/CaseComment) to our commissioners before the deadline this Friday, July 31 (case # IPC-E-26-04). Once these gas plants are built, the money is spent, and there’s no going back.

*Lisa Young is the director of the Idaho chapter of the Sierra Club.*
