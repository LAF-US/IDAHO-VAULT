Idaho House Speaker Mike Moyle is challenging the skyrocketing assessed value of his family’s property again — one parcel even ballooned from a value of $18,000 to over $2.7 million.

Moyle’s mother had appealed those assessments to the Ada County commissioners. But during a [tense public hearing in July,](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/local/article316461211.html) the Moyles prevailed only on the southern portion of several parcels near State Street and State Highway 16 in Star. Now, after the commissioners ruled against them on the northern portion, they’re hoping to change their fortunes.

Moyle, who was first elected to the state Legislature in 1998, appealed on behalf of his family in late July to the Idaho Board of Tax Appeals for the three northern parcels, according to Daisy Lewis, a spokesperson with the Ada County Clerk’s Office, and a copy of his filing received via a records request. Moyle could not be reached for comment.

The saga started because Moyle’s family had been working on potential development plans, prompting the Ada County Assessor’s Office to travel to the land to see whether the area could still be considered agricultural or waste. The Assessor’s Office concluded that wasn’t the case and gave the properties development designations.

The sticking point on the northern parcels was what the land is used for.

The Assessor’s Office argued that the land, which could become a residential subdivision, showcased a completed private lake, and the Moyles had put in infrastructure improvements, such as sidewalks.

Kathlynn Ireland, assistant chief deputy in the Assessor’s Office, previously said that Moyle “admitted he had not planted any crops” and then “asserted that he would plant corn … by the end of the week.”

Moyle, for his part, said sweet corn could be planted throughout the year and that the land was used for agriculture.

“The lake’s done. It’s all waste. It’s a depleted gravel pit, and the corn’s planted back in there,” Moyle said at the time. “It’s now agriculture. “

Moyle texted the commissioners ahead of that hearing, saying in one message that he was moving the fall sweet corn “to the fields your assessor is full of crap about,” according to texts received via a records request.

“The use is ag,” Moyle wrote.

The Board of Tax Appeals, [which was established](https://bta.idaho.gov/about-the-bta/) in 1969, hears hundreds of appeals each year.

In his appeal filing, Moyle said that a parcel assessed at over $2.7 million should be valued at just under $21,000.

Another parcel, assessed at over $1.2 million, is actually worth just under $9,000, the filing said.

The third piece of land is worth only $2,600, Moyle said, even though the county valued it at over $350,000.

The Board of Tax Appeals typically holds hearings within 90 to 120 days after it acknowledges someone’s appeal, according to its website.

Those hearings are public, according to a July email from Ireland, with the Ada County Assessor’s Office. The board has to release its decisions by May 1 of the next year, Ireland said.
