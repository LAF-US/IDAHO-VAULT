CALDWELL, Idaho — A gay couple is recovering after Caldwell Police said they were attacked near Indian Creek Plaza on Saturday night.

The two men, Eric Reed and Juan Olvera, said they were leaving Acapulco, a nearby restaurant, when multiple men reportedly yelled homophobic slurs toward them.

Olvera and Reed said they were chased through the parking lot and assaulted by one of the men where the railroad tracks meet 9th Street.

"I literally thought I was going to die, and I don't even know how to explain the fear I have right now. We haven't left the house for the past few days," Olvera said. "What's going to happen to the next person? Are they going to kill them? I literally felt they were going to kill us for being gay. It's a scary thing to live through, and I never would wish this on anyone," said Olvera.

Police later arrested 55-year-old Pedro Villarreal III of Caldwell and charged him with misdemeanor battery.

Reed needed six stitches on his lip. Olvera had bruises and cuts. The couple said they had been together for 15 years and had never gone through anything like this.

Eric Reed had to get six stitches after he was beaten up in Caldwell Saturday night.

"I just feel so scared that this could happen to my partner and me, and just seeing my partner's face afterwards," Olvera said.

Caldwell police said Idaho law did not allow a hate crime charge in the case.

"Idaho doesn't specifically have a code that covers hate crime, unfortunately. It covers crimes that involve national origin, things of that nature, but unfortunately, sexual orientation is not one of those that is actually covered under Idaho statute," said Lt. Jeffrey Peterson.

It's difficult when the law ties their hands in these types of cases, according to Lt. Peterson.

"It is frustrating. We're here to serve the citizens of Caldwell and Idaho, and unfortunately, when we're unable to give them the justice that they feel that they need, under the application of the law, it is frustrating," said Lt. Peterson.

Olvera and Reed said they are conservative and want people to come together.

"As politics continue to clash, everybody needs to put that away and remember we're all Americans in the end. All of us bleed the same, and we're all Americans," Olvera said.

Caldwell Mayor Eric Phillips sent KTVB a statement regarding the alleged attack.

"As Mayor, I want to be clear that we do not support violence in any form in our community. Everyone deserves to feel safe at our public events and throughout the City of Caldwell. We are aware of the incident that occurred at the Caldwell Night Market this weekend, and we are grateful for the swift response from law enforcement. We will continue to defer to the Caldwell Police Department as they complete their investigation," he wrote.
