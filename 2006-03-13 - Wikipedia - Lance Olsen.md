**Lance Olsen** (born October 14, 1956) is an American writer known for his experimental, lyrical, fragmentary, cross-genre narratives that question the limits of historical knowledge.

## Biography

Lance Olsen was born in [River Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Edge "River Edge"), [New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey "New Jersey"), United States. He received a B.A. degree from the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison "University of Wisconsin–Madison") (1978, honors, [Phi Beta Kappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phi_Beta_Kappa "Phi Beta Kappa")), an M.F.A. from the [Iowa Writers' Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Writers'_Workshop "Iowa Writers' Workshop") (1980), and an M.A. (1982) and Ph.D. (1985) from the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia").

For ten years he taught as associate and then full professor at the [University of Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Idaho "University of Idaho"); for two years, he directed the University of [[Idaho]]'s M.F.A. program. He has also taught at the [University of Iowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Iowa "University of Iowa"), the [University of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Virginia "University of Virginia"), the [University of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kentucky "University of Kentucky"), on summer and semester-abroad programs in Oxford and London, on a [Fulbright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulbright "Fulbright") in [Turku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turku "Turku"), Finland, and at various writing conferences. From 2007 to 2023, he taught experimental narrative theory and practice at the [University of Utah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Utah "University of Utah").[^1] From 2002 to 2018, he served as chair of the board of directors at Fiction Collective Two,[^2] or [FC2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiction_Collective_Two "Fiction Collective Two"); founded in 1974, FC2 is one of America's best-known ongoing literary experiments and progressive art communities. He was fiction editor at *Western Humanities Review* from 2007 to 2013. He served as Director of Creative Writing at the University of Utah from 2018 to 2019.

Olsen's wife is assemblage-artist [Andi Olsen](http://www.andiolsen.com/).

## Writing

Olsen is author of seventeen novels, one hypermedia text, six nonfiction books, five short-story collections, a poetry chapbook, and two anti-textbooks about experimental writing, as well as editor of two collections of essays about innovative contemporary fiction. His short stories, essays, poems, and reviews have appeared in hundreds of journals, magazines, and anthologies, including *[Conjunctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conjunctions_\(journal\) "Conjunctions (journal)")*, *[Fiction International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiction_International "Fiction International")*, *[Iowa Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iowa_Review "Iowa Review")*, *[Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Voice "Village Voice")*, *[Time Out New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Out_New_York "Time Out New York")*, *[BOMB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_\(magazine\) "Bomb (magazine)")*, *[Hotel Amerika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Amerika "Hotel Amerika")*, and *Best American Non-Required Reading*. He is known for his fictional biographies (examples of [historiographic metafiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historiographic_metafiction "Historiographic metafiction")), such as *[Nietzsche's Kisses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nietzsche's_Kisses "Nietzsche's Kisses")* and *[Head in Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_in_Flames "Head in Flames")*, for which he does extensive historical research,[^3] as well as his work in [avantpop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avantpop_\(artistic_movement\) "Avantpop (artistic movement)"), [postmodernism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism "Postmodernism"), [speculative fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction "Speculative fiction"), [experimental writing practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_literature "Experimental literature"), and critifiction (the blending of theory and narrativity in a single text).

The hypermedial version of his novel *10:01*,[^4] created in collaboration with artist [Tim S. Guthrie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_S._Guthrie "Tim S. Guthrie"), was published by the *Iowa Review Web* in 2005 and included in the *Electronic Literature Organization Collection: Volume One*.[^5] Olsen was a regular participant in the biennial [&NOW Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/&NOW_Festival "&NOW Festival"), a celebration of experimental and innovative writing, and has collaborated with a board member of &NOW, [Davis Schneiderman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Schneiderman "Davis Schneiderman"), on a series of short works.[^6]

## Awards

In March 2025, Olsen was awarded a Connecticut Artistic Excellence Grant.[^7] In May 2022, he was a fellow at The Rockefeller Bellagio Center on Lake Como, Italy.[^8] In the spring of 2018, he taught a seminar on Experimental Forms and delivered two lectures as Chaire des Amériques at the Institut des Amériques de Rennes at the University of Rennes.[^9] From May 2015 through April 2016, Olsen was a guest at the [DAAD Artists-in-Berlin Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DAAD_Artists-in-Berlin_Program "DAAD Artists-in-Berlin Program").[^10] He was the Mary Ellen von der Heyden Berlin Prize in Fiction Fellow at the [American Academy in Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_in_Berlin "American Academy in Berlin") from January through May 2013 [^11] and the Mellon International Visiting Senior Scholar at [Rhodes University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes_University "Rhodes University") in [Grahamstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grahamstown "Grahamstown"), South Africa, in October 2013.[^12] He is a [Guggenheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guggenheim_Fellowship "Guggenheim Fellowship") [^13] and a two-time [N.E.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Endowment_for_the_Arts "National Endowment for the Arts") fellowship recipient,[^14] winner of a [Pushcart Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushcart_Prize "Pushcart Prize"),[^15] and was the governor-appointed Idaho Writer-in-Residence from 1996 to 1998.[^16] His 1994 novel *[Tonguing the Zeitgeist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonguing_the_Zeitgeist "Tonguing the Zeitgeist")* was a finalist for the [Philip K. Dick Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_K._Dick_Award "Philip K. Dick Award"), and his work has been translated into Arabic, Croatian, Finnish, German, Italian, Polish, Russian, and Turkish.
