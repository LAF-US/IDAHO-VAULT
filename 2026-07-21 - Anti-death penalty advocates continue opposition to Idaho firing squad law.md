Opponents of the death penalty are continuing to speak out against [[Idaho]]'s use of capital punishment, including the [state's move to make the firing squad its primary execution method.](https://idahonews.com/news/local/firing-squad-set-to-become-primary-method-of-execution-in-idaho)

During a virtual news conference this week, advocates urged Idaho leaders to reconsider the use of the death penalty and reverse the state's decision to prioritize firing squad executions.

Among the speakers was Randy Gardner, whose brother, Ronnie Lee Gardner, was executed by firing squad in Utah in 2010.

"On June 18th, 2010, my brother was executed here in Utah by firing squad," Gardner said.

Gardner said he has remained active in death penalty advocacy since his brother's execution.

"I've been fighting against the death penalty ever since," Gardner said. "I understand Idaho's trying to bring back the firing squad. I've actually been there protesting the firing squad chamber that they just built. I think it's a terrible idea."

Gardner said his experiences following the execution shaped his views on capital punishment.

"I don't think there's any humane way to execute anybody," Gardner said.

The event was organized by anti-death penalty advocates, including Rev. Dr. Jeff Hood, founder of the [Execution Intervention Project](https://www.executionintervention.org/). The group recently installed a billboard in downtown Boise reading, "Idaho... Don't Shoot Tom Creech!"

"We're begging the people of Idaho to stop this, please," Hood said.

Hood said the billboard campaign is intended to encourage public discussion ahead of any future executions in Idaho. He said the group opposes all forms of capital punishment, not just firing squad executions.

Speakers at the news conference argued that executions affect more than the condemned person, citing impacts on victims' families, prison employees, spiritual advisors and witnesses.

Jan Powell, with Idahoans Against the Death Penalty, said opposition to capital punishment has existed in Idaho for decades and that advocates are focused on educating the public about the issue.

"Not everyone in Idaho supports guns, and not everyone in Idaho supports the death penalty," Powell said.

[Idahoans Against the Death Penalty](https://idahoansagainstthedeathpenalty.org/) and other advocacy groups say they plan to continue public education efforts as debate over capital punishment and execution methods continues in Idaho.
