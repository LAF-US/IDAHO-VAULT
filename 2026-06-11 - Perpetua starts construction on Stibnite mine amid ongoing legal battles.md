Work to build a new road to a gold and antimony mine in eastern Valley County is underway after a favorable court ruling for the mine.

Boise-based Perpetua Resources recently started a three-year, $2.6 billion construction phase needed to develop its Stibnite Gold Project following [a May 29 decision by U.S. District Judge Amanda Brailsford](https://advocateswest.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ECF-70-PI-Decision-May-29-2026.pdf).

Brailsford denied a bid by conservation groups to halt construction on the mine until a final ruling is issued in [a lawsuit challenging the legality of the approvals](https://valleylookout.com/2025/02/19/forest-service-sued-over-stibnite-mine-approval/) issued for the project by federal regulators.

The conservation groups have since [appealed that ruling](https://advocateswest.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/9ecf-5.1-SSFS-Emergency-Motion.pdf), but in the meantime, Perpetua is ramping up construction on key components of the mine, according to company officials.

“It’s disappointing that opponents continue trying to delay a project that is important to America’s national security, Idaho’s economy, and will bring environmental benefits to an abandoned mine site,” Perpetua CEO Jon Cherry said. “The construction work underway today is limited, carefully planned, and taking place largely in areas that have already been disturbed by past mining and wildfire activity.”

The conservation groups, which include Save the South Fork Salmon and the Idaho Conservation League, argue the work planned by Perpetua — specifically the construction of a new road to the mine site — threatens harm to clean water, fish, and wildlife.

“Allowing this massive mining project to move forward before the lawfulness of the approvals is resolved could cause serious and irreversible harm,” said Bryan Hurlbutt, an attorney representing the conservation groups.

Cherry disagreed and said Perpetua is “confident” in Brailsford’s original ruling, though the appeal filed by the conservation groups is still pending.

Perpetua is beginning the three-year construction phase with a focus on completing the most critical logistical components of the mine — power line upgrades, improved road access, and workforce housing.

Combined, the completion of those three components would position Perpetua to begin construction on ore processing facilities at Stibnite and the excavation of three open-pit mines.

## Burntlog Road construction begins

Following Brailsford’s ruling, crews began work on the 38-mile Burntlog Road, which Perpetua plans to use as the primary road to Stibnite for mining traffic.

Building the route involves upgrading 23 miles of existing roadway to be suitable for use by large trucks, along with building 15 miles of new roadway.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“We anticipate the Burntlog Route will be advanced enough to accommodate large loads in late 2027,” said Natalie Podgorksi, a Perpetua spokesperson.

The Burntlog Route is [preferred by Perpetua and the Forest Service](https://perpetuaresources.com/wp-content/uploads/2022-11-22-SDEIS-Infrastructure-Overview-1.pdf) because it is further from major waterways and has fewer avalanche paths than existing routes to the mine. However, the road is also routed through remote areas with no other roads and, in places, is near the Frank Church River of No Return Wilderness.

## Construction traffic

Mining traffic is currently traveling to Stibnite on Warm Lake Road, Johnson Creek Road, and Stibnite Road. Warm Lake Road will continue being used by mining traffic even once the Burntlog Road is completed.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

The construction workforce traffic, including large trucks delivering equipment and supplies, is expected to add about 65 vehicle trips per day to Warm Lake Road.

“Perpetua is limiting heavy equipment travel to Monday through Friday during business hours as much as possible,” Podgorski said. “Traffic in the area may move slower than usual due to convoys of construction vehicles, and there will be times when traffic is stopped briefly for one-way passing. We’ll work to keep delays as short as possible.”

Daily traffic estimates include commuters among Perpetua’s workforce, which for this summer is expected to total about 300 people. Currently, about 100 people are working on the project, Podgorski said.

“We are still in the process of building out our summer workforce,” she said. “We don’t have shuttles running yet but are evaluating opportunities.”

Many employees working at the housing facility are currently living at Stibnite or in rental housing Perpetua secured in Yellow Pine, though Podgorski declined to provide an exact number.

“Perpetua has expanded temporary workforce housing at Stibnite, making it possible for the majority of summer workforce to stay up on site during their shifts,” she said.

## Mine site housing a priority

The top construction priority at the mine site this summer is building a permanent workforce housing facility. Last fall, Perpetua awarded a $130 million contract for that project to [ATCO Structures of Spring, Texas](https://structures.atco.com/en-us.html).

Once complete, the modular housing is expected to house up to 1,000 workers and will include office space, kitchen and dining facilities, and recreational amenities, [according to ATCO](https://mma.prnewswire.com/media/2805376/ATCO_Ltd__ATCO_Structures_Accelerates_U_S__Expansion_with__179M.pdf?p=pdf).

The housing facility is being built from 363 modular units that are being prefabricated at ATCO factories before being trucked to Stibnite and assembled on site.

Perpetua is optimistic the housing facility will be ready for occupancy in 2027, Podgorski said.

## Power line upgrades

Upgrading power lines serving Stibnite is Perpetua’s other construction priority for this summer. Plans call for 64 miles of existing power lines to be upgraded, with nine miles of new power line to be built.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The work will double the electrical capacity of power lines along Idaho 55 between Lake Fork and Donnelly and along Warm Lake Road and Johnson Creek Road. Three new substations will also be built.

Last year, [Perpetua awarded a $90 million contract to Idaho Power](https://valleylookout.com/2025/02/27/perpetua-awards-90m-contract-to-idaho-power-amid-uncertain-construction-timeline/) to supply materials for the project. Completing the work is estimated to cost another $150 million.

Construction of the Stibnite mine is financed by [a $2.9 billion loan](https://valleylookout.com/2026/05/29/perpetua-approved-for-2-9b-loan-needed-to-build-stibnite-mine/) from the Export-Import Bank of the United States.

Perpetua plans to extract some $25 billion in gold, silver, and antimony from Stibnite, the site of historic mining operations during World War II and as far back as 1899.

The mine could produce an estimated 148 million pounds of antimony and 4.8 million ounces of gold, which would account for nearly all of the mine’s projected revenue.
