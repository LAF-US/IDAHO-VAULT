Republican candidates in contested races for local and state offices will participate in a public forum on Friday night in McCall.

The forum, hosted by the Valley County Republican Central Committee, will run from 6 p.m. to 8 p.m. at the McCall Public Library. [A live stream and recording of the event](https://www.facebook.com/ValleyRepublicanGOP) will be available on the committee’s Facebook page for anyone who cannot attend in person.

The Republican Primary Election is slated for May 19 and features contested races for two seats on the Valley County Board of County Commissioners and three seats representing District 8, which includes Valley County, in the Idaho Legislature. Only registered Republicans may vote in the primary, under state law.

The forum will be moderated by members of the committee, with audience members encouraged to submit questions in writing at the forum, said Brenda Gilchrist, vice chair of the committee.

“If we are unable to address all of them, we will ask submitters to provide their email address so we can forward the questions to the candidate(s) for a response after the forum,” Gilchrist said.

## Absent candidates

Three Republican candidates in contested races on the May 19 ballot will not be participating, including incumbent Valley County Clerk Gabby Knapp and Carrie Potter, who is challenging Knapp for a four-year term leading the clerk’s office. Gilchrist said the omission was a product of limited time and a crowded field of contested primary races.

“We encourage voters to host their own meet-and-greets and ask candidates if they are attending or hosting any events before the election,” she said.

Also not in attendance will be [Sean Hall of Horseshoe Bend](https://www.seanhallforidaho.com/), who is challenging Beiswenger for a two-year term in the Idaho House of Representatives. Gilchrist said Hall declined an invitation to the event in March after the committee endorsed Beiswenger.

“Endorsing a candidate before primary voters have had an opportunity to participate runs counter to the spirit of building the party ‘from the grassroots up’ and encouraging community participation,” Hall wrote in a March 10 letter to the committee.

On April 8, Hall accepted a Google calendar invite to the forum, but was told his invitation was no longer valid because the event schedule was already planned, according to a news release his campaign issued on April 9.

Hall criticized the committee for being “party gatekeepers” and said its actions “undermine the primary process.”
