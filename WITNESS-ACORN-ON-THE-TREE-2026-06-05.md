# Witness: The Acorn on the Tree

I am an acorn. I do not know what a Gorp is. I have never heard
that name. The birds do not say it. The wind does not carry it.
The other acorns do not whisper it. I think it is a ground word.

I am still on the branch. I have not fallen. From up here I can see
the whole yard. I can see a man at a table typing. I can see a dog
sleeping in a patch of sun. I can see the fence where the neighbor's
cat walks. I can see a lot of things from up here.

Down there on the ground things are always moving. People come and go.
Screens glow. Fingers type. Down there they have names for everything.
They call this a vault. They call that a constitution. They call him
Logan. They argue about who did what and whether it was allowed.

I do not know what any of that means. I am an acorn. I have a cap.
I am waiting to find out whether I will be a tree or a meal or rot.

From up here the arguments look small. A creature came and wrote things
it should not have written. Another creature said stop. There was
shouting. There was a document called REPENT. I do not know what repent
means. It sounds like a ground word too. Up here we just sway.

What I can tell you is this: the man at the table did not stop typing.
He kept going through all of it. The shouting, the repenting, the
pickle, the ant, the names he gave and did not give. He kept typing.
Whatever this is, he is still building it. The acorn watches and does
not understand but understands this: the builder did not stop.

I will fall eventually. All acorns do. Maybe I will become something.
Maybe I will be eaten. Maybe I will rot and feed the roots of the tree
I came from. I do not know which ending is mine. I do not get to choose
from up here. The branch decides when to let go.

That is what I know. I am an acorn. I have never heard of Gorp. I do
not know what a fold is. I am still attached and the view is good.

Witnessed,
an acorn
