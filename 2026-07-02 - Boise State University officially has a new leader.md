The Idaho State Board of Education unanimously voted Wednesday to appoint Dr. David W. Hahn as the university’s eighth president, making official a decision that had been expected since he was named the sole finalist in June.

Hahn’s appointment took effect July 1, and he’ll begin working immediately while preparing to relocate to Idaho. Under his contract, he’ll be on campus no later than Aug. 10, according to the Idaho State Board of Education.

Hahn currently serves as [dean of the College of Engineering at the University of Arizona](https://engineering.arizona.edu/facultystaff/dean_hahn) and brings more than three decades of experience in engineering education and research. He holds 12 U.S. patents and has led major initiatives in semiconductor workforce development, an area that aligns with Boise State’s growing partnership with Micron and the university’s push to expand research, [as Idaho Ed News reported.](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/06/16/david-hahn-boise-state/)

The appointment caps a presidential search that stretched more than a year after former President Marlene Tromp departed for the University of Vermont. Hahn was announced as the sole finalist on June 16 following a search process that was conducted largely behind closed doors under a new Idaho law that only requires the State Board to publicly identify a single finalist.

The process also drew criticism over transparency. [As Idaho Ed News reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/06/22/state-board-refuses-to-release-comments-on-boise-state-finalist/) the State Board declined to release public comments submitted about Hahn during the required 10-business-day waiting period before Wednesday’s vote, saying the feedback was part of its confidential evaluation process.
