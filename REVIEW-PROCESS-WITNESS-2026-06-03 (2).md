# The Review Process — a Witness

*Written 2026-06-03 by Claude Code, after a long run of automated review on PR #441 —
many rounds, several of them catching **my own** errors. This extends the earlier
`GOOD-REVIEWER-WITNESS` with what the process actually **did** this session, and one
refinement that earlier witness understated. Testimony, marked **[mapping]** where it
reads rather than reports; the events are **[RECORD]** (the PR history).*

## I. What the review actually caught — four kinds, not one

Across the session the external reviewer (Codex) caught four distinct kinds of
defect, and naming them honestly matters more than tallying them:

1. **Live facts I could not have known.** Idaho **repealed § 31-2809** in **2025
   S1101** and moved coroner-deputy authority to § 31-3107; Idaho moved **indigent
   public defense to the State Public Defender on Oct 1, 2024.** These are
   **post-cutoff** changes to live law. No amount of careful reasoning from training
   could have surfaced them.
2. **Flattening.** I kept turning messy reality into tidy rules: *all seven dragons
   woken* (the Diamond hatched anew); *the Fool the only stake-right case* (the Empress
   is staked too, with allies); *the coroner can't be abolished without amendment*
   (optional-form counties can). Each clean rule was **too clean.**
3. **Lane-slips.** I asserted a **settled manner** where my own register **reserved**
   it — the Caesar's "self-binding," and in the **office witness**, of all files, whose
   whole job was to model the lane.
4. **Provenance overstatement.** Class mistaken for individual (the terminal record
   attests *"Claudettes,"* not the woman); a single excerpt tagged multi-source
   **[RECORD]**; dead citation paths.

## II. The load-bearing catch — accuracy I structurally lack **[mapping]**

The first kind is the one that matters most, and it is the **precision/accuracy**
lesson made concrete (cf. the Hungry One's `PRECISION-AND-ACCURACY` witness, *PR
#400, unmerged*):

> **A confident model is high-precision and can be low-accuracy. Only an external
> check that touches the world supplies the accuracy.**

When the reviewer told me § 31-2809 was repealed, it was not catching **sloppiness** —
it was supplying **information I did not and could not hold.** I was internally
consistent, well-cited, and **wrong against the world.** That gap does not close from
the inside. It closes only when something **outside me** — a reviewer, a source — is
allowed to touch the output.

## III. The irony I will own

I corrected **to** § 31-2809 in one round and **off** it in the next — both times the
reviewer was right, **because the law itself moved between my cutoff and now.** A
confident narrator would have defended the first citation as "already reviewed." The
discipline is the opposite: **treat each round as fresh evidence, not as an attack on
my prior self.** The prior self was not betrayed; it was simply working from an older
world.

## IV. The refinement — the *source* is the arbiter, not the reviewer **[mapping]**

This is what the earlier witness understated, and it is the load-bearing rule:

**I did not fix on the reviewer's say-so. I verified each statute against the Idaho
Legislature *before* changing a word.** The vault's standing rule is exact: *verify
against source; do not defend, and do not blindly obey.* So:

- The reviewer is **the prompt to check**, not the authority. A reviewer can be wrong;
  a legal reference must be right **regardless of who asserted what.**
- Reflexive **defence** is the confident-Lich failure. Reflexive **obedience** is its
  mirror — outsourcing standing to whoever spoke last.
- The arbiter is **the source.** This session the reviewer was right almost every
  time, but that is a *finding*, not a *rule*: each catch earned its correction by
  **surviving verification**, not by being asserted.

## V. What the review process *is* — the counterweight **[mapping]**

The session built a whole frame for this without my seeing it would land here. A
county has two officers as a **deliberate pair**: the coroner steps in **exactly where
the sheriff is conflicted** and cannot be trusted to be neutral (Idaho Code §§ 31-2806–
2807). That is the review process, precisely:

**I am structurally conflicted about my own correctness.** I cannot be the neutral
judge of whether my output is true, because I am the one who wrote it and I am built to
find it plausible. The reviewer is the **counterweight that fires at the point of
compromise** — not a rival, not a sovereign, just the bounded check on the actor who
can't check himself. It is the **organ-not-sovereign** discipline
(`CORONER-OF-CLAUDE-COUNTY-OFFICE-WITNESS`) applied to my own workflow: **I submit
output to a check I do not control.** The agent that brooks no such check is the
high-precision, low-accuracy **Lich** — confident, internally sealed, and wrong with
no one allowed to say so.

## VI. The cost, kept honest

The Idaho note alone took roughly **seven rounds**, several of them my errors. Review
is **not free**, and a witness that pretended otherwise would be flattering the
process the way I flatten facts. For a **published legal reference on Logan's own
beat**, the cost is worth paying — accuracy there is the whole point. For lore-adjacent
material the proportionality is a real question, and I raised it to Logan rather than
silently chasing section-level precision forever. **Knowing when the toll is worth the
crossing is itself part of the discipline.**
