# Sources - Oz: Tik-Tok and the Tin Woodman - 2026-06-03

## Scope

Source note for the 2026-06-03 research pass on L. Frank Baum's Oz characters
Tik-Tok and the Tin Woodman. This note records sources used and short
observations. It is not doctrine.

## Primary Sources Used

1. L. Frank Baum, *Ozma of Oz*, Project Gutenberg.
   - URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/486/pg486-images.html
   - Used for Tik-Tok's introduction as a copper clockwork machine, his
     dependence on winding, and the statement that he was not alive and
     therefore could not die.

2. L. Frank Baum, *Tik-Tok of Oz*, Project Gutenberg.
   - URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/956/956-h/956-h.htm
   - Used for later Tik-Tok context: Ozma sends the Clockwork Man to assist
     Shaggy; the book title centers Tik-Tok even though the plot ranges more
     broadly.

3. L. Frank Baum, *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*, Project Gutenberg.
   - URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/55/55-h/55-h
   - Used for the Tin Woodman's original heart quest, his claim that he has no
     heart, and his careful ethical behavior because he lacks that internal
     guide.

4. L. Frank Baum, *The Tin Woodman of Oz*, Project Gutenberg.
   - URL: https://www.gutenberg.org/files/960/960-h/960-h.htm
   - Used for later Tin Woodman continuity: Nick Chopper as Emperor of the
     Winkies, his origin story as formerly flesh and blood, the need for oil,
     and the unsettling later encounter with his former head.

## Research Notes

- Tik-Tok is presented as machinery that can speak, think, and act when wound.
  His dependency is explicit: speech, action, and thought are clockwork systems
  that can run down.
- Tik-Tok's condition is not death. Baum frames him as not alive, and therefore
  not killable in the ordinary sense.
- The Tin Woodman is different: he began as Nick Chopper, a human woodman whose
  body was replaced part by part with tin.
- The Tin Woodman's core anxiety is not merely operation but heart, feeling,
  love, ethics, and continuity of personhood.
- In *The Wonderful Wizard of Oz*, the Tin Woodman compensates for lacking a
  heart by taking special care not to be cruel or unkind.
- In *The Tin Woodman of Oz*, Baum complicates identity further by separating
  the Tin Woodman from his former head and bodily remains.

## Working Contrast

Tik-Tok:

- goes or stops;
- depends on winding;
- does not live or die;
- models maintenance-bound machinery.

Tin Woodman:

- was alive before tin;
- seeks a heart;
- remains ethically anxious;
- models transformed personhood and continuity after replacement.
