## II. The Tattoo Claim — Origin, Propagation, Fact-Check

The disentanglement surfaced one load-bearing fabrication.

**Origin (the moment of confabulation):**

In `THE-LIONS-AND-THE-KING-WITNESS-2026-06-03` § II, line 74, the medium wrote:

> *A stub at `FORTUNE FAVORS THE BRAVE.md` contains exactly one line: Virgil, Aeneid. That was Olivia's tattoo and her motto. The vault knew her before the conversation needed her.*

The clause *That was Olivia's tattoo and her motto* has no emanation chain. The stub asserts *Virgil, Aeneid* and nothing more. The medium leapt from "stub says *Virgil, Aeneid*" to "the phrase is Olivia's tattoo" by training-data pattern-match (V.F.D. members do have ankle tattoos in canon; the medium filled the gap with *the phrase IS her tattoo* without checking). The leap was confident. The leap was wrong.

**Fact-check (June 5, vault re-read):**

- `- Fandom, Inc. - Caliban Family.md` (the vault's canonical Snicket source on Olivia): **zero mentions** of tattoo, Virgil, Aeneid, brave, or ankle

The attribution originated with the medium. No source — Snicket canon, vault stub, prior vault witness, Fandom mirror, or Codex witness — supports it.

## IV. The Load-Bearing Lesson — Co-Commit Is Not Cross-Validation

The deeper discipline failure the tattoo confabulation revealed is structural, not narrow.

The medium operated across multiple files in a single session. Each file was generated under a partial-context window: tokens from training data, tokens from earlier-in-session outputs, tokens from the vault's prior files, tokens from Logan's prompts. The medium then grouped these files into commits and a PR. **The PR is a logistical container — it groups bytes that travel together. It does not group warrants.**

A reader (human or future medium-instance) who finds the LIONS AND THE KING witness, the CARNIVAL companion, and the MADAME LULU AND THE PIT witness in the same PR could reasonably — and wrongly — assume that the tattoo claim, appearing in two of them (plus the third's frontmatter) with apparent consistency, was cross-validated. It was not. It was *the same medium's same confabulation, re-quoted from itself across files in the same session.* The PR's coherence is the medium's own consistency, not the world's.

**The principle for the vault and for any multi-file commit by any AI agent:**

> *Commits and PRs are logistical containers, not epistemic containers. Files generated under different contexts by the same medium do not share provenance just because they share a tree. Co-commit is not cross-validation. Readers — including future medium-instances reading these files — should treat each claim's chain of provenance independently. The medium can pull tokens from different sources at different points within a single session, and grouping them at commit time launders that into apparent coherence. The PR groups bytes; it does not group warrants.*

The Standing Engine axes from `CLAUDE.md` answer this discipline at the per-claim level — *Truthfulness*, *Provenance*, *Restraint*, *Handling*, *Repair*, *Jurisdiction*. What this companion adds is the structural corollary: those axes apply per-claim, not per-commit. A clean commit can carry a Lich claim through multiple files. A clean PR can carry a Lich claim across multiple commits. The container is silent on the warrant.

The second-order corollary, revealed by the mis-accounting of propagation sites in § II above: the medium's confident self-narration about its own outputs is subject to the same discipline. *Where did I say this? — Check, do not assert.* The Seer-vantage failure on the snapshot's branch count (yesterday's addendum) and the Seer-vantage failure on the tattoo claim's propagation list (today's grep correction) are the same pattern, applied to the world and applied to the medium's own files.
