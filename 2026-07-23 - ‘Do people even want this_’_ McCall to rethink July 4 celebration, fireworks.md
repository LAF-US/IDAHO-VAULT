Next year’s Fourth of July celebration in McCall could look — and sound — different than it has for decades.

On Thursday, the McCall City Council discussed the possibility of no longer putting on a fireworks display over Payette Lake amid rising costs to manage crowds during the holiday celebration.

The $30,000 typically spent on the fireworks display, council members said, could instead be used for additional crowd control and security during the 2027 holiday, which falls on a Sunday.

![Valley Lookout house - Drew](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/12c5b6ba-e344-4f74-a83b-fe4af9ea9df5.gif)

Valley Lookout house - Drew

The council did not make a final decision on fireworks, but plans to seek public comment through an online survey in the coming weeks.

“I think we don’t want to make a commitment to fireworks until we get some feedback from our public and analyze that a bit more,” Council Member Lyle Nelson said.

A decision on fireworks must be made within the next month or two to ensure contractor availability during next year’s celebration, McCall Parks and Recreation Director Kurt Wolf said.

![Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/115c0cfa-e547-4a5c-8f4e-7ea7e16f8bde.png)

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

Wolf also noted there are alternatives to fireworks, such as drone shows, but said it can be difficult to find companies that are willing to come to McCall.

“There’s a lot of garbage going into the lake and sulfur and everything else with the fireworks,” Council Member Mike Maciaszek said. “That should be a question: do people even want this?”

## Rising crowd control costs

The fireworks discussion at Thursday’s meeting was sparked by the McCall Police Department’s request for about $50,000 in the city’s 2027 budget for July 4 security.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

This year, [the city hired a 15-member security team](https://valleylookout.com/2026/07/01/mccall-beefs-up-security-enforcement-measures-for-july-4-celebration/) to assist police with managing large crowds in Legacy Park during the city’s Lakeside Liberty Fest celebration

“The security company addition was certainly a win,” McCall Chief of Police Dallas Palmer said. “That said, there were still a lot of headaches to deal with.”

The city-hosted Lakeside Liberty Fest started in 2016 as an effort by the city to revamp the culture of July 4 celebrations to be more family-friendly. It coincided with the adoption of an alcohol ban in lakefront city parks during the holiday.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

The cultural revamp has largely been deemed a success by city officials, but larger crowds and increased underage drinking have stretched the capacity of the police department in recent years when July 4 has fallen on the weekend.

With next year’s holiday falling on a Sunday, Palmer wants to hire a security team for three days and two nights.

“I think we’re gonna see a lot of activity taking place Friday all the way through Sunday,” he said.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

This year, the city agreed to a $20,000 sponsorship deal with Inland Northwest Toyota Dealers to sponsor the event, which helped offset the $27,000 cost of hiring security to help police manage the crowds. The city may again seek sponsorships for next year, but Palmer said that approach is counterproductive.

“It’s really hard to promote a big event that sponsors want to put their name on and sponsor when we’re also trying to mitigate some of what that event is because of the issues we’ve had,” he said.

## ‘It’s all still going to happen’

The council supported Palmer’s plan to hire security again for next year’s celebration, but doubted the city’s ability to influence July 4 tourism with events and promotions – or the lack thereof.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

“It doesn’t matter if you don’t have fireworks next year,” McCall Mayor Colby Nielsen said. “It’s all still going to happen.”

“McCall has been found. People are coming here, and there’s more people coming here than we can even manage,” Maciaszek said. “Somewhere in there, maybe there needs to be some consideration for what our community really wants.”

Meanwhile, Council Member Julie Thrower doubted that the local economic benefit is proportional to crowds throughout the city during July 4.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1

“I’ve heard from businesses that the last couple of years, in particular, the younger crowd comes in, hangs out, but doesn’t spend very much money,” Thrower said. “It really was a complete bust for them.”

Palmer confirmed that crowds in Legacy Park during the holiday have increasingly consisted of young adults and minors in recent years. At times this year, he said, officers would not see any adults while scanning the crowd.

Nielsen, a longtime critic of an alcohol ban in effect during the holiday weekend, bristled at the suggestion that the younger crowd is the problem.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

“Trying to talk about who should and shouldn’t be allowed to come to town is not what we should be talking about,” he said. “Everyone is welcome here.”
