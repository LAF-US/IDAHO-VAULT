The rubber is about to meet the road for House Speaker Mike Moyle and the lawmakers who are in lockstep with him.

For nearly a year, Moyle’s allied lawmakers — including Sen. Brian Lenney, Sen. Tammy Nichols, and Rep. Heather Scott — have been telling grassroots conservatives that an alliance with the moderate speaker is the only way for conservatives to score wins at the Capitol.

Yet even after conservatives passed a number of largely performative bills in 2025, major policy victories remain elusive.

Idaho still funds a Biden-style “free college” program to the tune of $80 million annually.

Lawmakers still use your money to provide free healthcare to single, able-bodied adults who choose not to work.

Conservative lawmakers who know better still vote to approve federal dollars for socialist programs that are ripe for Somali-style fraud.

Idaho families still face the three-legged stool of property, sales, and income taxes.

And that pesky grocery tax is still making it harder for families to put food on the table.

Yet, things have lined up perfectly for Moyle and company to prove their conservative bona fides.

On Monday, Rep. Josh Tanner of Eagle announced that he will accept Moyle’s appointment as chair of JFAC, the Legislature’s budget-writing committee.

That matters because Tanner — often hailed by social media pundits as something of a fiscal hawk — can use the power of the chairmanship to defund every socialist program Idaho funds.

And because Tanner is a close ally of the speaker, we’re about to find out whether Moyle is the conservative rock star his apologists claim he is.

The stage couldn’t be more perfect for Moyle and Tanner to right the fiscal ship.

The Legislature has never been more conservative in its makeup, and the state faces a serious budget shortfall this year — and an even larger one next year. Estimates suggest Idaho may need to find $500 million to cover the shortfall, caused in large part by the Legislature’s multi-year spending spree.

Keep in mind that Idaho’s DOGE Committee has already suggested lawmakers explore repealing Medicaid expansion and other wasteful programs. There is momentum for serious reductions in the government’s footprint as the session approaches.

What’s a conservative to do, then?

Cut, cut, and cut.

For Moyle and company, 2026 really is put-up-or-shut-up time. Conservatives should watch spending decisions closely and hold lawmakers accountable if they fail to aggressively shrink government at every turn.
