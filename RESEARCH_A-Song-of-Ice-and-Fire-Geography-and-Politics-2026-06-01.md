# Research: *A Song of Ice and Fire* — Geography & Political Structures

**Source:** Online research, fanned out across five angles and cross-checked.
**Compiled by:** Claude Code, 2026-06-01, on branch `claude/song-ice-fire-sCaAD`.
**Companion to:** `RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-2026-06-01.md` (real-world current-state survey). *This* file documents the **in-world** geography and political order of the fictional setting.

> **Provenance note (read first).** These are **fictional facts**: "verification" means consistency with **canonical sources** — the novels, *The World of Ice & Fire* worldbook, and the fan-maintained but book-faithful **A Wiki of Ice and Fire** (awoiaf.westeros.org). awoiaf and Wikipedia both returned **HTTP 403** to automated fetching, so claims rest on search-engine extracts of those canonical pages. Distances/dates that are **fan-derived** (not stated by GRRM) or **in-world legend** are flagged inline. Book canon is preferred over the TV adaptations throughout.

## PART I — GEOGRAPHY

### Westeros (the western continent)

- Westeros is the western continent, separated from **Essos** by the **Narrow Sea**. It is roughly comparable in scale to South America (a semi-canonical authorial comparison). [awoiaf: Westeros; reactormag]
- Commonly given dimensions — **~3,000 mi N–S** (Wall to southern Dorne), **~900 mi** at the widest E–W — are ⚠️ **fan-computed estimates**, anchored on the one firm figure: the Wall is **100 leagues (~300 mi) long**. [Atlas of Ice and Fire; awoiaf: The Wall]
- **"Seven Kingdoms" is historical** — it names the seven independent realms that existed *before* Aegon's Conquest. The realm today is administered as **nine regions**:

| Region | Ruling seat | House |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The North | Winterfell | Stark |
| The Iron Islands | Pyke | Greyjoy |
| The Riverlands | Riverrun (Harrenhal is the great ruin) | Tully |
| The Vale of Arryn | The Eyrie | Arryn |
| The Westerlands | Casterly Rock (city: Lannisport) | Lannister |
| The Reach | Highgarden | Tyrell |
| The Stormlands | Storm's End | Baratheon |
| The Crownlands | King's Landing | (the Iron Throne) |
| Dorne | Sunspear | Martell |

- The **Crownlands** was never a sovereign kingdom — contested territory Aegon seized as his foothold. [awoiaf: Crownlands]
- **King's Landing** — capital and most populous city, in the Crownlands at the mouth of the Blackwater Rush on Blackwater Bay; the Red Keep sits on Aegon's High Hill. [awoiaf: King's Landing]
- **The Wall** — ~300 mi long, ~700 ft tall; the Night's Watch built **19 castles** but garrisons only **3** at series start: **Castle Black**, **Eastwatch-by-the-Sea** (east), and the **Shadow Tower** (west). [awoiaf: The Wall; Night's Watch]
- **Beyond the Wall** — the **free folk** ("wildlings"); the **Haunted Forest** (taiga north of the Wall), the **Frostfangs** (mountains, hiding valleys like Thenn), and the far-north **Lands of Always Winter**. [awoiaf: Beyond the Wall; Frostfangs]
- **Key features:** the **Trident** river system (Red, Green, Blue Forks) drains the Riverlands; **the Neck** — a marsh bottleneck guarded by **Moat Cailin**, the strategic gate between North and south; the **Vale** is ringed by the Mountains of the Moon and entered via the **Bloody Gate**; **Dorne** holds the continent's only true desert (Boneway and Prince's Pass are its mountain entries). The **kingsroad** runs ~2,000 mi from Storm's End through King's Landing to the Wall. [awoiaf]
- **Defining environmental feature — the seasons:** irregular, **multi-year** summers and winters of unpredictable length (a season can last from ~2 years to over a decade); legend tells of **the Long Night**, a generation-long winter. GRRM has indicated the cause is **magical**, not astronomical — and unexplained in-world. [reactormag]

### Essos and the other continents

- **Essos** lies east across the Narrow Sea — the **largest** of the three known continents, far larger than Westeros, with no complete map; it spans the Shivering Sea (north) to the Summer Sea (south). Its interior is dominated by the **Dothraki Sea** grassland and, eastward, the **Red Waste**. [awoiaf: Essos]
- **The nine Free Cities** (eight of them former colonies/"daughters" of the Valyrian Freehold; **Braavos** is the exception — never part of the Freehold, founded in secret by escaped slaves): **Braavos, Lorath, Norvos, Qohor, Pentos, Myr, Tyrosh, Lys, Volantis.** Most hug the western coast; **Norvos and Qohor** are inland.
  - **Braavos** — the youngest, the "Secret City," founded in hiding by escaped slaves; guarded by the **Titan**, powered by the **Iron Bank**.
  - **Volantis** — the eldest, "the First Daughter of Valyria," among the most powerful.
- **Valyria & the Doom:** the **Valyrian Freehold** rose on the Valyrian peninsula after its people found dragons in the **Fourteen Flames** (a volcano ring). The **Doom** — a simultaneous eruption of all fourteen — destroyed it, drowning the peninsula into the avoided **Smoking Sea**. ⚠️ **Dating:** roughly **~100 years before Aegon's Conquest**, and the Conquest is ~300 years before the novels — so the Doom is **~400 years before the books' present**. (Bare "102/114 BC" figures count years-*before-Conquest*, not 400.) [awoiaf: Valyrian Freehold; Doom of Valyria]
- **Dothraki Sea & beyond:** a vast grassland between the Free Cities and the **Bone Mountains** (its hard eastern edge). **Vaes Dothrak**, the *only* Dothraki city, sits by the Mother of Mountains and the lake called the Womb of the World; its Eastern Market draws traders from Yi Ti, Asshai, and the Shadow Lands. [awoiaf]
- **Slaver's Bay:** built on the ruins of the **Ghiscari Empire (Old Ghis)** — destroyed by Valyria in antiquity. Its three great slaver cities are **Astapor, Yunkai, and Meereen** (each emblemed by a harpy); Daenerys conquers them. ⚠️ *[show]* The **Bay of Dragons** rename is an HBO outcome (after she leaves Meereen) — in the **books** the region is still **Slaver's Bay** and her war there remains unresolved. [awoiaf: Slaver's Bay]
- **The far east:** **Qarth** sits by the "Jade Gates" linking the Summer and **Jade Seas**; **Yi Ti** (⚠️ in-world legend dates it ~8,000 years, "oldest civilization"); **Asshai-by-the-Shadow** at the edge of the known world, on the **Shadow Lands** peninsula; islands **Leng** and **Ib/Ibben** (Shivering Sea). [awoiaf]
- **Other continents:** **Sothoryos** — third known continent, jungle, largely unexplored (per the worldbook, possibly larger than Essos); **Ulthos** — barely known, far southeast. ⚠️ Whether Ulthos is a continent or a large island is **deliberately ambiguous** in canon. [awoiaf: Sothoryos; Ulthos]

## PART II — POLITICAL STRUCTURES

### The Seven Kingdoms: the feudal order

- **Unification:** **Aegon I Targaryen**, aided by dragons, conquered six of the seven kingdoms in **Aegon's Conquest** (~2 years, ~300 years before the main series); **Dorne** held out. Aegon was crowned by the High Septon and founded a ~3-century dynasty; his coronation became the calendar epoch (years "**Before/After the Conquest**"). [awoiaf: Aegon's Conquest]
- **The Iron Throne** was forged from the swords surrendered by Aegon's *defeated* enemies, melted by **Balerion the Black Dread**'s fire. ⚠️ In the **books** it is described as made from **a thousand** surrendered swords — a deliberately mythologized figure, far more than any literal count. *[show]* The oft-quoted "fewer than 200" is an **HBO prop/continuity** number, not a book-canon correction. Because it holds only the swords of those who **gave battle**, none of the North's or the Vale's are in it — Torrhen Stark and Sharra Arryn submitted without fighting. [awoiaf: Iron Throne]
- **The feudal pyramid:** **King → Great Houses** (Lords Paramount / Wardens) **→ vassal "bannermen" lords → landed knights → smallfolk.** Aegon's first law, the **King's Peace**, requires lesser lords to bring disputes to their **liege**; disputes *between* Great Houses go to the Crown. [awoiaf: Lord Paramount; Laws and justice]
- **The Wardens** — four supreme regional military commanders (each expected to raise ~a quarter of the realm's armies in crisis): **North (Stark), East (Arryn), South (Tyrell), West (Lannister).** "Warden" (military command) and "Lord Paramount" (regional lordship) are *distinct* titles usually held by the same person. [awoiaf]
- **The small council** (advises the monarch): **Hand of the King** (chief deputy — commands armies, drafts law, dispenses justice, presides in the king's absence), **Master of Coin** (treasury), **Master of Laws**, **Master of Ships** (royal fleet), **Master of Whisperers** (spymaster), **Grand Maester** (Citadel's representative), and the **Lord Commander of the Kingsguard.** [awoiaf: Small council]
- **Succession & law:** most of Westeros follows **male-preference primogeniture** (eldest son, then younger sons, then daughters) — customary, not absolutely binding (cf. Viserys I naming Rhaenyra heir → the Dance of the Dragons). [awoiaf: Customs]
- **Dorne is the exception:** it joined **~100 years before the series by marriage, not conquest**, so it kept Rhoynish customs and more autonomy — its ruler is styled **Prince/Princess of Dorne**, and it practices **absolute primogeniture** (eldest child inherits regardless of sex). [awoiaf: Dorne]
- **The Iron Islands' "Old Way":** reaving over farming; **"paying the iron price"** (taking wealth by force) is honorable, the "gold price" (buying) shameful; the High King was historically chosen by **kingsmoot**; House Greyjoy's words — **"We Do Not Sow."** [awoiaf: Old Way; Iron price]

### Cross-cutting institutions (non-hereditary power)

- **The Night's Watch** — a brotherhood sworn **for life** to guard the Wall ("take no wife, hold no lands, father no children"), politically neutral ("takes no part"). Three orders: **rangers, builders, stewards**, under the **Lord Commander**. Once 19 castles and 10,000+ men; now ~3 manned castles and a few hundred men. [awoiaf: Night's Watch]
- **The Faith of the Seven** — dominant religion of the south; **one god, seven aspects**: Father, Smith, Warrior (male); Mother, Maiden, Crone (female); plus **the Stranger** (death). Led by the **High Septon** and the **Most Devout**; its militant arm — the **Faith Militant** ("Swords and Stars") — was **outlawed and warred upon by Maegor I**, then **disbanded/disarmed under Jaehaerys I** (the settlement struck with the High Septon after Maegor's death), and **revived** ~300 years later via the **High Sparrow** and Cersei. Rivals: the **old gods** (weirwoods; the North & free folk), the **Drowned God** (ironborn), and **R'hllor** (mostly Essosi). [awoiaf: Faith of the Seven; Faith Militant]
- **The Maesters & the Citadel** (Oldtown) — scholars/healers/advisers; each wears a **chain** of metal links, one per mastered discipline (Valyrian steel = the higher mysteries/magic, ~1 maester in 100). Governed by the **Conclave** of archmaesters; the **Grand Maester** serves the crown on the small council. [awoiaf: Maesters; Conclave; Grand Maester] → full treatment in `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-The-Maesters-and-the-Citadel-2026-06-02]]`.
- **The Kingsguard** — exactly **seven** white-cloaked knights, sworn for life (no land, wife, or heirs) to protect the king; deeds recorded in the **White Book** (kept in the White Sword Tower, Red Keep); led by a **Lord Commander.** [awoiaf: Kingsguard]
- **The Faceless Men** (Braavos) — assassin-priests of the **Many-Faced God** at the **House of Black and White** (its door one leaf weirwood, one ebony); predate the Doom; can change faces. The recurring Essosi institution that threads through the story. [awoiaf: Faceless Men]

### The Drowned God — the ironborn faith

The religion of the Iron Islands, predating the Andals; it sanctifies (rather than restrains) the ironborn reaving culture — the Drowned God is held to have made the ironborn "to reave, rape, carve out kingdoms, make their names known in fire and blood and song." [awoiaf: Drowned God; Ironborn]

- **Dualistic cosmology.** The **Drowned God** ("He Who Dwells Beneath the Waves") rules the sea from his watery halls; his eternal enemy is the **Storm God**, who dwells in a sky-castle and sends gales to dash ironborn ships on the rocks. The two have warred "for a thousand thousand years." [awoiaf: Drowned God; Storm God]
- **The afterlife** is a submerged Valhalla: a worthy ironman who drowns is called to the Drowned God's halls to feast, drink, and fight for eternity, attended by mermaids ("fish-wives"). When an ironman dies, the Drowned God "had need of a strong oarsman." [awoiaf: Drowned God] ⚠️ The exact "feasting... forever" wording is concept-confirmed but not verbatim-verified.
- **The creed:** call-and-response — *"What is dead may never die — but rises again, harder and stronger."* Spoken at deaths, oaths, and drownings. Its theological root: ⚠️ (*in-world myth*) the Drowned God was himself drowned by the Storm God and **rose again** as lord of the waters. [awoiaf: Drowned God]
- **Drowning rituals — baptism as literal near-death** (read as an inversion of Christian baptism):
  - At birth, a child is dedicated by immersion in seawater (inland, poured over the head): *"Let [name] your servant be born again from the sea, as you were. Bless him with salt, bless him with stone, bless him with steel."*
  - The deeper rite that makes a priest is **actual drowning + resuscitation** (the "kiss of life"); it does **not always succeed** — some are drowned and not revived. [awoiaf: Drowned men; Priest of the Drowned God]
- **The Drowned Men (priesthood)** — wandering holy men in mottled **green/grey/blue** robes over sealskin; they carry **driftwood cudgels** and skins of saltwater, and **never cut their hair/beard** with steel. They perform drownings and last rites, bless ships, and hold enough moral authority to incite rebellion against the southern faith. [awoiaf: Drowned men]
- **Aeron "Damphair" Greyjoy** — brother of Lord Balon and the most prominent Drowned Man; a wastrel remade into a fanatic by his own near-drowning at Fair Isle. In *A Feast for Crows* he convenes the **kingsmoot** after Balon's death (trying, and failing, to keep **Euron** off the Seastone Chair). ⚠️ Major book arc; heavily compressed in the show. [awoiaf: Aeron Greyjoy]
- **The Grey King & Nagga** ⚠️ (*in-world legend, mostly from* The World of Ice & Fire): the legendary first king ruled ~1,000 years, wed a mermaid, and slew the sea dragon **Nagga**; the Drowned God turned her bones to stone, and her **ribs** form the hall on **Old Wyk** where kingsmoots are held. The ironborn throne is the **Seastone Chair** (origin unknown). [awoiaf: Grey King; Nagga; Old Wyk]
- **Tied-in customs:** the **iron price** (wealth taken by force, honored) vs. the **gold price** (bought, shameful); **rock wives** (one freeborn wife) vs. **salt wives** (captured in raids); the **finger dance** (an axe-catching game). Most ironborn scorn the Faith of the Seven; the Andals failed to convert the islands and largely converted to the Drowned God instead. [awoiaf: Iron price; Salt wife; Finger dance; Drowned God]
- **Real-world inspiration:** ⚠️ (*interpretive/secondary*) GRRM has described the ironborn as loosely **Viking/Norse**; the watery halls parallel Valhalla and the drowning rite inverts Christian baptism.

### Essosi polities (non-feudal)

- **The Free Cities** are independent city-states, mostly **oligarchies of magisters / merchant princes** rather than monarchies:
  - **Pentos** — a **council of magisters** holds real power behind a largely ceremonial **Prince**, chosen from ~40 leading families and **sacrificed** in famine or after a lost war (four princes killed in one year ending 209 AC). [awoiaf: Prince of Pentos]
  - **Volantis** — ruled by **three triarchs**, elected annually by *all* freeborn landowners (regardless of sex) but drawn only from **Old Blood** Valyrian-descended families; factions are the **tigers** (aristocratic, pro-conquest) vs. **elephants** (merchant, pro-trade); a triarch's feet may not touch the ground during his term. [awoiaf: Triarch of Volantis]
  - **Norvos** — true power held by the **bearded priests**; **Qohor** — the cult of the Black Goat. [awoiaf: Free Cities]
  - **Braavos** — slavery banned (the **First Law**); ruled by an elected **Sealord** (for life). [awoiaf: Braavos; Sealord]
- **The Iron Bank of Braavos** — a genuine political force: **"The Iron Bank will have its due."** It funds the *enemies* of rulers who default and has toppled lords and princes. [awoiaf: Iron Bank]
- **The Valyrian Freehold** (historical) — pointedly **neither kingdom nor empire**: in principle all freeholders had equal say; in practice an **oligarchy** of ~40 noble families, dominated by the **dragonlords**. House Targaryen, a *minor* dragonlord family, survived the Doom only because Aenar the Exile had moved them to Dragonstone twelve years prior. [awoiaf: Valyrian Freehold; Dragonlord]
- **The Dothraki** — nomadic horse-lords in **khalasars** led by a **khal** (rules by strength); **bloodriders** ("blood of my blood") sworn to die with him; the **dosh khaleen** (widowed crones) at Vaes Dothrak command even khals' respect; a gift-economy with no walls or coin. [awoiaf: Dothraki; Khal; Dosh khaleen]
- **Slaver's Bay** — Ghiscari oligarchies: **Astapor** (the **Good Masters**, who train the eunuch **Unsullied**), **Yunkai** (the **Wise Masters**, bed-slaves), **Meereen** (the **Great Masters**, the largest city). [awoiaf]
- **Qarth** — nominally ruled by the **Pureborn** (the "Enthroned," in the Hall of a Thousand Thrones), contended by three merchant-prince guilds: the **Thirteen**, the **Ancient Guild of Spicers**, and the **Tourmaline Brotherhood.** [awoiaf: Qarth; Pureborn]
