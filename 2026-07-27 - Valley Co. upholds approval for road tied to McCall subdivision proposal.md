An approval of a new access road planned for a 176-home subdivision proposed in McCall was upheld on Monday by Valley County.

The Board of County Commissioners rejected an appeal from neighbors that argued the county could not evaluate the roadway separately from the developments it could eventually serve.

The decision upholds [the Valley County Planning and Zoning Commission’s June decision](https://valleylookout.com/2026/06/12/its-kind-of-a-chicken-egg-thing-valley-co-oks-road-tied-to-potential-mccall-subdivision-proposal/) to approve Stockton Boulevard, a new road that would connect Stockton Drive to The Woodlands subdivision in southeastern McCall.

The application, known as Pine Creek Ranch South, was submitted by Donnelly developer Craig Groves. In addition to Stockton Boulevard, it also creates four residential parcels on 90 acres in the McCall Impact Area.

Stockton Boulevard would provide the secondary access needed for [Woodlands 3, a proposed 176-home subdivision](https://valleylookout.com/2026/03/04/right-location-right-time-pine-creek-ranch-developer-airs-scaled-back-plan/) planned on a separate 68-acre parcel within the city limits. That application has been submitted to the City of McCall, but not yet accepted due to outstanding requirements Groves must meet.

“I can’t develop (Woodlands 3) without a secondary access,” Groves said. “I’m dead in the water — which is exactly what the opponents here in the audience want.”

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

Pine Creek Ranch South creates the roadway needed to develop Woodlands 3, Groves said, while deferring development of the four lots created around Stockton Boulevard until central water and sewer service are available.

“I think it’s a shame to just put more wells and septic systems on this 90 acres,” he said. “That’s why we’re going through this process right now.”

## Neighbors oppose ‘piecemeal’ approach

[The appeal filed by neighboring property owners](https://www.co.valley.id.us/meetingfiles/17108/attachments/4ea0c746095044009eae3354d0fc5670.pdf) argued the county should not approve the road without first knowing the residential development it would ultimately serve.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

“A local government cannot approve an application based upon assumptions that unresolved issues will be addressed later,” Boise attorney Abigail Germaine wrote in a June 22 appeal letter. “The standards established by Valley County Code require present compliance, not future speculation.”

Neighbors echoed those concerns during Monday’s public hearing, citing concerns related to traffic, density, road maintenance, and dust.

“This is a piecemeal development,” said Melissa Daniels, a resident of The Woodlands. “It should not be approved in pieces.”

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1

Pam Wissenbach, who lives along Stockton Drive, questioned whether future development would be compatible with the existing neighborhood. Another resident, Pat Allen, argued the county should require a complete development plan before approving the roadway.

“No one knows what a development lot is. Why aren’t you guys questioning that?” Allen said. “They should be coming in with a plan and tell you exactly what’s happening with those lots.”

“My grandkids, my dog, they’re going to suffer from your decision,” he added.

## County: ‘speculation’ irrelevant to decision

The commissioners, however, emphasized that they are required by state law and county code to evaluate only the application before them — a roadway and four residential lots.

“We absolutely cannot make decisions based off speculative information in the future,” Commission Chair Sherry Maupin said.

P&Z’s approval included conditions addressing future development. If additional subdivisions are proposed on the four newly created parcels, future developers would be required to complete studies identifying necessary roadway and infrastructure improvements.

If Stockton Boulevard connects to Woodlands 3 or another future development on the adjacent 68-acre parcel, a traffic study would be required to evaluate impacts on nearby roads, including Stockton Drive, Sheila Lane, Fox Ridge Lane and Deinhard Lane.

“For me, I feel like all the bases have been covered,” Commissioner Neal Thompson said.

The commissioners also noted that the secondary access would “benefit” existing homeowners in The Woodlands, which currently has only one outlet onto Spring Mountain Boulevard.

![City of McCall - Visiting? 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/57592d17-0c69-4d28-a6e9-c5d629733da1.png)

“There needs to be a secondary access point,” Maupin said. “You get a fire at the front of that subdivision, and you guys are all in big trouble.”

The application for Pine Creek Ranch South follows a previous proposal for the two properties totaling 158 acres that are owned by Groves.

Last fall, Groves’ request to annex the 90-acre parcel into the sewer district serving McCall was denied, effectively ending the original proposal known as Pine Creek Ranch, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2025/10/16/no-deal-sewer-district-rejects-pine-creek-ranch-annexation-request/).
