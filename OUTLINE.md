
# IDAHO EXPERIENCE

"Silver and gold in the sunlight blaze, and romance lies in her name." Since the very beginning, Idaho has been a muse for pioneering writers and poets and artists and promoters singing the praises of our rugged home. "Idaho Experience: Pioneering Artists (wt)" introduces you to a cast of colorful characters who capture the raw beauty of Idaho.

## I. Geological Surveys

As the photographs, drawings, and paintings from four western scientific expeditions arrived back east, the wild landscapes they portrayed astonished people's imaginations. Segment includes imagery of eastern Idaho from Red Rock Pass to the Tetons to Henry's Lake (East Idaho from Utah border along Wyoming border to Montana border into Yellowstone) as well as Shoshone Falls. Despite the length of the text, these three men are told together as one shorter segment.

\- Thomas Moran

\-- Becomes one of the famous American names in painting; expedition photos

\-- Digital copies of paintings in the public domain (Smithsonian, Getty)

\- Timothy O'Sullivan

\-- Expedition survey photographer, compared Shoshone Falls to Niagara Falls

\-- Digital copies of photographs in the public domain (Smithsonian, USGS)

\- William Henry Jackson

\-- Civil War veteran, photographer, business, pro- Native Americans, painter

\-- Digital copies of photographs in the public domain (Smithsonian, USGS)

\-- Digital copies of paintings (300 dpi) available from National Parks Service

## II. Gold Rush

The Oregon Trail and the California Gold Rush brought people out west to the coast... People then came east into the mountains seeking the same things they sought "out west," namely freedom and an opportunity. Charles Ostner was a wanderer and prolific professionally trained artist who chased opportunities across the country. James Castle was a self-taught artist who depicted his home. These two men lived in Garden Valley/Boise but stand as individual segments.

\- Charles Ostner

\-- Painter and illustrator and sculptor: famous George Washington at capitol

\-- Physical works available at Idaho State Archives

\- James Castle

\-- Painter and illustrator and collagist: House site is a museum in Boise

\-- Physical works available at Boise Art Museum

## III. End of the Frontier

As the folks living in burgeoning western towns and cities reminisce about the glory days, people are very self-aware during this era about the disappearance of the frontier. Just like the country, Idaho was maturing and figuring out what it wanted to be when it grows up. These two women work best as individual segments, but it is possibly a fun contrast to emphasize how much Hallock Foote hated living in Idaho/Boise and how much Edwards Green loved it there.

\- Mary Hallock Foote

\-- Writer, illustrator, depicted self as "a Victorian Gentlewoman in the Far West"

\-- Homesite at base of Lucky Peak Dam with Morrison-Knudsen Foundation park

\-- Digital copies of illustrations in the public domain, "Angle of Repose" book

\- Emma Edwards Green

\-- Illustrator and artist who creates the Idaho State Seal, Boisean

\-- Physical works available at Idaho State Archives and some digital copies

## IV. Moving Pictures

Just like the still cameras of the years before, the motion picture cameras of Hollywood also found Idaho to be a great backdrop for the picturesque ideal of the frontier. "Told in the Hills" is a classic wild west pulp fiction flick put to the silver screen, while Nell Shipman's filmography is a standalone authorial voice. These two stories work best as complimentary segments, the same heads talking about two film tales and the man whose obsession kept them around.

\- Told in the Hills (1919)

\-- Early Hollywood era production; Kamiah, Lawyer Canyon, Nez Perce

\- Remastered footage available from Kenworthy Performing Arts Center

\-- Nell Shipman

\- Early Hollywood star who wanted out, studio at Lionhead on Priest Lake

\- Digitized short films available from Boise State University Archives

## V. Contemporary Americana

Even as new technologies change the world, the tried-and-true methods still stick around. These 20th century artists bring us back around to classical painting and traditional fiber arts made in and about Idaho, which even in the modern era still capture the spirit of the west. These three people are separate artists in different geographic areas, best as individual segments, but all three serve as public-facing community artists whose reputation and work helped keep their medium alive.

\- Cowboy Joe Breckenridge

\-- Rodeo cowboy, painter and muralist, notorious for painting as "payment" for beer

\-- Physical works available at Museum of North Idaho (and Enaville, Boville, etc.)

\- Fred Ochi

\-- Painter, sign lettering business, has descendants in IF and the LA art scene

\-- Physical works at Museum of Idaho, digital copies The Art Museum of Eastern Idaho

\- Rose Frank

\-- Cornhusk weaving flat bags, traditional practice influenced by settlement

\-- Learned practice, National Endowment for the Arts 1991 Heritage Fellow

\-- Physical works hopefully at Spaulding park site in archives, waiting to hear back
