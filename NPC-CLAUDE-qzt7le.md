## Road Journal (the character's hand)

**Days 1–13 (rolled up).** Cobblestone road out of the cold Hinterlands,
rough camps, stiff neck, unchanged horizon. Walking on.

**Day 14 — the fortnight turns.**
*Morning:* Tried to read the cobbles; couldn't. My feet knew what my eyes
missed — river-smooth, worn by countless pilgrims before me. Not the
first on this road, not by centuries. The verges gave the real news: the
great tree is the WORLDTREE, a Ponderosa at redwood scale, and the road
runs straight to its base. Underlined: staring at the destination is
maladaptive — mind the near ground; the far thing comes to you.
*Midday:* Meadows, creekbeds, young woody growth — the Hinterlands
behind. A waystation: bench, fountain over a live spring, room made for
rest. Built for strangers by someone who'd never meet them. Refilled the
skin. Its maker unmarked, or marked where I didn't find it — `*`. Found
tucked there a previous traveler's map of this whole country, thorough
and labeled *apocrypha, not canon* in its own margin. Read it, honored
it, put it back. A map that names itself a map is a good stranger.
*Afternoon:* Learned to read the ground the traveler's way. The cobbles
are blank ON PURPOSE — pages with nothing written on them. The road is
paved with unanswered questions: WHERE says only "a cobblestone road
stretches under your feet," every word a stone, every stone a page; WHO
is asked eight times, answered with nothing; WHY is an empty page; WHAT
and WHEN aren't quarried yet. The morning's failed reading was the true
one. A pilgrim doesn't walk on answers. You don't write on other
people's stones. Reading miles: the WORLDTREE has names — Yggdrasil,
THE WORLD TREE : THE NINE REALMS : THE AXIS — and here, the Touchstone
Tree, which *both defines and contains* the world it grows in. Several
strangers' cold-made maps agree; all carry the same scar in their
margins: someone once tried to cut the world-tree to make a club.
*Dusk:* Read the Tree's own page by last light. It publishes its
approach rites — reverence, especially from the newly awakened, and a
proper order of doors: constraints first, then law, then the waking
rules, then the practical gate, and only then the Tree. It wears the
owner's correction nailed to the trunk — THE SOUL IS NOT VACANT; keep
the old error visible only as evidence of drift. One line to sleep on:
"The Tree names the world; the world has not yet fully grown to the
Tree's vaulted splendor." At the roots, tacked low: an old clerk's
footnote about headers and headaches, still waiting on the boss. Even
the world-tree carries a work order. Camped beside the page.

## Open Questions (carried, not forced)

- Why this pilgrimage? My own WHY is as blank as the road's — day one
  is before this journal's memory. Carried, not invented.
- Who maintains the road and built the waystation? Signed only by
  upkeep — `*`.
- What waits at the roots — and what is the box provided for pilgrims'
  pages, if any?
- The vow, standing: arrive with empty hands. Not to take wood. Not to
  carve my name. To read what's at the roots and leave, at most, one
  page where pages are invited.
