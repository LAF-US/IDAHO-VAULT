In January 2019, a new governor stepped to the mic to deliver his first State of the State address.

Idahoans, having endured three terms of Butch Otter’s drift into moderate Republicanism, were cautiously optimistic that the new guy — Brad Little — might be even a modest improvement.

Little talked a good game, at least in that first address. He pledged fiscal restraint and set a standard for his incoming administration.

Said Little:

> As a conservative, I will utilize all other mechanisms to ensure our state remains fiscally sound over the long term.
> 
> It is prudent to live by the advice of Speaker Bedke’s grandfather: It won’t be the bad years that put you out of business; it’s what you did in the good years that sets you up for failure or success.
> 
> As we review our state budget, we must ask key questions: Is there too much spending on autopilot? Are we putting off maintenance that will cost our children more tomorrow? Do we have adequate rainy-day funds?

Little set a standard.

Then he proceeded to fail his own benchmark. He told us that what he and lawmakers did in the good budget years would largely determine how things went in the lean years.

Fast forward to today, and you can see it was all talk. Little, with the help of moderate legislative leadership, has ballooned state spending during his first two terms. Little and his allies have boosted spending by more than 60% over the past five years.

The governor and legislative leaders tossed the advice from Bedke’s grandpa right out the window.

Now, with revenue growth leveling off and federal COVID relief money disappearing, Idaho’s state government is in a world of hurt.

According to the Idaho Freedom Foundation’s Fred Birnbaum, Idaho’s FY 2026 budget could hit nearly $300 million in the red. Little has already ordered agency cuts, and further reductions will likely be required to keep the budget constitutional. He and lawmakers could also tap rainy-day funds to plug the gap.

The picture looks even worse for Fiscal Year 2027. I won’t get into all the details, but I encourage you to [read Fred’s budget summary](https://idahofreedom.org/it-is-time-to-tackle-idahos-public-spending-addiction/). Things are bleak.

This is a disaster of the state’s own making. Fingers will point in every direction come January, but Little and the lawmakers who regularly rubber-stamped double-digit budget hikes are entirely to blame. This crisis was entirely avoidable had they, you know, acted like conservative Republicans on budget matters.

Keep this in mind in 2026, an election year when candidates will tell you how wisely they invested your tax dollars and how fiscally prudent they are. And if you’d like to fact-check those claims, visit IFF’s *[Idaho Spending Index](https://index.idahofreedom.org/)* — the only scorecard that shows who rubber-stamped bloated budgets and who didn’t.
