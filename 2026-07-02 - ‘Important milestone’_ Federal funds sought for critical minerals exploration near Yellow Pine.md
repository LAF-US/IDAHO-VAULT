An Australian mining company conducting exploratory drilling near Yellow Pine is hoping to enlist the help of the federal government.

[Resolution Minerals](https://resolutionminerals.com/) of Adelaide, Australia, recently applied for funding from the U.S. Department of War to fund exploration of Antimony Ridge, one of two key zones in the company’s 23-square-mile Horse Heaven Project.

The amount of funding sought was not specified in a news release, and the company did not respond to Valley Lookout’s request for comment. The funding is related to exploration for tungsten and antimony — both of which are classified as critical minerals by the federal government.

Resolution is currently developing an Antimony Ridge exploration plan to be reviewed by the Boise National Forest, the lead permitting agency for the project.

The Boise Forest previously told Valley County officials that the permitting sought by Resolution would authorize about five years of exploratory drilling and about 250 bore holes, [as Valley Lookout reported](https://valleylookout.com/2026/04/21/stibnite-scale-mining-exploration-proposed-near-yellow-pine/).

Forest officials described the proposal as comparable in scale to exploration completed more than a decade ago for Perpetua Resources’ Stibnite mine, which shares its western boundary with the Horse Heaven project. A public comment period on the proposal is expected later this year once it is submitted for environmental review.

No mining has been authorized within the Horse Heaven Project zone so far. Resolution would first need to submit a mining plan for environmental review, which would likely not happen until after exploration is complete.

## Defense consortium membership

Resolution’s application for federal funding coincides with the company’s recent admission to the [U.S. Defense Industrial Base Consortium](https://www.dibconsortium.org/), a group of more than 1,800 companies positioned to assist with the national defense supply chain.

Resolution U.S. Operations CEO Craig Lindsay called the company’s membership in the consortium an “important milestone” in its critical minerals strategy.

“Through DIBC membership, we gain access to a network of government agencies, industry participants, and potential funding opportunities that can support the advancement of our projects,” Lindsay said.

Among the companies in the consortium is Perpetua, which is currently starting a three-year construction phase to build the Stibnite mine after it was approved last year by the Payette National Forest.

Perpetua has received [more than $80 million in federal funding for its Stibnite project](https://valleylookout.com/2025/05/28/ground-to-round-perpetua-gets-another-6-9m-from-u-s-military/), including $59 million through the Department of War. However, the company’s funding was not awarded through the consortium, according to McKinsey Lyon, a Perpetua spokesperson.

“Since our awards, the Industrial Base Policy office developed (the consortium) to process applications for industrial base needs,” Lyon said.

Lyon said membership in the consortium is “not a complicated process.”

“You just pay a fee and join as a member,” she said.

Overall, the consortium has awarded more than $400 million in funding since 2024. In March, it awarded [$27 million to U.S. Antimony Corp.](https://www.war.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/4421101/department-of-war-invests-27m-for-the-domestic-excavation-extraction-processing/) to improve the company’s antimony smelter facility in Thompson Falls, Montana.

Stibnite is expected to produce some 100 million pounds of antimony, while Resolution’s [early exploration indicates significant antimony deposits](https://resolutionminerals.com/investor-centre/high-antimony-recoveries-in-metallurgy-from-antimony-ridge/) within the Horse Heaven Project.

Earlier this year, Resolution’s Antimony Ridge exploration project was designated by the Trump administration as a [FAST-41 project](https://www.permitting.gov/projects/title-41-fixing-americas-surface-transportation-act-fast-41) to expedite permitting and reviews of the project by federal agencies.
