The Idaho Commission on Hispanic Affairs, a woke agency long targeted by conservative activists, will live on for another year—thanks to Idaho House Speaker Mike Moyle.

### Key takeaways:

- The House State Affairs Committee killed a bill Thursday that would have ended the Hispanic Commission.
- On that vote, **five Republican lawmakers joined two Democrats** to block the bill. The measure **died on a tie vote**, 7-7.
- Last week, Speaker Mike Moyle reworked the committee, removing two lawmakers, including one staunch conservative. He replaced them with a moderate lawmaker from Eastern Idaho. That Moyle-appointed lawmaker voted to protect the woke commission.

### Further context:

For years, conservative activists and groups like the Idaho Freedom Foundation have called for the end of the Idaho Commission on Hispanic Affairs.

Critics argue the agency is unnecessary and has pushed woke policies wherever it can.

On Thursday, Rep. Heather Scott attempted to end the agency through legislation. She brought a bill to the House State Affairs Committee that would repeal the commission’s authorizing code section.

It was not to be.

The committee blocked the bill’s introduction on a 7-7 tie vote. On that vote, **five Republicans voted with two Democrats** to stop the bill before it could even advance.

One of those Republicans was freshman Rep. Erin Bingham of Idaho Falls, who replaced Wendy Horman in the Statehouse.

Bingham was appointed to the House State Affairs Committee last week by Speaker Mike Moyle. The speaker—who has broad authority to reshape committees as he sees fit—removed Reps. Clint Hostetler and Vito Barbieri and replaced them with Bingham.

That maneuver saved the Hispanic Commission.

Had Hostetler, a staunch conservative and member of the Gang of 8 Caucus, retained his seat on the panel, the vote would have been **8-6 in favor of introduction**.

The bill’s defeat is particularly tough for Scott, who aligned herself with Moyle last year after months of fighting the speaker’s agenda.

The defeat is also an early embarrassment for a cadre of Moyle’s social media loyalists, including Sen. Brian Lenney, who promised repeal this year.
