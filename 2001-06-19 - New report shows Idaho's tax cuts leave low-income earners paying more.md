BOISE, Idaho — A [new report](https://idahofiscal.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/5-14-How-Federal-and-State-Tax-Cuts-Shift-Costs-onto-Idaho-Families-and-Communities.pdf) from the [Idaho Center for Fiscal Policy](https://idahofiscal.org/) finds that [federal and state tax cuts](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/capitol-watch/2026-legislative-session-sharp-partisan-divides-over-tax-cuts-medicaid-reductions/277-cb6548e8-ee63-4509-bc00-7b33a79610d0) have reduced public revenue in Idaho while shifting more costs onto families and local governments.

The report found that 73% of the overall tax benefits went to the top 20% of income earners.

"Idaho's tax cuts are currently regressive, which means that those with the lowest incomes actually pay more on taxes as a percentage of their income that those with the highest incomes, so that where the tax code is not very fair where everyone is not paying a similar percentage of their income on taxes," Roberts said. "That can show up in some of these findings where those with the lowest incomes are paying more than those with the highest. It's not the straight dollar amount; it's the percentage of income that we look at."

Roberts said the report looks at tax changes since 2018, including the federal [Tax Cuts and Jobs Act](https://www.irs.gov/tax-cuts-and-jobs-act) and the [One Big Beautiful Bill Act](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/The-One-Big-Beautiful-Bill-Legislation-for-Historic-Prosperity-and-Deficit-Reduction-1.pdf), along with Idaho's own income tax cuts. She said the public often hears about tax cuts and an increase in take-home pay, but that framing misses the tradeoffs when those cuts are paid for through reduced services.

The report also shows budget cuts have shifted costs to Idaho families through higher property taxes, tuition and fees, and reduces access to health care and found assistance. Roberts said schools had to rely more heavily on supplemental levies because discretionary K-12 funding has not increased for two years, even as costs for utilities, maintenance, supplies, software and insurance have continued to rise.

"The budget cuts that happened this last year, colleges and universities were hit very hard, in the tens of millions of dollars, um, and lost their budgets," Roberts said. "Shortly after the State Board of Education approved tuition and fee increases."

Roberts said the report's goal is to help Idahoans understand how tax policy affects public services and to encourage them to talk with lawmakers about their priorities.

She also said federal changes have shifted more costs to states, including work requirements for some SNAP and Medicaid recipients and the loss of enhanced premium tax credits, which she said caused some families' health insurance premiums to double. Roberts said those federal changes add to the pressure on Idaho after state lawmakers reduced the tax base and approved additional budget cuts.
