The term " **sweater girl** " was made popular in the 1940s and 1950s to describe Hollywood actresses like [Lana Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Turner "Lana Turner"), [Jayne Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayne_Mansfield "Jayne Mansfield"), and [Jane Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Russell "Jane Russell"), who adopted the popular fashion of wearing tight, form-fitting [sweaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweater "Sweater") that emphasized the woman's [bustline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bustline "Bustline").[^1] [^2] The sweater girl trend was not confined to Hollywood and was viewed with alarm by some. In 1949 a [Pittsburgh police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_police "Pittsburgh police") superintendent even singled out the sweater girl as a symptom of the moral decline of postwar youth:

> "Women walk the streets, their curves accentuated by their dresses," Superintendent of Police Harvey J. Scott said. "But our real problem is with [bobby soxers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_soxer_\(music\) "Bobby soxer (music)"). They are the sweater girls—just kids showing off their curves and apparently liking it. What kind of mothers and wives are they going to be?" [^3]

## Fashion influence

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/41/Patti_Page_1955.JPG/250px-Patti_Page_1955.JPG)

Patti Page, 1955.

New, soft fabrics like [cashmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashmere_wool "Cashmere wool") and [angora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angora_wool "Angora wool") were being used to make sweaters. The conical shape bra, sometimes called a [bullet bra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_bra "Bullet bra"), raises and separates the breasts. In its original form, the look was often tied to the promotion of new [bra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brassiere "Brassiere") technology.

Interest in the bullet bra revived after [Madonna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_\(entertainer\) "Madonna (entertainer)") wore a cone bra during her 1990 [Blond Ambition World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blond_Ambition_World_Tour "Blond Ambition World Tour").[^4] The bra was designed by [Jean Paul Gaultier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Paul_Gaultier "Jean Paul Gaultier") who was inspired by the vintage Perma-Lift bullet bra of the 1940s. By that time, the style was regarded as erotic and provocative. The bullet bra has also become popular with [burlesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burlesque "Burlesque") and [rockabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockabilly "Rockabilly") enthusiasts, both of which draw inspiration from the 1950s.

## Popular culture

[Lana Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lana_Turner "Lana Turner") 's appearance in the 1937 film *[They Won't Forget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Won't_Forget "They Won't Forget")* in a tight-fitting knit top is sometimes considered the first case of the "sweater girl", as Hollywood publicists sought for a catchy phrase to describe the impact she made on the screen. Movie magazines nicknamed her "The Sweater Girl," just as [Ann Sheridan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Sheridan "Ann Sheridan") was "The Oomph Girl," [Dorothy Lamour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorothy_Lamour "Dorothy Lamour") "The Sarong Girl," and [Clara Bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Bow "Clara Bow") "The [It girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It_girl "It girl")."

*[Sweater Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweater_Girl_\(film\) "Sweater Girl (film)")* is the name of a 1942 film written by [Robert Blees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Blees "Robert Blees") and [Beulah Marie Dix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beulah_Marie_Dix "Beulah Marie Dix"), directed by [William Clemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Clemens_\(film_director\) "William Clemens (film director)") and starring [Eddie Bracken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddie_Bracken "Eddie Bracken"), [June Preisser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Preisser "June Preisser"), [Phillip Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phillip_Terry "Phillip Terry"), and [Betty Jane Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betty_Jane_Rhodes "Betty Jane Rhodes").[^5]

From the 1944 *[Army–Navy Screen Magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army%E2%80%93Navy_Screen_Magazine "Army–Navy Screen Magazine")* No. 20, a one-reel short that showed portions of a special *[Armed Forces Radio Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_Radio_Network "Armed Forces Radio Network")* recording session, [Bob Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope "Bob Hope") introduces "sweater girl" [Judy Garland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judy_Garland "Judy Garland"). Before she sings " [Over the Rainbow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_the_Rainbow "Over the Rainbow") ", they perform a short comic sketch, Garland asking why men are so crazy about sweater girls. Hope says he does not know and wisecracks, "That's one mystery I'd like to unravel".[^6]

In September 1968 an obscure clerical worker named [Francine Gottfried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francine_Gottfried "Francine Gottfried") briefly attained international celebrity as "Wall Street's Sweater Girl" as large crowds of gawking men and newspaper reporters awaited her arrival at the [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street "Wall Street") subway stop each morning and mobbed her on her way to work.[^7]
