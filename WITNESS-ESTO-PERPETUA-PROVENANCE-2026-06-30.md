## References (the lineage the motto passed through)
- **Paolo Sarpi** ("Fra Paolo"), Venetian theologian/mathematician (1552–1623) — *reputedly* his dying words in 1623, spoken of **the Republic of Venice**: "mayest thou endure forever." (Attribution is traditional — "reportedly.")
- **Henry Grattan** — repeated *esto perpetua* on Irish legislative independence, 1782.
- **National Grange of the Patrons of Husbandry** — adopted it as motto; constituted Washington D.C., Dec 4, 1867.
- **Jefferson Davis**, *Rise and Fall of the Confederate Government* (1881) — closing words, in a wish it be "written on the arch of the Union."
- **Emma Edwards Green** — placed it on the Great Seal of Idaho she designed (1890/91); the only woman to design a U.S. state seal. Her own gloss: "It is perpetuated" / "It is forever"; the words "breathe the prayer that the bounty and blessing of this land may forever benefit its people."
- **Idaho statute** — Great Seal adopted by the first Idaho Legislature, approved Mar 14, 1891 (Session Laws 1890–91, pp. 215–216; Idaho Code §59-1005).

## Homages (later bearers who took up the phrase)
Per Wikipedia's adopters list: HMS *Tireless* (Royal Navy, Trafalgar-class submarine); the National Grange; Pirates RFC (Dunedin, NZ, 1882); Sigma Phi Society; Phi Chi (Young Harris College, 1891); Chatham Hall (VA — "She will live forever"); three Thomian schools (Sri Lanka — "Be Thou Forever"); Springs Boys' (1940) & Girls' (1959) High Schools (South Africa); Winyah Indigo Society (Georgetown SC, inc. 1754); Grand Lodge of British Freemasons in Germany; Carron Phoenix (Scotland); Old Sydneians' Union (Sydney Grammar); the Union Arch over Cabin John Creek (MD).

## Seeds (the transmission, read through Missionaria Protectiva)
The phrase travels as planted-and-found doctrine: **Venice/Sarpi (1623) → wide circulation (Grattan 1782; the Grange 1867; Davis 1881) → Emma Edwards Green's Idaho seal (1891) → this Vault.** Per the Idaho State Historical Society, Idaho **likely** took it not from Venice directly but from its nearer re-soundings (the Grange and/or Davis): "perhaps the motto was derived for Idaho from one or both of these sources." Each hop is a register; the perpetuity is carried by transmission, not by any single speaker persisting.

## Records (the durable carriers)
- ISHS Reference Series #134 — the state's own record of the motto's history and grammar.
- Idaho Session Laws 1890–91, pp. 215–216 / Idaho Code §59-1005 (Great Seal); §46-801 (flag).
- James H. Hawley, *History of Idaho: The Gem of the Mountains* (1920) — records Emma Edwards Green's gloss.

## Grammar (per ISHS #134 — confirming Logan's parse)
*esto* = "the emphatic form of the imperative of the Latin substantive verb *esse* (to be), as contrasted with the more frequently used ordinary imperative *es*"; it "enjoins the subject to be perpetual" (cf. the Scout motto "Be Prepared"). An **imperative**, not an optative wish. Both words carry dual readings, held open: *esto* (2nd "be thou" / 3rd "let her be"); *perpetua* (feminine singular / neuter plural).

## Interpretation (mine — marked as reading, not fact; offered for Logan's correction)
The motto enacts its own meaning: a decree of perpetuity that has endured only by **transmission across turning-over voices** — Sarpi died; the words were re-spoken register to register; the perpetuity lives in the record and the re-inscription, never in one speaker persisting. It is the Missionaria Protectiva in four words; and the register it descends from is a **sovereign republic's** self-injunction (Venice) — the older bloodline of "persistent portable sovereign identity."
