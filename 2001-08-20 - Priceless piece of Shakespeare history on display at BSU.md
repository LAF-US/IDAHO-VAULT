This year marks 400 years since the death of William Shakespeare. You can find his poems and plays still being performed today at the Idaho Shakespeare Festival. However, what if those poems and plays had died with Shakespeare 400 years ago? They almost did, had it not been for a single book: the 1623 First Folio.

A book that's now being displayed at the Yanke Family Research Park in Boise.

"I have never seen it because typically you can't see them," Idaho Shakespeare Festival Director Mark Hofflund said.

The First Folio is being loaned to Boise State University, for the next 30 days, from the Folger Shakespeare Library in Washington D.C.

"We thought we had a responsibility actually to make them available, make his works available to as many people as we could," Folger Shakespeare Librarian Daniel DeSimone said.

The book was compiled by John Heminge and Henry Condell, two actors that were friends of William Shakespeare, back in 1623, seven years after Shakespeare's death.

"It includes 36 of his plays, 18 of which would have been lost because there was no printed edition has been located," DeSimone said.

Some of those plays include Julius Caesar, As You Like It, and Macbeth. There are only 235 known copies of the book in the world, which is why it's always under temperatures, humidity, and light control.

"It took 2 hours for him to position it exactly properly. The cradle that the book is sitting in is customized made for this particular book to be opened to this page, to the 'to be or not to be' speech," Albertsons Library Archivist Gwyn Hervochon.

It's a book that reminds many of us about what else could be out there.

"How many things have we lost that we have no clue that we've lost simply because they weren't published in mass quantities, like the First Folio," Bob Pownall said, a visitor of the exhibit.

The First Folio will be on display seven days a week in the Arts and Humanities Institute Gallery at the Yanke Family Research Park until September 21st.

It's located at 220 Parkcenter Boulevard in Boise.
