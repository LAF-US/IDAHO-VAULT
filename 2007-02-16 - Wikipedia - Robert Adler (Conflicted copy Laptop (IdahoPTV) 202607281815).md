**Robert Adler** (December 4, 1913 – February 15, 2007) was an [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria "Austria") -American inventor who held numerous [patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent"). He worked for [Zenith Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith_Electronics "Zenith Electronics"), retiring as the company's Vice President and Director of Research. His work included developing early sound-based remote controls for televisions, which were the standard for 25 years until replaced by infrared (IR) remotes that could transmit more complex commands.

## Achievements

Adler was born in [Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna "Vienna") in 1913, the son of Jenny (née Herzmark), a doctor, and Max Adler, a social theorist.[^2] [^3] [^4] He earned a Ph.D. in [Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics "Physics") from the [University of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Vienna "University of Vienna") in 1937. Following [Austria's annexation by Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria's_annexation_by_Nazi_Germany "Austria's annexation by Nazi Germany") in 1938, Adler, who was [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew "Jew"), left the country. He traveled first to [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium "Belgium"), then to [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England "England"), where he acted on the advice of friends, who recommended that he emigrate to the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States "United States").[^5] After emigrating to the United States, he began working at [Zenith Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith_Electronics "Zenith Electronics") in the research division in 1941. In his lifetime, Adler was granted more than 180 US [patents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent "Patent").[^1]

## Contributions to the remote control

The invention Adler is best known for is the wireless [remote control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_control "Remote control") for [televisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television "Television"). While not the first remote control, its underlying [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology "Technology") was a vast improvement over previous remote control systems.

The " [Zenith Flash-Matic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash-Matic "Flash-Matic") " remote control, invented by [Eugene Polley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugene_Polley "Eugene Polley"), another [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer "Engineer") at [Zenith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenith_Electronics "Zenith Electronics"), was the first wireless remote control, replacing the signal cable–based remote control devices, which never were a success. The Flash-Matic used directional [flashlight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashlight "Flashlight") in the transmitter device, and photocells in the television set itself. One of the major shortcomings of this technology was that if the television set was exposed to direct sunlight, it could inadvertently trigger one of the remote control functions. The company president sent the engineers back to the drawing board to come up with a better solution.

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/46/Zenith_Space_Commander_600.jpg/250px-Zenith_Space_Commander_600.jpg)

A Zenith Space Commander 600 remote control.

A system based on [radio waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_waves "Radio waves") was briefly considered but rejected because the signals could easily travel through walls and could inadvertently change the channel on a neighbor's television. Furthermore, the marketing people at Zenith desired a remote control which did not require batteries, as it was perceived at the time that if the battery died, the customer might think something was wrong with the television set itself.

Adler's solution was to use sound waves to transmit signals to the TV. The first remote control he developed, the "Space Commander", used [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum "Aluminum") rods, analogous to [tuning forks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuning_fork "Tuning fork"), struck by hammers toggled by the buttons on the device, to produce high-frequency tones that would be interpreted to control functions on the television set.

In the 1960s, Adler modified the remote control to use [ultrasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound "Ultrasound") signals, a technology which went on to be used in television sets manufactured for the next 25 years, until replaced by infrared systems which could transmit more complex commands but required batteries to run.

## Professional accomplishments

By the time of his retirement from Zenith, officially in 1982, Adler was the company's Vice President and Director of Research. He remained a technical advisor to Zenith until 1999. In 1980, Adler was awarded the [Edison Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_Medal "Edison Medal"). In 1997, Adler and Polley were jointly awarded an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award "Emmy Award") by the [National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academy_of_Television_Arts_and_Sciences "National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences"). Adler's last patent application was filed on October 6, 2006, for work on [touchscreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchscreen "Touchscreen") technology.[^6] [^1]

## Death

Robert Adler died in a [Boise, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boise,_Idaho "Boise, Idaho"), [nursing home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursing_home "Nursing home") of [heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_failure "Heart failure") at age 93.[^7] [^8]
