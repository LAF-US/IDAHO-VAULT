**Isabel Brown** (born May 27, 1997) is an American right-wing [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative "Conservative") political activist, media personality, political commentator, and author. She hosts *The Isabel Brown Show* with [Matt Fradd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Fradd "Matt Fradd") on [The Daily Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Wire "The Daily Wire"). As of 2026, she is a [Turning Point USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Point_USA "Turning Point USA") spokesperson.

## Early life and education

Brown was born and grew up in [Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado "Colorado") and worked at the [United States Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") and the [White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House "White House").[^1] Brown earned her [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") in biomedical sciences from [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University "Colorado State University"); she later got her [Master of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Science "Master of Science") in biomedical sciences policy and advocacy from [Georgetown University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown_University "Georgetown University").[^2] [^3]

## Career

### Activism

Brown started her career as a college student through politics and student activism [^4] on her college campus at [Colorado State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_University "Colorado State University").[^5] [^6] [^7] While at college, Brown became involved with the conservative nonprofit organization [Turning Point USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_Point_USA "Turning Point USA").[^8]

From this involvement, she participated in and focused on campus events, political debates, and discussions and issues in America such as free speech, education policy, and ideological diversity.[^4] [^9] [^10] [^11]

Her role in student activism earned her early visibility within conservative [Gen-Z](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen-Z "Gen-Z") communities as she took part in organizing and speaking at politically related events, a lot of the time debating with Gen-Zs, which helped her recognition beyond just her university environment.[^12] [^13]

In September of 2025, days after the [assassination of Charlie Kirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Charlie_Kirk "Assassination of Charlie Kirk"), Brown spoke on the topic of the future of Kirk's company, Turning Point USA; she stated that she "vows to bring Kirk's TPUSA to a generation".[^14]

Brown recorded a video addressing Kirk's death, she said in the video that she believes it was "a far-right activist and organiser", also saying that it was "the hardest day of my life", she also praised Kirk as a "mentor and friend of the past eight years".[^15]

### Media

In later years, Brown transitioned into media commentary, rooting from her activism, which led to appearances and interviews with news companies, such as being one of the main guests on the [CNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN "CNN") election night livecast.[^3] [^16] [^17]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/53/Isabel_Brown_%2852586071637%29.jpg/250px-Isabel_Brown_%2852586071637%29.jpg)

Brown speaking with attendees at the 2022 AmericaFest at the Phoenix Convention Center in Phoenix, Arizona.

During this period, she began producing her own commentary content on social media platforms, focusing on cultural and political topics. Her content discusses the same problems that she focuses on in her activism career.[^7]

As her online following grew, Brown began appearing more frequently in other conservative content. This included guest appearances, panel discussions, and collaborations with media organizations, such as [The Daily Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Wire "The Daily Wire").[^18] [^19]

In 2023, Brown started *The Isabel Brown Show*, hosted by herself and Australian-American author, podcaster, and [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") motivational speaker [Matt Fradd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Fradd "Matt Fradd"); the show initially grew on independent platforms and later relaunched on *The Daily Wire*.[^20] [^21]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/5/58/Isabel_Brown_%2852251739046%29.jpg/250px-Isabel_Brown_%2852251739046%29.jpg)

Brown speaking with attendees at the 2022 Student Action Summit at the Tampa Convention Center in Tampa, Florida.

In March 2026, *[The View](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_View "The View")*, an American daytime talk show, criticized Brown for her comments made during an appearance at the Conservative Political Action Conference (CPAC) encouraging young Americans to marry and have children early in life.[^22] A clip of Brown saying people should have “more kids than they can afford before they think they’re ready” got backlash online.[^23] [^24]

The clip was talked about by co-hosts [Whoopi Goldberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whoopi_Goldberg "Whoopi Goldberg") and [Ana Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Navarro "Ana Navarro"), who criticized Brown's beliefs as irresponsible and dismissive of financial problems facing most families.[^25] Brown later said that her comments were taken out of context and that she was promoting family life and parenthood.[^26] [^27] In April, 2026, [Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Platforms "Meta Platforms") threw a "Save the West" party for Brown to celebrate her milestone on their platforms.[^21]

## Political views

Brown is a conservative right-wing political activist and a pro-family advocate. In May of 2025 she spoke at a university about "the feminist lie" and said she believes women can make lifestyle choices without long-term consequences.[^16]

## Personal life

Brown has been married to Brock Belcher since June 2024, with whom she has a child.[^1] [^28]
