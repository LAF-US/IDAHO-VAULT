Boise-based Albertsons is running a trial on a new tracking tag in some local stores, but the company says it isn’t tied to some of its other tracking initiatives.

The company has placed small white radio frequency identifier (RFID) tags on bakery items in some stores. The tags are printed with the Albertsons logo, the name of the product, how much it sells for and a “sell thru” date. The tags also indicate they include an RFID device.

Albertsons isn’t new to using the small tracking devices. In 2004, it [rolled out](https://progressivegrocer.com/albertsons-launches-rfid-technology-program) an RFID program to track products through its supply chain, with the tags placed on pallets and cases — but not individual products. In 2007, sister chain Safeway also [adopted](https://www.rfidjournal.com/news/safeway-tracks-shipments-to-alaska-stores-dcs/80070/) RFID technology in its supply chain. In the mid-2010s, Albertsons acquired Safeway and [largely moved to its technology platform, as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2016/8/22/albertsons-is-becoming-safeway-but-its-good-for-boise/).

But this appears to be the first time Albertsons has placed tracking tags on individual items.

“We are currently testing RFID tags on bakery items in a small number of Albertsons stores in Boise,” Albertsons Companies Senior Director of External Corporate Communications Sarah Holland said. “We are using these tags to track inventory and product expiration dates.”

Albertsons has been adopting a large number of new technology initiatives in its stores.

![Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/6f251bf1-00e6-48d7-8365-570820fe299b.gif)

Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow

As [BoiseDev reported earlier this year](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/02/02/albertons-tracking/), it is adding tracking devices on carts and baskets at hundreds of stores around the country. The company says it is using the trackers to allow it to serve customized ads to customers, though it claims it is not using the devices to track individual shopper behavior. The technology provider says the trackers can be “connected to loyalty and transaction data,” but Albertsons said it is not currently using that capability.

Albertsons has also [worked to roll out digital shelf tags,](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/08/04/albertsons-electronic-shelf-labels/) but stresses it is not using them to change prices dynamically.

The new RFID tags on bakery items, Holland said, only work when paired with an employee’s reader to help with inventory tasks.

“The tags in no way track customer data or purchases,” Holland said. “We do not have plans to expand the RFID tags to other products or stores at this time.”
