TWIN FALLS, Idaho (KMVT/KSVT) — Twin Falls County commissioners have terminated their contract with Pillar Power Productions after a crack was discovered in the Centennial Park boat launch, halting all activity at the ramp.

[**RELATED: Crack closes Centennial boat ramp in Twin Falls County**](https://www.kmvt.com/2026/07/31/crack-closes-centennial-boat-ramp-twin-falls-county/ "https://www.kmvt.com/2026/07/31/crack-closes-centennial-boat-ramp-twin-falls-county/")

Pillar Power Productions had used the Centennial Park access point to transport loads for a hydroelectric facility upgrade. The closure of the ramp directly impacted the company’s operations under the contract.

Commissioners met with the owners of Pillar Power Productions as part of an agreement between the two parties. The meeting gave the company an opportunity to present its perspective before commissioners decided next steps.

[**RELATED: Pillar Power Productions begins equipment launch to hydroelectric facility**](https://www.kmvt.com/2026/05/16/pillar-power-productions-begins-equipment-launch-hydroelectric-facility/ "https://www.kmvt.com/2026/05/16/pillar-power-productions-begins-equipment-launch-hydroelectric-facility/")

After reviewing engineering documents presented at the meeting, commissioners said they still do not know what caused the crack in the ramp. The focus, they said, is getting the launch repaired and reopened safely.

Twin Falls County Commissioner Rocky Matthews said the ramp will remain closed until officials can confirm it is safe for use.

“Currently there is a crack and it needs to be addressed, which means until we can make it safe for the public of any kind — public or commercial — we can’t let anybody launch,” Matthews said.

Commissioner Matthews said the county is working with multiple departments to determine the extent of the repairs and how they will be funded. He said fall would be the best opportunity to complete the work and expressed hope that repairs would be finished before next year’s boating season.

The ramp will remain closed until officials can at least make it safe for lightweight recreational boats.
