# Audrey D. Redheart, 81, Spalding

Audrey D. Redheart, a longtime Clearwater River area resident, died Thursday, Aug. 29, 2002, at Sacred Heart Medical Center in Spokane. She was 81.

Audrey was a well-respected artist and traditional teacher in her own right and as a tribal elder. She displayed unique, finely contoured forms of Nez Perce traditional weaving.

She was an apprentice to Rose Frank to learn the art of cornhusk weaving through the [[Idaho]] Arts Commission in the early 1970s. Since then she taught many who were interested in learning the art.

Audrey had well-conditioned techniques, which had been accomplished through cornhusk weaving, bead work and other culture items and in the making of tepees.

She was a member of the Lapwai Butterfly Club, sharing her knowledge and helping others learn the basics of the art of beadwork and cornhusk weaving. She will surely be missed.

During the 1970s, she was active in bringing these talents to young Nez Perce women. Now in our present decade, Audrey has shared, appeared and worked hard at what she reflects as an artist.

She was born July 16, 1921, in Lenore to Alexius Pinkham and Annette Blackeagle. She attended Orofino schools and graduated from Lapwai High School. Audrey helped her mother with Root Feast ceremonies in Lapwai and Pendleton, Ore. She and her mother would be the first to arrive to prepare for serving the people and the last to leave, cleaning the kitchen and building.

It was important for Audrey to travel to the Pendleton Roundup every September to assist her mother in setting up camp and participating in all the activities. After her mother's passing, she and her sister, Loretta continued to set up camp.

During World War II, from 1941 to 1942, she worked as a riveter for Boeing. Her husband, Aaron Gould was killed while at war.

She married Frank Weaskus Sr. and he later passed away.

With her father and stepmother, Elsie, she participated in the Northwest Folklife Festival in 1972 through 1974. She assisted in setting up cultural demonstrations and helped with teaching the dances and songs that were performed during the festival.

Audrey married Jesse Redheart March 3, 1955, in a traditional marriage trade. Their marriage was recognized by common law.

Audrey traveled with Jesse to many appaloosa horse shows to help him participate in the costume classes, in which he was undefeated, tying for first place only once. She helped complete his regalia with assistance from her mother. She was a great help with taking care of about 20 head of horses, especially when some of the horses would get out.

Audrey performed public demonstrations through the local schools at their request.

She worked at the Nez Perce National Park in Spalding as a cultural demonstrator starting in June 1981 and retiring in 1998. Audrey was a major planner and implementer of the Cultural Days.

Survivors include her husband, Jesse at their home in Spalding; four sons, Lyle P. Gould of Lenore, Frank W. Weaskus of Spalding, James D. Redheart of Lapwai and Jason B. Redheart of Salem; Ore.; two daughters, Yvonne A. Nomee of Plummer and Jessica L. Redheart of Lewiston; four brothers, Alexius M. Pinkham Jr. of Anchorage, Alfred Pinkam and Allen V. Pinkham, both of Lenore, and Alvin Pinkham Sr. of Spalding; one sister, Loretta Alexander of Pendleton; 20 grandchildren; 20 great-grandchildren; and numerous nieces and nephews.

She was preceded in death by her mother, Annette Burke; father, Alexius Pinkham Sr.; brother, Albert Pinkham Sr.; sisters, Prisilla Pinkham and Bernadine Greene; two sons, Vonley Weaskus and Vernon Dean Redheart; granddaughter, Cora Holt; and great-granddaughter, Audrey Ann Peters.

The dressing ceremony and memorial service were Friday. The sunrise service is at 6 a.m. today at the family home with burial at the Red Bear Cemetery in Lenore.
