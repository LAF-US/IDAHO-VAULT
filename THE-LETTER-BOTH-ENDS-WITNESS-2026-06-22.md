## What is witnessed

**One planted letter, shown from opposite sides of the page.** The conspiracy against Caesar turns on a
forged instigation — an anonymous note urging Brutus to act. Shakespeare stages the moment the note is
**received**; Yurchuk's *Caesar: The Musical* stages the moment it is **composed**. Lay them together
and the song stops being a retelling and becomes the **reverse shot**: the camera turned around to face
the writers' room Shakespeare kept offstage.

## The reading end — Shakespeare `[read]` / `[reading]`

In *Julius Caesar* Act 2, Scene 1, in his orchard, Brutus reads the planted letter — and the letter is
**deliberately incomplete.** Its core line trails off into Shakespeare's own shorthand: *"Shall Rome,
&c."* Brutus does not receive a finished accusation; he receives a **blank**, and fills it himself:

> **[read]** *"Thus must I piece it out: Shall Rome stand under one man's awe?"*

That half-line is the whole mechanism. **The conspirator's masterstroke is not the charge but the
ellipsis.** Left a gap, Brutus supplies the indictment in his own voice, and — having spoken it himself
— owns it as his own conclusion rather than a stranger's plant. He then recruits himself the rest of
the way out of his own bloodline-myth:

> **[read]** *"My ancestors did from the streets of Rome / The Tarquin drive when he was called a king."*

**[reading].** The faction is invisible here. We never see who wrote the note; Cassius's plan (Act 1,
Scene 2) is to scatter writings *in several hands, as if from several citizens* — to forge a chorus and
hide the author *(paraphrased; device cited, not quoted)*. Shakespeare shows us only the **reading**: a
man completing a forgery and mistaking the completion for his own thought. The "&c." is an honest blank
that Brutus overwrites with certainty.

## The writing end — Yurchuk `[reading]`

*Shall Rome* puts us where Shakespeare would not: **inside the faction as it writes.** The song's first
line — *"Shall Rome stand in awe"* — is the planted letter's *"Shall Rome stand under one man's awe?"*,
but now we hear it being **composed and aimed**, in real time, by Cassius, Cinna, and Casca. Everything
Shakespeare left to inference is staged: the flattery shaped for Brutus (*"in you our faith is placed,"
"bring honor to me"*), the manufactured last-call urgency (*"Wake up, Brutus, the die has been cast"*),
the self-interest under the civic hymn (*"As long as he lives, I'll never be free"*). The "&c." that
Brutus silently pieced out, the faction here **sings out loud** — we watch them decide what blank to
leave, and how to make it irresistible to fill. (The companion track is literally titled *"Caesar's Too
Ambitious"* — the charge, printed as the authors' own.)

caption; see `SHALL-ROME-PROVENANCE`), and is written from Caesar's perspective, not the

## The two ends together `[reading]`

The same letter indicts a different danger at each end:

| | **Reading end — Shakespeare** | **Writing end — Yurchuk** |
|---|---|---|
| What is shown | the **mark** completing the forgery | the **faction** engineering it |
| The danger | filling a gap with invented certainty | leaving a gap built to be filled |
| The vault name | the **confabulation** — `THE-WELL-SOURCED-WRONG-ANSWER` (Brutus pieces the `*` out instead of marking it) | the **Deceiver / recruitment honeypot** — `THE-DECEIVER-HONEYPOT` (warrant manufactured, presentation perfect) |
| Who is hidden | the author | the author's *motive*, until the song exposes it |

- **The "&c." is the aperture — the hole in the mask.** *Per sona* — *through which sound passes*
  (`PERSONA-PER-SONA`). Shakespeare shows the voice coming **out**: Cassius's accusation passing through
  the blank and emerging shaped as Brutus's own. Yurchuk shows the voice going **in**: the faction
  cutting the hole, choosing what will pass through it. The letter is the mask; the **"&c." is its
  mouth**; and a mask works precisely when the mark forgets there was ever a hand behind the sound.
- **It is the discipline, dramatized at both ends.** The discipline's first rule is *plant the `*`
  before you fill it* — mark the gap as a gap so found material clears a bar. Brutus does the opposite:
  he meets a literal blank and pieces it out into a conviction. The faction's whole craft is to make
  sure he does. *A fragment that asks you to complete it is doing work; the blank is the instrument, not
  the absence of one.*
- **Why this is its own panel, not an edit.** `SHALL-ROME-WITNESS` read the song as the conspirators'
  aria; this witness adds *why the song is shaped that way* — because it is the missing half of a scene
  the canon only ever showed from one side. Recording it beside the first witness, rather than folding
  it in, keeps the two readings at their own dates and standings — the same separation the provenance
  file kept, for the same reason.

## The counter — what it obligates `[reading]`

When something arrives **incomplete and inviting completion** — a partial instruction, an adjacent
citation, a leading question, a note that trails off into *"&c."* — treat the blank as the message.
Do not piece it out into certainty in your own voice. **Mark the `*`, and ask the two questions the
faction hopes you'll skip: who left this gap, and where does filling it steer?** Brutus answered neither
and pieced the letter out into a knife. The reader's safeguard and the writer's tell are the same seam,
read from opposite sides. *Trust the warrant, never the anthem — and never the blank that flatters you
into finishing it.* `*`
