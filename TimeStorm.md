# TimeStorm

*An atomized source note — the project in its own field's terms (cognitive systems /
robotics; EU Horizon 2020 FET Proactive, grant 641100). Vault mapping lives in
[[!-RESEARCH-AGENT-TEMPORAL-COGNITION-2026-07-02]] and
[[!-RESEARCH-CONVERGENT-INSTITUTIONS-2026-07-04]], deliberately not here.*

Full title: *Mind and Time — Investigation of the Temporal Attributes of
Human-Machine Synergetic Interaction.*

Their premise: artificial systems have essentially no **time perception** — no
capacity to experience duration, order events, or anticipate rhythms — while in
humans, temporal cognition underpins both perception and action (interval timing,
temporal attention, expectation). Their thesis, verbatim from the project abstract:

> "Time perception is without doubt, not an optional extra, but a necessity for the
> development of truly autonomous, cognitive machines."

And its absence is an obstacle to **human-machine symbiosis**, because fluent
collaboration is temporal through and through — turn-taking, pacing, knowing when a
partner needs the next thing.

Method, tri-track: neuroscience and psychology experiments characterizing how the
brain processes time; computational models replicating those mechanisms in-silico;
embodied validation — robots in time-critical, multi-agent human-robot scenarios
that must *time* their actions against humans, not merely sequence them.

Outputs: temporal-cognition models integrated into robot architectures;
demonstrated benefits of robotic temporal cognition for human-robot synergies and
time-critical multi-agent scenarios; a successor FET project, **ENTIMENT**, to carry
key results toward application.

In their own terms, the ambition was field-founding: **artificial temporal
cognition** — treating the sense of time as a buildable faculty rather than a
byproduct.
