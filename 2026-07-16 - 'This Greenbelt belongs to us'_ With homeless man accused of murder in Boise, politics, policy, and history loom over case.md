As July 5th turned to July 6th, Jordan Harbst, 25, of Boise, was riding an electric scooter down the Boise River Greenbelt when he was attacked and stabbed multiple times, leaving him dead near 11th St.

The next morning, his body was found, and police started an investigation into what happened.

Three days later, police told the public it [needed help tracking down the person responsible](https://www.cityofboise.org/news/police/2026/july/boise-police-seek-publics-help-in-ongoing-homicide-investigation/) for Harbst’s death, specifically asking for members of the public who “saw a person involved in or appearing to have recently been involved in a violent encounter.”

![Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/6f251bf1-00e6-48d7-8365-570820fe299b.gif)

Regence - 2:1 - Business Grow

The murder, a rare occurrence in Boise in recent years, rippled through the community. It started with the shock of losing a young resident riding a scooter along the city’s signature pathway, moved to concern over the police’s implication of danger to the community, then expanded as a suspect was arrested and political figures tangled over blame.

## Suspect’s arrest, history

![](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SMApKWg36FE)

[Officers arrested Ross Wardlaw](https://www.cityofboise.org/news/police/2026/july/update-boise-police-arrest-suspect-in-homicide-investigation/) a day later and charged him with murdering Harbst. According to KTVB, which had a reporter present working on an unrelated story, [police arrested Wardlaw](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/crime/man-accused-greenbelt-murder-makes-first-court-appearance/277-8a3592d8-d257-4c89-9319-074b4efd37fa) at the Corpus Commons cooling shelter, part of the Corpus Commons shelter for homeless people near Americana Blvd.

In June, Wardlaw appeared in a KIVI-TV story about the cooling shelter. [He told reporter Sahana Patel](https://www.kivitv.com/downtown-boise/boise-cooling-spaces-expand-to-protect-unhoused-residents-from-summer-heat-dangers) that he had been coming to Corpus Commons for five years.

![Mountain West Bank - June 2026 New - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/188d2339-7fdb-4f33-88cb-575d2b022912.jpg)

Mountain West Bank - June 2026 New - 2:1

“It is nice to be able to show up at a place that keeps you cool, and you don’t have to worry about getting a heat stroke or whatever,” Wardlaw said on June 23rd. “Some people might just use drugs to kill the pain, or you fall into an alcohol binge, which I’ve done, to just sort of numb the pain.”

Wardlaw had also been charged with battery twice and aggravated assault once in the last 13 months, [KTVB’s Morgan Romero reported](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/crime/criminal-record-man-accused-boise-greenbelt-murder-stretches-back-decades/277-b5a3310e-2cad-47d2-871a-2582818c09c9). In May, an Ada County jury acquitted Wardlaw of aggravated battery and aggravated assault charges, [KIVI’s Riley Shoemaker reported](https://www.facebook.com/watch/?v=1546521896951306). The incident happened near the Interfaith Sanctuary’s former location in 2025. A juror told the station he “felt sick” about the verdict, but said the defense made a case that Wardlaw was acting in self-defense, and the juror said he didn’t find sufficient evidence to convict.

[KTVB reported](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/crime/criminal-record-man-accused-boise-greenbelt-murder-stretches-back-decades/277-b5a3310e-2cad-47d2-871a-2582818c09c9) that Wardlaw had a long criminal history dating back to the early 2000s. The station said he had checked into Intermountain Hospital for mental health treatment earlier this year, and the Idaho Department of Health and Welfare said he’d made “excellent progress.”

## Moyle: ‘law needs to be followed’

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/IMG-3048-Large-IMG-3048-Large.jpeg)

A photo of Jordan Harbst is secured to a tree with ribbon near where he was killed along the Boise River Greenbelt earlier this month. Photo: Don Day/BoiseDev

Earlier this week, Idaho Speaker of the House Mike Moyle, R-Star, and Rep. Bruce Skaug, Judiciary and Rules Chairman, R-Nampa, issued a statement that said they were “furious” about Harbst’s murder.

“This attack is a tragedy,” Skaug said. “But it is even more horrific because it may have been prevented.”

During the 2025 legislative session, Skaug and Sen. Codi Galloway, R-Boise, [sponsored legislation](https://legislature.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/sessioninfo/2025/legislation/S1141E1.pdf) that criminalized sleeping on public sidewalks, in parks, in cars, or on other public property in cities larger than 100,000 people in the state. It gave the Idaho Attorney General the power to “bring a civil action” against jurisdictions that “knowingly violate the provisions” of the law. Penalties of $10,000 could be assessed for each violation.

![Mountain West Bank - June 2026 New - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/188d2339-7fdb-4f33-88cb-575d2b022912.jpg)

Mountain West Bank - June 2026 New - 2:1

“The law doesn’t need to be strengthened or changed — it just needs to be followed,” Moyle said. “Idahoans who use the Boise Greenbelt, float the Boise River, or visit a downtown restaurant should not have to fear for their safety because Boise Mayor Lauren McLean won’t follow the law.”

After the legislature passed the law, the City of Boise added a law that would allow [police to cite people who violated the law](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/06/24/camping-ban-10/) and fine them $10. The city codified its law as “The Galloway Law” in reference to Sen. Galloway’s sponsorship of the state bill. The bill changed the previous city policy, which only issued tickets when there was space available in the city’s homeless shelters, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/06/24/camping-ban-10/).

From July 2025 to last week, the city issued 112 tickets. In September of 2025, officers wrote 16 tickets, the highest in any single month, while in February of this year, only one ticket was written.

The city’s bike patrol unit said it had “cleaned up” 52 camps. Boise Police spokesperson Haley Williams said the citations are “not necessarily related to the 52 camps.”

BoiseDev presented Moyle with the data from BPD in a phone call this week. He said he is “glad they’re doing something about it,” but wants to see more done.

## Boise law, Idaho law, and the U.S. Supreme Court

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/IMG_2414-Large.jpeg)

A guest talks to volunteers at the Corpus Christi House day shelter mail room. Photo: Margaret Carmel/BoiseDev file

Boise’s policies on sleeping in public places have evolved and changed over the last 15 years, impacting the entire western region of the country.

Under former Mayor Dave Bieter, the city had a policy to ticket anyone the police found sleeping on the street. In 2012, several homeless people filed a federal lawsuit against the city, challenging the policy.

![Idaho Wine Commission - Wine Month - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/046b7150-73c1-4678-92a7-624fd8a8dce8.png)

Idaho Wine Commission - Wine Month - 2:1

Over six years, the case, Martin vs. Boise, wound its way through the court system. In 2018, the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals ruled the policy was unconstitutional. Before the ruling, the city changed its policy to only issue tickets to people who slept outside if there were no beds available in local shelters.

The ruling in the Boise case impacted all states in the Ninth Circuit, including California, Washington, and Oregon.

[As BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/06/28/scotus-homelessness-tickets/), Bieter decided to appeal the Ninth Circuit’s ruling to the U.S. Supreme Court, a decision McLean voted for while a member of the city council. During her campaign against Bieter, she publicly opposed the appeal. SCOTUS declined to rule on the case in 2019 and sent it back to district court. [Boise ended up paying a $1.3 million settlement in 2021](https://boisedev.com/news/2021/02/08/city-of-boise-will-pay-out-13-million-settlement-in-camping-ordinance-lawsuit/), which also had the city agree not to ticket or arrest homeless people for sleeping outdoors when shelter wasn’t available.

![Senior Edge Legal - Jun 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/8bc5656b-95db-4242-8441-fb6cbfe40e72.png)

Senior Edge Legal - Jun 2026 - 2:1

In 2023, the U.S. Supreme Court agreed to take up a similar case, Grants Pass vs. Johnson, which also had a ruling in the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals that ticketing people for sleeping outside in public when shelter space was available was cruel and unusual punishment. SCOTUS [overruled the circuit court](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/06/28/scotus-homelessness-tickets/) in 2024, which made a wider range of laws possible.

Idaho then [passed the new statewide law](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/03/25/homeless-camping-ban-little/), followed shortly thereafter by Boise’s law, [which it said aligned with state statute.](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/06/24/camping-ban-10/)

## Data over time

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/this-greenbelt-belongs-to-us-with-homeless-man-accused-of-murder-in-boise-politics-policy-and-history-loom-over-case-Code-Generated-Image.png)

While data on homelessness isn’t a perfect science, rules around federal dollars provide a yearly yardstick.

![Zamzows 116 - July - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/46bf9fe4-7aa0-4f20-a644-e8911393869f.jpg)

Zamzows 116 - July - 2:1

One night each January, volunteers fan out across Ada County to perform a count of the number of homeless people in the area. The effort, known as the Point-in-Time survey, is driven in part by requirements related to federal funding.

The methodology is standardized nationwide based on U.S. Housing and Urban Development rules.

For Ada County, the number of people identified as homeless in 2025 was 1,012, the third straight year of increase. The survey identified 711 residents living in shelters and 301 who were identified as unsheltered. Data for the 2026 survey have not yet been released.

![Commercial Tire - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/13cd7680-6f30-4701-82a1-9fb53cf3807b.jpg)

Commercial Tire - July 2026 - 2:1

The count of 1,012 is the highest in 12 years for Ada County, but remains below the tallies taken from 2009 to 2013 in the aftermath of the housing crisis of that era.

The count of unsheltered people is the highest ever recorded since 2007, when methodology was standardized nationwide.

While Ada County has grown by 47% since 2007, the rate of homeless residents per capita remains below the numbers seen between 2009 and 2013. The per capita numbers have also risen each year since 2022.

## Greenbelt safety

Boise isn’t new to violence on the Boise River Greenbelt. In the late 1990s and early 2000s, several women were killed along the pathway in Boise.

In 1998, Kay Lynn Jackson was walking to church on Palm Sunday when she was raped and killed on the greenbelt. Her case went unsolved until 2013, when DNA evidence linked her killing to Patrick Jon Zacharias. KTVB [reported Zacharias](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/greenbelt-killer-gets-another-life-sentence/277-273624673) admitted to the crimes, who said he “was an addict and essentially homeless” when the murder happened.

In the summer of 2000, Samantha Maher was walking to class at Boise State University when she was sexually assaulted and shot in the head. David Edward Payne was convicted of Maher’s killing in 2002, but the sentence was overturned. He was again convicted and sentenced to death in 2010. He [died in prison](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/crime/man-who-murdered-boise-state-student-dies-in-prison/277-416464812) in 2015.

![Zamzows - Huma Green July 26 - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/ab3dadc9-b06a-4345-84c2-8ac47ce56465.png)

Zamzows - Huma Green July 26 - 4:1

Later in 2000, flight attendant Lynn Henneman stayed the night in Boise after flying in from New York.

She took a walk along the greenbelt when she was attacked. When she didn’t show up for her flight the next morning, police began a search for the newly married 38-year-old. Her belongings were found scattered near the Main Street bridge.

Weeks went by until a fisherman cast a net in the river and found her body. The case went unsolved for nearly three years, until another woman, Cheryl Ann Hanlon, was killed in the Boise foothills. Police later identified Erick Virgil Hall, described as a “33-year-old transient,” as the suspect in both murders. A [jury convicted him and sentenced him to death.](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2004/oct/29/hall-trial-a-textbook-case-for-new-sentencing/) He died in prison last year.

![Highlander - 2:1 - June 2026](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f8979e92-c78b-4b98-a8b1-22a788385c9b.gif)

Highlander - 2:1 - June 2026

The city, under then-Mayor Brent Coles, made a number of changes to the greenbelt in the wake of Henneman’s killing. The city added additional lighting, emergency call boxes, pavement mile markers, and barriers on high points as the greenbelt went under bridges.

## ‘This Greenbelt belongs to us’

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/this-greenbelt-belongs-to-us-with-homeless-man-accused-of-murder-in-boise-politics-policy-and-history-loom-over-case-105803393-1783463266.jpg)

Jordan Harbst. Photo: Courtesy Jordan Harbst GoFundMe

After the spate of killings in the late 1990s and 2000s, there were no reports of murders along the popular pathway until last week, when Jordan Harbst was killed.

A GoFundMe [account has been established for Harbst’s family.](https://www.gofundme.com/f/support-for-the-harbst-family-after-tragedy) His family described him as “one of Boise’s brightest lights.”

![Mountain West Bank - June 2026 New - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/188d2339-7fdb-4f33-88cb-575d2b022912.jpg)

Mountain West Bank - June 2026 New - 2:1

During a vigil last week for Harbst, his father, Josh, sounded a note of hope, [as noted by KTVB](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/crime/friends-and-family-honor-jordan-harbst-greenbelt-candlelight-vigil/277-98dfc209-c4ea-44b6-bab6-73da72574440), for his son, for the community, and for the Boise River Greenbelt.

“We gather right here at midnight… to remind evil that this Greenbelt belongs to us. Please don’t let this be the last time you think of or speak of my son. We will continue his fight, and his story’s not over.”
