KUNA, Idaho — An experienced Idaho climber is missing after falling while attempting a solo summit of Mount Jefferson in Oregon, according to the Linn County Sheriff's Office.

Linn County deputies received a 911 call at about 11:35 a.m. on Friday, July 24 reporting that a climber had fallen from the mountain's summit area.

Two climbers who witnessed the incident told authorities they saw an unidentified man fall down the west side of Mount Jefferson near Red Saddle before disappearing into the Milk Creek drainage. The climber was described only as a man who may have been wearing a bright red jacket.

An Oregon Army National Guard helicopter searched the area and located what investigators believe was the climber's backpack and other gear on the west side of the summit. Crews were unable to locate the climber.

Ground search-and-rescue teams were then dispatched and hiked to the base of the mountain. Because of the late hour and challenging terrain, rescuers camped overnight and climbed the mountain the next day.

At about 6:08 a.m. on Saturday, July 25, the sheriff's office received a separate report of a missing climber who they said had failed to make “scheduled check-ins” with his wife.

Investigators later confirmed the gear recovered on the mountain belonged to Jared John Empey, 37, of Kuna. Authorities said Empey, described as an experienced climber, had set out to solo-summit the mountain.

Based on witness accounts and the location, authorities said Empey's fall was not survivable.

Recovery efforts continued Saturday, though officials cautioned that the location where Empey fell may make a recovery impossible. He has not been found as of Sunday afternoon.

The Oregon Army National Guard, Oregon Department of Emergency Management, Corvallis Mountain Rescue, Eugene Mountain Rescue, Idanha-Detroit Fire District and the U.S. Forest Service are assisting with the search effort, according to the sheriff’s office.

"Our hearts and thoughts go out to Jared's family and friends during this difficult time," the sheriff's office said in a statement.

Empey was the city treasurer in Kuna. City officials shared that he leaves behind eight children and his wife, who is pregnant. In a statement shared to Facebook, the city wrote that his "dedication, professionalism and kindness made a lasting impact on our city and the community we serve."

"Jared has been such an intricate part of our city family that this news is hard to process. As City Treasurer he has brought us to levels that didn't seem possible. We are certainly going to miss everything about him. Please keep his family in your thoughts and prayers as they now have to move forward. Until we meet again my friend," Mayor Joe Stear said.

## Body recovered from Snake River Canyon after early morning fall, sheriff's office says

First responders recovered the body of a person who they say fell from the edge of Snake River Canyon in Twin Falls on July 30. He has not been identified.

![](https://media.ktvb.com/assets/KTVB/images/c2f4ae2f-72ec-43a5-a6e9-8ca12c023d64/20260730T170419/c2f4ae2f-72ec-43a5-a6e9-8ca12c023d64_1920x1080.jpg)

Credit: Twin Falls County Sheriff's Office

TWIN FALLS, Idaho — Emergency crews have recovered the body of a young man after he fell from Snake River Canyon on Thursday morning, according to the Twin Falls County Sheriff's Office.

According to the sheriff's office, multiple crews were on scene this morning, including the Special Operations Rescue Team from Magic Valley Paramedics, the Twin Falls Fire Department, and Air St. Luke's.

A representative from the sheriff's office said six "teenage boys" visited the Visitor Center around 6 in the morning. Four of them drove down into the canyon, while two remained at the top. The sheriff's office confirmed that the young man fell 150 feet down the canyon. His body was recovered through a joint effort among emergency responders by 9:49 a.m.

The Twin Falls County Sheriff's Office said they would not release a name, but extended condolences to the family and friends of the victim.
