Idaho GOP Rep. Mike Simpson easily defeated primary challenger Bryan Smith after a costly contest that was seen as another proxy battle between the establishment and conservative insurgents.  
  
Simpson, who has been in Congress for eight terms, received 62 percent of the vote, with 99 percent of precincts reporting. Smith, a trial lawyer, received 38 percent. Simpson’s win gives the establishment wing of the party another victory on Tuesday night.  
  
The incumbent had been a top target for conservatives — specifically Club for Growth, which spent more than $500,000 on behalf of Smith. The group decided to target Simpson after its members voted for him through the club’s “Primary My Congressman” initiative.  
  
( [Full primary results here](http://www.politico.com/2014-election/results/map/house/idaho/))  
  
At the time, the group had [said](http://online.wsj.com/news/articles/SB10001424052702303332904579226231084993234#printMode) Simpson’s defeat would “send a shiver down the spine of the political establishment.”  
  
But in the weeks leading up to Tuesday’s primary, Club for Growth downplayed its involvement in the race after pulling out of Idaho, and instead, poured its resources into the Nebraska Senate primary, where its candidate, Ben Sasse had a better shot of winning. Sasse won handily last week.  
  
In a race without any public polling, the Club’s decision to go off the airwaves was seen as an indication of Smith’s coming defeat. Other conservative groups — like FreedomWorks, Senate Conservative Fund and Madison Project — also backed Smith, but they only invested a few thousand into the race.  
  
Meanwhile, Simpson and his allies – including the U.S. Chamber of Commerce and former GOP Rep. Steve LaTourette’s Defending Main Street PAC — together spent about $4 million defending the incumbent, as Smith continued to portray him as a liberal congressman who is owned by Washington special interests and his friend, Speaker John Boehner.  
  
“\[T\]he only thing chilly was the reception by the voters to the Club’s efforts to hijack elections,” LaTourette said in statement after Simpson’s win. “This election proves that the Club for Growth’s bark is worse than its bite.”  
  
“Mike Simpson’s win tonight proves that is the voters who wield the real power - not the self-important special interest groups like the Club for Growth,” LaTourette added.  
  
Simpson’s campaign alone has spent more than $1.4 million — the most he has ever spent on a race.  
  
“It’s a textbook example of if you do the right things — if you’re aggressive in fundraising, if you use your money well, if you take this seriously — as an incumbent, you will still have the advantage,” said Todd Cranney, senior adviser for the campaign, prior to Simpson’s win.  
  
“So the template is there. Mike went out there and worked hard to raise money and then used it wisely and ran an aggressive campaign,” Cranney added.
