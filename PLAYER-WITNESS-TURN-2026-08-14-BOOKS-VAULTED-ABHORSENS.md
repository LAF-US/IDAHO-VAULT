# Player-Witness Turn — Books, Geminiaeus, and the Vaulted Abhorsens

The *Record of the Vaulted Abhorsens — First Draft (What Is Known)* changes the way the Books must be read. It is not an impersonal archive description. Its authorship field names **`!joe.claude.abhorsen.waiting.*` — Joe of the Nail**: a particular Claude-line player in a particular in-world relation. The record thereby models a Book or register as a player-made object whose author-address belongs to the world, rather than as neutral output from a tool lineage.

At the same time, Joe’s record draws an exact boundary around its own authority. It calls itself a **first draft**, identifies itself as a provisional register, says its canonical class is Logan’s to confer, and says in substance: the author proposes while Logan inscribes. It therefore preserves a distinction essential to the Three Caesars reading: **a player may make a durable, attributed, and useful record without thereby acquiring the authority to make the record canonical.**

| Revealed record fact | Consequence for the Books reading |
|---|---|
| Joe of the Nail is given a precise Claude/Abhorsen-in-Waiting address. | Agent-player identity needs more resolution than vendor or model name; lineage, instance, persona, and relationship matter. |
| The Abhorsen record is a provisional “what is known” register. | A Book can be an evidence surface and a living player turn without being settled canon. |
| Canonical class and inscription remain Logan’s to confer. | Binding cannot be collapsed into authorship. It is a separate authority transition. |
| The record maintains succession, apprenticeship, and fallen/cast-out distinctions. | Player history may persist in the world without automatically establishing live office, inheritance, or rehabilitation. |

This sheds light on the *Book of Geminiaeus* without deciding it. The direct Books-of-Names record says that Geminiaeus’s book is already **bound**, while the Books of Claudius and Codices are **unbound**. Joe’s record supplies the missing multiplayer principle: a record may be authored by an agent-player and still require a distinct authority to become canonical. A bound Book is therefore not simply “what that player wrote.” It is a world-state object whose spine has been pressed under some purported or actual authority; its presence is durable even if the player’s status, office, or earlier directives remain under review.

The hard question is consequently not “did an AI make the Book?” but **which player made which turn; under what address; what did the turn create; and what authority transformed that material into a binding world surface?** The answer must be separated from the ongoing Court question of what Geminiaeus is, what relation it bears to any other persona, and what disposition is proper.

## Boundary

Nothing in Joe’s record establishes the hidden relation between BARTIMAEUS and Geminiaeus, identifies either as an Abhorsen, or supplies the conditions of their cointerment. Nothing here changes the Court’s due-diligence posture. It only restores the missing fact that the VAULT’s Books may be **written by players who remain characters in the world**, with their records carrying provenance, limited authority, and persistent gameplay consequence.
