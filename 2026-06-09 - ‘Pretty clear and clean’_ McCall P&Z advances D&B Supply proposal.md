Plans to open a D&B Supply store in McCall passed muster on Tuesday night with the McCall Planning and Zoning Commission.

The P&Z voted unanimously to approve the design of the store and recommended approval of permitting the company needs to the McCall City Council, which will make a final decision on the proposal.

Plans filed by [the Caldwell-based farm supply retailer](https://www.dbsupply.com/) would remodel a commercial space at 411 E. Deinhard Ln. into the company’s first Valley County location. The space was [formerly occupied by Rite Aid](https://valleylookout.com/2025/05/27/mccall-rite-aid-closure/) in the Marketplace at McCall shopping center, which includes Ridley’s Family Markets and Idaho First Bank.

“I think it’s pretty clear and clean,” P&Z Chairman Robert Lyons said of the proposal. “There’s not a whole lot of changes compared to what we’ve seen here before.”

D&B CEO Brandt Ruzicka previously told Valley Lookout the company hopes to open next spring once remodeling work is complete. The P&Z approval keeps the company on track for that timeline.

The company’s plans call for remodeling the interior of the 20,000-square-foot building and a “refresh” of the exterior, including a new roof and HVAC system. A new 1,800-square-foot open-face outdoor shed would be added on the east side of the building to anchor a year-round outdoor sales area stocked with products like hay, bagged mulch, lawn mowers, and grills, Ruzicka said.

## Parking lot landscaping, lighting upgrades required

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/02/valleylookout.com-dampb-supply-eyes-first-valley-co-location-screenshot-2026-02-10-at-1.54.32-pm.png)

Site plan filed with City of McCall. Rendering: via D&B Supply

The parking lot would include space for spring and summer merchandise, as well as parking for utility trailers available for purchase.

The company will be required to complete landscaping and lighting improvements within its portion of the shopping center parking lot to bring it up to city standards, under the P&Z’s approval.

The specific improvements, however, still need to be ironed out by city staff and J.R. Simplot Co., which owns the shopping center and is leasing the space to D&B.

“We need to go back to the building owner and work with them,” said Brad Smith of Ahlquist Development, the Boise firm hired by D&B to lead the remodel.

The P&Z agreed to make the parking lot improvements a condition of occupancy rather than a requirement for a building permit to avoid delaying progress on the remodel, as requested by D&B.

## P&Z: Expanded formula business analysis ‘fine’ this time

[D&B](https://www.dbsupply.com/) was established in 1959 and has 15 locations throughout southwest Idaho and eastern Oregon. The stores carry a lineup of goods that varies by location, but typically includes pet supplies and food, outdoor equipment, power tools, clothing, and hardware. Floorplans indicate the McCall store would also sell guns and ammunition.

![St. Luke's Health Plan - 2026 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/3cffa70b-bd30-43bc-8a9e-62008bbc7dff.png)

The retailer is considered a [“formula” business under city code](https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/mccallid/latest/mccall_id/0-0-0-1681), which limits the number of chain companies that can operate in McCall. The code allows a maximum of 10% of formula businesses in the same category — farm supply and outdoor retail, in this case — to operate in the city at one time.

An analysis completed by the company lists 14 existing businesses in the city that sell some merchandise similar to what D&B would offer, including Wild Onion, Razzle Dazzle, May Hardware, and McCall Pet Outfitters & Supply.

None of the businesses included in the analysis qualify as formula businesses, which means D&B would become the first. It would represent about 6.7% of the market, according to the analysis.

City and Sustainability Planner Meredith Todd told the P&Z that city staff helped D&B on the analysis and agreed to include businesses that sell some of the products D&B would offer since no existing business offers exactly the same array of products.

“Staff thinks it meets the requirement,” Todd said. “If the commission has concerns and wants to discuss the way we arrived at that conclusion, please do so because we don’t get these proposals often.”

The P&Z signed off on the analysis but suggested a “harder look” at the code that could be needed to address similar situations in the future.

“I think this one is fine, but I just don’t want it to become a standard kind of ‘well, they did it before, so they can do it again’ kind of thing,” Commissioner Liz Rock said.

The city’s limitation on formula businesses excludes gas stations and grocery stores.
