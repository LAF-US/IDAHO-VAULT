A long-percolating plan for apartments near the Boise River in a quiet part of downtown is moving forward again, this time with nearly twice as many proposed units and a new 16-story tower.

*BoiseDev members got this story before anyone else. [Sign up](https://boisedev.com/first/) to support Boise Dev’s journalism and get future stories in your inbox and be the first to know.*

Late last year, Urban Capital Partners submitted a new proposal to the City of Boise to build a large-scale apartment complex with three buildings and 713 apartments on a site it owns at the corner of 27th St. and Fairview Ave. Developers held a neighborhood meeting on the project late last month, and city staff will review the plans next week.

[Initially, as BoiseDev reported,](https://boisedev.com/news/2021/01/25/27th-fairview-boise/) a different developer, Kal Pacific, proposed two apartment buildings and office building for the site. The proposal would have included 354 apartments as well as a four-floor office building along the Boise River Greenbelt. In 2024, UCP took up the project. Both UCP and Kal Pacific are controlled by Don Veasy of Temecula, California.

## Three phases

The first phase of the project would build a seven-story apartment building with 185 units, consisting of 32 studios, 108 one-bedroom, and 45 two-bedroom apartments. The first floor would include commercial space as well as parking. The second floor would largely feature parking spaces, while the third through seventh floors would consist of apartments. The third floor would include a pool, spa, sports court, as well as fire pits and a community room. One- and two-bedroom units would include private balconies. The building would include 5,962 square feet of retail space, primarily along Fairview Ave.

The second phase would build a second seven-story apartment building, this one consisting of 330 units with 75 studios, 214 one-bedroom and 41 two-bedroom apartments. It would follow a similar pattern to the first building. This building would include 18,452 square feet of retail space.

The third portion of the project is the largest and most visible, and would add a 16-story tower to the skyline on the western edge of the downtown area. It would bring 198 apartments to the site, with 50 studios and 148 one-bedrooms. A parking garage would take up the first-floor levels of the building, with an amenity deck on the fifth floor, and apartments from the sixth to 15th floors. The third building would feature 2,027 square feet of retail

In total, the 27th and Fairview project would include 799 bedrooms across the 713 apartments. A total of 717 parking spaces would be included in the three parking garages. The project would also include a total of 840 spaces to park bikes, meeting the city’s requirements for both the apartments as well as the retail space.

## Roads reconfigured

The site, once home to Goodman Oil and vacant for more than a decade, would be reconfigured to extend Whitewater Park Blvd. across Fairview Ave., which would then connect to the existing Fletcher St. in front of the now-closed Symposion bar. A former automotive service building at the corner of 27th and Fairview would be torn down. Huraa Architecture of Utah wrote in a letter to the city last week that ACHD approved the changes to the street network in May.

The new street network would connect to the Boise River Greenbelt, Huraa said in its letter.

“The project maintains and enhances the pedestrian connection to the Boise River Greenbelt, including a greenbelt connection at the southwest corner of the site that will be enhanced and tied into the sidewalk network for safe pedestrian and bicycle circulation, in coordination with the city of Boise,” Grant J. Seaman of Huraa wrote.

If approved, Seaman said work on the infrastructure updates, including the new roads and sidewalks, would start in the fourth quarter of this year and would take six to eight months to complete. The first seven-story building would start in the second quarter of 2027 and take two years to build. The second seven-story building would not start until mid-2029 and would take as long as three years to build. The final phase, the 16-story tower, would start work in 2032 and, if the schedule holds, would open sometime in late 2034.

## Next steps

An online offer memorandum for the project indicates the first phase alone would cost $50 million to build. The memo indicates the third phase could be built or ultimately sold.

A portion of the site used to house the Goodman Oil Company from the 1920s until 2009. That site was a former EPA brownfield, and the subject of $730,000 in fines in 2009 from the EPA, according to a report from The Blue Review.

The project is largely allowed by the city’s zoning code to move forward with minimal approvals as proposed. It will need approval for design review from the city. A hearing date has not yet been set.
