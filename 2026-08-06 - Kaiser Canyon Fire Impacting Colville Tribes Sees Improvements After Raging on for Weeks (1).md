#### Note: This story is based on the latest available information as of 3 p.m. PST, Aug. 6. Please refer to your local government, fire agency websites and social media for the most timely information regarding evacuations and road closures. For information on where to donate, check with the tribes being impacted by fires on their websites and Facebook pages.

The Kaiser Canyon fire is at [52% containment](https://inciweb.wildfire.gov/incident-information/wacoa-kaiser-canyon), as it continues blazing through lands belonging to the Confederated Tribes of the Colville. It’s burned nearly 137,000 acres of the 1.4-million acre reservation since it began just over three weeks ago.

In an update posted to the Colville Tribal Broadcast, News and Information [Facebook Page,](https://www.facebook.com/CCTBroadcasts) Kris Baumgartner, operations sections chief, said there have been considerable improvements in the area.

At noon on Aug. 6, Highway 155 reopened with speed limitations. Baumgartner noted there might be delays and potential stops due to crews working on the roadways. He also said all side roads in the area will remain closed, and that travelers and residents can expect to see heavy equipment such as engines in the area.

He also noted an improvement in the Kartar area.

“We’re still going to have folks driving around and putting out hot spots they see poking up deeper in the black,” Baumgartner said.

Fire crews have carried out successful containment efforts in Nespelem. Residents can expect the area to be under containment and have a fire crew presence the next few weeks.

Four hot shot crews are tackling a 30-mile area from Mill Creek to North Star Creek and Trail Creek, according to Baumgartner. Three of the crews joined the effort on Aug. 5.

“It’s very steep rocky ground going through creek drainages and up and down. It’s very tough ground to be in so that’s why we have crews – like hot shot crews – to get in there,” he said. “It’s a lot of scouting, seeing where we can put in line and be successful.”

Additionally, Baumgartner said there’s the potential for air aid to come in as well to help put out the fires.

According to a brief from Jason Devcich, incident commander for the Colville Agency, increased fire behavior is expected with the warming and drying of the area during Aug. 6 and Aug. 7. Additionally, air quality in the area is at an unhealthy level with the Omak, Nespelem, Keller, Grand Coulee and Coulee Dam areas expected to reach hazardous levels due to the fire and neighboring fires.

For local air quality information go to the [Washington Smoke Information Site](https://wasmoke.blogspot.com/).

The Omak Public Library is providing N-95 masks to the public at 30 South Ash St.

The Kaiser Canyon Fire information line at 509-800-8123 is open from 8 a.m. to 8 p.m. Information, including about evacuations and road closures, is also available through the [Colville Tribal Broadcasts, News and Information](https://www.facebook.com/CCTBroadcasts) Facebook page and by email at 2026kaisercanyon@firenet.gov.
