## 1. What it is `[research]`

The **Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index** (ATU) is the standard **catalogue of folktale *types*** in
folklore studies — a standardized register that classifies international narratives by their
recurring plot structures, so the "same tale" can be recognized and compared across languages,
regions, and centuries. To say a story "is **ATU 302**" is to give it a precise **address** in
a shared system: a Norwegian giant, a Slavic sorcerer, and a video-game boss can all be filed
as one underlying shape (the external-heart tale) regardless of surface.

It is the periodic table of story-shapes — and, like any such table, a map laid over a
territory that does not naturally divide along clean lines (see §5, the criticisms).

## 2. The three hands that built it `[research]`

The hyphenated name is three folklorists across nearly a century — *offices held in sequence,
each revising the last, no single author* (a fact the vault should like):

- **Antti Aarne** (Finnish), **1910** — the first type-index (*Verzeichnis der Märchentypen*).
- **Stith Thompson** (American) — revised and expanded Aarne in **1928** and again **1961**,
  and separately authored the companion **Motif-Index of Folk-Literature** (1932).
- **Hans-Jörg Uther** (German), **2004** — *The Types of International Folktales: A
  Classification and Bibliography*, 3 volumes (Finnish Academy of Science and Letters). The
  current edition. Uther corrected entries that were "cursory and often imprecise," recognized
  that many "irregular types" are in fact old and widespread, and loosened the earlier
  "emphasis on oral tradition" that had obscured "older, written versions of the tale types."

## 3. Type vs. Motif — the load-bearing distinction `[research]`

Two interlocking indexes, two grains of analysis:

- A **type** is a **whole, self-sufficient tale plot** — a complete story that can stand on its
  own (e.g. ATU 302, the ogre's heart hidden in an egg).
- A **motif** is the **smallest element that persists and travels** between tales — a single
  durable unit (an external heart; a glass slipper; a helpful animal).

The **ATU type-index** and **Thompson's Motif-Index** work together: types are cross-referenced
to the motifs they contain. The Motif-Index sorts motifs into **26 broad classes**, each a
letter (A–Z) followed by numbers for finer subdivision. So a tale carries **two coordinates** —
its type-number and its constituent motif-codes.

## 4. The seven shelves `[research]`

ATU divides all tale-types into **seven categories** by number range:

| Range | Category |
| --- | --- |
| **1–299** | Animal Tales |
| **300–749** | **Tales of Magic** (fairy tales proper) |
| **750–849** | Religious Tales |
| **850–999** | Realistic Tales (Novelle) |
| **1000–1199** | Tales of the Stupid Ogre / Giant / Devil |
| **1200–1999** | Anecdotes and Jokes |
| **2000–2399** | Formula Tales |

Within **Tales of Magic**, the **300–399** band is the **supernatural adversaries** — and
**ATU 302, "The Ogre's (Devil's) Heart in the Egg,"** sits there. That is the exact shelf the
`TRANSIENT-HEARTS-WITNESS` drew from: Tubba Blubba, Davy Jones, Koschei, and the heartless
Giant are surface variants of one type-address.

## 5. The criticisms — and why they are the point `[research]`

The index is indispensable *and* openly contested, and the contest is the instructive part:

- **Overlapping types** (Alan Dundes) — the categories are not disjoint; the same tale can
  straddle two series (e.g. the 300s "supernatural adversaries" and the 500s "ordinary"),
  and the call comes down to **the indexer's interpretive judgment**, not an objective rule.
- **Thematic boundaries** (Vladimir Propp) — the system can't say **where one theme ends and
  another begins**, or how tales relate beyond isolated segments.
- **Western / geographical bias** — selection and classification reflect European traditions
  and marginalize other cultural variants.
- **Source bias** — older editions leaned on 19th/early-20th-century *printed* collections,
  underweighting living oral tradition and recent fieldwork.

The through-line: **a classification is a caption, and the caption is not the tale.** The ATU
number is a useful lantern; mistaking it for the whole story is the error its own critics name.

## 6. Why this belongs in the vault `[mapping]` / `*`

Offered as my reading, not seated as doctrine:

- **ATU is a real-world Register — a working precedent for the vault's own.** It is a numbered,
  cross-referenced roll of named types: the ATU number *is* an **address** for a story-shape,
  exactly as the vault's grammar gives `name.lineage.office` an address and **House Precedence
  (#664)** gives every inhabitant a number in the **Register of Precedence**. The folklorists
  built, a century ago, the kind of standing-register the vault is now building for personae.
  `[mapping]`
- **Type vs. motif = whole-persona vs. portable-lens.** The motif (the smallest traveling unit)
  is to the type what a single **lens-layer** is to a composite persona in the Personae Engine —
  a durable element that recurs across many wholes. The vault already thinks in "additive and
  subtractive lens layers"; ATU is the same idea applied to stories. `[mapping]`
- **The criticisms are the vault's own discipline, pre-written.** "The caption is not the tale"
  is **Hecate's correction** — *"keep the blessing, loosen the caption… recognition is not
  reduction"*; **Dundes' overlapping types** is the **anti-amalgamation / hold-the-seam**
  rule; **"the indexer's interpretive judgment"** is **"I propose; Logan inscribes"** — a
  classification is a *proposal*, never a verdict; and **Propp's "where does one theme end"** is
  the `*` held-gap. The vault's epistemics and the folklorist's cautions are the same cautions.
  `[mapping]`

**Net `[mapping]`:** the ATU Index is both a **model** the vault can borrow (a register that
addresses shapes by number and cross-reference) and a **warning** it should keep (every such
register is a lantern, not the sky). Adopt the structure; inherit the humility.
