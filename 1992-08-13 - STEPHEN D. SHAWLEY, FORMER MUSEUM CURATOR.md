# STEPHEN D. SHAWLEY, FORMER MUSEUM CURATOR

ELK CITY A burial service was held on family land at Elk City Thursday for Stephen D. Shawley, former curator at the Nez Perce Historical Park museum at Spalding and retired owner of Magic Video at Lewiston.

Shawley, 42, died of AIDS-related causes Wednesday at his Lewiston home.

He was curator of the Nez Perce Historical Museum when it opened in 1983. He was instrumental in researching and designing the exhibits there.

He also was a recognized authority on Nez Perce Indian life and his graduate thesis is noted by many as the definitive study on Nez Perce material culture.

While curator at the historical museum, he also played a key role in putting together the General Store museum at Spalding.

He taught Indian crafts and culture across the country to adult and youth members of the Boy Scouts of America. He had been a scout himself, achieving the Eagle Scout rank and earning the Distinguished Service Award from the Order of the Arrow.

As an adult, he served on the national Boy Scout committee on Indian events.

He was born Sept. 22, 1949, at Clarkston, to William J. and Joyce Ann Florence Shawley. He grew up in Kamiah and graduated from high school there in 1967.

He attended the University of Idaho at Moscow, where he was a member of Alpha Kappa Lambda fraternity and was involved in numerous campus activities. He was named an outstanding senior and also won the top national award from his fraternity during his senior year.

He was a member of the scholastic honoraries Phi Beta Kappa, Pi Kappa Mu and Phi Kappa Phi.

As an undergraduate, he developed the first correspondence course in anthropology in Idaho. He graduated with a bachelor's degree in 1971 and with a master's degree in 1972.

He was employed at Cheney Cowles Memorial Museum from 1973 to 1975 and later worked for the Appaloosa Horse Museum at Moscow and did contract museum work before joining the National Park Service as curator at Spalding.

In 1986, he purchased The Peddler video store at Lewiston and eventually expanded the business to three stores and several out-of-town routes. The business' name was later changed to Magic Video. He sold the stores last year because of his illness.

He was an avid hunter and fly fisherman and enjoyed canoeing and traveling. He visited Europe last summer.

He spent his last days at the Lewiston home he shared with his longtime companion, Joel R. Stevenson.

Survivors include his mother, Joyce Shawley at Kamiah; two brothers, James Michael Shawley of Chicago and Timothy Shawley of Clarkston; and a sister, Jennifer Shawley of Pullman.

His father died previously.

The family suggests memorials be contributions to the Stephen D. Shawley Endowed Scholarship Fund, in care of the University of Idaho Foundation, Moscow, Idaho 83843.
