The City of Boise paid a large severance payment to departed Chief of Staff Courtney Washburn after her departure earlier this year.

The city paid Washburn $196,593.12, according to the records obtained by BoiseDev under the Idaho Public Records Act. The payment included $112,845 in severance, $76,684.35 in unused leave time, and a $7,063.77 benefit distribution.

As of 2024, Washburn was the city’s seventh-highest-paid employee, with an annual wage of $200,470, [according to a database maintained](https://govsalaries.com/washburn-courtney-203751246) by GovSalaries. That’s more than her former boss, Boise Mayor Lauren McLean, whose pay is set by city code and in 2024 made $153,980.

As BoiseDev [reported in February](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/02/17/courtney-washburn/), Washburn left the role after nearly six years. McLean made a statement calling Washburn a “trusted advisor and fierce advocate for our city” and said she wished “her the best and look forward to the impact she’ll make next.”

Under the city’s policy, for an employee to receive severance, the mayor must decide to remove the employee from their job.

“When the city exercises the at-will option and severs the employment of… mayor’s office staff member… that individual, in the mayor’s sole discretion, may be eligible to receive a severance package,” the policy notes. To receive severance, the employee must not have been “terminated for documented misconduct or unsatisfactory performance,” and must also sign a separation agreement with the city.

City spokesperson Maria Weeg, cited a confidential agreement with Washburn and did not respond to questions about the severance agreement. Washburn did not comment.

[The city’s policy](https://www.cityofboise.org/departments/human-resources/employee-policy-handbook/section-200-employee-selection-classification-and-status-change/201d-at-will-employment-regulation/#:~:text=III.%20SEPARATION%20AGREEMENT%20FOR%20DEPARTMENT%20DIRECTORS%2C%20SENIOR%20MANAGERS%2C%20FIRE%20AND%20POLICE%20COMMAND%20STAFF%2C%20%26%20MAYOR%E2%80%99S%20OFFICE%20STAFF) states that it calculates the severance payment at up to 12 weeks of base pay. That would equate to about $46,000 based on her 2024 salary. It’s not clear why Washburn’s severance was more than twice that amount at $112,845.

City Council President Meredith Stead said the council was “read in” on the severance payment. There is no record that the council voted to authorize the severance, and it’s unclear exactly how the payment was funded.

Weeg told BoiseDev that the funds would come from the mayor’s office budget, but didn’t elaborate further.

## A prior high-level payment

This isn’t the first time McLean has authorized a severance payment to a leader in her office.

During reporting for this story, BoiseDev requested records of prior severance payments for Washburn’s predecessor in the role, Jade Riley.

![Freeform - Invested in Boise - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/8d57412c-01ee-47ef-b704-9f71ec57d3ba.png)

Freeform - Invested in Boise - 2:1

In 2020, former City of Boise Chief Operating Officer Jade Riley left the city for a new job with the City of Ketchum. Riley had served the city under former Mayor Dave Bieter, and decided to stay on after Bieter lost reelection to McLean.

In a 2020 news release, the city said Riley was leaving for the Ketchum job, and McLean presented him with a key to the city in recognition of his efforts.

Despite leaving voluntarily for a new role, McLean elected to pay Riley severance. He was paid a total amount of $118,579.99, which included a severance payment of $94,069.73, unused leave of $10,342.42, and a benefit distribution of $14,167.84.

Weeg told BoiseDev that in Riley’s case, “there was an agreement outlining the terms of his employment in place to ensure a smooth transition.” Riley did not comment.

Riley’s severance payment came just a few months after Bieter had elected to exercise the at-will option for four other staffers in his office before the transition to McLean, paying out $99,151 in severance spread across four individuals. One of those people, Amber Pence, was [paid severance](https://boisedev.com/news/2020/02/19/dave-bieter-severance-pence/) after being rehired by Bieter after his loss to McLean. Pence worked 23 days for the city, having previously resigned to work for Bieter’s campaign, and was paid $28,314 in severance.
