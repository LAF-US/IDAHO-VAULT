# Ostrom Design Principles

*An atomized source note — the concept in its own field's terms (political economy /
institutional analysis). Vault mapping lives in
[[!-RESEARCH-CONVERGENT-INSTITUTIONS-2026-07-04]], deliberately not here.*

In 1968, Garrett Hardin's "tragedy of the commons" argued that any shared,
depletable resource — pasture, fishery, aquifer — is doomed: each user gains
individually by overusing it, so rational users destroy it unless it is privatized
or state-regulated.

Elinor Ostrom, a political scientist, spent decades on field studies of real
commons — Swiss alpine pastures, Spanish irrigation *huertas*, Japanese village
forests, Maine lobster grounds — and found Hardin empirically wrong: communities
self-govern shared resources successfully, sometimes for centuries, with neither
market nor state. Comparing commons that endured against those that collapsed, she
distilled **eight design principles** (*Governing the Commons*, 1990):

1. **Clearly defined boundaries** — of both the resource and the community of users.
2. **Congruence** between the rules and local conditions.
3. **Collective-choice arrangements** — those bound by the rules can participate in
   changing them.
4. **Monitoring**, by monitors accountable to the users.
5. **Graduated sanctions** — first offenses punished lightly, escalating with
   repetition.
6. **Cheap, accessible conflict-resolution** forums.
7. **Minimal recognition of the right to organize** — external authorities do not
   override the community's self-governance.
8. **Nested enterprises** — for large systems, governance layered in concentric
   tiers ("polycentricity").

She won the 2009 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economics — the first woman to do so. Her
core claim, in her own terms: institutions are sets of working rules, and durable
self-governance is a *craft* with observable design regularities — not a miracle,
and not a market.

Field vocabulary: common-pool resource (subtractable, costly to exclude from),
appropriators, polycentric governance, the Institutional Analysis and Development
(IAD) framework.
