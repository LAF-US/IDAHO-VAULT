A drone inspecting power lines last weekend near Cascade was damaged after being struck by a shotgun blast, according to the Valley County Sheriff’s Office.

The incident occurred shortly after 5 p.m. on June 6 while the pilot of the drone — a contractor hired by Idaho Power — was flying around power lines along West Mountain Road near Doriann Drive at the far southern end of Lake Cascade.

“It looks like Idaho Power had people out flying their power lines, preparing for wildfire season, to identify areas to mitigate fire fuel around their lines and poles,” Valley County Sheriff Kevin Copperi said. “It was reported that the pilot of the unmanned aircraft (drone) thought that his unit was hit by gunfire. The pilot landed his drone, and it appeared to have been struck by shotgun pellets.”

Deputies identified a suspect in the shooting after interviewing witnesses, but Copperi said formal charges have not yet been filed because evidence is still being reviewed, including video footage from the drone that was struck.

“I would urge folks to keep in mind that you do not own the airspace above your property,” he said. “You can’t shoot down drones, that is a violation of federal law.”

The suspect, whose identity has not been released due to the pending investigation, would be facing felony malicious injury to property if damage to the drone exceeds $1,000, Copperi said, adding that Idaho Power reported each drone cost about $20,000.

Felony malicious injury to property carries a minimum prison sentence of one year and a fine of up to $1,000, [under Idaho law](https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/title18/t18ch70/sect18-7001/).

An investigation into the incident by the Federal Aviation Administration and the Federal Bureau of Investigation is ongoing, Copperi said.

## More drone inspections planned in Valley County

![](https://valleylookout.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/06/valleylookout.com-auto-draft-drone-patrol-1.jpeg)

Idaho Power has a fleet of about 30 drones and 25 pilots. Photo: Courtesy Idaho Power

Idaho Power has more drone inspections planned across Valley County through late July, said Sven Berg, a spokesperson for the utility company.

![Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/115c0cfa-e547-4a5c-8f4e-7ea7e16f8bde.png)

Western States - Lookout creative - 4:1

Power lines around Lake Cascade, near the end of Spink Lane, and along the southeast side of Payette Lake are all slated for inspection this summer, Berg said.

“We mostly use drones to inspect power lines and other equipment. That could include substations, power plants, battery installations, and other assets,” he said. “In certain circumstances, they help us inspect equipment faster and more safely.”

The drone struck last weekend was operated by a contractor hired by Idaho Power, Berg said, although the utility company has a fleet of about 30 drones and 25 licensed pilots.

Berg admitted it is “hard to identify” who drones belong to while they’re in the air, but said flights for powerline inspections always remain above land subject to utility easements. The drones also only record footage of utility equipment.

“When Idaho Power employees or contractors operate drones, the pilots will be near the drone,” Berg said. “Contractors’ vehicles are marked with graphics identifying them as Idaho Power contractors. The pilots wear safety vests and carry letters from Idaho Power.”
