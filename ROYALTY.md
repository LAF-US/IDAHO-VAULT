# ROYALTY: Lineage as Power

## Definition

**Royalty** is the institutionalization of hereditary sovereignty—where bloodline determines the right to rule, governance, and social hierarchy. It represents the prescriptive application of lineage to political power structures.

## Core Principles

### Hereditary Rule

- Primogeniture: Firstborn (traditionally male) inherits title and power
- Agnatic succession: Male-line only inheritance
- Cognatic succession: Allows female inheritance
- Salic law: Explicit exclusion of female-line inheritance

### Divine Right

Historical justification that rulers are chosen by divine will:

- Mandate of Heaven (Chinese concept)
- Divine Right of Kings (European concept)
- Sacred kingship (various cultures)

### Dynasty

A sequence of rulers from the same family:

- Habsburgs (13th-20th century, Europe)
- Tudors (15th-17th century, England)
- Romanovs (17th-20th century, Russia)
- Hanoverians (1714-1901, UK; succeeded by Saxe-Coburg and Gotha, renamed Windsor in 1917)

## Lineage Connection

### The Royal Breeding Program

Royal marriages throughout history functioned as strategic breeding programs:

- Political Alliances: Marriages between royal houses to prevent war
- Territorial Consolidation: Combining lands through marriage
- Legitimacy Reinforcement: Strengthening claims through prestigious bloodlines
- Inbreeding: Common practice to maintain pure bloodlines, with genetic consequences

### Genetic Consequences

Famous examples of royal inbreeding:

- Habsburg Jaw: Mandibular prognathism from cousin marriages
- Hemophilia (a distinct mechanism, not inbreeding): an X-linked mutation arising in Queen Victoria and carried to her descendants by ordinary hereditary transmission
- Mental Health: Various monarchs exhibited traits linked to inbreeding depression
- Fertility Issues: Many royal lines ended due to inability to produce heirs

## Historical Examples

### European Royalty

- Plantagenets: Ruled England 1154-1485
- Capetians: Ruled France 987-1328
- Habsburgs: Bella gerant alii, tu felix Austria nube

### Non-Western Royalty

- Pharaohs: Divine kings of Egypt, often married siblings
- Emperors of Japan: Unbroken line since 660 BC (legendary)
- Mughals: Descendants of Timur and Genghis Khan
- Zulu Kings: Shaka Zulu created powerful kingdom

### Modern Constitutional Monarchies

- United Kingdom: House of Windsor
- Japan: Imperial House of Japan
- Scandinavia: Various royal houses
- (Distinct category) Middle East: Saudi Arabia is an absolute monarchy and the UAE a federation of hereditary emirates - modern monarchies, but not constitutional monarchies in the sense above
