COUNCIL, Idaho — A landslide has closed a section of US Highway 95 between Council and New Meadows on Sunday, March 16.

In an update on Monday, March 17, Idaho Transportation Department (ITD) announced that the highway will remain closed between Council and New Meadows. The landslide has impacted hundreds of feet of road along the Weiser River.

According to the Idaho Transportation Department, regional traffic is being directed to use State Highway 55 as a detour around the closure. Local traffic will only be permitted between Council and Glendale, and between New Meadows and Pine Ridge. A news release said all other drivers would be turned around at the barricades in Council and New Meadows.

Engineering crews were on site Monday morning to assess the damage. A news release said a drill crew would be on site Tuesday to investigate the soil and slide information for a possible retaining wall.

“We aim to have one lane opened at some point this week, but just for cars,” District Engineer Jason Brinkman said. “We are concerned about allowing wider, heavier commercial vehicles on this narrow area until the repair work is complete.”

ITD will be monitoring the area as this closure continues.

“These natural weather events are nearly impossible to predict,” Brinkman said. “Fortunately, given this season of storms, we are ready to respond and expedite this project.”

SUN VALLEY, Idaho — Sun Valley Resort made history by earning the first-ever Dark Sky Lodging & Resort Certification for a resort in the United States, officials announced Monday.

The certification, which was awarded through the DarkSky Approved Lodging Program, highlights what [Sun Valley Resort](https://www.ktvb.com/article/news/local/sun-valley-resort-on-spring-operations-lift-replacements/277-82542c83-18b3-4ec7-a213-211c74dbcf2d) described as a “commitment to preserving night skies through responsible lighting practices, environmental stewardship and ongoing efforts to reduce light pollution in central Idaho's high alpine environment.”

"At Sun Valley, the outdoors has always been at the center of the guest experience," [said Pete Sonntag](https://www.facebook.com/sunvalley/posts/pfbid02RKGNq3k8Z7ijwXHCRUeouiEdxRmP1QYvA11raZhWBF3q7iKC3y2dPK6gqvkhsK5sl), COO at Sun Valley Resort. "Preserving the natural beauty of this place, including the night sky, is an important part of protecting what makes the experience here so special for future generations. We hope this certification raises awareness and positions Sun Valley as a model for how resorts can do right by the environment and their guests."

To achieve the certification, the resort said it complied with DarkSky standards, which include covering habitat protection, public awareness efforts regarding light pollution and attempts to limit artificial lighting around the mountain.

A representative for Sun Valley said the certification shows “world-class hospitality and environmental stewardship can coexist industry-wide.”

Sun Valley Resort shared the following list of stargazing spots in a press release:

- *Bald Mountain (Roundhouse) – The marquee experience with panoramic sky views above the valley light*
- *Anywhere out Trail Creek (east of Sun Valley) – A classic Sun Valley setting with open meadow views and minimal surrounding light*
- *Dollar Mountain Lodge – Wide open skies with a low ambient light footprint, perfect for stargazing without a telescope*
- *Sun Valley Resort – Resort-wide dark sky compliance means even the village offers a strong stargazing baseline - Check out the Sun Valley Pavilion Lawn, Whitecloud Trail System and the Hemingway Memorial areas.*
- *Galena Summit (nearby, Hwy 75) – One of the darkest spots in the Central Idaho Dark Sky Reserve, approximately 30 miles north of Sun Valley*

The first major event after the certification is "Stars Over Sun Valley," scheduled for the peak of the Perseid Meteor Shower, on Aug.. 12 at Sun Valley Resort. Tickets are $125 per person.
