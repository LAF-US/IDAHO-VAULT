The 2026 legislative session is here, and it’s time to dust off the crystal ball and put it to good use.

There’s a lot happening as lawmakers return to the Capitol, and the upcoming elections only add to the intrigue.

Without further delay, here are a few things I think will (and won’t) happen this year.

**All of the constitutional officers will win re-election:** This might be the easiest and safest bet of them all. The primary is roughly five months away, and not a single serious challenger has emerged for any of the seven statewide offices. There are myriad reasons this could be happening, but mostly I think it’s because a ton of change is coming in 2028 and 2030. Statewide politicians are calculating and waiting to see what Brad Little, Jim Risch, Mike Crapo, and Mike Simpson do in the coming years. The 2026 cycle will be chaotic, but it won’t hold a candle to what’s coming in 2028 and 2030.

**Idaho will not repeal the free college program or Medicaid expansion:** House Speaker Mike Moyle danced around the Medicaid expansion repeal issue at a press conference last week. He told reporters lawmakers need to fund fixes to the program but stopped short of promising repeal. That might be because Gov. Brad Little is a fan of expansion and would veto a repeal bill. Is this Legislature courageous enough to buck Little on this? Absolutely not.

The same goes for Launch, the $80 million free college scheme Little championed. Moyle has quietly signaled support for the handout program, and while social media warriors demand it be scrapped, lawmakers won’t listen.

**Republican numbers in the Capitol will shrink:** There was a common refrain at the Idaho GOP Winter Meetings in Meridian this weekend: fundraising is slow. That’s not good when activists will likely put pro-abortion and pro-weed initiatives before voters, driving up leftist turnout. While some lawmakers in reliably red districts will survive the onslaught, many will not. I expect Sen. Codi Galloway, Rep. Tonya Burgoyne, Rep. Jack Nelsen, Rep. Mike Pohanka, and Rep. Dori Healey will not survive this election year. With few pickup opportunities left for the Idaho GOP, the Republican statehouse caucus will shrink.

**Neither Scott Herndon nor Branden Durst will return to the Statehouse:** Former state Sen. Scott Herndon will once again go to war with leftist Republican Jim Woodward for the District 1 Senate seat. Alas, he will not prevail. Herndon angered a number of conservatives when he supported the legislative pay hike last year. His backing of Branden Durst during the West Bonner School District debacle didn’t help, either. Beyond that, District 1 is somewhat purple, and the left has figured out how to organize there. I don’t think Herndon can beat Woodward in a primary, and even if he could, there’s a solid chance he’d lose in the general election with abortion on the ballot.

Durst is a slightly different story. He’s reeling and has lost consecutive GOP primary races. In fact, Durst hasn’t won anything since he was elected to represent southeast Boise as a Democrat about a decade ago. He’s taking on Camille Blaylock, who should never have won her seat while holding an F rating from the NRA. That said, Durst is divisive and off-putting. He will struggle to convince conservatives he’s one of them, and his considerable baggage will cost him yet another election.

**The Legislature will not repeal the grocery tax:** This should shock no one. Lawmakers overspent for years, and the state faces a $555 million budget deficit in Fiscal Year 2027, which begins July 1. There will be no standalone tax cut bill this year, and the state’s federal tax code conformity bill will be marketed as a tax cut by Republicans eager to deliver something—anything—for conservatives. Beyond that, Mike Moyle continues to oppose repeal for no good reason. He’s blocked it dozens of times while allowing far more liberal bills, including Launch, to receive hearings and passage through the Capitol.

**The grocery tax repeal measure will not make the ballot:** Dorothy Moon and the Idaho Republican Party will fail to gather enough signatures to put grocery tax repeal on the ballot in 2026. That’s by design. This is a total setup—though it wasn’t originally intended to be one. The party has the infrastructure and organization to get this done, but it isn’t doing it. Moon will tell you how hard she tried, but that’s simply not true. No one is devoting meaningful time or energy to the effort, and a proposal to form a subcommittee to work on it was recently shot down by Moon herself.

**Idaho will defeat the abortion and weed initiatives:** I have no idea exactly how this will get done, but I still have faith Idahoans believe in life. I also think there are enough ex-Californians here to tip the scales against the marijuana initiative.

**Moyle will attack free speech:** Last year, Mike Moyle introduced two bills hostile to free speech, including one to let a State Ministry of Truth fact-check campaign ads. Really. Moyle’s critics have grown louder and more numerous, and the Speaker doesn’t take kindly to criticism. Recent polling by an unknown group and the Idaho GOP suggest something in the works to silence or at least scare Moyle’s critics. It won’t pass, but it will again expose Moyle as a thin-skinned tyrant.

I think that’s it—for now. I may update this list as the year progresses, and I’ll be sure to timestamp each update as I do.
