A powerful Republican Speaker of the House is looking for ways to make out-of-staters pay for his state’s government costs.

Stop me if you’ve heard this one before.

Utah’s Republican Speaker of the House, Mike Schultz, wants to rework his state’s tax code so that out-of-state residents help pay to upgrade Utah’s roads and bridges. The measure would also give a significant tax break to Utah drivers, while hiking gas prices for drivers in Nevada, Idaho, and elsewhere.

I won’t get into all of the details, but the short version is this: gas produced and refined in Utah but shipped out of state is exempt from Utah’s gas tax. That makes sense, because the gas tax has historically been seen as a user fee to cover road and bridge repairs. Buy gas in Idaho, you’re driving in Idaho and paying for the privilege.

The Utah Speaker would change that by taxing gas at the refinery before it’s shipped out of state. Utah drivers could see a 15-cent-per-gallon drop in gas prices, while it’s unclear just how much more Idaho drivers would pay.

The proposal is nothing more than a Republican ploy to get someone else to foot the bill for government.

The plan has caught the attention of another powerful Speaker of the House: Idaho Republican Mike Moyle. In a clip posted by The Ranch Podcast, [Moyle slams the idea](https://youtube.com/shorts/iokf3ri72iw?si=mi0Xe4_K0VlURN-D).

There is deep irony here. Moyle himself is the architect of a similar policy.

Moyle has been Idaho’s biggest defender of Idaho’s grocery tax, blocking bills to repeal it dozens of times. One of his primary arguments for keeping the immoral tax?

Idaho would lose revenue from out-of-staters if the state were to repeal the grocery tax.

In fact, just last year, Moyle complained about the prospect of losing that revenue.

“If you get rid of the grocery tax, then you don’t get anything from the out-of-staters that come in here and buy groceries,” Moyle said in a press conference last January.

Huh.

Moyle and his apologists, now including longtime repeal proponent Heather Scott, claim the state takes in roughly $34 million from tourists buying groceries in Idaho. Their math is fuzzy and not supported by much data, because retailers do not track that information for the state.

The answer for Utah and Idaho is simpler.

Cut spending to provide tax relief, instead of looking for someone else to foot the bill for government’s myriad programs, commissions, boards, and agencies.

This should be a slam dunk in Idaho, a state traditionally seen as a conservative stronghold. Yet Moyle has overseen dramatic growth in state government.

The conservative Idaho Freedom Foundation revealed that Moyle presided over a 60% increase in government spending over the last six years, adding more than $5 billion to the budget.

Instead of looking for someone else to pay the bill, Moyle and company should end the free college handout, end Medicaid expansion, and cut the public schools budget. Doing those three things alone would save nearly $200 million annually and open the door for broad-based tax cuts.

There’s nothing conservative about growing government by forcing someone else to pay for it.
