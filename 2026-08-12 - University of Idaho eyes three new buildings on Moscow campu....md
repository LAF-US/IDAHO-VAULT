# University of Idaho eyes three new buildings on Moscow campus

## University seeking state approval for projects, including ‘Top Secret Facility’

[

![Anthony Kuipers avatar](https://public-assets-prod.pubgen.ai/brand_b31a1432-2c12-4a67-8456-0baf383b7aac/asset_37b14eed-73b1-4bc7-89e5-32040363ce5c.jpg?w=3840)

Anthony KuipersNews reporter

](https://www.dnews.com/writers/anthony-kuipers)

![Story image](https://public-assets-prod.pubgen.ai/brand_4fc3eb37-4c8a-4951-9324-365f6e4b53fd/asset_e064beb1-1dee-4685-8bcc-4ebc3da81b22.png?w=3840)

The University of Idaho is asking the state for approval to construct three new buildings on the Moscow campus, including a “Top Secret Facility.”

The Idaho State Board of Education will consider these capital projects during its meetings Wednesday and Thursday.

According to meeting materials, the Top Secret Facility is a proposed 3,360-square-foot research facility for the College of Engineering. It is meant to support “advanced cybersecurity and critical infrastructure research,” according to UI.

It will meet federal security requirements and be used to partner with government and national laboratories for projects requiring a high level of security clearance.

The UI says its current cybersecurity research is conducted in facilities not designed to support secure or classified research, which limits the type of work the researchers can do.

The UI is asking for $5.6 million in state funds for the $8.3 million project.

The most expensive project is a proposed 100,000-square-foot, four-story building that will serve as a new engineering and science facility.

The total project cost is $115 million.

The building will offer laboratories, specialized equipment and research environments to train students in engineering, science, healthcare and “emerging technologies,” according to UI.

The UI says available laboratory space on campus is limited and many of its engineering and science programs are held in facilities with outdated infrastructure.

The university is also planning to create a Joint Military Science and Veterans Assistance Center facility.

This facility is supposed to serve the Reserve Officers’ Training Corps (ROTC) and various branches of the military that are hosted at UI.

The UI claims the new Veterans Assistance Center will provide customer assistance, support, programs and wellness services to veterans and their dependents during their academic career.

The UI is asking the state for permission to spend $1.7 million on the building’s design. The full project cost is estimated to be $15.8 million.
