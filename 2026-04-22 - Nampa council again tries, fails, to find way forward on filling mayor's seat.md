The Nampa City Council continues to grapple with which person to appoint as its next mayor, including whether to consider two potential appointees the council already voted against.

The council held a special meeting on Monday to discuss next steps in the selection process put in motion by the death of Nampa Mayor Rick Hogaboam in March. Monday’s discussion followed a special council meeting on April 13 in which the council voted against appointing former Nampa Mayor Debbie Kling to the post, and the city’s current chief of staff, Clay Long, and members expressed interest in a formal application process, [as BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2026/04/15/nampa-mayoral-appointees-april-15/).

But by Monday, most council members said they would prefer not to initiate the collection of applications through a formal application process, saying it could prolong the appointment process. Council members said they would like to propose potential appointees and schedule council meetings to ask the prospects questions and discuss their responses and experience in a public interview process.

The council discussed several potential appointees to interview: Debbie Kling and Clay Long, who were previously considered, as well as Darl Bruner and Ben Adams. However, the meeting ended without a future meeting date, and with a lack of consensus on which candidates should be invited to interview.

## City leaders, and a senator

During the April 13 meeting, the council already declined to appoint Kling and Long as mayor in two different votes, as BoiseDev reported. But on Monday, Council Member Natalie Jangula said she wanted to give potential appointees the same consideration and, if the council were to move forward with a candidate interview process, she wanted Kling and Long to have the same opportunity.

“We didn’t really have an opportunity to fully discuss them or ask questions,” Jangula said. She said she had tallied the 104 comments she received from the public — 66% supported appointing Kling, 18% Long, and 16% others.

But some council members said they did not believe interviewing Kling and Long would change the outcome of the council’s previous 4-2 votes against each of them.

“Regarding Debbie Kling, I have worked with her for eight years,” Council Member Victor Rodriguez said.

Rodriguez voted against Kling’s appointment on April 13. “I don’t know of any conversation that would change my vote,” though he said he would be amenable to interviewing them.

Council Member Debbie Skaug floated Bruner for consideration. Bruner served on the council for 8 years. In 2024, he retired from working as Northwest Nazarene University’s director of alumni relations, a role he occupied for 30 years, [according to a statement from the university](https://nnu.edu/nnu-announces-new-director-of-alumni-relations/).

All council members expressed interest in interviewing Bruner. Council Member Dale Reynolds said he was concerned that Bruner stated in a letter given to the council that he could work 45 hours per week on average, and that consequently, he would accept a reduced salary rate.

“I see this as lacking sufficient hours to get the job fully accomplished,” Council Member Dale Reynolds said. “I understand that position, Darl, because of where you’re at with, you’re retired, and I don’t blame you, but we’re the third largest city in Idaho. It’s very much a full-time job, and there’s a lot that goes into it.”

Council Member Victor Rodriguez suggested Ben Adams as a potential appointee. Adams was elected to the Idaho Senate in 2021, and assumed office in 2022. He is currently running for reelection.

Though council members voiced support for interviewing Adams, some said they were concerned that he did not have experience in city business.

## Three motions, two votes

Ultimately, the council was divided on which potential appointees to move forward with.

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

A vote to simply meet to discuss further on April 27 ended in a tie vote.

Council Member Sebastian Griffin then moved to appoint Darl Bruner. Council members said they would rather proceed with some kind of interview process. The motion deadlocked in a tie.

Council President David Bills, who is currently acting as mayor, moved to interview only Long and Bruner. But Skaug said she wanted Adams to be interviewed, and Jangula said she would not support the vote. Bills declined to modify his motion to add Kling and Adams.

![SERVPRO Boise - Jan25](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/5b2a85ae-933e-4c98-82a5-e207d65304e9.png)

Jangula said, tearfully, “I think that this process needs to allow for all of us to have equal opportunity to bring names forward. I’m not here to change anybody’s vote, and I probably won’t, but I’m not okay going through a process and not allowing everyone equal opportunity.”

Bills said he would withdraw the motion and adjourned the meeting.
