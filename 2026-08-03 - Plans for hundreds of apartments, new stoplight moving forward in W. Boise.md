An apartment complex approved by the City of Boise nearly four years ago is finally in the design review phase.

In 2022, the Boise City Council approved the development of Westlock Village, an apartment complex proposed for 9474 W. State St. by Kinterra Property Group.

This spring and summer, the developer submitted plans for design review for the project.

## More apartments, similar amenities, a multi-year construction timeline

Current design review plans under consideration by city staff call for 252 apartments spread between six three-story buildings. When the project was approved in 2022, it called for 224 apartments spread between five buildings, [BoiseDev reported](https://boisedev.com/news/2022/08/08/matlock-village-state-st/).

Current plans show similar amenities to the initial plans, including a dog park, a one-story clubhouse, and a pool.

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/plans-for-hundreds-of-apartment-homes-new-stoplight-moving-forward-in-w-boise-Screenshot-2026-07-2.jpeg)

A preliminary plat for Westlock Village. Map: Via KM Engineering

A tentative work timeline submitted by the developer estimates the completion of the city’s design review process in late August, with a final plat recorded by October 9. Work could begin in mid-December on tasks like excavation, dry utilities, and sewer, and would run through May 2027, followed by construction.

The tentative timeline lists clubhouse construction starting in May and the building of residential buildings over the next couple of years.

It lists January 2, 2029, as the target date for the project to receive its certificate of occupancy.

## Plans to bring new stoplight to W. State St.

One key piece of the plans’ approval involved extending Ulmer Ln. through the project, and adding a stoplight at the intersection of Ulmer Ln. and State St.

![Boise Parks & Rec - Activity Guide August - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/2a472358-d841-43cb-92a9-38111f86cacf.png)

Boise Parks & Rec - Activity Guide August - 2:1

At the 2022 planning and zoning commission hearing for the project, residents expressed concern about overcrowding and traffic congestion that the project could generate, and the lack of a nearby stoplight. The closest stoplights to the project site currently — Horseshoe Bend Rd. to the west, and Bogart Ln. to the east — are about half a mile away in either direction.

Duncan Ln., which intersects with State St., marks the eastern border of the property. Though a representative of the project’s engineering team expressed interest in putting a stoplight on Duncan Ln., the commission approved the project with the stipulation that a stoplight be built at Ulmer Ln. in order for the project to receive a certificate of occupancy.

But building a signal proved difficult due to a need for the landowner to acquire right-of-way property on either side of Ulmer Ln. and across State St. to the south — some from Foothills Christian Church, and some from an office complex, as BoiseDev reported. The developer appealed the commission’s decision to the city council, hoping it would remove the stoplight requirement. The council voted to uphold the commission’s decision, echoing the need for a new stoplight in that stretch of State St.
