[Meridian’s “hole”](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/02/14/union-93-liens-lawsuits/) not only has new owners, it also has a new name.

As BoiseDev reported, the abandoned concrete and rebar structure sitting in the shadow of Meridian’s City Hall was promised to become Union 93 before [financial tumult brought the project to a halt.](https://boisedev.com/news/2024/09/12/union-93-meridian-forclosure/) In October, Treasure Valley developers Tommy Ahlquist of Ahlquist and Caleb Roope of Pacific Companies [purchased the property,](https://boisedev.com/news/2025/10/09/ahlquist-pacific-companies-union-93-meridian/) promising to turn a new leaf on the project.

Roope and Ahlquist had to wait through a six-month redemption period before work could be done to transform the property. Now, the rebranded Heritage Square is the newest promise for the land.

“We’re here to get something done and do the best job we can, and look forward to some really positive things developing from this project,” Roope said in a promotional video for Heritage Square. “For us, it’s just really an opportunity to fix a problem, but also to create a great place for people to come and shop and work and live.”

Plans for Heritage Square call for offices, retail, restaurants, public gathering spaces, and a few potential additions in the surrounding area.

## New plans, same location

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/IMG_4088-2048x1536.jpg)

Work has stopped on the Union 93 project in Meridian. (Photo: Don Day/BoiseDev)

For offices, a large building with 120,000 square feet of office space and 350 parking stalls is planned. Next to it, the unfinished concrete structure currently protruding above ground is planned to be repurposed into two multi-family apartment towers. The exact number of units and parking spaces for the towers is still being determined.

![Watkins Carter - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/f002ca13-0527-4831-9254-99ce5fa33461.jpg)

Watkins Carter - 2:1

According to Ahlquist, some reconfiguration is needed to the multi-family structure. While former project developer Galena Opportunity Fund and principal Bill Truax promised car stackers for their parking, Roope plans a more traditional parking garage. This will decrease the number of spaces available, but it will also bring down the cost.

Ahlquist said Roope is working with the City of Meridian, so his team can enter the structure to perform cleanup and preservation work so it can be used in the finished project. According to Ahlquist, exposed wood in the structure has rotted, and the structure itself needs “immediate attention” so it doesn’t degrade any further.

“Right now, we’re pretty optimistic that as long as we’re able to go do the work that we need to do to preserve it and get going on this, we’re going to be fine,” Ahlquist said.

![208 Market - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/2de7e0a8-34ae-41b8-b550-948663e8552e.gif)

Ahlquist also hopes to bring significant retail space to the area. While he and Roope are still working out the balance between retail and amenity space for the apartments, Ahlquist said he’d ideally like to see 70,000 to 80,000 square feet of retail space, with approximately 60% dedicated to restaurants.

Ahlquist said his goal at the end of this project is for people’s answer to the question “what do you want to do tonight?” to be “let’s go to downtown Meridian.”

“If we’re going to do that, we really want the plaza and the walkability of this to be like off-the-charts good, and so we want to start working right now with the city. In fact, we are saying, ‘Okay, what do we need? What are your priorities? How does this work? How does it fit with everything else going on?’ And then, ‘how do we create amenities that really make people want to come down here?’”

![Corwin Ford Nampa - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/02b5ccc2-e925-42c6-9e39-de8bd0223814.jpg)

Ahlquist said the company is planning on a public plaza with fountains, art, and other public amenities for the space as well.

“Heritage Square was very intentional. If you study the history of downtown Meridian, there’s a lot of it, and we want to do art and interactive family activities as part of the project that really embrace the heritage of downtown Meridian,” Ahlquist said.

Some of these heritage motifs include railroads and farming.

Ahlquist said he and Roope hope to begin construction on the project by the end of this year or the beginning of next year. He said it’s estimated that the project’s full buildout will take 20 to 24 months.

“For years, this corner represented what wasn’t happening. Soon, it will represent everything that’s possible when we come together,” Ahlquist said in a promotional video.

## ‘Destination Downtown’ in the works?

Big plans are also in the works for the surrounding area. Ahlquist says one of the amenities he’s hoping to bring into the area is a hotel.

![208 Market - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/2de7e0a8-34ae-41b8-b550-948663e8552e.gif)

208 Market - July 2026 - 2:1

Last week, Meridian’s urban renewal agency, Meridian Development Corporation, put out a request for proposals on a site located across the street from Heritage Square. According to the RFP documents, the agency is looking for a “quality commercial or mixed-use development” for the.67 acre property.

Ahlquist said additionally, MDC is looking into building a 1,200-seat performing arts center nearby, though things are in the very early stages. MDC Administrator Ashley Ford-Squyres said Babcock Design has completed phase one of a three-phase feasibility study for the agency. The other two phases, she said, are anticipated to be completed by the end of August.

“As a result, it is premature for MDC to comment on potential recommendations or conclusions at this time,” Ford-Squyres said.

![Corwin Ford Nampa - July 2026 - 2:1](https://static-v2.boisedev.com/assets/02b5ccc2-e925-42c6-9e39-de8bd0223814.jpg)

Corwin Ford Nampa - July 2026 - 2:1

One of the sites Babcock is studying is the Old Fashioned Feed Mill, which is owned by members of the Zamzows family. Ahlquist said he held meetings with the family about their property, but says things are still “very early.” MDC has not been involved in the meetings.

![](https://boisedev.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Screenshot-2026-07-1-Screenshot-2026-07-1-3.jpeg)

Aerial rendering of the proposed Heritage Square project in Meridian. A potential performing arts center is pictured on the right. Rendering: Via Babcock Design

“We are trying to help because it’s obviously helpful to our project if we can make it work to figure out a way to put a performing arts center in downtown Meridian as part of our project,” Ahlquist said.

It’s unclear what role urban renewal will play in the project. Earlier this year, Meridian Mayor Robert Simison said it’s possible the city and Meridian Development Corporation would need to sunset the Union District early to “fully maximize this opportunity.” Simision said if that happened, MDC could establish a new district. The surrounding Downtown District is scheduled to sunset at the end of 2026.

208 Market - July 2026 - 2:1

Ford-Squyres said “discussion is still ongoing at this time, and MDC, nor the city, has made any formal determinations related to that district.”
