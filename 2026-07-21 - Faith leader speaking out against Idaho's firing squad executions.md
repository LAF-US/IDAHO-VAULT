BOISE, Idaho — A new billboard went up in Boise with the message, "Don't shoot Tom Creech! Stop firing squad executions." It was paid for by the Execution Intervention Project. Their leader, Rev. Dr. Jeff Hood, is one of the most outspoken advocates against capital punishment in the nation.

Ada County prosecutors [described Creech as a serial killer](https://adacounty.id.gov/prosecutor/press-releases/ada-county-prosecutors-office-statement-following-thomas-creechs-commutation-hearing/) convicted in five separate murders. They said he had admitted to killing more than 40 people. Creech received his death sentence in 1983 after beating a fellow inmate to death.

Idaho lawmakers adopted the firing squad method after several failed attempts to execute Creech. The medical team members were unable to find a vein while trying to place an IV line for a lethal injection.

"But I was pulled to this place of saying, 'Idaho, don't shoot Tom Creech,' because I want the people of Idaho to understand that if Tom Creech is assassinated, killed, murdered in a firing squad execution, it is the people of Idaho who will be responsible," Hood said in a press conference on Tuesday as NonStop Local listened in.

Hood said he and his followers opposed not only firing squad executions but all execution methods.

The state Legislature approved firing squad as the primary means of execution in 2025.

At the time, [the Idaho Statesman quoted](https://www.idahostatesman.com/news/politics-government/state-politics/article273481685.html) part of a letter from Gov. Brad Little after he signed the bill.

"While I am signing this bill, it is important to point out that fulfilling justice can and must be done by minimizing stress on corrections personnel," Little said. "For the people on death row, a jury convicted them of their crimes and they were lawfully sentenced to death. It is the responsibility of the state of Idaho to follow the law and ensure that lawful criminal sentences are carried out."

Still, Hood and his supporters said the use of firing squad is cruel.

"I am concerned about the people of Idaho and their souls. Because this is evil. It's a warning. You don't have to do this. You don't have to have this on your conscience," Hood said. "If Governor Little is so interested in these firing squads happening, then he needs to pick up a gun himself."

Little responded to Hood's challenge in a statement to NonStop Local.

“I have long made clear my support of capital punishment. I support the Idaho Legislature’s actions in setting the policies around methods of execution in the State of Idaho. As governor, my job is to follow the law and ensure that lawful criminal sentences are carried out as ordered by the courts."

During Tuesday's conference, Hood also spoke about being in the execution chamber and touching inmates at the moment of death. Hood previously sued to be allowed to do this. When asked whether it is still something he would want if the execution was completed by firing squad, Hood said he would do so if asked in Idaho or anywhere else.
