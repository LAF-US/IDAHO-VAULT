# The Dragons

> The weapon at the center of the saga: living fire that made a minor exile family the masters of a continent — and could not, in the end, be kept. One of a trilogy with `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-House-Targaryen-2026-06-02]]` (the bloodline) and `[[RESEARCH_A-Song-of-Ice-and-Fire-Aegons-Conquest-2026-06-02]]` (the war).
>
> **Provenance.** Tiers flagged: **[canon]**, **[legend]** (in-world myth), **[theory]** (in-world/fan speculation the text plants), **[show]** (TV divergence), **[GRRM]** (authorial commentary). ⚠️ awoiaf/Wikipedia blocked direct fetch; claims rest on search-extracts — verify quotes against the books/*Fire & Blood* before publication.

## Nature & biology

- **[canon]** Fire-breathing winged reptiles with **two legs plus wings (wyvern-type anatomy), not four legs** — GRRM's rationale is that no real animal has six limbs, and the **HBO dragons follow this** (two hind legs, wings as forelimbs). ⚠️ The "four-legged dragon" turns up mainly in **heraldry and sigil art**, not the on-screen creatures.
- **[canon]** Dragons **never stop growing while alive and free** — age tracks size; confinement stunts them. A free dragon can reach a ~20-ft wingspan within ~18 months.
- **[canon]** Long-lived: 200+ years (Balerion died of old age at over 200, in 94 AC).
- **[canon]** **Dragonfire** melts steel and stone and fuses sand to glass.
- **[theory]** Dragons are bound up with **magic itself** — magic in the world is widely believed to **wax and wane with the number of living dragons** (their death ~153 AC tracked a weakening of magic; Daenerys's hatchlings, a resurgence). Stated as in-world belief, not confirmed mechanism.

## Origins

- **[canon]** The Valyrian account: dragons came from **Valyria**, tied to the **Fourteen Flames** (its ring of volcanoes).
- **[legend]** The Asshai'i account: dragons were **brought to Valyria from the Shadow Lands**, the Valyrians taught to tame them by a vanished people.
- **[theory]** **Septon Barth** speculated Valyrian **bloodmages bred dragons from wyvern stock** (with fire-wyrms said to predate them in the Flames). ⚠️ Countered in-world by Maester Vanyon's claim that dragons existed in Westeros *before* Valyria rose. (→ the Barth thread in the Doom note.)

## Dragonlords & dragonbinding

- **[canon]** Valyria's ~40 **dragonlord families** combined wealth, high birth, and **sorcery** to bind and ride dragons in war.
- **[canon]** Control ran through **binding spells and sorcerous horns**. **Dragonbinder** ("the hellhorn") — a Valyrian-steel-banded dragon horn whose glyphs read, in part, *"Blood for fire, fire for blood"*; whoever sounds it **dies** (lungs charred). Per **Moqorro's** reading of the glyphs the horn binds dragons to its master — but ⚠️ *[theory]* in the published books that is only his interpretation; **the horn has not yet been tested on a dragon on-page.** The recurring motif: a dragon is *said* to be commanded only by blood, sacrifice, and magic — never simply *owned*.

## The Targaryen dragons

- **[canon]** House Targaryen brought **five dragons** to Dragonstone before the Doom.
- **BALERION, the Black Dread** — Aegon I's mount; jet-black, titanic; the **last living creature to have seen Valyria before the Doom**; his fire **forged the Iron Throne**. Later ridden by Maegor I, Aerea, and Viserys; died of old age in **94 AC**, over 200 years old.
- **VHAGAR** (Visenya's) and **MERAXES** (Rhaenys's) — the Conquest's other two; all three fought together only at the **Field of Fire**. Meraxes was downed in Dorne (10 AC); Vhagar lived to become the monstrous aged dragon of the **Dance of the Dragons**.
- **Dance dragons** *(from* Fire & Blood; *dramatized in* House of the Dragon*)*: **Vermithor** ("the Bronze Fury," Jaehaerys I's), **Sunfyre** (Aegon II's, golden — devoured Rhaenyra), **Caraxes** ("the Blood Wyrm," Daemon's), **Syrax** (Rhaenyra's), **Meleys**, **Vermax**, **Seasmoke**, **Tessarion**.

## Decline & extinction

- **[canon]** The **Dance of the Dragons (129–131 AC)** killed nearly all the dragons. The survivors **shrank and sickened**; the last clutches failed to hatch.
- **[canon]** The **last dragon died young in 153 AC**, under **Aegon III "the Dragonbane"** — a small, stunted, withered green female; her five eggs never hatched.
- **[theory]** Why they died out: rumor that **Aegon III poisoned** them (he loathed dragons after watching Sunfyre eat his mother); the **maester conspiracy** (→ the Maesters node); magic waning; and **confinement in the Dragonpit** stunting them (consistent with the size-tracks-freedom biology). Nine Essosi mages were brought to hatch eggs by magic — and **failed**.
- **[canon]** The **Storming of the Dragonpit** (during the Dance): a starving King's Landing mob led by "the Shepherd" broke into the Dragonpit and **killed four dragons** (Shrykos, Morghul, Tyraxes, Dreamfyre); a fifth, Syrax, died above the city. The great dome was broken and never restored — the people, en masse, killing the gods'-fire that ruled them.

## Daenerys's dragons

- **[canon]** **DROGON** (black), **RHAEGAL** (green), **VISERION** (cream-gold) — hatched from three petrified eggs on **Khal Drogo's funeral pyre**, with the maegi **Mirri Maz Duur** burned in it (*"only death can pay for life"*): the **first dragons in ~150 years**. Named for Drogo, Rhaegar, and Viserys. "Fire made flesh." ⚠️ *[show]* Viserion's resurrection as an ice dragon is show-only; no book canon.

## Dragonglass & Valyrian steel

- **[canon]** **Dragonglass = obsidian** (Valyrians: *"frozen fire"*) — sharper than steel, brittle, and one of the few things that **kills the Others**. ⚠️ *[GRRM]* in-world obsidian has magical properties real obsidian lacks.
- **[theory/implied]** **Valyrian steel** was spell-forged in the Fourteen Flames, *"almost certainly with the assistance of dragonfire"* — the method lost in the Doom, so the dragonfire link is implied, not confirmed.

## Theme — the nuclear deterrent

**[GRRM]** Martin has called the dragons **"the nuclear deterrent"** — Daenerys with three of them is "the most powerful person in the world" — *but* added that the power to destroy "doesn't mean we can achieve specific geopolitical goals; power is more subtle than that." **[reading]** It threads the saga's spine: a dragon, like the Iron Throne it forged, is **power that cannot be safely held** — bound only by blood and sacrifice, lethal to its wielder when it slips the leash, and (the Dragonpit) able to be torn down by the very people it was meant to overawe. (→ the Empty Office note.)
